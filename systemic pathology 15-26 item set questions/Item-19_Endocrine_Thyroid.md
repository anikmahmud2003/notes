# Part A — Thyroid Neoplasms & Hyperthyroidism (Q1–4)

## Q1. Name the thyroid neoplasms.

### Classification of Thyroid Neoplasms

| Category | Types |
|---|---|
| **Benign** | **Follicular adenoma** (solitary, encapsulated, intact capsule — the key feature), Hürthle cell (oxyphil) adenoma |
| **Malignant — Follicular epithelium** | **Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) 80–85%**, **Follicular thyroid carcinoma (FTC) 10–15%**, Poorly differentiated carcinoma, **Anaplastic (undifferentiated) carcinoma <5%** |
| **Malignant — Parafollicular C cells** | **Medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC) ~5%** |
| **Other** | Primary thyroid lymphoma (usually diffuse large B-cell or MALT; often in background of Hashimoto), Squamous cell carcinoma (rare), Metastases to thyroid |

### Key Molecular Features

| Tumor | Key Genetics |
|---|---|
| **Papillary** | **BRAF V600E 50–80%**, RET/PTC fusions, NTRK fusions |
| **Follicular** | **RAS 30–50%**, PAX8–PPARG fusion t(2;3), PIK3CA |
| **Anaplastic** | **TP53**, CTNNB1 (β-catenin), TERT |
| **Medullary** | **RET** (germline in MEN-2; ~50% somatic in sporadic) |

### Precursor Relationships
```
Papillary microcarcinoma → Conventional PTC
NIFTP → Encapsulated follicular variant PTC
Follicular adenoma → Follicular carcinoma (with capsular/vascular invasion)
```

> 🎯 "PTC = most common (85%); FTC = capsular/vascular invasion; MTC = C cells + calcitonin + amyloid + RET; Anaplastic = TP53, death <1 yr."

## Q2. Define hyperthyroidism and thyrotoxicosis.

### Definitions

🔴 **Thyrotoxicosis = the clinical syndrome of excess thyroid hormone action in the tissues, regardless of source.** This is the broader term — it can result from overproduction of thyroid hormones, release of preformed hormones (thyroiditis), exogenous hormone intake, or ectopic sources.

🔴 **Hyperthyroidism = a specific cause of thyrotoxicosis where the thyroid gland itself is overfunctioning.** This is the subset of thyrotoxicosis due to increased synthesis and secretion of thyroid hormones by the thyroid gland.

### Relationship
- **All hyperthyroidism causes thyrotoxicosis** (but not all thyrotoxicosis is hyperthyroidism)
- **Thyrotoxicosis WITHOUT hyperthyroidism:** subacute thyroiditis (viral release of preformed hormones), struma ovarii (ectopic thyroid tissue in ovarian teratoma), factitious thyrotoxicosis (exogenous thyroxine ingestion), ectopic thyroid tissue
- **TSH level distinguishes:** In true hyperthyroidism, TSH is suppressed (except TSH-secreting pituitary adenoma). In thyroiditis/factitious/ectopic, TSH is also suppressed (low radioiodine uptake differentiates these from Graves)

### Clinical Features of Thyrotoxicosis
- **Hypermetabolic state:** weight loss despite increased appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, warm moist skin, increased BMR
- **Cardiac:** tachycardia, palpitations, atrial fibrillation (older patients), heart failure
- **Sympathetic overactivity:** tremor, anxiety, insomnia, lid lag, wide staring gaze
- **GI:** hyperdefecation
- **Musculoskeletal:** proximal muscle weakness (thyroid myopathy), osteoporosis
- **Reproductive:** menstrual irregularities, gynecomastia in men
- **Thyroid storm:** abrupt severe thyrotoxicosis — fever + tachycardia out of proportion; medical emergency; untreated → cardiac arrhythmia → death

> 🎯 "Thyrotoxicosis = excess hormone in tissues (any cause); hyperthyroidism = thyroid overfunction. TSH is the best single screening test."

## Q3. Mention the causes of thyrotoxicosis.

### Causes of Thyrotoxicosis

#### A. With Hyperthyroidism (TSH ↓) — Thyroid is Overactive

| Cause | % of Cases | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Graves disease** 🔴 | **~85%** | Most common cause; diffuse goiter + TSI (thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin); diffuse ↑ radioiodine uptake |
| **Toxic multinodular goiter (Plummer disease)** | ~10–15% | Autonomous nodules in long-standing MNG; ↑ radioiodine uptake in hot nodules |
| **Toxic adenoma (solitary toxic nodule)** | ~3–5% | Single hyperfunctioning nodule; TSHR or GNAS mutations; ↑ uptake in nodule, suppressed surrounding tissue |
| **Iodine-induced (Jod-Basedow)** | Rare | Excess iodine in predisposed individuals (e.g., amiodarone, contrast dye) |
| **Neonatal thyrotoxicosis** | Rare | Maternal Graves → transplacental TSI → fetal/neonatal thyrotoxicosis |

#### B. Secondary Hyperthyroidism (TSH ↑ or inappropriately normal)
- 🔴 **TSH-secreting pituitary adenoma** — the ONLY cause with TSH elevated

#### C. Without Hyperthyroidism (TSH ↓) — Hormone Release, Not Overproduction

| Cause | Key Features |
|---|---|
| **Subacute granulomatous (de Quervain) thyroiditis** | Painful thyroid; viral; ↓ radioiodine uptake |
| **Subacute lymphocytic (painless) thyroiditis** | Postpartum in up to 5%; autoantibodies |
| **Struma ovarii** | Ovarian teratoma with ectopic functional thyroid tissue |
| **Factitious thyrotoxicosis** | Exogenous thyroxine intake; low thyroglobulin |
| **Ectopic thyroid tissue** | Struma lymphomatosa, metastatic follicular carcinoma (rare) |

> 🎯 "Graves = 85% of thyrotoxicosis; thyroiditis causes thyrotoxicosis WITHOUT hyperthyroidism (↓ radioiodine uptake)."

## Q4. Enumerate the causes of hyperthyroidism.

### Causes of Hyperthyroidism

🔴 **Hyperthyroidism = thyroid gland overproduces hormones → TSH suppressed (except pituitary cause)**

| Cause | Mechanism | Key Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Graves disease** | Autoantibodies (TSI) stimulate TSH receptor → diffuse hyperplasia | Most common (~85%); diffuse goiter with bruit; ophthalmopathy; dermopathy; ↑ diffuse radioiodine uptake |
| **Toxic MNG** | Autonomous nodules secrete hormones independent of TSH | Long-standing MNG → 10% develop autonomous nodules; ↑ uptake in hot nodules |
| **Toxic adenoma** | Somatic TSHR/GNAS mutations → autonomous function | Single hot nodule; female predominance |
| **TSH-secreting pituitary adenoma** | Pituitary tumor secretes TSH → thyroid stimulation | Rare; only cause with ↑ TSH; MRI shows pituitary mass |
| **Struma ovarii** | Ectopic thyroid tissue in ovarian teratoma produces T3/T4 | Low radioiodine uptake in neck; high uptake in pelvis |
| **Iodine-induced** | Excess iodine substrate in predisposed | Amiodarone type 1 vs type 2; contrast dye |
| **Gestational trophoblastic disease** | hCG mimics TSH | High hCG → transient hyperthyroidism |
| **Metastatic follicular carcinoma** | Functional thyroid tissue in mets | Very rare |

### Graves Disease vs Toxic MNG vs Toxic Adenoma — Key Differentiation

| Feature | Graves | Toxic MNG | Toxic Adenoma |
|---|---|---|---|
| Goiter type | **Diffuse** | Multinodular | Solitary nodule |
| Radioiodine uptake | **Diffuse ↑** | Focal ↑ (hot nodules) | Focal ↑ (single hot nodule) |
| TSH receptor antibodies | **TSI +** | − | − |
| Eye changes | **Present (ophthalmopathy)** | Absent | Absent |
| Age | 20–40 yr | Older adults | 30s–50s |

> 🎯 "Graves = diffuse ↑ uptake + ophthalmopathy; toxic MNG = focal hot nodules in long-standing goiter; toxic adenoma = single hot nodule."

# Part B — Thyroiditis & Goiter (Q5–7)

## Q5. Short note: Hashimoto thyroiditis.

### Definition
🔴 **Hashimoto thyroiditis (chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis) = the most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient areas** and the most common nonendemic goiter in children. An autoimmune disorder characterized by thyroid destruction by autoreactive lymphocytes.

### Epidemiology
- Peak: **45–65 years**; **F:M = 10–20:1**
- Most common cause of primary hypothyroidism in developed countries
- Associated with other autoimmune diseases (T1D, SLE, myasthenia gravis, Sjögren syndrome, pernicious anemia)

### Pathogenesis
🔴 **Breakdown of self-tolerance** → lymphocytic attack on thyroid follicular cells:
1. **CD8+ cytotoxic T-cell killing** of follicular cells (primary mechanism)
2. **CD4+ Th1 cells → IFN-γ → macrophage activation** → tissue damage
3. **Antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC)** — minor role

**Autoantibodies:**
- 🔴 **Anti-thyroid peroxidase (anti-TPO)** — in vast majority (>90%)
- 🔴 **Anti-thyroglobulin** — in ~60–80%
- Both are markers of autoimmune destruction, not primary mediators

**Susceptibility genes:** HLA-DR3, DR5; CTLA4, PTPN22, IL2RA (shared with Graves and T1D)

### Morphology
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Gross** | Diffuse, symmetric enlargement of both thyroid lobes; firm, pale, lobulated cut surface |
| **Microscopy** | 🔴 Dense **lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate** with prominent **germinal centers**; atrophic thyroid follicles lined by **Hürthle (oxyphil/oncocytes) cells** — abundant eosinophilic granular cytoplasm (packed mitochondria, a metaplastic response to chronic injury); **minimal/no colloid**; fibrosis (does NOT extend beyond capsule vs Riedel) |

### Clinical Features
- **Painless, firm, diffuse goiter** (the "classic" presentation)
- Gradual onset of **hypothyroidism:** fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance, constipation, dry skin, menstrual irregularities, depression
- **Hashitoxicosis** — transient phase of thyrotoxicosis (from follicular disruption releasing preformed hormones) → hypothyroidism
- In some cases: presents as hypothyroidism from the start (no goiter phase)

### Associations
- Other autoimmune diseases (T1D, Addison, pernicious anemia, Sjögren, MG, celiac)
- 🔴 **↑ risk of extranodal marginal zone B-cell lymphoma** (MALT lymphoma) of thyroid
- Possible predisposition to papillary carcinoma (controversial)

### Treatment
- **Levothyroxine replacement** (lifelong for hypothyroidism)
- Monitoring TSH levels

> 🎯 "Hashimoto = anti-TPO + anti-Tg + Hürthle cells + lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate + germinal centers → hypothyroidism. Most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient areas."

## Q6. What do you mean by goiter? Tell its pathogenesis/ Give the pathogenesis of goiter in iodine deficiency.

### Definition
🔴 **Goiter = abnormal enlargement of the thyroid gland.** It is a descriptive term, not a specific diagnosis. Can be diffuse or nodular, toxic or nontoxic.

### Classification
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Diffuse nontoxic (simple) goiter** | Symmetrical enlargement without nodules or hyperfunction |
| **Multinodular goiter (MNG)** | Long-standing goiter converted to irregular nodular enlargement |
| **Toxic goiter** | Goiter with hyperthyroidism (Graves, toxic MNG) |

### Pathogenesis of Goiter in Iodine Deficiency

🔴 **The fundamental mechanism: impaired thyroid hormone synthesis → compensatory TSH elevation → follicular hypertrophy/hyperplasia → thyroid enlargement.**

#### Step-by-Step Pathogenesis:

```
Iodine deficiency (dietary insufficiency)
   ↓
↓ Iodide trapping by thyroid follicular cells
   ↓
↓ Thyroid hormone (T3/T4) synthesis
   ↓
↓ Serum T3/T4 levels
   ↓
Loss of negative feedback on anterior pituitary
   ↑ TSH secretion
   ↓
TSH stimulates thyroid follicular cells
   → Hypertrophy (larger cells) + Hyperplasia (more cells)
   ↓
THYROID ENLARGEMENT = GOITER
   ↓
Compensatory increase in iodide trapping efficiency
   → May restore euthyroidism
   → Or persistent goiter with euthyroid state
```

#### Two Phases of Goiter Development:

| Phase | Morphology | Clinical |
|---|---|---|
| **Hyperplastic phase** | Follicles lined by crowded columnar cells; reduced colloid; mitoses present | Diffuse, firm enlargement; usually euthyroid |
| **Colloid involution phase** | Follicles lined by flattened cuboidal cells; abundant colloid accumulated | Soft, cystic enlargement; "colloid goiter" |

#### Goitrogens — Substances That Inhibit Thyroid Hormone Synthesis
- **Brassicaceae vegetables:** cabbage, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts, turnips
- **Cassava** — contains thiocyanate (inhibits iodide transport)
- **Drugs:** lithium, amiodarone, sulfonamides, aminobenzoic acid derivatives
- **Thiocyanate, perchlorate** — inhibit sodium-iodide symporter (NIS)

#### Endemic vs Sporadic Goiter
| Feature | Endemic | Sporadic |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | >10% population affected | Isolated cases |
| Cause | Iodine-deficient soil/water | Goitrogens, hereditary enzyme defects, drugs |
| Geography | Himalayas, Andes, central Africa, inland China | Worldwide |
| Frequency | 200 million at risk globally | Less common |

#### Dyshormonogenetic Goiter
- Inborn errors of thyroid hormone synthesis (autosomal recessive)
- Defects in: thyroglobulin synthesis, iodide trapping, organification, coupling, deiodinase
- Presents in childhood as large goiter with euthyroid or hypothyroid state

### Clinical Features
- **Goiter:** visible/palpable enlargement; may cause compressive symptoms (dysphagia, dyspnea, hoarseness)
- Most patients are **euthyroid** (compensated)
- Serum TSH at upper limit of normal
- Large goiters → compressive symptoms, cosmetic concerns, retrosternal extension

> 🎯 "Goiter = iodine deficiency → ↓T3/T4 → ↑TSH → follicular hypertrophy/hyperplasia → thyroid enlargement. Endemic = >10% population affected."

## Q7. What is Grave's disease? Give the pathogenesis of Grave's disease.

### Definition
🔴 **Graves disease = the most common cause of endogenous hyperthyroidism** (~85% of thyrotoxicosis cases). An autoimmune disorder characterized by stimulatory autoantibodies against the TSH receptor, causing diffuse thyroid hyperplasia, hyperthyroidism, and characteristic extrathyroidal manifestations.

### Epidemiology
- Peak: **20–40 years**
- **F:M = 10:1**
- 1.5–2% of US women
- Monozygotic concordance 30–40% (dizygotic <5%)
- Genetic associations: HLA-DR3, CTLA4, PTPN22, IL2RA, TSHR locus

### Pathogenesis — Detailed

🔴 **Core mechanism: thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin (TSI) mimics TSH → uncontrolled thyroid hormone production.**

#### The Autoimmune Cascade:

```
Loss of immune tolerance to TSH receptor
   ↓
B-cell activation → production of autoantibodies
   ↓
TSH RECEPTOR ANTIBODIES:
   1. TSI (Thyroid-Stimulating Immunoglobulin) = ~90%
      → Mimics TSH → activates adenylyl cyclase → ↑cAMP
      → ↑ thyroid hormone synthesis + secretion
      → ↑ iodide uptake + organification
      → Thyroid growth (diffuse hyperplasia)
   2. Blocking antibodies (rare)
      → Block TSH binding → HYPOTHYROIDISM (rare presentation)
   3. Neutral antibodies (no functional effect)
```

#### Extrathyroidal Manifestations:

##### 1. Ophthalmopathy (30–50% of patients)
- 🔴 **CD4+ T cells** infiltrate retro-orbital tissues
- Release **cytokines** → stimulate fibroblasts → **glycosaminoglycan (GAG) deposition** in extraocular muscles and retro-orbital connective tissue
- **Edema, mononuclear cell infiltrate, ↑ adipocytes** → swelling of extraocular muscles
- **Proptosis (exophthalmos)** — eye pushed forward
- Can persist/progress despite treating thyrotoxicosis
- Severe → corneal exposure → ulceration; compressive optic neuropathy

##### 2. Pretibial Myxedema (Dermopathy) — <5% of patients
- Localized accumulation of **GAGs (hyaluronic acid)** in dermis of pretibial skin
- Non-pitting edema, thickened, "orange peel" appearance
- Usually over pretibial area (also dorsum of feet/hands)

##### 3. Thyroid Acropachy — Very rare
- Clubbing of fingers and toes
- Periosteal new bone formation

#### Why Does Autoimmunity Develop?
- Genetic susceptibility (HLA-DR3, CTLA4, PTPN22, TSHR polymorphisms)
- Environmental triggers: stress, smoking (↑ ophthalmopathy), iodine excess, postpartum state, infection
- Defective regulatory T-cells → failure to suppress autoreactive clones

### Morphology
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Gross** | Diffuse, symmetric enlargement (up to >80 g); beefy red "meaty" cut surface; no capsule |
| **Microscopy** | 🔴 **Tall, crowded follicular cells forming papillae WITHOUT fibrovascular cores** (vs papillary carcinoma); **scalloped colloid margins** (active resorption); T-cell–predominant lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate with germinal centers |
| **Iodine pretreatment** | Involution + colloid accumulation → may mimic MNG |

### Clinical Features
- **Goiter:** diffuse, symmetric, non-tender, firm; **audible bruit** (increased vascularity)
- **Hyperthyroidism:** weight loss, heat intolerance, tremor, tachycardia, palpitations
- **Eye signs:** lid lag, wide staring gaze (sympathetic overactivity) + true exophthalmos (ophthalmopathy)
- **Skin:** warm, moist, pretibial myxedema
- **Cardiac:** atrial fibrillation (older patients), high-output heart failure
- **Thyroid storm:** fever + tachycardia out of proportion → medical emergency

### Diagnosis
| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| **TSH** | **Suppressed** (best single screening test) |
| **Free T4/T3** | **Elevated** |
| **TSH receptor antibodies (TRAb/TSI)** | **Positive** (~90%) |
| **Radioiodine uptake** | 🔴 **Diffusely increased** (vs focal in toxic adenoma/MNG; decreased in thyroiditis) |
| **Anti-TPO** | May be positive (coexists with other autoimmune diseases) |

### Treatment
| Modality | Details |
|---|---|
| **β-blockers** | Propranolol — symptomatic relief (tachycardia, tremor, anxiety) |
| **Thionamides** | Methimazole or PTU — block thyroid hormone synthesis; first-line medical therapy |
| **Radioactive iodine (I-131) ablation** | Definitive treatment; destroys thyroid tissue; takes 6–18 weeks; results in hypothyroidism requiring levothyroxine |
| **Thyroidectomy** | For large compressive goiters, pregnancy (2nd trimester), or failure of other treatments |

### Association with Other Autoimmune Diseases
- T1D, Addison disease, pernicious anemia, SLE, vitiligo, myasthenia gravis

> 🎯 "Graves = TSI → TSH receptor stimulation → diffuse hot goiter + ophthalmopathy + dermopathy. Diffuse ↑ radioiodine uptake. Scalloped colloid. 10:1 F:M."

# Part C — Papillary Carcinoma & Investigation (Q8–9)

## Q8. Short note: Papillary carcinoma of thyroid.

### Definition
🔴 **Papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC) = the most common thyroid malignancy (~80–85% of thyroid cancers)** and the most common endocrine malignancy overall. Arises from follicular epithelial cells.

### Epidemiology
- Peak: **25–50 years** (younger than follicular)
- **F:M = 3–4:1**
- Incidence rising (more microcarcinomas found incidentally)
- Most common radiation-associated thyroid cancer (Chernobyl children)

### Pathogenesis
| Genetic Alteration | Frequency | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **BRAF V600E** | **50–80%** | Point mutation → constitutive MAPK activation; associated with classic and tall-cell variants; aggressive features |
| **RET/PTC fusions** | ~10–20% | Chromosomal rearrangement (10q11); radiation-associated; children > adults |
| **NTRK fusions** | ~5% | Fusion oncogenes; across all ages |
| **RAS** | ~10–15% | More in follicular variant PTC |

### Morphology — EXAM FAVORITE

#### Gross
- Single or multiple; may be multifocal (20–40%)
- Cut surface: white-gray, solid, well-circumscribed or infiltrative
- May show psammoma bodies (gritty sensation on cutting)

#### Microscopy — Nuclear Features 🔴🔴
🔴 **The nuclear features can diagnose PTC even WITHOUT papillae!**

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Orphan Annie eye nuclei** | Finely dispersed chromatin → **optically clear, ground-glass** appearance; most characteristic feature |
| **Nuclear grooves** | Longitudinal folds in the nuclear membrane |
| **Nuclear pseudo-inclusions** | Invaginations of cytoplasm into the nucleus (appear as intranuclear cytoplasmic inclusions) |
| **Psammoma bodies** | 🔴 **Concentrically calcified spheres** — essentially NEVER in follicular or medullary carcinoma; strong indicator of PTC on FNA |

#### Architectural Patterns
| Pattern | Description |
|---|---|
| **Classic (papillary)** | Branching papillae with fibrovascular cores lined by tumor cells |
| **Follicular variant** | Papillary nuclear features + follicular architecture |
| **Tall cell variant** | Tall cells (height >3× width); older patients; aggressive; virtually always BRAF V600E |
| **Diffuse sclerosing** | Children/young; dense fibrosis + squamous metaplasia; lymph node mets in almost all; RET/PTC ~½ |
| **Encapsulated follicular variant** | May reclassify as NIFTP if no invasion |

#### NIFTP (Noninvasive Follicular Thyroid Neoplasm with Papillary-like Nuclear Features)
- 🔴 **Encapsulated, no capsular/lymphatic/vascular invasion** → ultra-low risk
- "Carcinoma" removed from name
- **Lobectomy alone suffices** (avoids total thyroidectomy complications)
- Excellent prognosis

### Spread
- 🔴 **Lymphatic spread** is the dominant route → **cervical lymph node metastases** in up to 50%
- Blood vessel invasion is **uncommon** (especially in small lesions)
- May be **multifocal** (satellite lesions within the gland)
- Distant mets: lung, bone (rare, usually late)
- **Cervical lymph node mets do NOT worsen prognosis** (unlike in most other cancers)

### Clinical Features
- Asymptomatic thyroid nodule (most common presentation)
- **Cervical lymph node mass** — may be the presenting complaint (especially in children/young adults)
- Cold on scintigraphy
- Incidental finding on ultrasound (thyroid incidentaloma)

### Prognosis
- 🔴 **10-yr survival >95%** (excellent overall)
- Microcarcinomas (<1 cm): near-100% cure with lobectomy
- **Poorer prognosis with:** age >40, extrathyroidal extension, distant metastases, tall-cell variant
- 5–20% recurrence locally; 10–15% distant mets

### Treatment
- **Surgery:** total thyroidectomy or lobectomy (based on size/risk)
- **Radioactive iodine (I-131)** ablation for high-risk cases
- **TSH suppression** with levothyroxine
- **Monitoring:** serum thyroglobulin + neck ultrasound

> 🎯 "PTC = most common thyroid cancer; Orphan Annie nuclei + grooves + pseudo-inclusions + psammoma bodies; lymphatic spread; BRAF V600E; >95% 10-yr survival."

## Q9. What are the thyroid function tests? How hypo and hyperthyroidism and its types can be diagnosed by these tests?

### Thyroid Function Tests

| Test | What It Measures | Normal Range | Clinical Utility |
|---|---|---|---|
| **TSH** 🔴 | Thyroid-stimulating hormone from pituitary | 0.4–4.0 mIU/L | 🔴 **Best single screening test** for thyroid dysfunction; most sensitive (changes first) |
| **Free T4 (FT4)** | Unbound thyroxine (active hormone) | 0.8–1.8 ng/dL | ↑ in hyperthyroidism; ↓ in hypothyroidism |
| **Free T3 (FT3)** | Unbound triiodothyronine (more potent) | 2.3–4.2 pg/mL | ↑ in T3 toxicosis (early hyperthyroidism); may be normal in some hyperthyroid patients |
| **Total T4** | Total thyroxine (bound + unbound) | 4.5–12.5 µg/dL | Affected by binding proteins (pregnancy, oral contraceptives, liver disease) |
| **Total T3** | Total triiodothyronine | 80–200 ng/dL | Same limitations as total T4 |
| **Anti-TPO** | Anti-thyroid peroxidase antibodies | Negative | Positive in Hashimoto thyroiditis (>90%) and Graves disease (~75%) |
| **Anti-Tg** | Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies | Negative | Positive in Hashimoto (~60–80%); interferes with thyroglobulin monitoring |
| **TRAb/TSI** | TSH receptor antibodies | Negative | Positive in Graves disease (~90%); TSI = stimulatory; blocking antibodies cause hypothyroidism |
| **Thyroglobulin** | Protein made by thyroid follicular cells | 1.5–38.5 ng/mL | Tumor marker for differentiated thyroid cancer (PTC/FTC) after total thyroidectomy |
| **Calcitonin** | Hormone from parafollicular C cells | <10 pg/mL | Tumor marker for medullary thyroid carcinoma |
| **Radioiodine uptake (RAIU)** | Iodine trapping by thyroid | 5–35% at 24 hr | Differentiates causes of thyrotoxicosis |
| **Thyroid ultrasound** | Structural assessment | — | Evaluate nodules, guide FNA |
| **FNA cytology** | Cell morphology | — | Gold standard for thyroid nodule diagnosis |

### Diagnosing Hyperthyroidism

| Test Pattern | Diagnosis | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **↓ TSH + ↑ FT4** | **Overt hyperthyroidism** | Classic pattern; TSH suppressed by negative feedback |
| **↓ TSH + normal FT4 + ↑ FT3** | **T3 toxicosis** | Early or mild hyperthyroidism; FT3 elevated before FT4 |
| **↓ TSH + normal FT4 + normal FT3** | **Subclinical hyperthyroidism** | Biochemical only; no symptoms; monitor for progression |
| **↑ TSH + ↑ FT4** | **Secondary hyperthyroidism** | TSH-secreting pituitary adenoma (very rare); only cause with ↑ TSH |

#### Differentiating Causes of Hyperthyroidism with Radioiodine Uptake:

| RAIU Pattern | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| **Diffusely increased** | **Graves disease** |
| **Focally increased (hot nodule)** | **Toxic adenoma** |
| **Patchy increased (hot nodules + suppressed surrounding)** | **Toxic MNG** |
| **Decreased** | **Thyroiditis** (de Quervain, lymphocytic), **factitious**, **ectopic** (struma ovarii) |

### Diagnosing Hypothyroidism

| Test Pattern | Diagnosis | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| **↑ TSH + ↓ FT4** | **Overt primary hypothyroidism** | Most common pattern; thyroid failure → TSH elevated by loss of feedback |
| **↑ TSH + normal FT4** | **Subclinical primary hypothyroidism** | Biochemical only; no symptoms; monitor or treat if TSH >10 |
| **Normal/↓ TSH + ↓ FT4** | **Central (secondary) hypothyroidism** | Pituitary or hypothalamic disease; TSH not elevated despite low FT4 |
| **↑ TSH + ↓ FT4 + ↑ anti-TPO** | **Hashimoto thyroiditis** | Autoimmune destruction; most common cause in iodine-sufficient areas |

#### Causes of Primary Hypothyroidism:
- 🔴 **Hashimoto thyroiditis** (most common in iodine-sufficient areas)
- **Iodine deficiency** (most common worldwide)
- Postablative (surgery, radioiodine, external radiation)
- Drugs (lithium, amiodarone, interferon-α)
- Congenital (dyshormonogenetic goiter, thyroid agenesis)
- Infiltrative (amyloidosis, sarcoidosis, hemochromatosis)

### Diagnostic Algorithm

```
Suspected thyroid dysfunction
   ↓
Measure TSH (best single test)
   ↓
TSH low → Measure FT4 and FT3
   ↓ FT4/FT3 elevated → HYPERTHYROIDISM → RAIU to differentiate causes
   ↓ FT4/FT3 normal → Subclinical hyperthyroidism → Monitor
   ↓
TSH high → Measure FT4
   ↓ FT4 low → HYPOTHYROIDISM → Anti-TPO to confirm Hashimoto
   ↓ FT4 normal → Subclinical hypothyroidism → Monitor or treat
   ↓
TSH normal → Usually euthyroid (rare TSH-secreting pituitary adenoma if FT4/FT3 high)
```

### Special Situations
- **Sick euthyroid syndrome (non-thyroidal illness):** ↓T3 + normal/↓T4 + TSH normal/low (sick patients)
- **Pregnancy:** ↑ TBG → ↑ total T4/T3; free T4 may be low-normal; TSH lower trimesters
- **Amiodarone:** type 1 (iodine-induced hyperthyroidism, ↑ uptake) vs type 2 (destructive thyroiditis, ↓ uptake)

> 🎯 "TSH = best screening test. ↓TSH + ↑FT4 = hyperthyroidism. ↑TSH + ↓FT4 = hypothyroidism. RAIU differentiates Graves (diffuse ↑) from thyroiditis (↓)."

# Part D — Clinical Case & Thyroglossal Duct Cyst (Q10–11)

## Q10. A female of 24 years presenting with neck swelling and leg edema. Her TSH is done and found high. What is your provisional diagnosis? What investigations do you have to do further?

### Analysis of the Case
- **Age/Sex:** 24-year-old female
- **Presentation:** Neck swelling (goiter) + Leg edema (peripheral edema/myxedema)
- **TSH: HIGH** → indicates **primary hypothyroidism** (thyroid is failing → pituitary compensates with ↑ TSH)

### Differential Diagnosis

| Diagnosis | Probability | Supporting Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Hashimoto thyroiditis** 🔴🔴 | **Most likely** | Young female; goiter + hypothyroidism (↑ TSH); most common cause of hypothyroidism in iodine-sufficient areas |
| **Iodine deficiency goiter** | Possible | Depends on geographic location; endemic areas |
| **Subacute thyroiditis (late phase)** | Less likely | Usually painful, history of viral illness |
| **Drug-induced hypothyroidism** | Possible | Lithium, amiodarone — ask drug history |
| **Post-radiation hypothyroidism** | Possible | History of neck radiation |
| **Congenital hypothyroidism** | Unlikely at 24 | Usually diagnosed at birth |

### Leg Edema in Hypothyroidism
- 🔴 **Myxedema** = non-pitting edema from accumulation of **glycosaminoglycans (hyaluronic acid)** in dermis and subcutaneous tissue
- Also: periorbital edema, facial puffiness, cool pale skin
- Severe: **myxedema coma** (medical emergency with hypothermia, altered consciousness, cardiorespiratory failure)

### Provisional Diagnosis
🔴 **Hashimoto thyroiditis with primary hypothyroidism** (most likely)

### Further Investigations

| Investigation | Purpose | Expected Finding |
|---|---|---|
| **Serum FT4** | Confirm hypothyroidism | **↓ Low** (overt hypothyroidism) |
| **Serum TSH** | Already done | **↑ High** (confirming primary) |
| **Anti-TPO antibodies** 🔴 | Confirm autoimmune etiology | **Positive** in >90% of Hashimoto |
| **Anti-thyroglobulin antibodies** | Additional autoimmune marker | Positive in ~60–80% |
| **Thyroid ultrasound** | Assess gland morphology | Diffuse, heterogeneous, hypoechoic gland; may show pseudonodules |
| **Thyroid FNA** | If nodules present or suspected malignancy | Lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate + Hürthle cells |
| **CBC** | Associated anemia | Normocytic anemia (common in hypothyroidism) |
| **Lipid profile** | Dyslipidemia | ↑ Total cholesterol, ↑ LDL, ↑ triglycerides |
| **Serum creatine kinase** | Muscle involvement | May be elevated |
| **ECG** | Cardiac effects | Bradycardia, low voltage, T-wave changes |
| **Blood glucose** | Associated autoimmune conditions | Screen for T1D |
| **Vitamin B12** | Associated pernicious anemia | May be low |
| **Calcium, phosphate** | Associated hypoparathyroidism (APS-1) | If indicated |

### Associated Autoimmune Conditions to Screen
- T1D (fasting glucose, HbA1c)
- Pernicious anemia (B12, anti-intrinsic factor)
- Celiac disease (anti-tTG)
- Addison disease (cortisol, ACTH)
- Vitiligo (clinical)
- Sjögren syndrome (clinical, anti-SSA/SSB)
- Rheumatoid arthritis (RF, anti-CCP)

### Treatment
- **Levothyroxine replacement** — starting dose 1.6 µg/kg/day (adjust based on TSH)
- Monitor TSH every 6–8 weeks until stable
- Screen for associated autoimmune conditions
- Lifelong therapy

> 🎯 "24 yr female + goiter + edema + ↑TSH = Hashimoto thyroiditis → confirm with ↓FT4 + anti-TPO. Treat with levothyroxine; screen for associated autoimmunity."

## Q11. Notes on Thyroglossal duct cyst.

### Definition
🔴 **Thyroglossal duct cyst = the most common clinically significant congenital anomaly of the thyroid gland.** A remnant of the thyroglossal duct — the tract along which the thyroid descends from the foramen cecum of the tongue to its final position in the anterior neck during embryological development.

### Embryology
- Thyroid gland develops from the **floor of the pharynx** (foramen cecum) at ~3–4 weeks gestation
- Descends along the thyroglossal duct to its final position in the anterior neck
- The duct normally **obliterates** by the 7th–10th week
- **Failure of obliteration** → persistence of thyroglossal duct → cyst formation

### Epidemiology
- 🔴 **Most common congenital neck mass** (after branchial cleft anomalies)
- Accounts for ~70% of congenital neck midline masses
- Can present at any age; most common in children (but also adults)
- **75% occur below the hyoid bone** (the hyoid bone is intimately related to the duct)

### Pathology
| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| **Location** | 🔴 **Midline anterior neck** (or slightly off-midline); intimately attached to hyoid bone |
| **Size** | 1–4 cm; may enlarge with upper respiratory infections |
| **Lining epithelium** | **Variable:** squamous (high lesions near foramen cecum) or respiratory (ciliated columnar, lower lesions near thyroid) |
| **Wall** | Fibrous wall with **intense subjacent lymphocytic infiltrate** (characteristic) |
| **Contents** | Mucinous or serous fluid; may be infected → abscess |
| **Thyroid tissue** | May contain ectopic thyroid tissue in the wall |

### Clinical Features
- **Painless, fluctuant midline neck mass** that moves with:
  - 🔴 **Deglutition** (swallowing) — attached to hyoid bone
  - 🔴 **Protrusion of tongue** — attached to foramen cecum via fibrous band
- Usually 1–4 cm; may enlarge during URTI
- **Infection:** painful, red, may drain externally → sinus tract
- **Recurrent infections** → fibrosis, sinus formation

### Differential Diagnosis
| Diagnosis | Distinguishing Features |
|---|---|
| **Dermoid cyst** | Does NOT move with swallowing or tongue protrusion |
| **Ectopic thyroid** | Solid; moves with swallowing; may be the ONLY functioning thyroid tissue |
| **Submental lymph node** | Lateral to midline; associated with dental/oral infection |
| **Branchial cleft cyst** | Lateral neck; 2nd branchial cleft most common |
| **Lipoma** | Soft, compressible, transillumination positive |
| **Thyroid malignancy** | Hard, fixed, may have lymphadenopathy |

### Investigations
- **Thyroid function tests** — ensure normal thyroid function before surgery
- **Ultrasound** — confirm cystic nature; locate normal thyroid gland
- **Thyroid scintigraphy (scan)** — rule out ectopic thyroid (if scan shows absent thyroid in normal position → the ectopic tissue may be the only functioning thyroid)
- **FNA** — if suspicious features (solid component, suspicious cytology)

### Treatment
🔴 **Sistrunk procedure** = the gold standard surgical treatment:
1. Complete excision of the cyst + entire thyroglossal duct tract
2. **Resection of the central portion of the hyoid bone** (where the duct passes through)
3. Excision of a cuff of tissue up to the foramen cecum
4. **Recurrence rate <5%** with Sistrunk procedure (vs 30–50% with simple excision)

### Complications
- **Infection** → abscess formation → may require I&D before definitive surgery
- **Recurrent sinus tract** (if incompletely excised)
- **Rarely: thyroglossal duct cyst carcinoma** — usually papillary thyroid carcinoma (~1%); requires total thyroidectomy + neck dissection
- **Recurrence** if Sistrunk procedure not performed

### Key Exam Points
- 🔴 Moves with swallowing and tongue protrusion (hyoid connection)
- 🔴 Sistrunk procedure = excision + central hyoid resection + tract up to foramen cecum
- 🔴 Must confirm presence of normal thyroid gland in normal position before surgery (ectopic thyroid may be the only functioning tissue)

> 🎯 "Thyroglossal duct cyst = most common congenital neck midline mass; moves with swallowing + tongue protrusion; Sistrunk procedure; rule out ectopic thyroid."
