# Ch 11 — Gestational Trophoblastic Disease     <i>Full-detail flashcards (Q→A) · All abbreviations expanded · Robbins 4-pillar answers</i>
### 🧠 Big Picture — Gestational Trophoblastic Disease (GTD)
<b>GTD</b> (gestational trophoblastic disease) = a <b>spectrum of tumours and tumour-like conditions</b> arising from abnormal proliferation of <b>placental tissue</b> — either villous or trophoblastic. All are characterised by production of <b>human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)</b>, the key tumour marker.
The full classification (VIVA favourite): <b>1.</b> Hydatidiform mole (complete & partial — <i>benign</i>) · <b>2.</b> Invasive mole (<i>locally invasive</i>) · <b>3.</b> Placental-site trophoblastic tumour (PSTT — <i>intermediate</i>) · <b>4.</b> Choriocarcinoma (<i>frankly malignant</i>).
🔴 <b>Risk of progression:</b> molar pregnancy may persist → <b>invasive mole</b> or undergo malignant change → <b>choriocarcinoma</b> (50% of choriocarcinomas arise from complete moles).
📌 <b>Complete vs Partial mole — the crux:</b> Complete = 46,XX (<i>all-paternal</i> "androgenesis"), no embryo; Partial = triploid 69,XXY (2 paternal + 1 maternal), embryo present, less severe trophoblastic hyperplasia.
🎯 <b>One-liner:</b> <i>"Grape-like" hydropic villi (USG snowstorm) + hCG >1 lac unit → think GTD; malignant worry = choriocarcinoma (lung + vagina mets).</i>
Robbins 10e pp.1033–1034 · Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, pp.292–295 · BRS Pathology 5e p.310 · Lecture DMC

## 2 · Hydatidiform Mole
<b>Short note: Hydatidiform mole — definition, age, clinical features. (SU-20Nv) (Very Important for VIVA)</b>

<b>Hydatidiform mole:</b> characterised by <b>cystic swelling of the chorionic villi</b>, accompanied by variable <b>trophoblastic proliferation</b>. Associated with an <b>increased risk</b> of persistent trophoblastic disease (<b>invasive mole</b>) or <b>choriocarcinoma</b>.

<b>Diagnosis:</b> usually diagnosed during <b>early pregnancy</b> (average <b>9 weeks</b>) by pelvic sonography (USG = ultrasonography).

Age of incidence

Molar pregnancy can develop at any age, but the risk is <b>higher at the two ends of reproductive life</b> — in <b>teenagers</b> and between <b>40 and 50 years</b> of age.

Clinical characteristics

Vaginal bleeding
Spontaneous miscarriage
Rapid increase in uterine size (uterus often too large for the supposed state of gestation)
hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) levels greatly exceed those of a normal pregnancy of similar gestational age

🎯 <i>Hydatidiform mole can be mistaken for a normal pregnancy, but the uterus is often too large for gestational age and hCG is very high.</i>

Ref: Robbins 10e p.1033

<b>🔴 Mention the morphology of hydatidiform mole (gross + microscopic). (CU-12Ju)</b>

Macroscopic (Gross)

Uterine cavity is filled with a delicate, friable mass of thin-walled, translucent, cystic, grape-like structures consisting of swollen edematous (hydropic) villi.
Fetal parts are frequently seen in partial moles.

Microscopic

Complete moles: abnormalities involve all (or most) of the villous tissue. The chorionic villi are enlarged, scalloped in shape with central cavitation (cisterns), and lack adequately developed vessels.
Partial moles: only a fraction of the villi are enlarged and edematous; the trophoblastic hyperplasia is focal and less marked than in complete moles.

Fig: Complete hydatidiform mole · Ref: Robbins 10e p.1033; BRS Pathology 5e p.310

## 3 · Complete vs Partial Hydatidiform Mole — Differences (Important for VIVA)
<b>🔴 Mention the differences between complete mole and partial mole. (Important for VIVA)</b>

<table>
<tr><th>Traits</th><th>Complete mole</th><th>Partial mole</th></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Ploidy / karyotype</strong></td><td><span class="hot">Diploid (46,XX or 46,XY)</span></td><td><span class="hot">Triploid (69,XXY or 69,XXX)</span></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Genetic derivation</strong></td><td>2 sperm + empty egg → <strong>androgenesis</strong> (all-paternal)</td><td>2 sperm + 1 egg (two paternal + one maternal haploid set)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Embryo / fetus</strong></td><td><span class="hot">Absent</span></td><td><span class="hot">Present</span></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Fetal parts</strong></td><td>Absent</td><td>Present</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Villi</strong></td><td>More edematous, <strong>hydropic villi</strong> (affects all/most villi)</td><td>Few hydropic villi (focal)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Trophoblastic hyperplasia</strong></td><td>Marked / diffuse</td><td>Focal and less marked</td></tr>
</table>

🎯 <b>"Complete = Clean (no baby)":</b> Complete → no embryo, diploid, all-paternal, aggressive-looking; Partial → embryo present, triploid, two paternal + one maternal. <i>Complete mole has higher risk of choriocarcinoma.</i>

Ref: Lecture DMC

## 4 · Laboratory Diagnosis of Hydatidiform Mole
<b>How is hydatidiform mole diagnosed in the laboratory? (SU-06/05Ja)</b>

Ultrasonogram (USG): reveals "snowstorm appearance" of the mole — diagnostic.
Serum hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin): markedly elevated.
X-ray chest: to exclude metastatic choriocarcinoma.

Urine examination

hCG level — elevated
Pregnancy test — positive (+ve)

Biopsy

<b>a. Macroscopic:</b> a mass of <b>grape-like structures of varying sizes</b> filling the uterus.

<b>b. Microscopic:</b>

Marked proliferation of syncytial or cytotrophoblastic epithelium
Marked thinning of the stromal tissue due to accumulation of fluid
Absence of blood vessels
The villus pattern is distinctly maintained

🎯 <b>USG snowstorm + hCG >1 lac + +ve pregnancy test</b> = molar pregnancy; chest X-ray to rule out metastatic choriocarcinoma.

## 5 · Invasive Mole
<b>Short note: Invasive mole — morphology, clinical features, treatment. (CU-12Ju)</b>

<b>Invasive mole:</b> an <b>infiltrative lesion that penetrates or even perforates the uterine wall</b>.

Morphology

Invasion of the myometrium by hydropic chorionic villi, accompanied by proliferation of both cytotrophoblast and syncytiotrophoblast.
The tumour is locally destructive and may invade parametrial tissue and blood vessels.
Hydropic villi may embolize to distant sites, such as lungs and brain, but do NOT grow in these organs as true metastases — and even without chemotherapy they eventually regress.

Clinical features

Vaginal bleeding
Irregular uterine enlargement
Persistently elevated serum hCG

Treatment

The tumour responds well to chemotherapy, but may result in <b>uterine rupture</b>, necessitating <b>hysterectomy</b>.

Fig: Invasive mole presenting as a hemorrhagic mass adherent to the uterine wall · Ref: Robbins 10e p.1034

## 6 · Choriocarcinoma
<b>Short note: Choriocarcinoma — precuror conditions, morphology, clinical features, metastases. (Arif 2025)</b>

<b>Gestational choriocarcinoma:</b> a <b>malignant neoplasm of trophoblastic cells</b> derived from a previously normal or abnormal pregnancy, such as an extrauterine <b>ectopic pregnancy</b>. It is <b>rapidly invasive and metastasizes widely</b>, but once identified <b>responds well to chemotherapy</b>.

Precursor conditions (where it arises)

50% arise in complete hydatidiform moles
25% in previous abortions
Approximately 22% follow normal pregnancies
The remainder occur in ectopic pregnancies

Morphology

<b>Macroscopic:</b> soft, fleshy, <b>yellow-white tumour</b> that usually has large pale areas of <b>necrosis</b> and extensive <b>haemorrhage</b> (bulky hemorrhagic mass invading the uterine wall).

<b>Microscopic:</b>

Consists entirely of proliferating syncytiotrophoblasts and cytotrophoblasts
Chorionic villi are absent (KEY distinction from mole)
Mitoses are abundant and sometimes abnormal
The tumour invades the underlying myometrium, frequently penetrates blood vessels, and in some cases extends onto the uterine serosa and into adjacent structures

Clinical features

Irregular vaginal spotting of a <b>bloody, brown fluid</b>.

Metastases

<b>Widespread metastases are characteristic</b>; the most common sites are the <b>lungs (50%)</b> and <b>vagina (30%–40%)</b>, followed in descending order of frequency by the <b>brain, liver, bone, and kidney</b>.

🎯 <b>No villi + both trophoblasts + blood-vessel invasion</b> = choriocarcinoma. <i>"Lungs (50%) & vagina (30–40%)"</i> are the classic mets.

Ref: Robbins 10e p.1034

<b>🎯 True/False practice — Hydatidiform mole & choriocarcinoma</b>

<b>Positive pregnancy test is observed in: amenorrhoea, hydatidiform mole, choriocarcinoma, endometrial hyperplasia, dysgerminoma. (DU-12Ja)</b>
Item <b>Amenorrhoea = False</b> · <b>Hydatidiform mole = True</b> · <b>Choriocarcinoma = True</b> · <b>Endometrial hyperplasia = False</b> · <b>Dysgerminoma = False</b> <i>(ch18 MCQ)</i>

<b>Gestational trophoblastic diseases include: hydrocele, hydatidiform mole, invasive mole, leiomyosarcoma, embryonal carcinoma. (RMU-22M)</b>
Item <b>Hydrocele = False</b> · <b>Hydatidiform mole = True</b> · <b>Invasive mole = True</b> · <b>Leiomyosarcoma = False</b> · <b>Embryonal carcinoma = False</b> <i>(ch18 MCQ)</i>

## 7 · Case Vignette (Important for VIVA)
<b>🔴 A 3-month pregnant woman, 40 yrs of age, comes with vaginal bleeding. Ultrasonogram of lower abdomen shows cystic changes in uterus and grape-like clusters. What is the provisional diagnosis and the most serious complication?</b>

<b>Provisional diagnosis:</b> <b>Hydatidiform mole</b>.

<b>Most serious complication:</b> <b>Choriocarcinoma</b>.

🎯 <b>Vaginal bleeding + enlarged-for-dates uterus + USG "grape-like" / snowstorm</b> in a 40-yr-old → molar pregnancy; the feared progression is <b>choriocarcinoma</b>.

## 8 · Pseudocyesis & Urine Differentiation
<b>What is pseudocyesis?</b>

Pseudocyesis

<b>Pseudocyesis:</b> a condition in which a patient has <b>nearly all of the usual signs and symptoms of pregnancy</b> — such as <b>enlargement of the abdomen, weight gain, cessation of menses, and morning sickness</b> — but the patient is <b>not actually pregnant</b>.

It is usually seen in women who either are <b>very desirous of having children</b> or <b>wish to avoid pregnancy</b>. Treatment is usually done by psychiatric means.

<b>How can you differentiate the urine of a pregnant lady from the urine of a trophoblastic tumour patient by examining urine? (RU-07Ju)</b>

Differentiation by urine examination

The key is the <b>hCG (human chorionic gonadotropin) level</b>:

Pregnant woman: hCG < 1 lac unit
Trophoblastic tumour: hCG > 1 lac unit, and it may be up to 5 lacs

## 🔁 Rapid-Fire MCQ & Viva Sweep (incl. folded Systemic Pathology MCQs)
<b>GTD — True/False & one-liners (test yourself, then reveal)</b>

Gestational trophoblastic disease includes (RMU-22M): Hydrocele (T/F), Hydatidiform mole (T/F), Invasive mole (T/F), Leiomyosarcoma (T/F), Embryonal carcinoma (T/F)?

Hydrocele = <b>False</b> · hydatidiform mole = <b>True</b> · invasive mole = <b>True</b> · leiomyosarcoma = <b>False</b> · embryonal carcinoma = <b>False</b>.

Positive pregnancy test is observed in (DU-12Ja): amenorrhoea (T/F), hydatidiform mole (T/F), choriocarcinoma (T/F), endometrial hyperplasia (T/F), dysgerminoma (T/F)?

Amenorrhoea = <b>False</b> · hydatidiform mole = <b>True</b> · choriocarcinoma = <b>True</b> · endometrial hyperplasia = <b>False</b> · dysgerminoma = <b>False</b>.

🎯 Commonest site of choriocarcinoma metastasis? · Second commonest?

<b>Lungs (50%)</b> then <b>vagina (30–40%)</b>; then brain, liver, bone, kidney.

🎯 Karyotype of complete mole? · Of partial mole?

Complete = <b>diploid 46,XX (or 46,XY)</b>, all-paternal (androgenesis) · Partial = <b>triploid 69,XXY (or 69,XXX)</b>, 2 paternal + 1 maternal.

🎯 In which type of mole is an embryo present? · Which has the higher risk of choriocarcinoma?

Embryo present = <b>partial mole</b> · Higher choriocarcinoma risk = <b>complete mole</b> (50% of choriocarcinomas arise from complete moles).

🎯 What is the diagnostic USG finding of hydatidiform mole? · What is the chorionic-villi state in choriocarcinoma?

USG = <b>"snowstorm" appearance</b> (grape-like cystic clusters) · Choriocarcinoma = <b>chorionic villi are ABSENT</b>.

🎯 hCG level in trophoblastic tumour vs normal pregnancy?

Trophoblastic tumour = <b>>1 lac unit (up to 5 lacs)</b> · Normal pregnancy = <b><1 lac unit</b>.

🎯 What percentage of choriocarcinoma arises from complete mole, abortions, normal pregnancies?

<b>50% complete mole · 25% abortion · ~22% normal pregnancy</b> · remainder ectopic.

🎯 Pseudocyesis — is the patient actually pregnant? What is the treatment?

No — <b>not pregnant</b>, only signs/symptoms of pregnancy; treat by <b>psychiatric means</b>.

🎯 Treatment of invasive mole?

<b>Chemotherapy</b> (responds well); may need <b>hysterectomy</b> if uterine rupture.
