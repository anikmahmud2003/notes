🧠 Big Picture — Electrolyte & Acid-Base Balance (boss-level overview)

Normal serum electrolytes (mmol/L or mEq/L): Na⁺ 135–145 · K⁺ 3.6–5.1 · Cl⁻ 95–107 · HCO₃⁻ 21–29 · Ca²⁺ 2.1–2.6. Electrolytes = salts/metallic components dissolved in serum; they carry an electrical charge and are required for nerve conduction, heart & skeletal muscle contraction, hydration status, and pH maintenance.

Each electrolyte has a low state (hypo) and a high state (hyper)—memorise the causes + clinical features of each: Hypo-/Hyper-natraemia (Na), -kalaemia (K), -calcaemia (Ca).

Acid-base physiology: normal blood pH 7.35–7.45 (arterial 7.45, venous 7.35). The key equation (Henderson-Hasselbalch) links pH to the HCO₃⁻ / pCO₂ ratio. Four primary disorders:

DisorderPrimary defectMechanism
Metabolic acidosis↓ HCO₃⁻↑ acid production / ↓ acid removal / HCO₃⁻ loss → ↓ pH
Metabolic alkalosis↑ HCO₃⁻Base accumulation / acid loss (other than HCO₃⁻) → ↑ pH
Respiratory acidosis↑ pCO₂Hypoventilation → CO₂ retention → ↓ pH
Respiratory alkalosis↓ pCO₂Hyperventilation → CO₂ loss → ↑ pH

Golden ABG (arterial blood gas) interpretation summary:

  • 🔴 ↑pH + ↑HCO₃⁻ = metabolic alkalosis; ↑pH + ↓pCO₂ = respiratory alkalosis.
  • 🔴 ↓pH + ↓HCO₃⁻ = metabolic acidosis; ↓pH + ↑pCO₂ = respiratory acidosis.
  • 📌 Respiratory/metabolic: CO₂ = respiratory side (lungs); HCO₃⁻ = metabolic side (kidneys). Compensation: metabolic disorders are compensated by the lungs (pCO₂ change), respiratory disorders by the kidneys (HCO₃⁻ change).
  • 🎯 ROME mnemonic: Respiratory Opposite, Metabolic Equal — in respiratory disorders the pH and pCO₂ move in opposite directions; in metabolic disorders pH and HCO₃⁻ move in the same direction.

Anion gap (AG) note: AG = [Na⁺] − ([Cl⁻] + [HCO₃⁻]). Normal ≈ 12 ± 4 mmol/L. Increased AG metabolic acidosis in DKA (diabetic ketoacidosis), lactic acidosis, methanol, renal failure (e.g. ketones, lactate, methanol, uraemia). Normal (hyperchloraemic) AG in diarrhoea, renal tubular acidosis (RTA).

1 · Serum Electrolytes — Composition & Normal Values

What are the electrolytes that are done to evaluate a patient? Give their normal levels. (DU-16Ja)

Serum electrolytes are the salts and metallic components that are dissolved within the blood serum (serum = the liquid portion of blood). Electrolytes carry an electrical charge, either negative or positive; the negatively charged electrolytes are balanced by the positively charged electrolytes.

Components of serum electrolytes with normal values: the five principal measured serum electrolytes are sodium (Na⁺) 135–145 mmol/L, potassium (K⁺) 3.6–5.1 mmol/L, chloride (Cl⁻) 95–107 mmol/L, bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) 21–29 mmol/L, and calcium (Ca²⁺) 2.1–2.6 mmol/L (9–11 mg/dl).

2 · Hypokalaemia (K⁺ < 3.5 mmol/L)

🔴 Mention the causes (CU-18Nv), clinical features and investigations of hypokalaemia. What are the complications of hypokalaemia? (CMU-21M) Short note on: Hypokalaemia.

Definition: hypokalaemia = decreased plasma K⁺ (potassium) level less than 3.5 mmol/L.

Ref: Davidson's/20e/432

Causes of hypokalaemia (🔴 Very Important for VIVA)

  1. Inadequate intake in diet
  2. Loss of K⁺ through GIT (gastrointestinal tract):
    • a) Severe vomiting
    • b) Diarrhoea
    • c) Nasogastric aspiration
    • d) Intestinal fistula
    • e) Bowel obstruction
  3. Alkalosis due to any cause
  4. Endocrine disorders:
    • a) Cushing's syndrome
    • b) Conn's syndrome (primary hyperaldosteronism)
  5. Diuretic therapy
  6. Corticosteroid therapy
  7. Prolonged I.V. (intravenous) therapy
  8. Insulin excess
  9. Mg (magnesium) depletion

Ref: Davidson's/21e/439,440

Clinical features / consequences / effects of hypokalaemia

  • i) Muscular weakness
  • ii) Tiredness
  • iii) Functional bowel obstruction due to paralytic ileus
  • iv) Hypokalaemic nephropathy
  • i) Flaccid paralysis
  • ii) Mental confusion
  • v) Cardiac arrhythmia
  • iii) (Finally) cardiac arrest

Ref: Davidson's/21e/439

Diagnosis / investigations

Measurement of plasma electrolytes, bicarbonate, urine potassium, and sometimes of Ca & Mg is usually sufficient to establish the diagnosis.

ECG (electrocardiogram) change

Typical ECG changes occur, affecting particularly the T wave (e.g. flattened/inverted T wave, U waves).

🎯 "HypoK = Low & Lethargic": causes → vomiting, diarrhoea, diuretics, Conn's/Cushing, alkalosis; complications → arrhythmia → cardiac arrest (the killer).

Ref: Davidson's/21e/439

3 · Hyperkalaemia (K⁺ > 5 mmol/L)

🔴 Write 5 causes of hyperkalaemia. (CU-12Ja) Short note: Hyperkalaemia.

Definition: hyperkalaemia = increased plasma K⁺ (potassium) level above the upper limit of normal range (>5 mmol/L). Severe hyperkalaemia occurs when K⁺ level goes above 6.5 mmol/L, which can participate in cardiac arrhythmia.

Causes of hyperkalaemia (🔴 Very Important for VIVA)

  1. Excessive IV (intravenous) infusion
  2. Acute and chronic renal failure
  3. Acidosis due to any cause
  4. Leakage of cellular K⁺ in diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
  5. Acute starvation
  6. Gross haemorrhage
  7. Dehydration
  8. Adrenocortical failure (Addison's disease)
  9. Drugs (NSAIDs = non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, ACE inhibitors = angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitors, β-blockers, prolonged heparin therapy, spironolactone)
  10. Insulin deficiency and severe hyperglycaemia
  11. Severe tissue injury such as:
    • a) Massive road traffic accident
    • b) Bomb blast
    • c) Massive haemolysis
    • d) Chemotherapy
    • e) Severe burn

Ref: Davidson's/21e/439–441; Lecture SSMC

Clinical features

  1. Progressive muscular weakness
  2. Paralysis
  3. Diastolic cardiac arrest

Ref: Davidson's/21e/440

Diagnosis

Measurement of plasma electrolytes, creatinine, and bicarbonate.

ECG (electrocardiogram) change

Typical ECG changes occur — peaking of the T wave is an early ECG sign, but widening of the QRS complex presages a dangerous cardiac arrhythmia.

🎯 "HyperK = High & Hyperactive (then stops)": causes → renal failure, acidosis, DKA, haemolysis/burns, Addison's, K⁺-sparing drugs; ECG → peaked T → wide QRS → diastolic cardiac arrest.

Ref: Davidson's/21e/440

4 · Hyponatraemia (Na⁺ < 135 mmol/L)

🔴 Short note on: Hyponatraemia. State the causes and effects of hyponatraemia. (RU-07Ja) What are the features of hyponatraemia? (SU-07Ju)

Definition: hyponatraemia = low levels of sodium in serum below normal (<135 mmol/L).

Causes of hyponatraemia

1. Hypovolaemic hyponatraemia (sodium deficit with a relatively smaller water deficit):

  • Renal Na⁺ losses: diuretic therapy (especially thiazide), adrenocortical failure
  • Gastrointestinal Na⁺ losses: vomiting, diarrhoea
  • Skin Na⁺ losses: burns

2. Euvolaemic hyponatraemia (water retention alone):

  • Primary polydipsia
  • Excessive electrolyte-free water infusion
  • Syndrome of inappropriate ADH (antidiuretic hormone) secretion (SIADH)
  • Hypothyroidism

3. Hypervolaemic hyponatraemia (Na⁺ retention with relatively greater water retention):

  • Congestive cardiac failure (CCF)
  • Cirrhosis of liver
  • Nephrotic syndrome
  • Chronic renal failure (during free water intake)

Ref: Davidson's/21e/435

Effects / features of hyponatraemia

  • i) May be asymptomatic
  • ii) Anorexia, nausea & vomiting
  • iii) Confusion
  • iv) Lethargy
  • v) Seizures
  • vi) Coma
  • vii) Death

Ref: Davidson's/21e/435

🎯 "Low Na = Neurons complain": causes classified by volume status (hypovolaemic / euvolaemic / hypervolaemic); effects = CNS — confusion → seizures → coma → death.

Ref: Davidson's/21e/435

5 · Hypernatraemia (Na⁺ > 150 mmol/L)

🔴 Mention the causes of hypernatraemia. (CMU-21M) Short note: Hypernatraemia.

Definition: hypernatraemia = increased level of sodium in the serum above normal (>150 mmol/L).

Causes of hypernatraemia

1. Hypovolaemic hypernatraemia (sodium deficit with a relatively greater water deficit):

  • Renal Na⁺ losses: diuretic therapy (especially osmotic diuretic, or loop diuretic during water restriction), glycosuria
  • Gastrointestinal Na⁺ losses: colonic diarrhoea
  • Skin Na⁺ losses: excessive sweating

2. Euvolaemic hypernatraemia (water deficit alone):

  • Diabetes insipidus (central or nephrogenic)

3. Hypervolaemic hypernatraemia (Na⁺ retention with relatively less water retention):

  • Enteral or parenteral alimentation
  • IV or oral salt administration
  • Chronic renal failure (during water restriction)

Ref: Davidson's/21e/437

Clinical features of hypernatraemia

  • Cloudiness
  • Dizziness
  • Disorientation
  • Convulsion
  • Confusion
  • Weakness
  • Coma
  • Death

Ref: Davidson's/21e/437

🎯 "High Na = Hot & Dry": causes grouped by volume status; hallmark of water-loss hypernatraemia = diabetes insipidus; features = CNS symptoms (disorientation → convulsion → coma → death).

6 · Hypocalcaemia (Ca²⁺ < normal)

🔴 What are the causes of hypocalcaemia? (RU-08Ju) Mention 4 causes of hypocalcaemia. (CU-12Ju) Short note: Hypocalcaemia.

Definition: hypocalcaemia = decreased blood calcium level below normal.

Causes of hypocalcaemia (🔴 Very Important for VIVA)

  1. Hypoalbuminaemia
  2. Alkalosis
    • Respiratory e.g. hyperventilation
    • Metabolic e.g. Conn's syndrome
  3. Vitamin-D deficiency
  4. Chronic renal failure
  5. Hypoparathyroidism
    • Complication during thyroid surgery
    • Haemochromatosis
    • Wilson's disease
  6. Pseudohypoparathyroidism
  7. Acute pancreatitis
  8. Hypomagnesaemia

Ref: Davidson's/21e/766

Clinical features

  1. Tetany (characteristic triad of carpopedal spasm, stridor and convulsion)
  2. Papilloedema
  3. Prolongation of the ECG (electrocardiogram) Q-T interval, which may predispose to ventricular arrhythmias
  4. Epilepsy
  5. Psychosis
  6. Cataract
  7. Rickets in children and osteomalacia in adults

Ref: Davidson's/21e/766

🎯 "Low Ca = Chronic Twitching": tetany (carpopedal spasm + stridor + convulsion) is the hallmark; Q-T prolongation → ventricular arrhythmia; rickets/osteomalacia.

🔴 What are the causes of hypercalcaemia? (RU-08Ju) Mention 4 causes of hypercalcaemia. (CU-12Ju) Short note: Hypercalcaemia.

Definition: hypercalcaemia = excess blood calcium level above normal.

Causes of hypercalcaemia

  1. Primary or tertiary hyperparathyroidism
  2. Familial hypocalciuric hypercalcaemia
  3. Malignancy (e.g. lung, breast, renal, ovarian, colonic & thyroid carcinoma, lymphoma, multiple myeloma)

  8. Milk-alkali syndrome
  9. Thiazide diuretics
  10. Glucocorticoid deficiency

  1. Polyuria
  2. Polydipsia
  3. Renal colic
  4. Lethargy
  5. Anorexia
  6. Nausea
  7. Dyspepsia
  8. Peptic ulceration
  9. Constipation
  10. Depression
  11. Drowsiness
  12. Impaired cognition

Ref: Davidson's/21e/765

9 · Metabolic Acidosis

🔴 What is metabolic acidosis? (DU-18Nv, CU-17M) Write down the causes of metabolic acidosis. (DU-18Nv,16Ja,12Ju,11/05Ja, CU-16/12Ju) Short note: Metabolic acidosis / Causes of metabolic acidosis. (DU-22Nv/M,15/08Ju,07Ja,06S, CU-20Ju,16Ja,15/13/11Ju, DU-17M)

Definition: metabolic acidosis = the primary decrease in plasma [HCO₃⁻] (bicarbonate) which arises either from increased production or decreased removal of acids other than H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid), or by loss of HCO₃⁻ from the body.

Or — decreased pH due to HCO₃⁻ deficit is called metabolic acidosis.

Primary defect: ↓ plasma HCO₃⁻ due to any cause.

Mechanism / consequence: ↓ plasma HCO₃⁻ → ↓ ratio of HCO₃⁻ / CO₂ → ↓ pH → metabolic acidosis.

Causes of metabolic acidosis (🔴 Very Important for VIVA)

  1. Addition of excessive metabolic acids to plasma:
    • a. Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
    • b. Prolonged starvation
    • c. Lactic acidosis
    • d. Methanol poisoning
  2. Failure to excrete acid at a normal rate:
    • a. Renal failure
  3. Loss of HCO₃⁻:
    • a. Severe diarrhoea
    • b. Intestinal, pancreatic or biliary fistula
    • c. Vomiting of intestinal content
    • d. Renal tubular acidosis (RTA)
  4. Drugs:
    • a. K⁺-sparing drugs, NH₄Cl (ammonium chloride), aspirin or methyl alcohol

Ref: Guyton/11e/397; Davidson's

Lab findings in metabolic acidosis (CU-16Ju)

ParameterFinding
pHDecreased
Serum HCO₃⁻↓ HCO₃⁻ (primary defect)
PCO₂ (partial pressure of carbon dioxide)↓ PCO₂ (compensatory response — hyperventilation)

🎯 Remember inversions: metabolic acidosis ↔ hypokalaemia of the gut is a trap — but in ACIDOSIS serum K⁺ RISES (K⁺ shifts out of cells in exchange for H⁺).

10 · Metabolic Alkalosis

🔴 What is metabolic alkalosis? (DU-18Nv) Write the causes of metabolic alkalosis. (CU-12Ju) What are the conditions that may lead to metabolic alkalosis? Give the biochemical changes in such a patient. (DU-04M) Short note: Metabolic alkalosis. (DU-09/08Ja,07Ju, BUP-22M)

Definition: metabolic alkalosis = primary increase in plasma [HCO₃⁻] (bicarbonate) due to the accumulation of base or loss of acid other than HCO₃⁻.

Or — alkalosis characterised by an increase in plasma HCO₃⁻ and a decrease in plasma [H⁺] (hydrogen ion concentration) is called metabolic alkalosis.

Primary defect: ↑ plasma HCO₃⁻ due to any cause.

Mechanism / consequence: ↑ plasma HCO₃⁻ → ↑ ratio of HCO₃⁻ / CO₂ → ↑ pH → metabolic alkalosis.

Causes of metabolic alkalosis (🔴 Very Important for VIVA)

  1. Administration of diuretics
  2. Excess aldosterone secretion
  3. Vomiting / aspiration of gastric contents (loss of HCl = hydrochloric acid)
  4. Injudicious ingestion of alkaline drugs (e.g. NaHCO₃ = sodium bicarbonate)
  5. Migration of H⁺ into ICF (intracellular fluid) in K⁺ deficiency
  6. Massive blood transfusion, containing citrate

Ref: Guyton/11e/397

Biochemical changes in metabolic alkalosis: ↑ plasma HCO₃⁻, ↑ pH, compensatory ↑ PCO₂ (hypoventilation).

🎯 "Metabolic alkalosis = Vomiting / Diuretics / Aldosterone": remember the "chloride-responsive" trio — vomiting (loss of HCl), diuretics, and mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) excess.

11 · Respiratory Acidosis

🔴 Write in short about respiratory acidosis. (DU-21Ja,20Nv/M)

Definition: respiratory acidosis = primary increase in PCO₂ (partial pressure of carbon dioxide) where there is impaired excretion of CO₂.

Or — a rise in arterial PCO₂ due to hypoventilation with consequent decrease in pH is called respiratory acidosis.

Primary defect: ↑ arterial PCO₂ due to any cause.

Mechanism / consequence: hypoventilation → ↓ CO₂ elimination → ↑ arterial PCO₂ → ↑ ratio of HCO₃⁻ / CO₂ → ↓ pH → respiratory acidosis.

Causes of respiratory acidosis (🔴 Very Important for VIVA)

The ultimate cause is hypoventilation, which occurs due to:

  • Chronic lung disease e.g. chronic bronchitis, emphysema, pulmonary fibrosis
  • Acute bronchial asthma
  • Acute pulmonary oedema
  • Pneumonia
  • Unconscious patient
  • Myasthenia (gravis)
  • Poliomyelitis
  • Damage of respiratory centre (e.g. morphine poisoning)
  • Chest deformities — kyphosis, lordosis etc.

Ref: Guyton/11e/397; Lecture SSMC

🎯 "Respiratory acidosis = CO₂ retention": anything that causes hypoventilation (lung disease, muscle weakness, CNS depression, chest deformities).

12 · Respiratory Alkalosis

🔴 Short note on: Respiratory alkalosis.

Definition: respiratory alkalosis = primary decrease in plasma PCO₂ (partial pressure of carbon dioxide) due to hyperventilation, resulting in increased pH.

Primary defect: ↓ arterial PCO₂, caused by hyperventilation due to any cause.

Mechanism / consequence: hyperventilation → ↑ CO₂ elimination → ↓ arterial PCO₂ → ↑ ratio of HCO₃⁻ / CO₂ → ↑ pH → respiratory alkalosis.

Causes of respiratory alkalosis (🔴 Very Important for VIVA)

Any cause of hyperventilation:

  1. High altitude (physiological)
  2. Hysterical attacks
  3. Salicylate poisoning
  4. High fever
  5. Meningitis, encephalitis
  6. Cerebral tumour
  7. Hepatic failure

Ref: Lecture SSMC

🎯 "Respiratory alkalosis = hyperventilation": altitude, hysteria, salicylate, fever, CNS lesions, hepatic failure — "H-H-S-F-M-C-H".

13 · Clinical Cases — Electrolyte & Acid-Base Status

🔴 A male of 55 years presents with excessive vomiting. He gives history of long standing peptic ulcer disease. What is the likely diagnosis of his present illness? What will be his electrolyte and acid-base status? (DU-16Ju)

Diagnosis: Gastric outlet obstruction due to pyloric stenosis (due to long standing peptic ulcer disease).

Acid-base status: Metabolic alkalosis (hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis).

Electrolyte status:

  • Hyponatraemia (low Na⁺)
  • Hypokalaemia (low K⁺)
  • Hypochloraemia (low Cl⁻)
  • Hypocalcaemia (low Ca²⁺)

Ref: Bailey & Love's/25e/1066

🎯 Vomiting → loss of HCl → hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis + hypokalaemia + hyponatraemia + hypocalcaemia.
🔴 A 10 year old boy develops severe diarrhoea. What changes do you expect in his electrolyte and acid-base status? (DU-10Ja) Write down the electrolyte pattern of a case of acute diarrhoea. (DU-18Nv)

Acid-base status: Metabolic acidosis (due to loss of HCO₃⁻ in stools).

Electrolyte status:

  • Hypokalaemia (due to loss of K⁺ in stools)
  • Hyponatraemia (due to loss of Na⁺ in stools and oral intake of free water)
  • Hypochloraemia (low Cl⁻)
  • Reduced HCO₃⁻ level
🎯 Diarrhoea → loss of HCO₃⁻ in stool → metabolic acidosis (opposite of vomiting → metabolic alkalosis). Cl⁻ follows Na⁺ and K⁺ losses → hypochloraemia.
🔴 A male of 65 years with longstanding chronic duodenal ulcer has developed excessive vomiting. What will be his electrolyte and acid-base status? (DU-10/09/08Ju,07Ja)

Acid-base status: Metabolic alkalosis (hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis).

Electrolyte status:

  • Hyponatraemia (low Na⁺)
  • Hypokalaemia (low K⁺)
  • Hypochloraemia (low Cl⁻)
  • Hypocalcaemia (low Ca²⁺)

Ref: Bailey & Love's/25e/1066

🎯 Same pattern as pyloric stenosis: excessive vomiting → loss of HCl → hypochloraemic metabolic alkalosis with hyponatraemia, hypokalaemia, hypocalcaemia.
🔴 A man aged 25 years is in shock following a road traffic accident. He passed 40 ml urine during the last 4 hours. What will be his acid-base and electrolyte status? (DU-09Ja)

Diagnosis: The patient is suffering from acute renal failure (ARF) due to hypovolaemia (shock → renal hypoperfusion; oliguria — only 40 ml urine in 4 hours).

Acid-base status: Metabolic acidosis (lactic acidosis) — due to tissue hypoperfusion (shock) → anaerobic metabolism → lactate accumulation.

Electrolyte status:

  • Hyperkalaemia (due to tissue haemolysis and acidosis)
  • Hyponatraemia (due to excess plasma loss)
  • Hypocalcaemia (due to renal failure and reduced renal production of 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol, the active vitamin D)
🎯 Shock → acute renal failure → the triple danger: metabolic (lactic) acidosis + hyperkalaemia + hypocalcaemia.

🎯 One-liners: What does hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis suggest? What causes metabolic acidosis in DKA? What is the ECG hallmark of hypokalaemia? What is the early ECG sign of hyperkalaemia?
