### 🧠 Big Picture — Diseases & Tumours of the Skin (boss-level overview)
The skin is the <b>largest organ of the body</b>, built of <b>two layers — epidermis + dermis</b> (overlying the subcutis). Almost every skin disease can be filed under <b>six big headings</b>:
<b>1.</b> Disorders of pigmentation & melanocytes (Vitiligo · Albinism · Freckle · Melasma · Lentigo · Nevus=benign · Melanoma=malignant) · <b>2.</b> Disorders of epidermal maturation (Ichthyosis, Urticaria) · <b>3.</b> Inflammatory dermatoses — acute (Urticaria/hives, acute eczematous dermatitis, erythema multiforme) & chronic (Psoriasis, Seborrheic dermatitis, Lichen planus) · <b>4.</b> Blistering (bullous) diseases — inflammatory (Pemphigus, Bullous pemphigoid, Dermatitis herpetiformis) & non-inflammatory (Epidermolysis bullosa, Porphyria) · <b>5.</b> Disorders of epidermal appendages & infections (Acne, Rosacea; warts, Molluscum, Impetigo, superficial fungal infections) · <b>6.</b> Tumours & tumour-like lesions.
<b>Tumour triangles to remember:</b> benign (squamous papilloma, seborrheic keratosis, keratoacanthoma, nevus) → pre-malignant (actinic/solar keratosis, Bowen disease, xeroderma pigmentosum, erythroplasia of Queyrat, leukoplakia, radiodermatitis, dysplastic nevus) → malignant (<b>BCC</b>=basal cell carcinoma "rodent ulcer" [most common], <b>SCC</b>=squamous cell carcinoma "Marjolin ulcer" [2nd most common], <b>malignant melanoma=MM</b> [most deadly], Merkel cell carcinoma). Locally malignant: BCC + dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans.
Robbins 10e, Ch 25 (The Skin), pp. 1133–1169 · Arif 15e (2025) Vol-2, pp.336–347
## 1 · Normal Structure of the Skin
<b>Describe the normal structure of the skin (epidermis + dermis) and its cell types.</b>

<b>Normal skin = 2 layers:</b> the epidermis (surface) and the dermis (deeper), lying over the <b>subcutis</b> (subcutaneous fat). The lining epithelium of skin is <b>keratinized stratified squamous epithelium</b>.

EPIDERMIS — 5 layers (base → surface)

Stratum basale (stratum germinativum) — the basal (germinative) layer.
Stratum spinosum — prickle-cell layer. (Basal + spinosum together = "basal layer".)
Stratum granulosum — granular layer.
Stratum lucidum — present exclusively in the palms and soles.
Stratum corneum — superficial layer of dead keratinocytes, devoid of nuclei.

Four types of cells in the epidermis

<table>
<tr><th>Cell</th><th>Key feature</th></tr>
<tr><td>1. <strong>Squamous epithelial cells (keratinocytes)</strong></td><td>Main structural cells of the epidermis</td></tr>
<tr><td>2. <strong>Melanocytes</strong></td><td>Responsible for the production of <span class="hot">melanin</span></td></tr>
<tr><td>3. <strong>Langerhans cells</strong></td><td>Intraepidermal dendritic cells; <span class="good">bone-marrow-derived cells of the mononuclear–phagocytic system</span> (i.e. antigen-presenting cells)</td></tr>
<tr><td>4. <strong>Merkel cells</strong></td><td>Neuroendocrine/touch receptor cells</td></tr>
</table>

DERMIS — 2 parts

Superficial — papillary dermis (contains papillary blood vessels; forms dermal papillae).
Deeper — reticular dermis.

The dermis contains the <b>cutaneous appendages (adnexal structures)</b>: sweat glands (eccrine & apocrine), sebaceous glands, hair follicles, arrectores pilorum (arrector pili muscles) and nails. The reticular dermis also holds the arrector pili muscle, hair root, apocrine and eccrine ducts/glands, as seen in the labelled skin section.

🎯 Epidermal layers base→surface: <b>"Can Some Girls Let Curls Stay"</b> → Corneum · Lucidum · Granulosum · Spinosum · (Germinativum). <i>Lucidum = only in palms & soles (L for "L"imited / LUxury).</i>

## 4 · Psoriasis (Chronic Inflammatory Dermatosis)
<b>Short note: Psoriasis of the skin. (CMU-24M)</b>

<b>Psoriasis:</b> a chronic inflammatory dermatosis affecting about <b>2% of the population</b>.

Characteristics

Usually appears first between 15 and 30 years of age.
Lesions: brownish-red papules and plaques, sharply demarcated, covered with fine silvery-white scales.
When scales are removed by gentle scraping, fine bleeding points appear → "Auspitz sign".
Common sites: scalp, upper back, sacral region, and extensor surfaces of the extremities — especially knees and elbows.
In ~25% of cases, peculiar pitting of the nails is seen.
Psoriatic arthritis resembling rheumatoid arthritis occurs in ~5% of cases, but rheumatoid factor (RF) is absent.

Histopathology (fully developed lesions)

Acanthosis with regular downgrowth of rete ridges to almost the same dermal level, with thickening of their lower portion.
Elongation & oedema of the dermal papillae with broadening of their tips.
Suprapapillary thinning of the stratum malpighii.
Absence of the granular cell layer.
Prominent parakeratosis.
Presence of "Munro microabscesses" in the parakeratotic horny layer — diagnostic of psoriasis.

🎯 Psoriasis histo = <b>REPAIR</b> (Regular elongation of rete ridges + Absent granular layer + Munro microabscess + Parakeratosis + Auspitz sign). <i>Joint = seronegative arthritis (RF absent).</i>

Robbins 10e pp.1153–1159; Harsh Mohan 7e p.768 (figure)

## 7 · Basal Cell Carcinoma (BCC / Rodent Ulcer)
<b>Short note: Basal cell carcinoma / rodent ulcer. Give its etiology, pathogenesis and morphology. (CMU-23M/20Ju/19Nv, RMU-23M/19/18Nv, BUP-21Nv/M, SU-13Ja, DU-10/08Ju, RU-17Nv/Ja)</b>

<b>Basal cell carcinoma (BCC, "rodent ulcer"):</b> malignant proliferation of the <b>basal cell (stem cell) of the epidermis</b>.

It is the most common of all malignant skin tumours, arising from the basal layer of the epidermis and its appendages. Typically a <b>locally invasive, slow-growing tumour of middle age that rarely metastasises</b> (i.e. a locally malignant tumour).

Sites (important for VIVA)

Chronic sun-exposed sites in lightly pigmented elderly adults.
Most commonly on the face (90%), usually above a line from the angle of the mouth up to the ear lobule.

Aetiology (etiology)

i) Age: mostly after 40 years.
ii) Race: lightly skinned races (little melanin) are more susceptible than Black individuals.
iii) Precancerous conditions: Xeroderma pigmentosum (XP), basal cell nevus syndrome.
iv) Environmental factor: chronic sun exposure (UV=ultraviolet radiation → DNA damage).

Morphology

Macroscopic

Ulcerated (most common pattern): a slow-growing small nodule undergoes central ulceration with pearly, raised, rolled margins. The tumour enlarges by burrowing and destroying tissues locally like a rodent → hence "rodent ulcer".
Non-ulcerated (less frequent pattern): pearly and shiny papules, often containing dilated (telangiectatic) vessels.

Microscopic

Tumour cells resemble those of the <b>normal basal cell layer</b> of the epidermis. Two growth patterns:

Multifocal growth (superficial spreading BCC): extends over the skin surface several square centimetres or more.
Nodular lesions (nodular BCC): growing downwards deeply into the dermis.

Metastasis

Rarely metastasises (locally malignant tumour).

🎯 BCC = <b>"Basal Cell = Burrowing Cancer"</b> — locally destructive, rarely spreads, rodent-ulcer with pearly rolled margins, face/upper sun sites, age >40, light skin.

Robbins 10e p.1147; Harsh Mohan 7e p.775

## 8 · Squamous Cell Carcinoma (SCC / Marjolin Ulcer)
<b>Short note: Squamous cell carcinoma (Marjolin ulcer). Give its morphology. (CMU/RMJ-style VIVA)</b>

<b>Squamous cell carcinoma (SCC, "Marjolin ulcer"):</b> the <b>second most common skin cancer</b>, arising on <b>sun-exposed sites in older people</b>.

<b>Incidence:</b> higher in <b>men</b> than in women.

Pathogenesis (etiopathogenesis)

The most important cause of cutaneous SCC is <b>DNA damage induced by exposure to UV light</b>. Tumour incidence is <b>proportional to the degree of lifetime sun exposure</b>.

Predisposing factors (12)

Prolonged sun exposure
Immunosuppressive therapy
Industrial carcinogens (tars and oils)
Chronic ulcers and draining osteomyelitis
Old burn scars (Marjolin ulcers)
Arsenic exposure
Psoriasis
HIV (human immunodeficiency virus) infection
Ionizing radiation
(In the oral cavity) tobacco and betel-nut chewing
Precancerous conditions — xeroderma pigmentosum, actinic keratosis
Human papillomavirus (HPV)

Sites

May arise on any part of the skin and mucous membranes lined by squamous epithelium. <b>Most common locations:</b> the face, <b>pinna of the ears, back of the hands</b>, and <b>mucocutaneous junctions</b> such as the lips, anal canal and glans penis.

Morphology

Macroscopic — two patterns

More common: an ulcerated growth with elevated and indurated margin.
Less often: a raised fungating (polypoid) growth without ulceration.

Microscopic

SCC in situ (CIS): cells with atypical (enlarged and hyperchromatic) nuclei involving all levels of the epidermis.
Invasive SCC: variable degrees of differentiation — from tumours of polygonal cells in orderly lobules with numerous large areas of keratinization, to highly anaplastic neoplasms showing only abortive, single-cell keratinization (dyskeratosis).

Investigations

Skin biopsy (shave biopsy, punch biopsy, or excisional biopsy)
USG (ultrasonography) / CT (computed tomography) scan to identify nodal disease
FNAC (fine-needle aspiration cytology) from the lymph node
MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) to identify local extension

🎯 SCC = <b>"Sun + Scar → Cancer"</b>. Marjolin ulcer = SCC in old burn scar. Sun-exposed sites, men>women, keratinizing cells, metastasises (unlike BCC). N.B.: SCC may arise in skin, oral cavity, oesophagus, lungs, urinary bladder, salivary gland, cervix.

Robbins 10e p.1146; Harsh Mohan 7e p.774

## 9 · Nevus (Benign Melanocytic Tumour)
<b>Short note: Nevus / Melanocytic nevus (common mole). (DU-03S)</b>

<b>Nevus:</b> the term denotes a <b>benign tumour of melanocytes</b>. Most melanocytic nevi are <b>acquired</b>.

Types of nevus

Congenital nevus
Blue nevus
Spindle and epithelioid cell nevus (Spitz nevus)
Halo nevus
Dysplastic nevus (may transform into malignant melanoma)

Morphology

Gross

Tan to brown, uniformly pigmented, small (usually <b><6 mm</b> across), solid regions of relatively flat (<b>macules</b>) to elevated skin (<b>papules</b>) with <b>well-defined, rounded borders</b>.

Microscopic

Nevus cells may be epithelioid, cuboidal, fusiform or dendritic.
Nuclei are uniform and rounded in contour, contain inconspicuous nucleoli, and show little or no mitotic activity.

🎯 Nevus = <b>NEVer malignant</b> (unless it becomes <i>dysplastic</i> → melanoma). Small (<6 mm), symmetric, uniform nuclei, no mitosis.

Robbins 10e p.1136; BRS Pathology 5e p.344

## 10 · Malignant Melanoma (MM)
<b>Short note: Malignant melanoma / Melanoma. Give its morphology. (Very Important for VIVA; CU-16/14/12Ju, CMU-21M/15Ju)</b>

<b>Malignant melanoma (MM):</b> the <b>most deadly of all skin cancers</b>, arising from <b>melanocytes or nevus cells</b>.

<b>Incidence:</b> most common in <b>fair-skinned persons</b>; can occur at <b>all ages</b>.

Predisposing factors

Excessive exposure of white skin to sunlight
Persistent change in the appearance of a mole
Presence of a pre-existing naevus (especially dysplastic naevus)
Family history of melanoma in a patient with atypical mole
Higher age of the patient
Exposure to certain carcinogens

Sites

Skin (men: upper back; women: back and the leg); other locations — face, soles, palms and nail-beds.
Oral and anogenital mucosa (oropharynx, gastrointestinal and genitourinary tracts).
Oesophagus · Conjunctiva · Orbit · Meninges.

Clinical features

Usually asymptomatic.
Itching or pain may be early manifestations.
Majority of lesions are greater than 10 mm in diameter.
Change in colour, size, or shape in a pigmented lesion is the most consistent clinical sign.

Clinical warning signs — the <b>ABCDEs</b> of melanoma

Asymmetry
B — Irregular Borders
C — Change in Colour
D — Increasing Diameter
E — Evolution / change over time, especially if rapid

Other warning features

Pigmented lesions >6 mm in diameter
Any change in appearance
New onset of itching or pain

Growth pattern

Radial growth (initial phase): growth in all directions but predominantly lateral, within the epidermis and papillary zone of the dermis. During this stage melanomas do NOT metastasise; clinical cure is frequent.
Vertical growth (later phase): growth extends into the reticular dermis or beyond. Prognosis varies with the depth of the lesion. During this phase, lymphatic or haematogenous metastasis may occur.

Morphology

Macroscopic

Striking variation in colour — shades of black, brown, red, dark blue, and gray.
Occasionally zones of white or flesh-coloured hypopigmentation appear.
Borders are irregular and often notched.

Microscopic

Melanoma cells are larger than normal with large nuclei, irregular contours and prominent red (eosinophilic) nucleoli.
The appearance of the tumour cells is similar in radial and vertical phases.

🎯 Melanoma mnemonics: <b>ABCDE</b> = Asymmetry, Border, Colour, Diameter, Evolution. Growth = <b>"Radial first (safe), Vertical later (dangerous)"</b>. Most deadly skin cancer, fair skin, UV exposure.

Robbins 10e p.1139; BRS Pathology 5e p.347; Harsh Mohan 7e p.778

## 🔁 Rapid-Fire MCQ & Viva Sweep (incl. folded Systemic Pathology MCQs)
<b>Skin — True/False MCQs folded from V2-ch18 + one-liner mnemonics (test yourself, then reveal)</b>

Disorders of the skin due to melanocyte defect (VIVA): Vitiligo (T/F), Melasma (T/F), Nevus (T/F), Psoriasis (T/F), Acne vulgaris (T/F)? (CMU-22M)

Vitiligo = <b>True</b> · Melasma = <b>True</b> · Nevus = <b>True</b> · Psoriasis = <b>False</b> (chronic inflammatory dermatosis, not a melanocyte defect) · Acne vulgaris = <b>False</b> (appendageal disorder).

Benign adnexal tumours of the skin (VIVA): Keratoacanthoma (T/F), Trichoepithelioma (T/F), Benign fibrous histiocytoma (T/F), Cylindroma (T/F), Sebaceous adenoma (T/F)? (CU-19Nv)

Keratoacanthoma = <b>True</b> (benign epidermal/adnexal tumour) · Trichoepithelioma = <b>False</b> · Benign fibrous histiocytoma = <b>False</b> (a dermal tumour, not adnexal) · Cylindroma = <b>True</b> (sweat-gland adnexal tumour) · Sebaceous adenoma = <b>True</b> (sebaceous-gland adnexal tumour).

Pre-malignant lesions of the skin include (1): Paget disease (T/F), Psoriasis (T/F), Keratoacanthoma (T/F), Basal cell carcinoma (T/F), Leukoplakia (T/F)? (RU-17M)

Paget disease = <b>False</b> · Psoriasis = <b>False</b> · Keratoacanthoma = <b>False</b> (benign) · Basal cell carcinoma = <b>False</b> (already malignant) · Leukoplakia = <b>True</b>.

Pre-malignant lesions of the skin are (2): Seborrheic keratosis (T/F), Actinic keratosis (T/F), Leukoplakia (T/F), Nevus (T/F), Marjolin ulcer (T/F)? (RU-17M)

Seborrheic keratosis = <b>False</b> (benign) · Actinic keratosis = <b>True</b> · Leukoplakia = <b>True</b> · Nevus = <b>False</b> (benign; only <i>dysplastic</i> nevus is pre-malignant) · Marjolin ulcer = <b>False</b> (it is already an established SCC).

🎯 One-liners: Most common malignant skin tumour? 2nd most common? Most deadly skin cancer? Locally malignant tumours?

<b>BCC = most common</b> (rodent ulcer) · <b>SCC = 2nd most common</b> (Marjolin ulcer) · <b>Malignant melanoma = most deadly</b> · <b>Locally malignant: BCC + dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans</b>.

🎯 "Auspitz sign" & "Munro microabscess" = ? · "Rodent ulcer" = ? · "Marjolin ulcer" = ? · "ABCDEs of melanoma" = ?

Auspitz + Munro = <b>psoriasis</b> · Rodent ulcer = <b>basal cell carcinoma</b> · Marjolin ulcer = <b>SCC in an old burn scar</b> · ABCDE = <b>Asymmetry, Border, Colour, Diameter, Evolution</b>.

🎯 Stratum lucidum present only where? · Where are melanocytes/Langerhans cells/Merkel cells found? · Which layer is dead & anucleate?

<b>Stratum lucidum only in palms & soles</b> · Melanocytes, Langerhans cells, Merkel cells all in the <b>epidermis</b> (melanocytes & Merkel in basal layer; Langerhans intraepidermal dendritic) · <b>Stratum corneum</b> = dead keratinocytes, no nuclei.

🎯 Radial growth (initially) vs vertical growth (later) of melanoma — which metastasises?

<b>Radial growth: no metastasis</b> (cure frequent) · <b>Vertical growth: lymphatic/haematogenous metastasis</b> (<b>vertical = visceral spread</b>).

🎯 Sites of BCC vs SCC vs melanoma?

<b>BCC:</b> face (90%), above mouth–ear line, sun-exposed · <b>SCC:</b> face, pinna, back of hands, mucocutaneous junctions (lips, anal canal, glans penis) · <b>Melanoma:</b> men→upper back, women→back & leg; plus soles, palms, nail-beds & mucosal sites.
