### 🧠 Big Picture — Stool Examination (boss-level overview)
Stool (faecal) examination is a cornerstone of <b>clinical pathology</b> for evaluating gastrointestinal (GI) diseases. It follows a <b>five-part workflow</b>:
<b>1. Physical examination</b> → amount, consistency (semisolid), odour, colour (brown; pale in obstructive jaundice), presence of mucus and/or blood.
<b>2. Chemical examination</b> → occult blood test (OBT); faecal fat test (malabsorption); reducing substances (carbohydrate malabsorption); bilirubin (haemolytic jaundice).
<b>3. Microscopic examination</b> → trophozoites/cysts of protozoa (e.g., <i>Entamoeba histolytica</i>); larvae/ova of helminths; epithelial cells, pus cells, RBCs, macrophages (bacillary dysentery), muscle fibres.
<b>4. Culture of the specimen</b> → identification of pathogenic bacteria. <b>5. Culture sensitivity test</b> → guides antibiotic therapy.
Clinically, stool examination answers five critical questions: <b>(a)</b> parasitic infestation? <b>(b)</b> type of dysentery? <b>(c)</b> type of jaundice? <b>(d)</b> fat malabsorption? <b>(e)</b> occult GI bleeding?

## 1 · Laboratory Examinations Done with Stool
<b>What are the laboratory examinations done with stool (overview)?</b>

Laboratory examinations done with stool are divided into <b>five categories</b>:

Physical examination — amount, consistency, odour, colour, mucus, blood.
Chemical examination — occult blood, faecal fat, reducing substances, bilirubin.
Microscopic examination — protozoa, helminth larvae/ova, cells.
Culture of specimen — identify pathogenic bacteria.
Culture sensitivity test — guide antibiotic therapy.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.494–495 · Prof. Khaleque/378

<b>Describe the physical examination of stool.</b>

Amount — estimated grossly.
Consistency — normally semisolid.
Odour — characteristic; may change in malabsorption or infection.
Colour — normally brown; pale/clay-coloured in obstructive jaundice (absence of stercobilinogen).
Mucus — suggests inflammation (dysentery, IBD).
Blood-stained — visible (fresh) or occult (hidden) blood.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.494–495

<b>Describe the chemical examination of stool.</b>

Occult blood test (OBT) — detects trace/altered blood not visible to the naked eye.
Test for faecal fat — assesses fat malabsorption (steatorrhoea); elevated in cystic fibrosis of pancreas and obstructive jaundice.
Reducing substances — screens for unabsorbed sugars (e.g., lactose intolerance).
Bilirubin — normally absent from adult stool; present in haemolytic jaundice and diarrhoeal conditions.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.494–495

<b>Describe the microscopic examination of stool (parasites).</b>

Trophozoite of protozoa — e.g., Entamoeba histolytica (motile, invasive form).
Cyst of protozoa — dormant/transmissible form.
Larvae of helminths (parasitic worms).
Ova (eggs) of helminths — diagnostic for specific worm infestations.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.494–495

<b>Describe the cellular elements seen on microscopy of stool.</b>

<table>
<tr><th>Element</th><th>Significance</th></tr>
<tr><td>Epithelial cells</td><td>GI mucosal irritation/damage</td></tr>
<tr><td>Pus cells (WBCs)</td><td>Inflammatory/infectious lesions (bacillary dysentery, ulcerative colitis)</td></tr>

🎯 <b>Microscopy mnemonic "POMM":</b> Pus cells (inflammation) · Ova (worms) · Macrophages (bacillary dysentery) · Muscle fibres (malabsorption).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.494–495

<b>What is the role of culture and culture sensitivity in stool examination?</b>

4. Culture of the Specimen

Grows pathogenic bacteria (e.g., <i>Salmonella</i>, <i>Shigella</i>, <i>Vibrio cholerae</i>) to identify the causative organism.

5. Culture Sensitivity Test

Determines which antibiotics the identified pathogen is susceptible to → guides targeted therapy and helps prevent antimicrobial resistance.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.494–495

## 2 · Indications of Routine Stool Examination
<b>What are the indications of routine examination of stool?</b>

Routine stool examination is indicated in the following clinical scenarios:

Parasitic infestation — detection of ova, cysts, larvae, trophozoites (e.g., Ascaris, E. histolytica, hookworms).
Type of dysentery — differentiate bacillary dysentery (pus cells + macrophages, no parasites) from amoebic dysentery (trophozoites/cysts of E. histolytica).
Type of jaundice — pale stool = obstructive; normal/dark = haemolytic/hepatocellular; bilirubin present = haemolytic.
Fat in stool (steatorrhoea) — assess cystic fibrosis of pancreas (pancreatic insufficiency) and obstructive jaundice (bile salt deficiency).
Occult blood test (OBT) — detect any upper or lower GIT bleeding not visible to the naked eye.

🎯 <b>5 Indications mnemonic "P-D-J-F-O":</b> Parasites · Dysentery · Jaundice · Fat · Occult blood.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.495

## 3 · Occult Blood Test (OBT)
<b>🔴 What is the occult blood test (OBT)? Define it.</b>

The occult blood test (OBT) detects <b>altered blood or trace amounts of blood in stool that are not visible to the naked eye</b>. It is one of the most frequently examined investigations in clinical pathology.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.495–496 (DU-06S, BUP-22Nv, RU-19/18/17Nv, 16/14Ju, 13/12Ja, SU-17M, 11Ja, 09Ju, CU-16Ju, 07Ja)

<b>🔴 Name the methods used for occult blood test.</b>

Two commonly used methods:

Orthotoluidine test (benzidine-based peroxidase reaction)
Hema-Check slide test (rapid immunochromatographic card test)

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.495–496

<b>🔴 Describe the procedure of the orthotoluidine test.</b>

A. Orthotoluidine Test — Detailed Procedure

Make an emulsion of the stool (a pea-sized amount) in distilled water in a test tube.
Boil for 5 minutes and cool. (Destroys bacterial peroxidases that could give a false positive.)
In a clean test tube, place 4 drops of orthotoluidine reagent and 1 drop of hydrogen peroxide (H₂O₂); leave for 1 minute.
Add 1 drop of the stool suspension to the mixture.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.495–496

<b>🔴 How is the orthotoluidine test interpreted?</b>

<b>A green colour developing within 1 minute = positive result</b> → indicates occult (hidden) blood in the stool.

The reaction is based on the <b>peroxidase-like activity of haemoglobin</b>, which catalyses the oxidation of orthotoluidine by H₂O₂ to produce a green colour.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.495–496

<b>🔴 What are the precautions / sources of error in the orthotoluidine test?</b>

False positive may occur due to meat (red meat) or green vegetable products in the stool (both contain haemoglobin or plant peroxidases).
Therefore, the individual must not take meat or green vegetables for 3 days prior to the test.
Boiling the stool emulsion destroys bacterial peroxidases (reduces false positives from gut flora).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.495–496

<b>🔴 Describe the Hema-Check slide test.</b>

<b>B. Hema-Check Slide Test:</b> a rapid, <b>immunochromatographic test paper (slide)</b>. It detects human haemoglobin specifically (more specific than orthotoluidine). The slide is dipped in stool suspension and results read within minutes.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.495–496

<tr><td>1</td><td><strong>Bleeding peptic ulcer disease (PUD)</strong> — <em>H. pylori</em> or NSAIDs</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td><strong>Carcinoma (Ca.) of stomach</strong></td></tr>

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.495–496

<b>🔴 What is the clinical significance of the occult blood test?</b>

Used as a screening test for colorectal cancer (especially in individuals >50 years).
Evaluating iron-deficiency anaemia of unknown origin.
Monitoring GI bleeding.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 pp.495–496

The four <b>most common ova (eggs) of helminths</b> found in human stool:

## 5 · Melaena — Definition & Causes
<b>🔴 What is melaena? Define it.</b>

<b>Melaena is the passage of black, tarry, foul-smelling stools containing altered (digested) blood</b>, usually caused by <b>bleeding from the upper gastrointestinal tract (GIT)</b> — typically proximal to the ligament of Treitz (oesophagus, stomach, duodenum).

<b>Why black and tarry?</b> Haemoglobin is degraded by gastric acid and bacterial action → converted to <b>acid haematin</b> (a black pigment) → characteristic tarry appearance and smell.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.496 · Davidson's 23rd/781

<b>🔴 What are the causes of melaena (upper GIT bleeding)?</b>

<table>
<tr><th>#</th><th>Cause</th><th>Key Details</th></tr>
<tr><td>1</td><td><strong>Peptic ulcer disease (PUD)</strong></td><td><span class="hot">Most common cause</span>; risk factors: <em>H. pylori</em>, NSAIDs</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td><strong>Gastric erosions</strong></td><td>NSAIDs, alcohol, stress (Curling ulcer in burns; Cushing ulcer in CNS injury)</td></tr>
<tr><td>3</td><td><strong>Oesophageal varices</strong></td><td>Cirrhosis with portal hypertension ± portal vein thrombosis → dilated submucosal oesophageal veins rupture</td></tr>
<tr><td>4</td><td><strong>Cancer of stomach and oesophagus</strong></td><td>Gastric carcinoma (most common in males); oesophageal carcinoma</td></tr>
<tr><td>5</td><td><strong>Oesophagitis with hiatus hernia</strong></td><td>Acid reflux → mucosal inflammation/ulceration</td></tr>
<tr><td>6</td><td><strong>Vascular malformations</strong></td><td>Angiodysplasia (elderly); hereditary haemorrhagic telangiectasia (Osler-Weber-Rendu)</td></tr>
<tr><td>7</td><td><strong>Mallory-Weiss tear</strong></td><td>Longitudinal mucosal tear at gastro-oesophageal junction after forceful vomiting</td></tr>
</table>

## 6 · Fresh Blood in Stool (Haematochezia)
<b>🔴 What is haematochezia? Define it.</b>

<b>Haematochezia</b> = the passage of <b>fresh (bright red) blood per rectum</b>, typically indicating <b>bleeding from the lower gastrointestinal tract (GIT)</b> (distal to the ligament of Treitz — small intestine distal portion, colon, rectum, anal canal).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.496 · Davidson's 23rd/782

<b>🔴 Give the causes of fresh blood in stool (haematochezia).</b>

<table>
<tr><th>#</th><th>Cause</th><th>Key Details</th></tr>
<tr><td>1</td><td><strong>Dysentery</strong></td><td>Amoebic (<em>E. histolytica</em>) or bacillary (<em>Shigella</em>) → bloody diarrhoea with mucus</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td><strong>Bleeding piles (haemorrhoids)</strong></td><td>Dilated rectal/anal venous plexuses → painless bright red bleeding during defecation</td></tr>
<tr><td>3</td><td><strong>Malignant disease</strong></td><td>Ca. rectum, Ca. colon — <span class="hot">change in bowel habit + rectal bleeding in &gt;40 yr = colorectal cancer until proven otherwise</span></td></tr>
<tr><td>4</td><td><strong>Ulcerative colitis</strong></td><td>Chronic IBD — bloody diarrhoea, tenesmus, abdominal pain</td></tr>
<tr><td>5</td><td><strong>Crohn's disease</strong></td><td>Transmural granulomatous inflammation; less commonly bloody than UC, but fistulae, strictures</td></tr>
<tr><td>6</td><td><strong>Anorectal fissure and haemorrhoids</strong></td><td>Fissure = painful linear tear of anal mucosa → bright red bleeding on defecation</td></tr>
<tr><td>7</td><td><strong>Meckel's diverticulum</strong></td><td>Congenital remnant of vitellointestinal duct; ectopic gastric mucosa → ulceration → painless rectal bleeding (especially children); <strong>"Rule of 2s"</strong></td></tr>
</table>

🎯 <b>Haematochezia mnemonic:</b> Dysentery · Diverticula (Meckel's) · Malignancy · Ulcerative colitis · Crohn's · Fissure/Haemorrhoids.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.496 · Davidson's 23rd/782 · Lecture SSMC & DMC

<b>🔴 Distinguish melaena vs haematochezia.</b>

<table>
<tr><th>Feature</th><th>Melaena</th><th>Haematochezia</th></tr>
<tr><td>Appearance</td><td>Black, tarry, foul-smelling</td><td>Bright red / fresh blood</td></tr>
<tr><td>Source</td><td>Upper GIT (proximal to ligament of Treitz)</td><td>Lower GIT (distal to ligament of Treitz)</td></tr>

</table>

## 🔁 Rapid-Fire One-liners (test yourself, then reveal)
<b>Stool Examination — One-liners (test yourself, then reveal)</b>

🎯 Colour of normal stool? Pale/clay-coloured stool in which jaundice?

Normal = <b>brown</b> (stercobilinogen) · Pale = <b>obstructive jaundice</b>.

🎯 Bilirubin present in stool of which jaundice?

<b>Haemolytic jaundice</b> (absent in obstructive jaundice).

🎯 Macrophages in stool indicate what?

<b>Bacillary dysentery</b> (pathognomonic), not amoebic.

🎯 Undigested muscle fibres in stool suggest what?

<b>Pancreatic insufficiency</b> (improper protein digestion).

🎯 Orthotoluidine test uses which reagent? Green colour within 1 min means?

<b>Orthotoluidine + H₂O₂;</b> green = positive (occult blood).

🎯 Why avoid meat/green vegetables before OBT?

They contain haemoglobin/plant peroxidases → <b>false positive</b>.

🎯 Most common cause of melaena?

<b>Peptic ulcer disease (PUD)</b>.

🎯 Melaena = which GIT? Haematochezia = which GIT?

Melaena = <b>upper GIT</b> · Haematochezia = <b>lower GIT</b>.

🎯 Meckel's diverticulum rule of 2s?

<b>2 feet from ileocaecal valve · 2 inches long · 2% of population · symptomatic before age 2.</b>

🎯 Steatorrhoea in obstructive jaundice is due to what?

<b>Bile salt deficiency</b> → fat malabsorption.
