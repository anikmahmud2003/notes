### 🧠 Big Picture — Renal Function Test (RFT) — the boss-level overview
All kidney function tests fall into <b>three big buckets</b>: (1) Urine examination (routine physical, chemical, microscopic + special tests), (2) Blood analysis (blood urea, serum creatinine, serum uric acid, serum electrolytes), and (3) Renal clearance tests (creatinine clearance / GFR measurement) plus imaging & biopsy.
The <b>clearance concept</b> is the single most important idea — it measures the virtual volume of plasma cleared of a substance per minute, and it directly quantifies <b>GFR (glomerular filtration rate)</b>, RPF (renal plasma flow) and RBF (renal blood flow).
Two classic syndromes tie it all together: <b>Nephrotic</b> (protein-wasting → massive proteinuria, hypoalbuminaemia, oedema, hyperlipidaemia) vs <b>Nephritic/AGN</b> (inflammatory → haematuria, RBC casts, azotemia, hypertension, oliguria).
🎯 One-liner sweep: <b>Creatinine clearance = No.1 test</b> for GFR · <b>Serum creatinine = No.1 single test</b> in kidney function · <b>Inulin = gold standard</b> clearance (125 ml/min) · <b>RBC cast = hallmark of AGN</b> · <b>Hyaline cast = normal</b> · <b>E. coli = 70% of UTI</b>.

## 7 · Renal Clearance Test
🔴 What do you mean by renal clearance test?
Renal clearance test:
It is the virtual volume of plasma (in ml) that is completely cleared of a particular substance by both kidneys in each minute.
Or, renal clearance may be defined as the virtual volume of plasma which contains the amount of that substance excreted through urine per minute.
State the importance of renal clearance test.
🎯 Formula:
Renal clearance (ml/min) = (Concentration of substance in urine × Volume of urine [ml/min]) ÷ Concentration of substance in plasma

Renal Function Test — One-Liners & Mnemonics (test yourself, then reveal)
<b>What are the 3 normal clearance values? (inulin, creatinine, urea)</b>
Inulin 125 ml/min · Creatinine 70–140 ml/min · Urea 60–70 ml/min.
<b>Normal blood urea? Normal serum creatinine? Normal BUN?</b>
Urea 15–40 mg/dl (2.5–6.6 mmol/L) · Creatinine 0.7–1.4 mg/dl (60–120 μmol/L) · BUN 7–21 mg/dl.
<b>Normal uric acid? Normal urine volume per day? Normal specific gravity?</b>
Uric acid 2–7 mg/dl (0.12–0.42 mmol/L) · Urine 800–2500 ml/day (min 650) · S.G. 1.010–1.020.
<b>🎯 No. 1 single test in kidney function? No. 1 for GFR? Gold-standard clearance?</b>
Serum creatinine = No.1 single test · Creatinine clearance = No.1 for GFR · Inulin = gold standard (filtered but neither reabsorbed nor secreted).
<b>Why is serum creatinine more specific than blood urea?</b>
Because creatinine is produced at a constant rate from muscle and almost completely filtered at the glomerulus, so its changes reflect GFR; urea varies with protein intake, liver function and renal perfusion.
<b>RBC cast → ? WBC cast → ? Epithelial cast → ? Granular cast → ? Hyaline cast → ?</b>
RBC cast → AGN (hallmark) · WBC cast → pyelonephritis · Epithelial cast → renal tubular necrosis · Granular cast → glomerulonephritis · Hyaline cast → normal.
<b>Colour of urine tests: Heat coagulation → ? Benedict → ? Hay's → ? Fouchet's → ? Rothera → ? Orthotolidin → ?</b>
Heat = albumin · Benedict = sugar · Hay's = bile salts · Fouchet's = bilirubin · Rothera = ketones · Orthotolidin = blood (haematuria/haemoglobinuria).
<b>Ketone bodies are? Their normal blood/urine?</b>
Acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, acetone. Blood < 3 mg/dl (0.2 mmol/L); urine < 1 mg/day.
<b>Causes of ketonuria (physiological vs pathological)?</b>
Physiological: starvation, low-CHO diet, high-fat diet. Pathological: uncontrolled DM, severe dehydration after vomiting/diarrhoea, glycogen storage disease.
<b>Bence-Jones protein is? Its heat behaviour?</b>
Immunoglobulin light chain (κ or λ). Flocculates at 50–60 °C on slow heating, dissolves on boiling, reappears on cooling below 60 °C.
<b>🎯 One-liners: Most common organism of UTI? Most common primary cause of nephrotic syndrome in children? In adults? Hallmark cast of AGN? Best method to detect Bence-Jones protein?</b>
E. coli (~70% of UTI) · Minimal-change disease (children) · Membranous glomerulonephritis (adults) · RBC cast · Electrophoresis of concentrated urine.
<b>🎯 Define massive proteinuria? Microalbuminuria range? Narrow band on urine electrophoresis = ? Purple ring in Rothera's = ?</b>
Massive proteinuria = >3.5 gm albumin/24 hr · Microalbuminuria = 30–300 mg/day · Narrow band = Bence-Jones protein (monoclonal light chain) · Purple ring = ketone bodies present.
<b>🎯 Painless vs painful haematuria — pick the classic causes?</b>
Painless: IgA nephropathy, renal TB, renal cell carcinoma, bladder papilloma. Painful: renal stone, infection, trauma, polycystic kidney (clot colic), loin-pain-haematuria syndrome.
