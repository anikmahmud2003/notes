1 · VDRL Test (Syphilis Screening)

🔴 Write a short note on the VDRL (Venereal Disease Research Laboratory) test. What are its advantages and disadvantages?

VDRL (Venereal Disease Research Laboratory) test: the most commonly used simple and rapid screening test for syphilis, caused by the spirochaete Treponema pallidum.

  • It is non-specific (non-treponemal) but a useful screening test.
  • Becomes positive within 3–4 weeks of primary infection (after the primary chancre appears).
  • Generally becomes negative by 6 months after successful treatment — hence used to monitor treatment response.

Advantages

  1. Simple and easy to perform (flocculation test on a slide).
  2. Inexpensive — suitable for resource-limited settings.
  3. Suitable as a screening test: positive in approximately 70% of primary syphilis and 98% of secondary syphilis.

Disadvantages

  1. Non-specific — false-positive results occur in: malaria, measles, infectious mononucleosis (Epstein–Barr virus), leprosy, systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE), rheumatic disorders, pregnancy, advanced age, and other chronic infections.
  2. May become false-negative in late latent syphilis (prozone phenomenon — very high antibody titres that saturate the antigen).
  3. The VDRL antigen must be prepared fresh daily (commercially available antigen is a cardiolipin–lecithin–cholesterol mixture that loses potency rapidly).

Interpretation summary

Stage of SyphilisVDRL Sensitivity
Primary (chancre)~70%

2 · Causes of Hypoalbuminaemia

🔴 Mention and classify the causes of hypoalbuminaemia.

Hypoalbuminaemia: serum albumin concentration below the normal range (normal ≈ 3.5–5.0 g/dL). Albumin is synthesised exclusively by the liver and accounts for ~60% of total serum protein — its level reflects the balance between synthesis, intake, absorption, and loss.

Classification of causes (four-pillar framework)

MechanismExamples
1. Decreased protein intakeProtein-energy malnutrition (PEM) — kwashiorkor (protein-deficient diet), marasmus (total calorie deficiency), prolonged fasting, anorexia nervosa
2. Decreased protein absorptionProtein-losing enteropathy (PLE) — intestinal lymphangiectasia, coeliac disease, Crohn disease, Whipple disease; malabsorption syndromes
3. Decreased albumin synthesisChronic liver disease (CLD) & cirrhosis of the liver (the most common cause globally); acute liver failure; hepatocellular carcinoma; protein-calorie malnutrition impairs hepatic synthetic function
4. Increased protein lossRenal: nephrotic syndrome (massive proteinuria >3.5 g/day), acute glomerulonephritis (AGN)
GI: protein-losing enteropathy, burns (protein loss through denuded skin), protein-losing dermatoses, severe exudative conditions

Clinical significance of hypoalbuminaemia

  • Decreased oncotic pressure → oedema (peripheral, ascites, pleural effusion).
  • Altered drug binding — increased free fraction of highly protein-bound drugs (warfarin, phenytoin) → enhanced pharmacological effect and toxicity.
  • Decreased synthesis of clotting factors (some are albumin-associated) → prolonged prothrombin time (PT).
  • A reliable marker of chronic liver disease severity — the albumin level is part of the Child–Pugh and MELD (Model for End-Stage Liver Disease) scoring systems.
🎯 "4 I's of hypoalbuminaemia": Intake ↓ · Intestinal absorption ↓ · (hepatic) Insufficient synthesis ↑ · Increased loss (renal/GI/skin).

3 · Triple Antigen Test (Widal, Weil-Felix, Brucella Agglutination)

🔴 By which triple antigen test, which organisms can be detected? Describe each component.

The Triple Antigen test is a multiplex serological screening assay used to simultaneously detect agglutinating antibodies against three major febrile-illness pathogens. It is performed by mixing patient serum with a cocktail of three standardised antigens on a single slide or test card.

Antigen / TestOrganism DetectedKey Details
Widal testSalmonella typhi & paratyphi (enteric fever)Detects O (somatic) and H (flagellar) agglutinating antibodies. O antigen = current/recent infection; H antigen = past infection or immunisation. A four-fold rise in titre between acute and convalescent sera = diagnostic. Cross-reactions with other Salmonella species, malaria, and chronic liver disease may cause false-positives.
Weil-Felix testRickettsia species (spotted fever group & typhus group)Based on cross-reactivity between rickettsial antigens and Proteus OX-19, OX-2, OX-K antigens. OX-19 / OX-2 = epidemic / endemic typhus (R. prowazekii / R. typhi); OX-K = scrub typhus (R. tsutsugamuchi). Low sensitivity — largely historical but still asked in exams.
Brucella agglutination testBrucella species (brucellosis / undulant fever)Standard tube agglutination test (STAT) or Rose Bengal test. Titre ≥1:160 significant in endemic areas. Brucella melitensis, B. abortus, B. suis are the main human pathogens. False-positives: cholera vaccination (cross-reacts with H antigen of Brucella).

Advantages of the Triple Antigen approach

  • Single serum sample screens for three common causes of fever in one step.
  • Rapid, inexpensive, and suitable for primary-care / field-laboratory settings.
  • Useful when the clinical differential is broad (enteric fever vs rickettsial fever vs brucellosis).

Limitations

  • Non-treponemal and non-specific nature leads to cross-reactions (Widal may cross-react with other Salmonellae, malaria, TB; Weil-Felix has low sensitivity and specificity).
  • Titre interpretation requires clinical context and serial (acute + convalescent) sampling for Widal.
  • Brucella agglutination test may be falsely positive after cholera vaccination.
🎯 Triple Antigen = "W-W-B": Widal (Salmonella) · Weil-Felix (Rickettsia) · Brucella (Brucella).

Miscellaneous — True/False & one-liners (test yourself, then reveal)
Following are correctly matched: Ketonuria — old diabetes mellitus (T/F)? Increased lymphocytes — pyogenic meningitis (T/F)? Increased PT — overdose of anticoagulant (T/F)? Pseudocyesis — positive pregnancy test (T/F)? Albumin-globulin ratio altered — Crohn's disease (T/F)?
Ketonuria in old DM = True · increased lymphocytes in pyogenic meningitis = False (pyogenic = neutrophils, not lymphocytes) · increased PT in anticoagulant overdose = True · pseudocyesis (false pregnancy) = True (urinary hCG can be positive in pseudocyesis) · A:G ratio altered in Crohn's = False.
Which of the following values are within normal limits: FBS 5.5 mmol/L (T/F)? Serum creatinine 3.4 mg/dL (T/F)? Blood urea 120 mg/dL (T/F)? Triglycerides 180 mg/dL (T/F)? Serum PSA 0.4 U/L (T/F)?
FBS 5.5 mmol/L = True (normal fasting glucose <5.6 mmol/L) · S. creatinine 3.4 mg/dL = False (normal 0.6–1.2 mg/dL; 3.4 = renal failure) · Blood urea 120 mg/dL = False (normal 15–40 mg/dL) · Triglycerides 180 mg/dL = False (normal <150 mg/dL) · PSA 0.4 U/L = True (normal <4.0 ng/mL).
VDRL test becomes positive within how many weeks of primary syphilis? When does it generally become negative after treatment?
Positive within 3–4 weeks of primary infection. Becomes negative by 6 months after treatment.
VDRL test is positive in approximately what percentage of primary and secondary syphilis?
~70% of primary and ~98% of secondary syphilis.
Give 5 diseases that cause false-positive VDRL results.
Malaria, measles, infectious mononucleosis, leprosy, SLE, rheumatic disorders, pregnancy.
In the Triple Antigen test, which organism is detected by the Widal component?
Salmonella typhi & paratyphi (enteric fever).
Weil-Felix test detects antibodies against which group of organisms?
Rickettsia species — uses cross-reactive Proteus OX-19, OX-2, OX-K antigens.
The 4-pillar classification of hypoalbuminaemia causes: name the four mechanisms.
(1) Decreased intake (PEM) · (2) Decreased absorption (protein-losing enteropathy) · (3) Decreased synthesis (chronic liver disease / cirrhosis) · (4) Increased loss (nephrotic syndrome, AGN, burns).
🎯 One-liner: Triple Antigen = W-W-B. What does each letter stand for?
Widal (Salmonella typhi) · Weil-Felix (Rickettsia) · Brucella (Brucella species).
🎯 One-liner: Why must VDRL antigen be prepared fresh daily?
The cardiolipin–lecithin–cholesterol antigen mixture loses potency rapidly at room temperature and cannot be stored.
🎯 One-liner: VDRL is used to monitor treatment response in syphilis because…
Titres generally fall and become negative within 6 months of successful treatment — a persistently positive VDRL suggests treatment failure or reinfection.
🎯 One-liner: Most common cause of hypoalbuminaemia globally?
Chronic liver disease / cirrhosis — the liver is the sole site of albumin synthesis.
🎯 One-liner: What is the prozone phenomenon in VDRL?
In very high antibody titres (secondary syphilis), excess antibody saturates all antigen sites, preventing visible flocculation → false-negative result. Diluting the serum (quantitative VDRL) resolves this.
