### 🧠 Big Picture — Blood Glucose & Diabetes Mellitus (boss-level overview)
<b>Physiology first:</b> glucose is the body's energy currency. Its level is kept within a tight range by <b>insulin</b> (the only hormone that lowers blood glucose) and counter-regulatory hormones that raise it. Hyperglycaemia = blood glucose above normal; <b>diabetes mellitus (DM)</b> is a clinical syndrome of persistent hyperglycaemia from absolute or relative <b>insulin deficiency</b>.

## 2 · Diabetes Mellitus — Definition & Classification
<b>🔴 What is diabetes mellitus? (DU-22M,17Ja,08Ju, BUP-22Nv/M,21Nv/M, CMU-22Nv,05Ja, RU-18Ja) Classify diabetes mellitus. (DU-22M,17Ja,14Ju, CMU-22Nv)</b>

<b>Diabetes mellitus (DM):</b> a clinical syndrome characterised by an increase in plasma blood glucose (hyperglycaemia) due to <b>absolute or relative deficiency of insulin</b>. <i>(Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.722)</i>

Or, diabetes mellitus is a group of metabolic disorders sharing the common feature of <b>hyperglycaemia</b> caused by defects in <b>insulin secretion</b>, <b>insulin action</b>, or, most commonly, <b>both</b>. <i>(Ref: Robbins 10e p.1097)</i>

Classification of DM

<b>A. Type 1 diabetes (T1D)</b> — β-cell destruction, usually leading to absolute insulin deficiency:

Immune-mediated
Idiopathic (autoantibody-negative)

<b>B. Type 2 diabetes (T2D)</b> — combination of insulin resistance and β-cell dysfunction.

<b>C. Other specific types:</b>

Genetic defects of β-cell function: maturity-onset diabetes of the young (MODY), neonatal diabetes, maternally inherited diabetes and deafness (MIDD), defects in proinsulin conversion, insulin gene mutations.
Genetic defects in insulin action: type A insulin resistance, lipoatrophic diabetes.
Exocrine pancreatic defects ("pancreatogenic" or type 3C diabetes): pancreatitis, pancreatectomy/trauma, pancreatic cancer, cystic fibrosis, haemochromatosis, fibrocalculous pancreatopathy.
Endocrinopathies: acromegaly, Cushing syndrome, hyperthyroidism, pheochromocytoma, glucagonoma.
Infections: cytomegalovirus, Coxsackie B virus, congenital rubella.
Drugs: glucocorticoids, thyroid hormone, interferon-α, protease inhibitors, β-adrenergic agonists, thiazides, nicotinic acid, phenytoin (dilantin), Vacor.
Genetic syndromes associated with diabetes: Down syndrome, Klinefelter syndrome, Turner syndrome, Prader-Willi syndrome.

<b>D. Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM).</b>

Ref: Robbins 10e p.1099; Davidson's 23rd ed. p.733

<b>Mention the risk factors of diabetes mellitus. (BUP-21Nv/M)</b>

Key risk factors for developing diabetes mellitus:

Obesity (especially central/abdominal obesity)
Family history of diabetes (first-degree relatives)
Increasing age
Sedentary lifestyle / physical inactivity
Hypertension (>140/90 mmHg)
Dyslipidaemia (low HDL cholesterol ≤35 mg/dl, or high triglycerides ≥250 mg/dl)
History of gestational diabetes or delivery of a macrosomic baby
Previous impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) or impaired fasting glucose (IFG)
Ethnicity / genetic susceptibility
Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

🎯 <b>"ROAD-FIT"</b> — Risk factors: <b>R</b>elative (family history) · <b>O</b>besity · <b>A</b>ge · <b>D</b>yslipidaemia · <b>F</b>amily/ethnicity · <b>I</b>nactivity · <b>T</b>his hypertension & prior GDM.

🎯 <b>Classic triad "3-P":</b> Polyuria · Polydipsia · Polyphagia (+ weight loss) = the hallmark symptoms of diabetes.

Ref: Lippincott's 5th ed. p.337; Ganong 24th ed. p.449

<b>🔴 How will you diagnose / confirm a case of diabetes mellitus in a pathology laboratory?</b>

<b>Diagnosis of DM:</b> the term <i>diagnosis</i> refers to confirmation of diabetes in people who have symptoms, or who have had a positive screening test. The approach is two-part: (A) elicit symptoms, (B) laboratory investigations.

A. Symptoms suggesting diabetes

Common: <b>polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, tachycardia, weakness, weight loss.</b> Others: dehydration, hypotension, hypothermia, confusion, drowsiness, hyperventilation, muscle wasting, acidosis & coma.

<b>How will you confirm a case of diabetes mellitus?</b>

B. Laboratory investigations in DM

Fasting, random and post-prandial blood glucose concentration — diagnosis is confirmed by:
Fasting blood glucose >7.0 mmol/L (≥126 mg/dl)
Random blood glucose >11.1 mmol/L (>200 mg/dl)
Post-prandial blood glucose (2 hrs after meal) >11.1 mmol/L (>200 mg/dl)
In asymptomatic patients, two samples are required to confirm diabetes.
Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT):
Fasting plasma glucose >7.0 mmol/L (>126 mg/dl)
2 hours after 75 gm glucose load >11.1 mmol/L (>200 mg/dl)
Urine testing for glucose, ketone bodies, and proteins
Glycosylated Hb (HbA1c):
Normally 4–6% of total Hb is glycosylated
Diagnosis of diabetes is confirmed by HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (>6.5%)
Other investigations: full blood count, fasting lipid profile, blood urea and serum creatinine, serum electrolytes.

Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.726

🎯 <b>Nice to Know (definitions):</b> <i>Random</i> means without regard to time since last meal · <i>Fasting</i> means no calorie intake for at least 8 hours (but not more than 16 hours). <i>(Ref: Long Cases in Clinical Medicine 1st ed. p.610)</i>

<b>Write down the confirmatory biochemical tests for diabetes mellitus.</b>

<b>Confirmatory biochemical tests for DM:</b>

Either fasting plasma glucose (FPG) or random plasma glucose (RPG) can be used for the confirmation of diagnosis.
In symptomatic individuals, one abnormal plasma glucose measurement in the diabetic range confirms the diagnosis.
Asymptomatic individuals with a positive screening test (FPG or RPG) need another abnormal plasma glucose measurement on another day for confirmation.
OGTT is not recommended for routine use as a confirmatory test.

📌 <b>Definitions:</b> Fasting = no caloric intake for at least 8 h. Random = any time of day without regard to time since last meal. <i>(Ref: Manual of diabetes mellitus)</i>

Nice to Know — Screening tests for DM

Screening is recommended for all individuals with one or more major risk factors for developing DM.
FPG (fasting plasma glucose) is the laboratory test recommended for screening.
RPG (random plasma glucose) is an alternative test for screening.
When facilities for FPG or RPG are not available, CBG (capillary blood glucose) and UG (urinary glucose) are alternative screening tests for patients with classical symptoms of DM.

Ref: Manual of diabetes mellitus

## 4 · Diagnostic Criteria for DM (ADA & WHO)
<b>🔴 Write the diagnostic criteria for diabetes mellitus (as per ADA & WHO). (CMU-22M,21/20Nv) Give the WHO diagnostic criteria for diagnosis of diabetes mellitus. (DU-23Nv,21Ja,20Nv/M,19Nv,14/07Ju, BUP-22Nv,21Nv/M, CU-20M, RU-12Ja)</b>

<b>Diagnostic criteria for DM (ADA & WHO) — Very Important for VIVA:</b>

A single fasting plasma glucose >7.0 mmol/L (≥126 mg/dl) in symptomatic patients and on two separate occasions in asymptomatic patients.
A random plasma glucose >11.1 mmol/L (≥200 mg/dl) in an individual with classic signs/symptoms of diabetes (polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia & weight loss) and on two separate occasions in asymptomatic patients.
A 2-hour OGTT >11.1 mmol/L (>200 mg/dl) after a 75 gm load of glucose.
An HbA1c of ≥48 mmol/mol (>6.5%) is also diagnostic of diabetes.

🎯 <b>N.B:</b> As HbA1c reflects the last 2–3 months of glycaemia, it should <b>not</b> be used to diagnose diabetes where the duration of onset is short, i.e. in someone with suspected type 1 diabetes or severe symptomatic hyperglycaemia. <i>(Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.727)</i>

Ref: Lecture SSMC; Davidson's 23rd ed. p.727

<b>Mention the criteria of the patients who should be considered for diabetic testing.</b>

<b>Criteria for patients who should be considered for diabetic testing:</b>

All individuals 45 years of age or older (repeat at 3-year interval)
Persons with classic signs & symptoms of diabetes (polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia & weight loss)
Obesity
History of first-degree relations with diabetes
Women with gestational diabetes
Individuals with hypertension (>140/90 mmHg)
An HDL (high-density lipoprotein) cholesterol level ≤35 mg/dl or triglyceride (TG) level ≥250 mg/dl
Presence of impaired glucose tolerance on previous testing

Ref: Lecture SSMC

<b>Metabolic goals of management of DM:</b>

Target blood glucose level: fasting blood glucose <6 mmol/L
Target HbA1c: <7%
Target levels of blood lipids:

<b>🔴 What is hyperglycaemia sine glycosuria?</b>

<b>Hyperglycaemia sine glycosuria:</b> a condition in which the plasma glucose is above the normal renal threshold for glucose (<b>180 mg/dl</b>), but the urine is free from glucose.

<b>Cause:</b> this is due to a <b>raised renal threshold above normal</b>. This may occur in prolonged uncontrolled diabetes mellitus.

<b>Why is sugar absent in urine during hyperglycaemia sine glycosuria?</b>

Ref: Prof. Khaleque p.191

<b>🔴 A diabetic patient has blood glucose level more than 200 mg/dl, but he has no glycosuria. How can it be possible? (Very Important for VIVA)</b>

<b>Hypoglycaemia:</b> the condition when blood glucose level is reduced below normal (<2.2 mmol/L).

<b>Causes of hypoglycaemia (Very Important for VIVA):</b>

Diabetic patients taking anti-diabetic drugs with delayed, inadequate or missed meal, heavy exercise or alcohol

Addison's disease
Insulin-secreting tumour, e.g. insulinoma, retroperitoneal fibrosarcoma etc.
Malnutrition
Severe liver disease
Malabsorption, e.g. coeliac disease
Gastroparesis due to autonomic neuropathy
Breast-feeding by diabetic mother

1. Neuroglycopenic: confusion, drowsiness, speech difficulty, inability to concentrate, irritability, incoordination
2. Autonomic: sweating, trembling, hunger, anxiety

## 6 · Glycosuria
<b>🔴 What is glycosuria?</b>

<b>Glycosuria:</b> presence of glucose in urine which can be detected by conventional method (Benedict test). <i>(Ref: Prof. Khaleque p.202)</i>

<b>Causes:</b>

<b>Enumerate the causes of glycosuria. (BSFMMUS-23Nv, RU-MM/Ju, JU,14,13,11,07Ja, CU-14Ju/07Ja, SU-11Ju).</b>

Untreated diabetes mellitus (DM)
Renal glycosuria: blood glucose values are normal but sugar is present in urine due to low renal threshold value for glucose. There is reduced rate of reabsorption of glucose by kidney tubules, as occurs in serious tubular damage or inherited defect in tubular absorption.
Alimentary (lag storage) glycosuria: glycosuria developing after ingestion of a moderate amount of sugar or starch (which normally is dissipated without appearing in the urine) because the rate of intestinal absorption exceeds the capacity of the liver and other tissues to remove the glucose, thus allowing blood glucose levels to become high enough for renal excretion (above renal threshold, 10 mmol or 180 mg/dl). This may occur in:
Normal people
Liver disease
Hyperthyroidism
After gastric surgery (gastrectomy & vagotomy)
Fanconi syndrome: glucose, amino acid, and phosphate are excreted due to impaired absorption
Sometimes during pregnancy

Ref: Lecture SSMC; Prof. Khaleque p.192

## 7 · Oral Glucose Tolerance Test (OGTT / GTT)
<b>🔴 What do you mean by OGTT?</b>

Indication of OGTT — patients with:

Fasting plasma glucose: 6.1–6.9 mmol/L (110–125 mg/dl)
Random plasma glucose: 7.8–11.0 mmol/L (140–199 mg/dl)

Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.726

<b>Discuss the interpretation of OGTT. (DU-11Ja, BSFMMUS-23Nv, SUST-21Nv,20M,19Nv,15/13Ja,11Ju, RU-18M,16Ju, CU-14Ja,10Ju/Ja) How can you confirm OGTT?</b>

Preparation of the patient

Unrestricted CHO (carbohydrate) diet (minimum 150 gm/day) for 3 days before test
Avoidance of drugs affecting blood glucose level (e.g. corticosteroid, oral contraceptives etc.)
Treatment of any infection (if present)
Fasted overnight (for at least 8 hours) prior to the test
Avoidance of smoking & exercise prior to the test
Rest before test (for at least 30 minutes)
The person should sit throughout the procedure
No smoking is permitted during test interval
Caffeinated beverages should not be consumed
The person should not be bed-ridden or under stress

<b>Short note: GTT / OGTT. (DU-05S/Ja,04Ja, SU-22/19M,14Ju,06Ja,05M, RU-17M) Short note: OGTT and its interpretation. (DU-13Ju).</b>

Procedure

Fasting venous blood for plasma and corresponding urine sample is collected.
75 gm glucose dissolved in 250–300 ml of water is given by mouth within 5 minutes of fasting sample collection.
Venous blood and urine samples are collected 2 hours after the 75 gm glucose load — or at half-hourly intervals for at least two hours.
Plasma glucose on each blood sample and sugar on each urine sample is estimated.

Interpretation of OGTT curve

📌 <b>N.B:</b> For asymptomatic patients, <b>2 abnormal values greater than 11.1 mmol/L</b> are needed to establish the diagnosis of DM. <i>(Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.726)</i>

Nice to Know

In OGTT, testing of urine for glucose in all the samples is the usual procedure for detecting DM, but it has no diagnostic value because DM may exist without glycosuria if blood glucose level does not exceed the renal threshold.
Sometimes there may be glycosuria in the absence of DM, e.g. renal glycosuria, alimentary glycosuria.
Glycosuria frequently coexists with DM, but for diagnosis of DM glycosuria is not mandatory.

Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.726

IGT (impaired glucose tolerance)

<b>Laboratory diagnosis (by OGTT, WHO criteria):</b>

<b>🔴 What is IFG? (DU-15Ja,13Ju) Short note: Impaired fasting glucose (IFG). (Very Important for VIVA)</b>

<b>Impaired fasting glucose (IFG):</b> refers to a condition in which the fasting blood glucose level is consistently elevated above what is considered normal levels; however, it is not high enough to be diagnosed as diabetes mellitus.

Or, IFG is defined as the fasting glucose level between <b>6.1 to 6.9 mmol/L (110 to 125 mg/dL)</b> according to WHO.

Diagnostic criteria — WHO criteria for IFG

Fasting plasma glucose = 6.1 mmol/L (110 mg/dL) to 6.9 mmol/L (125 mg/dL)
2 hrs after OGTT glucose is normal, i.e. <7.8 mmol/L (<140 mg/dl)

Importance

These patients are prone to develop frank diabetes mellitus and cardiovascular disease.
There is a 50% risk over 10 years of progressing to overt diabetes.
IFG is a risk factor for mortality due to the serious complications of type-2 diabetes mellitus.

Advice to patients with IFG

Patients are advised for weight reduction of about 5–10% of their body weight, regular exercise and follow-up. Usually no drug therapy is recommended.

Ref: Lecture SSMC; Long Cases in Clinical Medicine 1st ed. p.610

<b>🔴 What is IGT?</b>

<b>Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT):</b> when a random or fasting blood glucose is suggestive but not diagnostic of DM — that is, when the values are above the upper limits of normal but below the values diagnostic of diabetes — it is called impaired glucose tolerance (IGT).

Or, when fasting glucose is less than 7 mmol/L, but during OGTT, 2 hours after the glucose load is <b>7.8 to 11.0 mmol/L</b>, it is called IGT.

<b>🔴 An obese male of 58 years complaining of loss of weight and mild generalized weakness. Laboratory investigations reveal fasting blood sugar 6.6 mmol/L and random level 10.0 mmol/L. Serum lipid level is also raised. What is the diagnosis?</b>

<b>🔴 An obese female aged 45 years with glucosuria, found during routine medical check-up. OGTT done and the results: Fasting 5.4 mmol/L (-), 1 hour 10.7 mmol/L (+), 2 hours 9.9 mmol/L (+). How will you interpret this OGTT result?</b>

<b>Interpretation of OGTT result:</b> Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT) <b>with renal glycosuria</b>.

Fasting 5.4 mmol/L → normal (<6.1)
1 hour 10.7 mmol/L → high (transient, above renal threshold)
2 hours 9.9 mmol/L → in IGT range (7.8–11.0 mmol/L) — suggestive but not diagnostic of DM
Urine sugar positive even though fasting blood glucose is normal → renal glycosuria (low renal threshold for glucose)

<b>What is the underlying cause for this?</b>

<b>Underlying cause of renal glycosuria:</b> in renal glycosuria, blood glucose values are normal but sugar is present in urine due to a low renal threshold value for glucose (reduced rate of reabsorption of glucose by kidney tubules — as occurs in serious tubular damage or inherited defect in tubular absorption).

## 9 · Glycosylated Haemoglobin (HbA1c)
<b>🔴 What is the significance of HbA1c? (CU-18Nv) Short note: Glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA1c). (DU-21Ja,20Nv/M,16Ju, CMU-21Nv,20Ju,16Ja,13Ju,11/10/09Ja) (Must be Asked in VIVA)</b>

<b>Glycosylated haemoglobin (HbA1c):</b> the most widely used measure of long-term glycaemic control in diabetes mellitus. It is produced by the glycosylation of haemoglobin at a rate proportional to the glucose concentration.

📌 <b>N.B:</b> Slow non-enzymatic covalent attachment of glucose to haemoglobin is called <b>glycosylation</b> of haemoglobin.

<b>Normal value:</b> 4.0 – 6.0% of total Hb (haemoglobin).

<b>Importance:</b>

Glycosylated haemoglobin provides an accurate and objective measure of glycaemic control over a period of weeks to months.
The measurement of HbA1c level in the blood is a useful adjunct in monitoring the management of DM.
The rate of formation of HbA1c is directly proportional to the ambient blood glucose concentration.
A rise of 1% in HbA1c corresponds to an approximate average increase of 2 mmol/L blood glucose.
A large shift in blood glucose control is rapidly accompanied by a change in HbA1c, detectable within 2–3 weeks.
It also provides an index of risk for developing diabetic complications.

Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.726; Lecture SSMC; Prof. Khaleque p.193

<b>What are the ketone bodies?</b>

<b>Ketone bodies / ketones:</b> acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate, and acetone collectively called ketone bodies. Excretion of more than a trace of these substances in the urine is called <b>ketonuria</b>.

<b>Mechanism of formation of ketonuria:</b> glucose provides energy to our body. If intake is insufficient or metabolism defective due to lack of insulin (as in untreated or uncontrolled diabetes), the body obtains energy by <b>breaking down fats & producing ketones.</b> Ketones are toxic to the brain and can cause coma (found in diabetic ketoacidosis). In ketoacidosis, <b>ketonuria is always present.</b> Urine ketone test detects <b>acetoacetate and acetone only.</b>

## 11 · Diabetic Ketoacidosis (DKA)
<b>🔴 How does ketoacidosis develop in cases of uncontrolled diabetes mellitus? (DU-24Nv,11Ju,10Ja,08Ju) Explain the mechanism of developing metabolic acidosis in diabetes mellitus. (DU-03M, RU-08Ju) Discuss in short about the pathogenesis of diabetic ketoacidosis. (DU-23Nv) Short note: Diabetic ketoacidosis. (DU-19M,18Ja) Short note on: Ketoacidosis. (DU-17Ja)</b>

<b>Ketoacidosis / diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA):</b> over-production of ketone bodies in long-standing uncontrolled or poorly controlled diabetes mellitus exceeds the buffering capacity of blood. As a result, blood pH is decreased and acidosis is produced — this is called ketoacidosis.

Mechanism of development of ketoacidosis in uncontrolled DM

Diabetes mellitus → ↓ plasma insulin level
Loss of the inhibitory effect of insulin on lipolysis → ↑ lipolysis
↑ Free fatty acid in plasma → ↑ β-oxidation
↑ Acetyl CoA (coenzyme A)
All the acetyl CoA cannot be utilised by the TCA (tricarboxylic acid / Krebs) cycle in the liver
Excess acetyl CoA is converted to acetoacetic acid & 3-hydroxybutyric acids
↑ Ketone bodies in blood
↑ Excretion of ketone body (and glucose) in urine → dehydration
↑ Ketone bodies in decreased volume of plasma → ↑ plasma ketone body concentration
→ Diabetic ketoacidosis

Ref: Lippincott's 3rd ed. p.195

Cardinal biochemical features

Hyperglycaemia
Hyperketonaemia
Metabolic acidosis

Clinical features

<b>A. Symptoms:</b>

Polyuria, thirst
Weight loss
Weakness
Nausea, vomiting
Leg cramps
Blurred vision
Abdominal pain

<b>B. Signs:</b>

Dehydration
Hypotension
Cold extremities / peripheral cyanosis
Tachycardia
Air hunger (Kussmaul breathing)
Smell of acetone
Hypothermia
Confusion, drowsiness, coma (10%)

Management

The principle components of treatment are:

The administration of short-acting (soluble) insulin
Fluid replacement
Potassium replacement
The administration of antibiotics if infection is present

Complications of diabetic ketoacidosis

Cerebral oedema
Acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)
Thromboembolism
Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC) (rare)
Acute circulatory failure

Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. pp.735–736

<b>🔴 A 26-year-old female was brought to the hospital with a recent history of polyuria, nausea, vomiting, and abdominal pain. Blood parameters on admission: glucose 19 mmol/L, serum electrolytes — Na 120 meq/L, Cl 85 meq/L, K 2.5 meq/L, HCO₃ 10 mmol/L. What is the likely diagnosis?</b>

Glucose 19 mmol/L is markedly elevated (>11.1 mmol/L, diabetic range). The low HCO₃ (10 mmol/L) confirms metabolic acidosis. Polyuria, nausea, vomiting and abdominal pain are classic presenting symptoms of DKA.

## 12 · Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)
<b>🔴 Define gestational diabetes mellitus. (DU-09Ja, RU-20Nv,18Ja) Short note: Gestational diabetes mellitus. (Important for VIVA)</b>

<b>Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM):</b> glucose metabolism changes during normal pregnancy. Gestational diabetes is defined as <b>diabetes with first onset or recognition during pregnancy.</b>

WHO suggests that all women meeting criteria for impaired glucose tolerance or diabetes after a <b>75 gm OGTT</b> should be classified as having gestational diabetes.

Risk factors for gestational diabetes

Obesity
Family history of type 2 diabetes
Previous glucose abnormality in pregnancy
Previous macrosomia (large baby)

Complications of gestational diabetes

Gestational diabetes is associated with an increased risk of later development of type 2 diabetes.
Maternal hyperglycaemia is associated with increased fetal size (fetal macrosomia) & obstructed labour.

Diagnosis of GDM — European criteria

Venous plasma glucose in the fasting state: >5.5 mmol/L (99 mg/dl)
2 hours after a glucose load: >9 mmol/L (162 mg/dl)

Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.752

<b>🔴 A 32-year-old pregnant woman case with high blood sugar level who had no diabetes mellitus before pregnancy. What is your diagnosis and how will you investigate & confirm the condition of the case? (RMU-24M)</b>

<b>Diagnosis:</b> <b>Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)</b> — diabetes with first onset or recognition during pregnancy.

<b>Investigations to confirm the case:</b>

OGTT (oral glucose tolerance test) with 75 gm glucose — the diagnostic test. WHO suggests all women meeting criteria for impaired glucose tolerance or diabetes after a 75 gm OGTT should be classified as having gestational diabetes.
European diagnostic criteria:
Venous plasma glucose in the fasting state: >5.5 mmol/L (99 mg/dl)
2 hours after a glucose load: >9 mmol/L (162 mg/dl)
Also: fasting blood glucose, random blood glucose, and urine testing for glucose / ketones as supportive tests.

Ref: Davidson's 23rd ed. p.752

<b>Blood Glucose & Diabetes — One-Liners & Mnemonics (test yourself, then reveal)</b>

🎯 One-liners: What is the renal threshold for glucose? What is the normal HbA1c?

<b>Renal threshold for glucose ≈ 180 mg/dl (10 mmol/L).</b> <b>Normal HbA1c = 4–6%</b> of total haemoglobin; DM diagnosed at HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (>6.5%); target <7% in management.

🎯 One-liner: Normal fasting / random blood glucose (mmol/L and mg/dl)?

<b>Fasting 3.6–6.1 mmol/L (65–110 mg/dl); random & 2-hr postprandial <7.8 mmol/L (<140 mg/dl).</b> DM: fasting >7.0 (≥126 mg/dl), random/2-hr >11.1 (≥200 mg/dl).

🎯 One-liner: "High blood sugar, no sugar in urine" = ? And "normal blood sugar, sugar in urine" = ?

High sugar, no urinary sugar = <b>Hyperglycaemia sine glycosuria</b> (raised renal threshold). Normal blood sugar with urinary sugar = <b>Renal glycosuria</b> (low renal threshold).

🎯 One-liner: Classic "3-P" symptoms of diabetes?

<b>Polyuria · Polydipsia · Polyphagia</b> (plus weight loss). These classic symptoms + random glucose >11.1 mmol/L confirm diagnosis.

🎯 One-liner: Which insulin-secreting tumour causes hypoglycaemia?

<b>Insulinoma</b> (pancreatic β-cell tumour).

🎯 One-liner: A 1% rise in HbA1c corresponds to how much average blood glucose rise?

≈ <b>2 mmol/L</b> average increase in blood glucose.
