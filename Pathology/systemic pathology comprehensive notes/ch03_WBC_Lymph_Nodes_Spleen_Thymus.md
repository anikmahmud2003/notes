# Ch 03 — Diseases of WBC, Lymph Nodes, Spleen & Thymus     <i>Full-detail flashcards (Q→A) · All abbreviations expanded · Robbins 4-pillar answers</i>
### 🧠 Big Picture — WBC, Lymph Nodes, Spleen & Thymus (boss-level overview)
This chapter is a <b>lymphoreticular "roadmap"</b>. It pivots on <b>lymph node enlargement (lymphadenopathy)</b> and how to walk it up to a diagnosis — from simple infection to lymphoma.
<b>The central axis:</b> <b>Lymphadenopathy</b> (generalised vs localised) → when it is neoplastic, we meet <b>LYMPHOMA</b> → divide it by its <b>two great families:</b>
🔴 <b>Hodgkin lymphoma (HL)</b> — has the <b>Reed-Sternberg (RS) cell</b> as its hallmark · monomorphic? <i>No — polymorphous</i> background · contiguous spread · extranodal involvement rare · high cure rate.
🔴 <b>Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL)</b> — <b>lacks RS cells</b> · monomorphic B-cell (85%) / T–NK-cell (15%) clone · non-contiguous spread · extranodal common (GIT, stomach, Waldeyer's ring) · lower cure rate.
📌 Then branch out: <b>Spleen</b> (functions, splenomegaly, hypersplenism, massive splenomegaly from chronic myeloid leukaemia CML / myelofibrosis / lymphoma) and <b>Thymus</b> (DiGeorge syndrome, follicular/thymic hyperplasia, thymoma = epithelial tumour).
🎯 <b>HL = RS cells + contiguous + curable</b> · <b>NHL = no RS + non-contiguous + incurable/low-cure</b>. <b>HL 👨 young adult, ♂ twice ♀; NS (nodular sclerosis) subtype favours young ♀.</b>

## 1 · Lymphadenopathy — Generalized
<b>🔴 Define generalized lymphadenopathy and write down its causes. (CU-08Ja, DU-12/07Ja, RU-20/18Nv)</b>

<b>Generalized lymphadenopathy:</b> when <b>2 or more groups of lymph nodes are enlarged</b>, the condition is called generalized lymphadenopathy.

Causes of generalized lymphadenopathy (<b>important for VIVA</b>)

<b>1. Infection:</b>

Viral: Infectious mononucleosis (Epstein–Barr virus / EBV), Cytomegalovirus (CMV), AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome — HIV)
Bacterial: Disseminated tuberculosis, brucellosis, cat scratch disease
Spirochaetal: Secondary syphilis
Protozoal: Toxoplasmosis
Helminthic: Filariasis
Fungal: Histoplasmosis, coccidioidomycosis

<b>2. Malignant disease:</b> Lymphoma · Leukaemia

<b>3. Collagen disease:</b> Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) · Rheumatoid arthritis

<b>4. Miscellaneous:</b> Sarcoidosis · Thyrotoxicosis · Drugs (e.g., phenytoin)

## 3 · Approach to Diagnosing Lymphadenopathy
<b>Give your approach to diagnose a case of lymphadenopathy. (RU-13Ju)</b>

Examination

Site
Number (single or multiple)
Size
Consistency:
Rubbery → Lymphoma
Firm & matted → TB (tuberculosis)
Hard → Malignancy
Stony hard → calcified lymph node
Soft, cystic → cold abscess
Tenderness (suggests acute inflammation)
Discrete or matted (matted in TB)
Fixation to underlying structure or overlying skin (in malignancy)
Skin (sinus, ulcer, signs of acute inflammation)

Investigation

CBC (complete blood count), ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate), PBF (peripheral blood film) — to exclude leukaemia, ↑ eosinophilia in lymphoma, atypical lymphocyte in infectious mononucleosis, and high ESR in TB.
Chest X-ray — TB, BHL (bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy) in lymphoma and lymphatic leukaemia.
If TB suspected → perform tuberculin test.
FNAC (fine needle aspiration cytology) or biopsy of LN (biopsy is preferable).
Bone marrow biopsy (leukaemia and lymphoma).
Other investigations (according to suspicion of cause): USG (ultrasonography) of abdomen — to observe hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, para-aortic and other lymphadenopathy; ANA (antinuclear antibody) in SLE (systemic lupus erythematosus).

Classification of tumours of lymph node

<b>2. T-cell neoplasms</b>

Ref: Robbins/10ᵉ/592; BRS Pathology/5ᵉ/180

<b>🔴 Define lymphoma / malignant lymphoma and classify it. (RMU-24M/22Nv/14/12Ju, CU-14Ja/11Ju/08Ja, RU-21/20M/17Ja)</b>

<b>Lymphoma:</b> the <b>neoplastic proliferation of cells native to the lymph nodes</b>. The cells are <b>lymphocytes and their precursors and histiocytes</b> (histiocyte derived from monocyte).

Classification of lymphoma

<b>1. Hodgkin lymphoma (HL):</b> malignant neoplasm of lymph node characterized by the presence of neoplastic giant cells called <b>Reed-Sternberg (RS) cells</b> and pleomorphic infiltration by lymphocyte, eosinophil, macrophage, plasma cell, neutrophil and follicular dendritic cells.

<b>WHO (World Health Organization) classification of Hodgkin lymphoma:</b>

A. Classical Hodgkin lymphoma: Nodular sclerosis · Mixed cellularity · Lymphocyte rich · Lymphocyte depleted
B. Non-classical Hodgkin lymphoma (classic RS cells absent or rare): Lymphocyte predominant

<b>2. Non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL):</b> malignant neoplasm of lymph node characterized by <b>monomorphic infiltration and absence of RS (Reed-Sternberg) cells</b>.

Classification of Non-Hodgkin lymphoma

A. REAL classification (Revised European-American classification of Lymphoid neoplasms) — see below.
B. Working classification — see below.

Ref: Robbins/10ᵉ/611; BRS Pathology/5ᵉ/179

## 5 · Reed-Sternberg (RS) Cell
<b>🔴 What is an R-S cell? Describe a typical RS cell, its variants/types, and their morphology. (DU-18/17/14/15/23/22/21/20/11/09/08/06, RMU-23/24M, CU-13/10/14Ja, RU-20/19/17/16/12/07/09Ju, SUST-21Nv, CMU-21M)</b>

<b>Reed-Sternberg (R-S) cells:</b> the <b>malignant lymphoid cells of B-cell origin</b> which is the hallmark of Hodgkin's disease. They are large tumour giant cells (~<b>45 μm in diameter</b>).

Classic (typical) RS cell — large tumour giant cell (45 μm)

Bi-lobed nucleus, with two halves often appearing as mirror images of each other.
Each nucleus contains a large inclusion-like nucleolus surrounded by a clear zone (halo) imparting an "owl-eye" appearance.
The nucleus is surrounded by non-malignant T-lymphocytes, plasma cells and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm.

<b>RS cells may also be present in some other conditions:</b>

Infectious mononucleosis
Non-Hodgkin lymphoma
CLL (chronic lymphocytic leukaemia)

Several variants of RS cells

Mononuclear variants: contain a single nucleus with a large inclusion-like nucleolus.
Lacunar cells: have a single multilobate nucleus, multiple small nucleoli, and abundant, pale-staining cytoplasm. In sections of formalin-fixed tissue, the cytoplasm is often torn away, leaving the nucleus lying in an empty space (a lacune). Characteristic of nodular sclerosis HL.
Lympho-histiocytic (L&H) variants ("popcorn cells"): have a delicate multilobed, puffy nucleus with abundant pale cytoplasm resembling popped corn ("popcorn cell"). Found in lymphocyte-predominant Hodgkin lymphoma.

Ref: Robbins/10ᵉ/612; Basic Hematology, Dr. Mainuddin/71; BRS Pathology/5ᵉ/179

## 6 · Hodgkin's Lymphoma (HL / Hodgkin Disease)
<b>🔴 What is Hodgkin's lymphoma? Give its criteria, clinical features, WHO classification and morphology. (DU-15/13/11/19/17/14/09, RMU-19Nv/24M, SU-19Nv/22/20/11/10/09/06/07/01, CU-13/10/16/15, RU-18/10/09, CMU-24M, BSFMMU'S-23Nv)</b>

<b>Hodgkin lymphoma:</b> malignant neoplasm of lymph node characterized by the presence of neoplastic giant cells called <b>Reed-Sternberg cells (RS cells)</b> and pleomorphic infiltration by lymphocyte, eosinophil, macrophage, plasma cell, neutrophil and follicular dendritic cells.

Criteria

Characteristically affects young adults (20–40 age group).
Males affected twice as commonly as females.
Arises in a single node or group of nodes and spreads first to anatomically contiguous lymphoid tissues.
Histological hallmark is presence of RS (Reed-Sternberg) cells.

Clinical features

May be asymptomatic.
May present with painless peripheral lymphadenopathy (usually rubbery and discrete, but may become matted).
Hepatosplenomegaly (enlargement of liver & spleen).
Systemic features ("B symptoms"): Fever (Pel-Ebstein fever), night sweats, weight loss, pruritus (itching).

Classification (WHO classification) — <b>V.V.I. for VIVA</b>

<b>A. Classical Hodgkin lymphoma (95% of cases):</b> Nodular sclerosis · Mixed cellularity · Lymphocyte rich · Lymphocyte depletion

<b>B. Non-classical Hodgkin lymphoma (5% of cases)</b> — classic RS cells absent or rare: Lymphocyte predominant

Morphology

Neoplastic giant cells (RS cells) are the true neoplastic element in Hodgkin disease.
Diagnostic (typical) RS cells are large cells with bi-lobed nucleus, the two halves often mirror images of each other. Each nucleus contains a large inclusion-like nucleolus surrounded by a clear zone (halo) imparting an "owl-eye" appearance. The nucleus is surrounded by non-malignant T-lymphocytes, plasma cells and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm.
Positive for CD15 & CD30, but negative for CD45.

Ref: Robbins/10ᵉ/611; Hoffbrand Haematology/6ᵉ/246; de Gruchy/5ᵉ/281

## 7 · Non-Hodgkin's Lymphoma (NHL)
<b>🔴 Classify non-Hodgkin's lymphoma according to the Working classification. Short note on Non-Hodgkin's lymphoma. (SU-14Ja/13Ju/13Ja/08Ju, RU-17Nv/21/20M/17Ja)</b>

<b>Non-Hodgkin's lymphoma (NHL):</b> malignant neoplasms of lymphoid cells or other cells native to lymphoid tissue characterized by <b>monoclonal proliferation of lymphoid cells of B-cell (85%) and T/NK-cell (15%) origin</b>.

Criteria

May arise at any age (peak incidence is 65–70 years).
Males affected twice as commonly as females.
Usual presentation is localized or generalized peripheral lymphadenopathy, which are discrete, painless and firm.
Spreads rapidly to non-contiguous areas.
Involvement of mesenteric, oropharyngeal lymphoid structures (Waldeyer's ring) and epitrochlear lymph nodes is very much common.
Extranodal involvement is also common, e.g., bone marrow, GIT (gastrointestinal tract), stomach, lung, thyroid, skin, testis, CNS (central nervous system), etc.
Systemic features are generally less common.
Histological hallmark is absence of RS (Reed-Sternberg) giant cells.

Etiology

Unknown in the majority of cases.
Infectious agents: virus (HTLV-1 = human T-lymphotropic virus-1, EBV = Epstein-Barr virus, HHV-8 = human herpesvirus-8, HIV-1 = human immunodeficiency virus-1, HCV = hepatitis C virus), bacteria (H. pylori = Helicobacter pylori → causes gastric lymphoma), protozoa (malaria → causes Burkitt lymphoma).

Classification of NHL

Various classifications exist: (1) REAL classification, (2) Working classification.

Working classification

Divides NHL into <b>three major prognostic subgroups</b> (depending on the rate at which cells divide):

<table>
<tr><th>Grade</th><th>Features</th></tr>
<tr><td><strong>1. Low grade</strong></td><td>Low cell proliferation rate · Asymptomatic for many years · Slow indolent course · <span class="warn">Incurable, but the patient survives for a long time</span></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>2. High grade</strong></td><td>High cell proliferation rate · Early symptoms common · <span class="hot">Fatal if untreated</span> · Responds better to treatment (potentially curable disease)</td></tr>

## 9 · Investigations of Lymphoma
<b>Write down the possible investigations to evaluate a suspected case of lymphoma. How will you diagnose a case of non-Hodgkin's lymphoma (NHL)? (CU-18Nv, CU-14Ja)</b>

<b>Possible investigations to evaluate a suspected case of lymphoma:</b>

CBC (complete blood count) with ESR (erythrocyte sedimentation rate) and PBF (peripheral blood film)
Chest X-ray
USG (ultrasonography) of whole abdomen
CT (computed tomography) scan of chest, abdomen, pelvis, neck — necessary for staging
PET (positron emission tomography) scan
FNAC (fine needle aspiration cytology) or biopsy of lymph nodes — biopsy is preferable (excisional or image-guided core needle biopsy), followed by histopathology and IHC (immunohistochemistry) — confirmatory
Bone marrow biopsy (preferably trephine biopsy from the unilateral iliac crest)
Liver function tests
Renal function tests

## 10 · Spleen
<b>What are the functions of the spleen? (with the normal splenic architecture)</b>

<b>Functions of the spleen:</b>

Phagocytosis of blood cells and particulate matter
Antibody production
Hematopoiesis (blood cell formation)
Sequestration of formed blood elements

<b>Normal splenic architecture</b> (for the diagram): the spleen has a <b>hilum</b> (indentation through which vessels enter/exit), a <b>capsule</b>, <b>white pulp</b> containing <b>lymphoid follicles with germinal centres</b>, and <b>red pulp</b> of splenic cords & sinusoids, all supported by trabeculae extending from the capsule.

Ref: Robbins/10ᵉ/629

<b>🔴 Mention the causes of splenomegaly. (Robbins)</b>

Causes of splenomegaly

<b>1. Infections:</b>

Nonspecific splenitis of various blood-borne infections (particularly infectious endocarditis)
Infectious mononucleosis · Tuberculosis · Typhoid fever · Brucellosis · Cytomegalovirus (CMV) · Syphilis · Malaria · Histoplasmosis · Toxoplasmosis · Kala-azar · Trypanosomiasis · Schistosomiasis · Leishmaniasis · Echinococcosis

<b>2. Congestive states related to portal hypertension:</b>

Cirrhosis of liver · Portal or splenic vein thrombosis · Cardiac failure

<b>3. Lympho-haematogenous disorders:</b>

Hodgkin lymphoma · Non-Hodgkin lymphoma · Multiple myeloma · Myeloproliferative disorders · Haemolytic anaemias

<b>4. Immunologic-inflammatory conditions:</b>

Rheumatoid arthritis · Systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE)

<b>5. Storage diseases:</b>

Gaucher disease · Niemann-Pick disease · Mucopolysaccharidoses

<b>6. Miscellaneous:</b>

Amyloidosis · Primary neoplasms and cysts · Secondary neoplasms

Ref: Robbins/10ᵉ/630

<b>What do you mean by hypersplenism?</b>

<b>Hypersplenism:</b> Enlargement of the spleen can cause a syndrome known as <b>hypersplenism</b>, characterized by <b>anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia</b>, alone or in combination.

<b>Probable cause of the cytopenias:</b> increased <b>sequestration of formed elements</b> and the consequent <b>enhanced phagocytosis by the splenic macrophages</b>.

Ref: Robbins/10ᵉ/629

<b>What are the causes of massive splenomegaly?</b>

<b>Causes of massive (huge) splenomegaly:</b>

Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML)
Myelofibrosis
Hemolytic anaemia
Chronic kala-azar
Chronic malaria
Lymphoma

Ref: Medicine Lecture SSMC

## 11 · Thymus
<b>Name some disorders of the thymus.</b>

<b>Disorders of the thymus:</b>

<b>1. Developmental disorders:</b>

Thymic hypoplasia or aplasia — seen in DiGeorge syndrome, accompanied by parathyroid developmental failure (hence hypocalcaemia + T-cell deficiency).
Isolated thymic cysts

<b>2. Thymic hyperplasia:</b> The term "thymic hyperplasia" is a bit misleading, as it usually applies to the appearance of <b>lymphoid follicles within the thymus</b>, a state referred to as <b>thymic follicular hyperplasia</b> (classically associated with <b>myasthenia gravis</b>).

<b>3. Thymic neoplasms:</b>

Germ cell tumours
Lymphomas
Carcinoids
Thymomas: tumours of thymic epithelial cells (associated with myasthenia gravis, pure red-cell aplasia, hypogammaglobulinaemia)

Ref: Robbins/10ᵉ/631

🎯 One-liner mnemonics:

<b>Hodgkin = "RS + young adult + contiguous + curable".</b> <b>NHL = "no RS + old (65–70) + non-contiguous + low cure".</b> <b>Consistency: rubbery = lymphoma, firm & matted = TB, hard = malignancy, stony hard = calcified, soft cystic = cold abscess.</b>

🎯 "Owl-eye" cell of Hodgkin disease = ? · "Popcorn cell" (L&H) variant of RS cell found in which HL subtype? · "Lacunar cell" characteristic of which subtype?

Owl-eye nucleus = <b>classic Reed-Sternberg cell</b> · popcorn/L&H cell = <b>lymphocyte-predominant (non-classical) HL</b> · lacunar cell = <b>nodular sclerosis HL</b>.

🎯 Which is the most common subtype of HL? Which subtype favours young women? Which HL subtype is CD15+ / CD30+ / CD45−?

Most common = <b>nodular sclerosis (NS)</b> · favours young women = <b>NS</b> · HL neoplastic RS cells are <b>CD15+ & CD30+, CD45−</b>.

🎯 Conditions where RS-like cells may also be found (apart from HL)?

<b>Infectious mononucleosis, non-Hodgkin lymphoma, and CLL (chronic lymphocytic leukaemia).</b>

🎯 What causes splenomegaly in the "storage disease" group?

<b>Gaucher disease, Niemann-Pick disease, mucopolysaccharidoses</b>.

🎯 What is the molecular/cellular basis of the cytopenias in hypersplenism?

<b>Increased sequestration of formed elements + enhanced phagocytosis by splenic macrophages</b> → anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia.
