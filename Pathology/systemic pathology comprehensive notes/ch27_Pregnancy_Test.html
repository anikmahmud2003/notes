🧠 Big Picture — Pregnancy Test (boss-level overview)

All pregnancy tests detect human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), a glycoprotein hormone produced by the syncytiotrophoblast of the placenta. hCG appears in blood as early as 6 days after conception and in urine as early as 14 days after conception.

🔴 Indications: (1) Early diagnosis of pregnancy · (2) Confirmation when foetal death is suspected · (3) Diagnosis of hydatidiform mole and choriocarcinoma · (4) Diagnosis of pseudocyst (false pregnancy with positive test).

📌 Methods (broad classification):

  • Modern / Immunological tests — cheap, reliable, accurate, quick (direct & indirect agglutination)
  • Biological tests — historically 99% accurate but costly & time-consuming (Friedman, Aschim-Zondek, Xenopus laevis, Galli-Mainini)
  • Radioimmunoassay (RIA) — high sensitivity
  • Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) — most widely used today
  • Ultrasonography — confirms intrauterine pregnancy
  • Radiological tests — plain X-ray (rarely used now)

🎯 Key principle: The presence of hCG in urine/blood is the basis of ALL pregnancy tests. Immunological tests use antigen–antibody reactions to detect hCG.

1 · Indications of Pregnancy Test

🔴 Enumerate the indications of pregnancy test. (CMU-23M; 15/12Ja; RMU-21Nv; 14Ja; 12/11/10/09Ju; 07Ja)

Four main indications:

  1. Diagnosis of pregnancy in an early stage — detection of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) confirms pregnancy before clinical signs appear.
  2. Confirmation when the foetus is suspected to be dead — a falling or absent hCG level suggests foetal demise.
  3. Diagnosis of hydatidiform mole and choriocarcinoma — these gestational trophoblastic diseases produce markedly elevated hCG levels.
  4. Diagnosis of pseudocyst — also called pseudocyesis (false pregnancy): a condition where the patient believes she is pregnant but no foetus is present; however, some cases may still show a positive test due to hormonal factors.
🎯 Mnemonic — "4 D's": Diagnose early pregnancy · Death of foetus confirmed · Detect mole/choriocarcinoma · Differentiate pseudocyst.

2 · Principle of Pregnancy Test

📌 What is the principle behind all pregnancy tests? (RU-14Ja; 10Ju)

Principle: The presence of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) in the urine of a pregnant woman forms the basis of all pregnancy tests.

Immunological basis: Pregnancy can be diagnosed by tests involving the principle of antigen–antibody reaction. In these tests:

  • hCG acts as the antigen.
  • Specific anti-hCG antibodies are used to detect it.
  • The reaction produces a visible change (agglutination, colour change on a strip, etc.).

Biological basis: hCG is a glycoprotein that mimics luteinising hormone (LH) and stimulates the corpus luteum to produce progesterone. Biological tests exploit this hormonal action in animals.

4 · Immunological (Modern) Tests

📌 Describe the immunological tests for pregnancy. What is the advantage of immunological methods?

Immunological tests are the modern, preferred methods — cheap, reliable, accurate, and provide quick results.

Two sub-types:

  1. Direct agglutination test (HCG agglutination test):
    • Uses latex particles coated with anti-hCG antibodies.
    • Patient's urine is mixed with the coated latex; if hCG is present → visible clumping (agglutination) occurs.
    • Positive result = pregnancy confirmed.
  2. Indirect agglutination test (Passive haemagglutination inhibition test):
    • Uses red blood cells (RBCs) coated with hCG antigen.
    • Anti-hCG antibody is added; if patient's urine contains hCG → it neutralises the antibody → no agglutination (inhibition = positive test).
    • If urine lacks hCG → antibody attacks RBC-coated antigen → agglutination occurs (negative test).

ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay): the most widely used pregnancy test today. Uses enzyme-labelled anti-hCG antibodies bound to a solid phase; addition of substrate produces a colour change if hCG is present. Available as rapid strip-test kits for home use.

5 · Biological Tests

🔴 Describe the biological tests for pregnancy — Friedman, Aschim-Zondek, Xenopus laevis, Galli-Mainini. Why are they largely obsolete?

Biological tests rely on the gonadotrophic action of hCG in laboratory animals. They are 99% accurate but costly and time-consuming, and have been replaced by immunological methods.

1. Friedman Test

  • Animal used: immature female rabbit.
  • Principle: hCG in the patient's urine stimulates the rabbit's ovary → formation of corpora haemorrhagica (haemorrhagic follicles) on the ovarian surface within 24–48 hours.
  • Positive result: red spots on ovaries at laparotomy.

2. Aschim-Zondek (A-Z) Test

  • Animal used: immature female rat or mouse.
  • Principle: hCG stimulates the ovary → ovulation + corpora lutea formation. The animals are killed and ovaries examined macroscopically.
  • Positive result: presence of large, bright red corpora haemorrhagica or corpora lutea on both ovaries.

3. Female Xenopus laevis (African Clawed Toad) Test

  • Animal used: female Xenopus laevis (clawed toad).
  • Principle: hCG induces ovulation in the toad. Eggs appear in the water or cloaca within 12–24 hours.
  • Advantage: faster than mammalian tests.

4. Galli-Mainini Test

  • Animal used: male frog (e.g., Rana pipiens).
  • Principle: hCG mimics LH → stimulates the testes → spermatozoa appear in the urine of the frog within a few hours.
  • Advantage: very rapid (results in 2–4 hours); uses male animals (no need for oestrous cycle timing).
🎯 Why biological tests are obsolete: need live animals, 24–48 hr wait (except Galli-Mainini), expensive, ethical concerns, not suitable for large-scale screening. Immunological/ELISA tests are faster, cheaper, and equally reliable.

6 · RIA & ELISA

📌 Write a short note on Radioimmunoassay (RIA) and ELISA for pregnancy detection.

Radioimmunoassay (RIA)

  • A highly sensitive in-vitro technique for measuring hCG in blood or urine.
  • Principle: competition between radiolabelled hCG and patient's hCG for binding sites on specific anti-hCG antibodies.
  • Measures even very low levels of hCG — useful for early pregnancy detection and monitoring gestational trophoblastic disease.
  • Limitation: requires specialised equipment, radioactive materials, trained personnel → not suitable for routine bedside or home testing.

ELISA (Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay)

  • The most widely used pregnancy test worldwide today.
  • Principle: anti-hCG antibodies are coated onto a solid phase (plate or strip). Patient's urine is added; if hCG is present → it binds to the antibody. A second enzyme-linked anti-hCG antibody is added → forms a "sandwich". Addition of substrate produces a visible colour change (positive result).
  • Sensitivity: can detect hCG levels as low as 20–25 mIU/mL.
  • Advantages: rapid (5–10 minutes), inexpensive, no special equipment, can be done at home (urine dip-stick kits).
  • Serum ELISA is more sensitive than urine ELISA and can detect pregnancy earlier.

7 · Imaging & Radiological Tests

📌 What is the role of ultrasonography and radiology in pregnancy testing?

Ultrasonography (USG)

  • Not a "pregnancy test" per se — it confirms the location and viability of a pregnancy once hCG test is positive.
  • Transvaginal USG: can detect a gestational sac from approximately 5 weeks (when hCG reaches ~1,500–2,000 mIU/mL).
  • Transabdominal USG: detects pregnancy from approximately 6–7 weeks.
  • Useful for: confirming intrauterine pregnancy, ruling out ectopic pregnancy, diagnosing hydatidiform mole (snowstorm pattern), assessing foetal viability.

Radiological Tests

  • Plain X-ray: historical role; can detect foetal skeleton after ~16–20 weeks. Rarely used today due to radiation risk.
  • Now replaced by USG for all obstetric imaging needs.

8 · Timing — When is hCG Detectable?

📌 When does hCG appear in blood and urine after conception? How does timing affect test selection?
SpecimenEarliest detection after conceptionNotes
Blood (serum)As early as 6 daysSerum hCG is detectable before urine hCG; serum ELISA/RIA is the earliest method
UrineAs early as 14 daysUrine dip-stick tests (ELISA-based) become positive ~2 weeks after conception; corresponds to ~1 day after missed period

Practical note: Most home pregnancy test kits detect urine hCG at a threshold of 20–25 mIU/mL. The most reliable results are obtained when testing is done after a missed period. Testing too early may yield a false negative.

🎯 Rule of 6 and 14: Blood = 6 days; Urine = 14 days. "6 for serum, 14 for pee."
