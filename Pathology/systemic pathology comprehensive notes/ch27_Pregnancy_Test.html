🧠 Big Picture — Pregnancy Test (boss-level overview)

All pregnancy tests detect human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), a glycoprotein hormone produced by the syncytiotrophoblast of the placenta. hCG appears in blood as early as 6 days after conception and in urine as early as 14 days after conception.

🔴 Indications: (1) Early diagnosis of pregnancy · (2) Confirmation when foetal death is suspected · (3) Diagnosis of hydatidiform mole and choriocarcinoma · (4) Diagnosis of pseudocyst (false pregnancy with positive test).

📌 Methods (broad classification): Modern / Immunological tests (direct & indirect agglutination) · Biological tests (Friedman, Aschim-Zondek, Xenopus laevis, Galli-Mainini) · Radioimmunoassay (RIA) · Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) · Ultrasonography · Radiological tests.

🎯 Key principle: The presence of hCG in urine/blood is the basis of ALL pregnancy tests. Immunological tests use antigen–antibody reactions to detect hCG.

1 · Indications of Pregnancy Test

🔴 Enumerate the indications of pregnancy test.

Four main indications:

  1. Diagnosis of pregnancy in an early stage — detection of hCG confirms pregnancy before clinical signs appear.
  2. Confirmation when the foetus is suspected to be dead — a falling or absent hCG level suggests foetal demise.
  3. Diagnosis of hydatidiform mole and choriocarcinoma — these produce markedly elevated hCG levels.
  4. Diagnosis of pseudocyst (pseudocyesis / false pregnancy) — patient believes she is pregnant but no foetus is present; some cases may show a positive test due to hormonal factors.
🎯 Mnemonic — "4 D's": Diagnose early pregnancy · Death of foetus confirmed · Detect mole/choriocarcinoma · Differentiate pseudocyst.

2 · Principle of Pregnancy Test

📌 What is the principle behind all pregnancy tests? (Immunological basis)

Principle: The presence of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) in the urine of a pregnant woman forms the basis of all pregnancy tests.

Immunological basis: involves the principle of antigen–antibody reaction:

  • hCG acts as the antigen.
  • Specific anti-hCG antibodies are used to detect it.
  • The reaction produces a visible change (agglutination, colour change on a strip, etc.).

Ref: Samson Wright's 13e/581 · Lecture SSMC · Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

📌 What is the biological basis of pregnancy tests?

hCG is a glycoprotein that mimics luteinising hormone (LH) and stimulates the corpus luteum to produce progesterone. Biological tests exploit this hormonal action in animals.

4 · Immunological (Modern) Tests

📌 Describe the direct agglutination test.

Direct agglutination test (HCG agglutination test):

  • Uses latex particles coated with anti-hCG antibodies.
  • Patient's urine is mixed with the coated latex; if hCG is present → visible clumping (agglutination) occurs.
  • Positive result = pregnancy confirmed.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

📌 Describe the indirect agglutination (passive haemagglutination inhibition) test.

Indirect agglutination test (Passive haemagglutination inhibition test):

  • Uses red blood cells (RBCs) coated with hCG antigen.
  • Anti-hCG antibody is added; if urine contains hCG → it neutralises the antibody → no agglutination (inhibition = positive test).
  • If urine lacks hCG → antibody attacks RBC-coated antigen → agglutination occurs (negative test).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

📌 What is the advantage of immunological methods?

Immunological tests are the modern, preferred methods — cheap, reliable, accurate, and provide quick results.

ELISA is the most widely used today, available as rapid strip-test kits for home use.

5 · Biological Tests

🔴 Describe the Friedman test.

1. Friedman Test

  • Animal used: immature female rabbit.
  • Principle: hCG stimulates the rabbit's ovary → formation of corpora haemorrhagica (haemorrhagic follicles) within 24–48 hours.
  • Positive result: red spots on ovaries at laparotomy.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

🔴 Describe the Aschim-Zondek (A-Z) test.

2. Aschim-Zondek (A-Z) Test

  • Animal used: immature female rat or mouse.
  • Principle: hCG stimulates the ovary → ovulation + corpora lutea formation. Animals killed and ovaries examined macroscopically.
  • Positive result: large, bright red corpora haemorrhagica or corpora lutea on both ovaries.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

🔴 Describe the Xenopus laevis test.

3. Female Xenopus laevis (African Clawed Toad) Test

  • Animal used: female Xenopus laevis (clawed toad).
  • Principle: hCG induces ovulation in the toad. Eggs appear in the water or cloaca within 12–24 hours.
  • Advantage: faster than mammalian tests.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

🔴 Describe the Galli-Mainini test.

4. Galli-Mainini Test

  • Animal used: male frog (e.g., Rana pipiens).
  • Principle: hCG mimics LH → stimulates the testes → spermatozoa appear in the urine of the frog within a few hours.
  • Advantage: very rapid (results in 2–4 hours); uses male animals (no need for oestrous cycle timing).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

🔴 Why are the biological tests largely obsolete?
🎯 Why obsolete: need live animals · 24–48 hr wait (except Galli-Mainini) · expensive · ethical concerns · not suitable for large-scale screening. Immunological/ELISA tests are faster, cheaper, and equally reliable.

6 · RIA & ELISA

📌 Write a short note on Radioimmunoassay (RIA) for pregnancy.
  • A highly sensitive in-vitro technique for measuring hCG in blood or urine.
  • Principle: competition between radiolabelled hCG and patient's hCG for binding sites on specific anti-hCG antibodies.
  • Measures even very low levels — useful for early pregnancy and monitoring gestational trophoblastic disease.
  • Limitation: requires specialised equipment, radioactive materials, trained personnel → not for routine bedside/home testing.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

📌 Write a short note on ELISA for pregnancy.
  • The most widely used pregnancy test worldwide today.
  • Principle: anti-hCG antibodies coated on a solid phase; patient's urine added; a second enzyme-linked anti-hCG antibody forms a "sandwich"; substrate produces a visible colour change (positive).
  • Sensitivity: detects hCG as low as 20–25 mIU/mL.
  • Advantages: rapid (5–10 minutes), inexpensive, no special equipment, home dip-stick kits.
  • Serum ELISA is more sensitive than urine ELISA and detects pregnancy earlier.

7 · Imaging & Radiological Tests

📌 What is the role of ultrasonography in pregnancy testing?

Ultrasonography (USG)

  • Not a "pregnancy test" per se — confirms the location and viability of a pregnancy once hCG test is positive.
  • Transvaginal USG: detects a gestational sac from ~5 weeks (hCG ~1,500–2,000 mIU/mL).
  • Transabdominal USG: detects pregnancy from ~6–7 weeks.
  • Useful for: confirming intrauterine pregnancy, ruling out ectopic pregnancy, diagnosing hydatidiform mole (snowstorm pattern), assessing foetal viability.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

📌 What is the role of radiological (X-ray) tests?

Radiological Tests

  • Plain X-ray: historical role; can detect foetal skeleton after ~16–20 weeks. Rarely used today due to radiation risk.
  • Now replaced by USG for all obstetric imaging.

8 · Timing — When is hCG Detectable?

📌 When does hCG appear in blood and urine after conception?
SpecimenEarliest detection after conceptionNotes
Blood (serum)As early as 6 daysDetectable before urine; serum ELISA/RIA is the earliest method
UrineAs early as 14 daysDip-stick (ELISA) positive ~2 weeks after conception ~1 day after missed period
🎯 Rule of 6 and 14: Blood = 6 days; Urine = 14 days. "6 for serum, 14 for pee."

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

📌 How does timing affect test selection?

Most home kits detect urine hCG at a threshold of 20–25 mIU/mL. Most reliable results are obtained after a missed period. Testing too early may yield a false negative.
