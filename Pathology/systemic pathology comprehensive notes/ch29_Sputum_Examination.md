# Ch 29 — Sputum Examination     <i>Full-detail flashcards (Q→A) · All abbreviations expanded · Robbins 4-pillar answers</i>
### 🧠 Big Picture — Sputum Examination (boss-level overview)
<b>Sputum</b> = mucus coughed up from the <b>lower airways</b> (distinguished from saliva which comes from the mouth). Examination is a key step in diagnosing <b>respiratory infections</b>, <b>malignancy</b>, and <b>parasitic disease</b>.
The workflow follows a logical path: <b>1. Collection</b> (clean/sterile container, morning sample, deep cough) → <b>2. Macroscopic (naked-eye) examination</b> — appearance, blood, amount, colour, consistency, odour → <b>3. Microscopic examination</b> — Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) stain for acid-fast bacilli (AFB) / tubercle bacilli, Gram stain for bacteria/fungi, Leishman stain for eosinophils, KOH mount for <i>Aspergillus</i>, unstained wet mount for parasites, Pap smear / H&E for malignant cells → <b>4. Culture & sensitivity</b> of the specimen.
🔴 <b>Must-know associations:</b> Rusty sputum = pneumonia · Pink frothy = pulmonary oedema · Anchovy-sauce = amoebic liver abscess · Putrid/foetid = bronchiectasis / lung abscess · Blood-stained = pulmonary tuberculosis (TB), bronchogenic carcinoma.

## 1 · Collection of Sputum
<b>❓ How is sputum collected? What precautions must be taken?</b>

Container: Sputum should be collected in a clean, dry, wide-necked container.
For culture: Should preferably be collected in a sterile container to avoid contamination.
Timing: Sputum is best collected in the morning before any mouth wash is used — this ensures maximal overnight secretions are present.
Technique: The person should cough deeply to produce a sputum specimen from the lower airways (not just saliva).

🎯 <b>Mnemonic:</b> "First thing in the morning, deep cough, clean wide jar" — best specimen!

## 2 · Naked-Eye (Macroscopic) Examination
<b>❓ What are the causes of blood in sputum (haemoptysis)?</b>

<b>Haemoptysis</b> = coughing up blood from the respiratory tract. Causes of <b>blood-stained sputum</b>:

<table>
<tr><th>#</th><th>Cause</th><th>Key Point</th></tr>
<tr><td>1</td><td><strong>Pulmonary tuberculosis (TB)</strong></td><td>Most classic cause; blood may be streaked or frank haemoptysis</td></tr>
<tr><td>2</td><td><strong>Bronchiectasis</strong></td><td>Dilated bronchi prone to recurrent infection &amp; bleeding</td></tr>
<tr><td>3</td><td><strong>Mitral stenosis</strong></td><td>↑Pulmonary venous pressure → haemorrhage</td></tr>
<tr><td>4</td><td><strong>Pneumonia</strong></td><td>"Rusty" sputum = haemolysed blood mixed with fibrin &amp; pus</td></tr>
<tr><td>5</td><td><strong>Lung abscess</strong></td><td>Necrotic lung tissue + blood</td></tr>
<tr><td>6</td><td><strong>Bronchogenic carcinoma</strong></td><td>Malignant tumour eroding vessels → blood-stained sputum</td></tr>
</table>

<b>❓ When is sputum offensive or foul-smelling (foetid)?</b>

Sputum is usually <b>odourless</b>. It becomes <b>offensive / putrid (foul-smelling / foetid)</b> in:

Bronchiectasis — pooling of secretions → bacterial overgrowth → foul odour
Lung abscess — necrotic tissue + mixed anaerobic infection
Gangrene of the lung — tissue necrosis + anaerobic bacteria

🎯 <b>"FOG" = Foetid odour → Bronchiectasis, lung abscess, Gangrene"</b>

## 3 · Microscopic Examination
<b>❓ Describe the Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) staining method. What is it used for in sputum examination?</b>

<b>Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) stain</b> = an <b>acid-fast staining</b> technique used for demonstration of <b>tubercle bacilli</b> (<i>Mycobacterium tuberculosis</i>) in sputum.

Principle

Mycobacteria have a waxy cell wall (mycolic acid) → resists decolorisation by acid-alcohol → "acid-fast bacilli" (AFB).
Carbol fuchsin (primary stain) penetrates the waxy wall with heat; once stained, the bacilli resist decolorisation by acid-alcohol → appear bright red against a blue background (methylene blue counterstain).

Procedure (simplified)

Prepare a thin smear of sputum on a glass slide.
Flood with carbol fuchsin and heat gently (steam) for 3–5 minutes.
Decolorise with 3% acid-alcohol.
Counterstain with methylene blue.
Examine under oil immersion (×1000).

<b>Finding:</b> <b>Bright red, slender, slightly curved bacilli</b> = acid-fast bacilli (AFB) = suspected <i>M. tuberculosis</i>.

<b>Use in sputum:</b> Primary screening for <b>pulmonary tuberculosis (TB)</b>. At least 3 sputum specimens (early morning) should be examined for best sensitivity.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498 · Robbins 10e, Respiratory infections

Special points

Gram stain is the most rapid & commonly used initial stain for any infected sputum.
Leishman stain — identifies eosinophils → important in bronchial asthma, allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA), parasitic infections.
KOH mount — essential for suspected Aspergillus (aspergilloma, ABPA).
Cytology (Pap / H&E) — for suspected malignancy, especially bronchogenic carcinoma.

<b>❓ What is the role of Gram staining in sputum examination?</b>

Gram stain is done for demonstration of:

Common bacteria — Gram-positive cocci (e.g., Streptococcus pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus) and Gram-negative bacilli (e.g., Klebsiella pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae).
<i>Actinomyces</i> species — Gram-positive, branching, filamentous bacteria (sulfur granules in actinomycosis).
Fungi — although special stains (PAS, GMS) are better, Gram stain can show yeast forms (Candida) and pseudohyphae.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498

<b>❓ What is the purpose of Leishman staining in sputum examination?</b>

Leishman stain (a Romanowsky-type stain similar to Giemsa) is used in sputum to identify:

Eosinophils — granules stain orange-red → suggest allergic conditions, bronchial asthma, parasitic infections, drug reactions.
Other leukocytes — neutrophils (polymorphonuclear cells) indicate bacterial infection.
Pus cells — confirms purulent sputum.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498

<b>❓ What is demonstrated by an unstained cover-slip preparation of sputum?</b>

An <b>unstained wet-mount (cover-slip) preparation</b> is used for demonstration of:

<i>Entamoeba histolytica</i> — motile trophozoites with ingested RBCs (if amoebic lung abscess / hepatic abscess has ruptured into bronchus).
Hydatid cyst — characteristic hooklets, scolices, or laminated membrane fragments.
<i>Paragonimus</i> (lung fluke) eggs — in parasitic pulmonary infections (endemic areas).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498

<b>❓ When is KOH preparation used in sputum examination? What does it demonstrate?</b>

<b>KOH (potassium hydroxide) preparation</b> is done for demonstration of <b>fungal elements</b>, specifically:

<i>Aspergillus</i> species — the most clinically important fungal pathogen detected this way in sputum.
KOH dissolves host cells and debris but leaves fungal cell walls (chitin) intact → clear visualisation of hyphae.
Aspergillus appears as septate hyphae with regular, dichotomous (45° angle) branching.

<b>Clinical settings:</b> suspected aspergilloma, allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA), invasive aspergillosis in immunocompromised patients.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498

<b>❓ How are malignant cells demonstrated in sputum? What stains are used?</b>

Demonstration of <b>malignant cells</b> in sputum is done by <b>sputum cytology</b>:

Papanicolaou (Pap) stain — the standard cytological stain; provides excellent nuclear detail for identifying malignant cells.
Haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stain — also used for sputum cytology smears; more familiar to histopathologists.

<b>What is looked for:</b> Cells with <b>nuclear pleomorphism, high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic (N:C) ratio, hyperchromasia, irregular nuclear membranes, and mitotic figures</b> → suggestive of bronchogenic carcinoma.

<b>Clinical use:</b> Particularly valuable for detecting squamous-cell carcinoma of the bronchus (exophytic endobronchial tumour → cells shed into sputum). Adenocarcinoma and small-cell carcinoma may also be detected but less reliably by sputum cytology alone.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498 · Robbins 10e, Lung tumours

<b>❓ What is the role of culture in sputum examination?</b>

<b>Culture of the sputum specimen</b> is essential for:

Identification of the causative organism in suspected respiratory infections — more sensitive & specific than Gram stain alone.
Antibiotic sensitivity testing — guides targeted antimicrobial therapy (reduces resistance).
Culture media used:
Blood agar & MacConkey agar — for common bacteria
Lowenstein-Jensen (LJ) medium — for Mycobacterium tuberculosis (ZN-positive cases)
Sabouraud dextrose agar — for fungi

## 4 · Indications of Sputum Examination
<b>❓ Enumerate the indications of sputum examination.</b>

Sputum examination is indicated in the following clinical situations:

A. Smear and Culture — identification of causative organism in suspected infection

<table>
<tr><th>Infection</th><th>Key Sputum Finding</th></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Pneumonia</strong></td><td>Gram stain + culture → identify bacterial pathogen (<em>S. pneumoniae</em>, <em>K. pneumoniae</em>, etc.)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Tuberculosis (TB)</strong></td><td>ZN stain for AFB + LJ culture → <em>M. tuberculosis</em></td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Fungal infection</strong></td><td>KOH mount + culture on Sabouraud agar → <em>Aspergillus</em>, <em>Candida</em>, etc.</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong><em>Pneumocystis jirovecii</em> (formerly <em>P. carinii</em>) in HIV/AIDS</strong></td><td>Induced sputum + GMS (Gomori methenamine silver) stain → cysts</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Bronchiectasis</strong></td><td>Culture (often polymicrobial: anaerobes + aerobes) for targeted antibiotic therapy</td></tr>
</table>

B. Cytological examination

<table>
<tr><th>Indication</th><th>Key Finding</th></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Malignant cells</strong></td><td>Pap / H&amp;E stain → bronchogenic carcinoma</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Viral inclusions</strong></td><td>Intranuclear or intracytoplasmic inclusions (e.g., CMV, herpes in immunocompromised patients)</td></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Asbestosis</strong></td><td><strong>Asbestos bodies</strong> (ferruginous bodies) — golden-brown, dumbbell-shaped, beaded structures in sputum → occupational asbestos exposure</td></tr>
</table>

🎯 <b>"5 Infections + 3 Cytology" = 8 indications:</b> Pneumonia, TB, Fungal, P. jirovecii/HIV, Bronchiectasis + Malignancy, Viral inclusions, Asbestosis.

## 🔁 Rapid-Fire MCQ & Viva Sweep
<b>Sputum Examination — True/False & one-liners (test yourself, then reveal)</b>

Best time to collect sputum for examination is in the evening (T/F)?

<b>False.</b> Best collected in the <b>morning</b> before any mouth wash — overnight secretions are maximal.

Rusty sputum is characteristic of bronchiectasis (T/F)?

<b>False.</b> Rusty sputum = <b>lobar pneumonia</b>. Bronchiectasis → foul-smelling, copious, blood-tinged or purulent sputum.

Pink frothy sputum is seen in pulmonary tuberculosis (T/F)?

<b>False.</b> Pink frothy sputum = <b>pulmonary oedema</b>. TB → blood-stained or haemoptysis.

Anchovy-sauce sputum is seen in amoebic liver abscess (T/F)?

<b>True.</b> Anchovy-sauce (brownish-red, thick) sputum = <b>amoebic liver abscess</b> that has ruptured into the lung/bronchus.

Ziehl-Neelsen stain is used for demonstration of tubercle bacilli (T/F)?

<b>True.</b> ZN stain = acid-fast stain → <i>Mycobacterium tuberculosis</i> appears as <b>bright red acid-fast bacilli (AFB)</b>.

KOH preparation is done for demonstration of Aspergillus species (T/F)?

<b>True.</b> KOH dissolves host cells, leaving fungal chitin walls intact → <i>Aspergillus</i> septate hyphae with 45° dichotomous branching visible.

Leishman stain is used to demonstrate acid-fast bacilli in sputum (T/F)?

<b>False.</b> Leishman stain identifies <b>eosinophils, leukocytes, and pus cells</b>. ZN stain is for AFB.

Scanty sputum is seen in lung abscess (T/F)?

<b>False.</b> Lung abscess → <b>copious</b> sputum. Scanty sputum = bronchitis, bronchial asthma, early pneumonia.

Foul-smelling (foetid/putrid) sputum is seen in bronchiectasis and lung abscess (T/F)?

<b>True.</b> Bronchiectasis, lung abscess, and gangrene → offensive/putrid sputum due to anaerobic bacterial overgrowth.

Papanicolaou (Pap) stain in sputum is used for demonstration of malignant cells (T/F)?

<b>True.</b> Pap stain (and H&E) used for sputum cytology → detection of bronchogenic carcinoma cells.

Green/yellow sputum is seen in viral pneumonia (T/F)?

<b>False.</b> Green/yellow sputum = <b>purulent conditions</b> (bacterial infections) due to neutrophil-derived myeloperoxidase. Viral sputum is typically mucoid/white.

🎯 "Anchovy-sauce" sputum = ? · "Rusty" sputum = ? · "Pink frothy" sputum = ?

Anchovy-sauce = <b>Amoebic liver abscess (ruptured into lung)</b> · Rusty = <b>Lobar pneumonia</b> · Pink frothy = <b>Pulmonary oedema</b>.

🎯 The unstained cover-slip preparation in sputum demonstrates which organisms?

<b>Entamoeba histolytica</b> (motile trophozoites), <b>hydatid cyst</b> elements (hooklets, scolices), and <b>Paragonimus</b> eggs (lung fluke).

🎯 Sputum examination for P. jirovecii (P. carinii) is indicated in which patient group?

<b>HIV/AIDS / immunocompromised patients</b> with suspected <b>Pneumocystis jirovecii</b> pneumonia (PCP). Induced sputum + GMS stain → cysts.

🎯 Asbestos bodies (ferruginous bodies) in sputum indicate which condition?

<b>Asbestosis</b> — occupational asbestos exposure. Golden-brown, beaded, dumbbell-shaped structures.

🎯 Gram stain of sputum demonstrates which organisms besides common bacteria?

<b>Actinomyces</b> species (branching filamentous, Gram-positive) and <b>fungi</b> (yeast forms like <i>Candida</i>).

🎯 How many sputum specimens should be examined for best TB screening?

<b>At least 3 early-morning specimens</b> should be collected and examined by ZN stain for best sensitivity of AFB detection.
