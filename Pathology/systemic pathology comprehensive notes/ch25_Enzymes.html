🧠 Big Picture — Enzymes of Clinical Importance (boss-level overview)

Clinical enzymes are intracellular proteins released into the blood when cells are damaged. Measuring their serum levels helps localise and quantify tissue injury. The key organ–enzyme map:

🔴 Heart: CK (Creatine Kinase) / CK-MB, AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase), LDH (Lactate Dehydrogenase), Troponins, Myoglobin.

🔴 Liver: ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase) = most liver-specific, AST, ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase), GGT (Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase), LDH.

🔴 Muscle: CK, AST, LDH, Aldolase.

🔴 Bone: ALP, ACP (Acid Phosphatase).

🔴 Pancreas: Amylase, Lipase.

🔴 Prostate: Prostatic Acid Phosphatase, PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen).

🔴 Genetic: Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD), Pyruvate Kinase.

Two critical concepts: (1) Isoenzymes — same enzyme, different tissue forms (e.g., CK-MM from muscle, CK-MB from heart, CK-BB from brain). (2) Cardiac markers have a time-release curve — CK-MB rises earliest (4–6 h), Troponins stay elevated longest (5–14 days) → guides diagnosis at different time points post-MI (Myocardial Infarction).

4 · SGPT & SGOT (Transaminases)

📌 Write a short note on SGPT and SGOT. What is their function and which is more specific for liver disease?

SGOT = AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase) · SGPT = ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase).

Function: Both transfer amino groups — ALT transfers from alanine, AST transfers from aspartate — to a ketoacid, producing pyruvate and oxaloacetate respectively.

Location: Both are located in the cytoplasm of the hepatocyte. Both are widely distributed in other tissues, but ALT activity outside the liver is low, so ALT is more specific for hepatocellular damage.

Normal values: AST = 10–45 IU/L · ALT = 10–50 IU/L.

🎯 ALT = "A" for "Alone in liver" (most specific) · AST = "A" for "Anywhere" (heart, muscle, liver).

5 · Cardiac Markers (Enzymes + Proteins)

🔴 What are cardiac markers? Name the enzymes and proteins used to detect myocardial infarction (MI). Give their timing of rise, peak, and return to normal.

Cardiac markers: intracellular enzymes or proteins released into blood following cardiac muscle damage due to ischaemia (e.g., MI) or other assaults. They show a serial change: initial rise → peak → fall.

Types of cardiac markers

A. Enzymes:

  1. Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK) / Creatine Kinase (CK) — especially CK-MB
  2. Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST / SGOT)
  3. Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH)
  4. Gamma-Glutamyl Transpeptidase (γ-GTP)

B. Other proteins:

  1. Troponins (TnI and TnT)
  2. Myoglobin

6 · Troponin — Best Indicator for MI

🔴 Why are troponin T and I the best indicators for MI? Write a short note on troponin.

Troponins are regulatory proteins that control calcium-mediated contraction of cardiac and skeletal muscle.

Types

  • Troponin I (TnI) — totally cardio-specific → called cardio-specific troponin-I (cTnI).
  • Troponin T (TnT) — not totally cardio-specific.
  • Troponin C (TnC) — not used as a cardiac marker.

Why TnI and TnT are the best indicators for MI: they are structural cardiac muscle proteins released during myocyte damage and necrosis. They have nearly complete cardiac tissue specificity + high sensitivity, making them the gold standard.

Kinetics

Not normally detectable in circulation. After acute MI:

  • Rise: at 4–6 hours
  • Peak: at 24–48 hours
  • Remain elevated: for 5–14 days

Other conditions where troponins may be elevated: minor degrees of myocardial damage, pulmonary embolus, septic shock, and pulmonary oedema.

7 · CK (Creatine Kinase / CPK)

🔴 Write a short note on CK (Creatine Kinase) / CPK. What are its isoenzymes and clinical importance?

Creatine Kinase (CK) or Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK) is an enzyme highly concentrated in brain, myocardium, and skeletal muscle. It is composed of two dimers designated "M" (muscle) and "B" (brain).

Isoenzymes (Isoforms)

IsoenzymeTissue Source
CK-MMSkeletal muscle
CK-MBCardiac muscle
CK-BBBrain

Normal values: Male: 55–170 U/L · Female: 30–135 U/L.

Clinical importance — CK is raised in:

  • Myocardial Infarction (MI)
  • Muscular dystrophy
  • Inflammatory myositis & vasculitis
  • Motor neuron disease
  • Alcohol & drugs
  • Trauma & strenuous exercise
  • Hypothyroidism & metabolic myopathy

8 · CK-MB Isoenzyme

🔴 What is the clinical importance of CK-MB? Give its normal value and role in MI.

CK-MB (Creatine Kinase MB isoenzyme): the cardiac-specific isoform of creatine kinase.

Normal value: <6% of total CK.

Clinical importance: In MI, the CK-MB cardiac-specific isoform is disproportionately elevated compared with total CK.

9 · Clinical Case — Acute Myocardial Infarction

🎯 A 56-year-old male presents with excruciating left-sided chest pain and sweating, not relieved by rest or sublingual nitroglycerine. What is your provisional diagnosis? What investigations and expected results?

Provisional diagnosis: Acute Myocardial Infarction (MI).

Investigations & expected results:

  1. ECG: ST-segment elevation · Pathological Q-wave formation · T-wave inversion.
  2. CK-MB: Markedly raised. Rises within 4–6 hours · stays elevated up to 48–72 hours.
  3. Troponin-I: Markedly raised. Rises within 4–6 hours · stays elevated up to 5–14 days.

11 · PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen)

🔴 Write the clinical importance of PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen).

PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen):

  1. Measurement of PSA is the most important test used in the diagnosis and management of prostate cancer.
  2. 20–40% of patients with serum PSA > 4.0 ng/mL will have prostate cancer on biopsy.
  3. High levels of serum PSA (>100 ng/mL) almost always indicate distant bone metastases.
  4. PSA is most useful for monitoring response to treatment and disease progression.
  5. It is also a tumour marker of some breast cancers.

12 · Clinical Case — Acute Pancreatitis

🎯 A 51-year-old chronic alcoholic man presents with central abdominal pain radiating to back. Serum amylase is 12,000 U/L (Normal <300 U/L). Provisional diagnosis? Type of necrosis?

Provisional diagnosis: Acute pancreatitis.

Type of necrosis in the peritoneum: Enzymatic fat necrosis (lipase released from damaged pancreas hydrolyses peripancreatic fat → free fatty acids → combine with calcium → chalky-white deposits = soap necrosis).

13 · ADA (Adenosine Deaminase)

📌 Write a short note on ADA (Adenosine Deaminase).

ADA (Adenosine Deaminase), also known as adenosine aminohydrolase, is an enzyme involved in purine metabolism. It is needed for the breakdown of adenosine from food and for the turnover of nucleic acids in tissues.

Primary function in humans: development and maintenance of the immune system.

Other roles: epithelial cell differentiation, neurotransmission, and gestation maintenance.

Clinical significance: ADA deficiency leads to severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID) and pulmonary fibrosis.

Enzymes — True/False & one-liners (test yourself, then reveal)
ALT is more specific than AST for liver damage (T/F)?
True. ALT activity outside the liver is low, making it liver-specific. AST is also found in heart, muscle, and RBCs.
AST is markedly raised in myocardial infarction (T/F)?
True. AST rises within 12 hours, peaks at 48 hours, normal in 3–4 days after MI.
ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase) is raised in obstructive jaundice (T/F)?
True. Marked increase in obstructive jaundice. Also raised in Paget's disease, osteomalacia, rickets, bone metastases.
LDH (Lactate Dehydrogenase) normal value is 208–460 IU/L (T/F)?
True. Raised in MI, hepatitis, muscle disease, leukaemia, malignancy, megaloblastic/haemolytic/pernicious anaemia.
CK (Creatine Kinase) is composed of M and B dimers (T/F)?
True. CK-MM = skeletal muscle, CK-MB = cardiac muscle, CK-BB = brain.
CK-MB normal value is <6% of total CK (T/F)?
True. CK-MB is disproportionately elevated in MI compared to total CK.
Troponin C (TnC) is the best cardiac marker (T/F)?
False. Troponin I (TnI) is totally cardio-specific and the best cardiac marker. Troponin C is not used as a marker.
Troponin levels remain elevated for 5–14 days after MI (T/F)?
True. Rise at 4–6 h, peak at 24–48 h, remain elevated 5–14 days.
Serum amylase is raised in acute pancreatitis (T/F)?
True. Normal <100 U/L; in acute pancreatitis can be markedly elevated (e.g., 12,000 U/L). Also raised in intestinal ischaemia, perforated peptic ulcer, ruptured ovarian cyst.
🎯 CK-MB peaks at what time after MI? Troponin returns to normal in how many days? LDH peaks at what time?
CK-MB peaks at 12–24 h. Troponin returns to normal at 5–14 days. LDH peaks at 2–3 days.
🎯 Which enzymes are tumour markers? Name two enzymes useful for prostate cancer diagnosis.
Acid Phosphatase (prostate cancer + bone metastases) and ALP (bone metastases). PSA is also a tumour marker for prostate cancer.
🎯 Serum amylase is the investigation of choice for which condition? What type of necrosis is seen?
Acute pancreatitis. Enzymatic fat necrosis of the peritoneum (chalky-white calcium-soap deposits).
🎯 Adenosine Deaminase (ADA) deficiency leads to what condition?
Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID) and pulmonary fibrosis.
🎯 GGT (Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase) is raised in which conditions?
Liver disease, obstructive jaundice, hepatitis, alcoholic liver cirrhosis, cholangitis, cholecystitis, alcohol excess.
Enzymes of medical importance are (T/F): amylase, CPK, lactate dehydrogenase, acid phosphatase, aldolase? (DU-17Ja, ch31)
All True. All five are enzymes of medical/clinical importance.
Biochemical markers of acute MI include (T/F): Troponin-T, LDH, Alkal phosphatase, CK-MB, γ-interferon? (RU-14Ja, 13Ju, ch31)
Troponin-T = True · LDH = True · ALP = False · CK-MB = True · γ-interferon = False.
Raised serum acid phosphatase includes (T/F): Paget's disease of bone, rickets, chronic liver disease, chronic prostatitis, osteoporosis? (DU-12Ju, ch31)
Paget's = True · Rickets = False · Chronic liver disease = False · Chronic prostatitis = True · Osteoporosis = False.
The preferred biochemical markers for diagnosis of acute MI include (T/F): LDH, Troponin-I, CK-MB, Troponin-T, SGPT? (DU-10Ja, ch31)
LDH = True · Troponin-I = True · CK-MB = True · Troponin-T = True · SGPT = False.
Following statements are true for ALP (T/F): Produced in bone and liver, increases in Paget's, marked increase in obstructive jaundice, increases in malnutrition, increases in pernicious anaemia? (DU-08Ju, ch31)
