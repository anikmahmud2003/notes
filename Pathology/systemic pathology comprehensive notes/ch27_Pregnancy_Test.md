# Ch 27 — Pregnancy Test     <i>Full-detail flashcards (Q→A) · one short question per card · inline True/False practice · Robbins 4-pillar answers</i>
### 🧠 Big Picture — Pregnancy Test (boss-level overview)
All pregnancy tests detect <b>human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)</b>, a glycoprotein hormone produced by the <b>syncytiotrophoblast</b> of the placenta. hCG appears in <b>blood as early as 6 days</b> after conception and in <b>urine as early as 14 days</b> after conception.
🔴 <b>Indications:</b> (1) Early diagnosis of pregnancy · (2) Confirmation when foetal death is suspected · (3) Diagnosis of <b>hydatidiform mole</b> and <b>choriocarcinoma</b> · (4) Diagnosis of <b>pseudocyst</b> (false pregnancy with positive test).
📌 <b>Methods (broad classification):</b> Modern / Immunological tests (direct & indirect agglutination) · Biological tests (Friedman, Aschim-Zondek, <i>Xenopus laevis</i>, Galli-Mainini) · Radioimmunoassay (RIA) · Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) · Ultrasonography · Radiological tests.
🎯 <b>Key principle:</b> The presence of hCG in urine/blood is the basis of ALL pregnancy tests. Immunological tests use <b>antigen–antibody reactions</b> to detect hCG.

## 1 · Indications of Pregnancy Test
<b>🔴 Enumerate the indications of pregnancy test.</b>

Four main indications:

Diagnosis of pregnancy in an early stage — detection of hCG confirms pregnancy before clinical signs appear.
Confirmation when the foetus is suspected to be dead — a falling or absent hCG level suggests foetal demise.
Diagnosis of hydatidiform mole and choriocarcinoma — these produce markedly elevated hCG levels.
Diagnosis of pseudocyst (pseudocyesis / false pregnancy) — patient believes she is pregnant but no foetus is present; some cases may show a positive test due to hormonal factors.

🎯 <b>Mnemonic — "4 D's":</b> <b>D</b>iagnose early pregnancy · <b>D</b>eath of foetus confirmed · <b>D</b>etect mole/choriocarcinoma · <b>D</b>ifferentiate pseudocyst.

## 2 · Principle of Pregnancy Test
<b>📌 What is the principle behind all pregnancy tests? (Immunological basis)</b>

<b>Principle:</b> The presence of <b>human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)</b> in the urine of a pregnant woman forms the basis of <i>all</i> pregnancy tests.

<b>Immunological basis:</b> involves the principle of <b>antigen–antibody reaction</b>:

hCG acts as the antigen.
Specific anti-hCG antibodies are used to detect it.
The reaction produces a visible change (agglutination, colour change on a strip, etc.).

Ref: Samson Wright's 13e/581 · Lecture SSMC · Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

<b>📌 What is the biological basis of pregnancy tests?</b>

hCG is a glycoprotein that mimics <b>luteinising hormone (LH)</b> and stimulates the corpus luteum to produce progesterone. Biological tests exploit this hormonal action in animals.

Ref: Samson Wright's 13e/581 · Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

## 4 · Immunological (Modern) Tests
<b>📌 Describe the direct agglutination test.</b>

<b>Direct agglutination test (HCG agglutination test):</b>

Uses latex particles coated with anti-hCG antibodies.
Patient's urine is mixed with the coated latex; if hCG is present → visible clumping (agglutination) occurs.
Positive result = pregnancy confirmed.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

<b>📌 Describe the indirect agglutination (passive haemagglutination inhibition) test.</b>

<b>Indirect agglutination test (Passive haemagglutination inhibition test):</b>

Uses red blood cells (RBCs) coated with hCG antigen.
Anti-hCG antibody is added; if urine contains hCG → it neutralises the antibody → no agglutination (inhibition = positive test).
If urine lacks hCG → antibody attacks RBC-coated antigen → agglutination occurs (negative test).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

<b>📌 What is the advantage of immunological methods?</b>

Immunological tests are the modern, preferred methods — <b>cheap, reliable, accurate, and provide quick results</b>.

<b>ELISA</b> is the most widely used today, available as rapid strip-test kits for home use.

## 5 · Biological Tests
<b>🔴 Describe the Friedman test.</b>

<b>1. Friedman Test</b>

Animal used: immature female rabbit.
Principle: hCG stimulates the rabbit's ovary → formation of corpora haemorrhagica (haemorrhagic follicles) within 24–48 hours.
Positive result: red spots on ovaries at laparotomy.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

<b>🔴 Describe the Aschim-Zondek (A-Z) test.</b>

<b>2. Aschim-Zondek (A-Z) Test</b>

Animal used: immature female rat or mouse.
Principle: hCG stimulates the ovary → ovulation + corpora lutea formation. Animals killed and ovaries examined macroscopically.
Positive result: large, bright red corpora haemorrhagica or corpora lutea on both ovaries.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

<b>🔴 Describe the Xenopus laevis test.</b>

<b>3. Female <i>Xenopus laevis</i> (African Clawed Toad) Test</b>

Animal used: female Xenopus laevis (clawed toad).
Principle: hCG induces ovulation in the toad. Eggs appear in the water or cloaca within 12–24 hours.
Advantage: faster than mammalian tests.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

<b>🔴 Describe the Galli-Mainini test.</b>

<b>4. Galli-Mainini Test</b>

Animal used: male frog (e.g., Rana pipiens).
Principle: hCG mimics LH → stimulates the testes → spermatozoa appear in the urine of the frog within a few hours.
Advantage: very rapid (results in 2–4 hours); uses male animals (no need for oestrous cycle timing).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493 · Lecture SSMC

<b>🔴 Why are the biological tests largely obsolete?</b>

🎯 <b>Why obsolete:</b> need live animals · 24–48 hr wait (except Galli-Mainini) · expensive · ethical concerns · not suitable for large-scale screening. Immunological/ELISA tests are faster, cheaper, and equally reliable.

## 6 · RIA & ELISA
<b>📌 Write a short note on Radioimmunoassay (RIA) for pregnancy.</b>

A highly sensitive in-vitro technique for measuring hCG in blood or urine.
Principle: competition between radiolabelled hCG and patient's hCG for binding sites on specific anti-hCG antibodies.
Measures even very low levels — useful for early pregnancy and monitoring gestational trophoblastic disease.
Limitation: requires specialised equipment, radioactive materials, trained personnel → not for routine bedside/home testing.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

<b>📌 Write a short note on ELISA for pregnancy.</b>

The most widely used pregnancy test worldwide today.
Principle: anti-hCG antibodies coated on a solid phase; patient's urine added; a second enzyme-linked anti-hCG antibody forms a "sandwich"; substrate produces a visible colour change (positive).
Sensitivity: detects hCG as low as 20–25 mIU/mL.
Advantages: rapid (5–10 minutes), inexpensive, no special equipment, home dip-stick kits.
Serum ELISA is more sensitive than urine ELISA and detects pregnancy earlier.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

## 7 · Imaging & Radiological Tests
<b>📌 What is the role of ultrasonography in pregnancy testing?</b>

Ultrasonography (USG)

Not a "pregnancy test" per se — confirms the location and viability of a pregnancy once hCG test is positive.
Transvaginal USG: detects a gestational sac from ~5 weeks (hCG ~1,500–2,000 mIU/mL).
Transabdominal USG: detects pregnancy from ~6–7 weeks.
Useful for: confirming intrauterine pregnancy, ruling out ectopic pregnancy, diagnosing hydatidiform mole (snowstorm pattern), assessing foetal viability.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

<b>📌 What is the role of radiological (X-ray) tests?</b>

Radiological Tests

Plain X-ray: historical role; can detect foetal skeleton after ~16–20 weeks. Rarely used today due to radiation risk.
Now replaced by USG for all obstetric imaging.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

## 8 · Timing — When is hCG Detectable?
<b>📌 When does hCG appear in blood and urine after conception?</b>

<table>
<tr><th>Specimen</th><th>Earliest detection after conception</th><th>Notes</th></tr>
<tr><td><strong>Blood (serum)</strong></td><td><span class="hot">As early as 6 days</span></td><td>Detectable before urine; serum ELISA/RIA is the earliest method</td></tr>

</table>

🎯 <b>Rule of 6 and 14:</b> Blood = 6 days; Urine = 14 days. "6 for serum, 14 for pee."

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2, p.493

<b>📌 How does timing affect test selection?</b>

Most home kits detect urine hCG at a threshold of <b>20–25 mIU/mL</b>. Most reliable results are obtained <b>after a missed period</b>. Testing too early may yield a false negative.
