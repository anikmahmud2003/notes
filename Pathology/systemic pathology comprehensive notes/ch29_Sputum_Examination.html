🧠 Big Picture — Sputum Examination (boss-level overview)

Sputum = mucus coughed up from the lower airways (distinguished from saliva which comes from the mouth). Examination is a key step in diagnosing respiratory infections, malignancy, and parasitic disease.

The workflow follows a logical path: 1. Collection (clean/sterile container, morning sample, deep cough) → 2. Macroscopic (naked-eye) examination — appearance, blood, amount, colour, consistency, odour → 3. Microscopic examination — Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) stain for acid-fast bacilli (AFB) / tubercle bacilli, Gram stain for bacteria/fungi, Leishman stain for eosinophils, KOH mount for Aspergillus, unstained wet mount for parasites, Pap smear / H&E for malignant cells → 4. Culture & sensitivity of the specimen.

🔴 Must-know associations: Rusty sputum = pneumonia · Pink frothy = pulmonary oedema · Anchovy-sauce = amoebic liver abscess · Putrid/foetid = bronchiectasis / lung abscess · Blood-stained = pulmonary tuberculosis (TB), bronchogenic carcinoma.

1 · Collection of Sputum

❓ How is sputum collected? What precautions must be taken?
  • Container: Sputum should be collected in a clean, dry, wide-necked container.
  • For culture: Should preferably be collected in a sterile container to avoid contamination.
  • Timing: Sputum is best collected in the morning before any mouth wash is used — this ensures maximal overnight secretions are present.
  • Technique: The person should cough deeply to produce a sputum specimen from the lower airways (not just saliva).
🎯 Mnemonic: "First thing in the morning, deep cough, clean wide jar" — best specimen!

❓ What are the causes of blood in sputum (haemoptysis)?

Haemoptysis = coughing up blood from the respiratory tract. Causes of blood-stained sputum:

❓ When is sputum offensive or foul-smelling (foetid)?

Sputum is usually odourless. It becomes offensive / putrid (foul-smelling / foetid) in:

  • Bronchiectasis — pooling of secretions → bacterial overgrowth → foul odour
  • Lung abscess — necrotic tissue + mixed anaerobic infection
  • Gangrene of the lung — tissue necrosis + anaerobic bacteria
🎯 "FOG" = Foetid odour → Bronchiectasis, lung abscess, Gangrene"

3 · Microscopic Examination

❓ Describe the Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) staining method. What is it used for in sputum examination?

Ziehl-Neelsen (ZN) stain = an acid-fast staining technique used for demonstration of tubercle bacilli (Mycobacterium tuberculosis) in sputum.

Principle

  • Mycobacteria have a waxy cell wall (mycolic acid) → resists decolorisation by acid-alcohol → "acid-fast bacilli" (AFB).
  • Carbol fuchsin (primary stain) penetrates the waxy wall with heat; once stained, the bacilli resist decolorisation by acid-alcohol → appear bright red against a blue background (methylene blue counterstain).

Procedure (simplified)

  1. Prepare a thin smear of sputum on a glass slide.
  2. Flood with carbol fuchsin and heat gently (steam) for 3–5 minutes.
  3. Decolorise with 3% acid-alcohol.
  4. Counterstain with methylene blue.
  5. Examine under oil immersion (×1000).

Finding: Bright red, slender, slightly curved bacilli = acid-fast bacilli (AFB) = suspected M. tuberculosis.

Use in sputum: Primary screening for pulmonary tuberculosis (TB). At least 3 sputum specimens (early morning) should be examined for best sensitivity.

❓ What is the role of Gram staining in sputum examination?

Gram stain is done for demonstration of:

  • Common bacteria — Gram-positive cocci (e.g., Streptococcus pneumoniae, Staphylococcus aureus) and Gram-negative bacilli (e.g., Klebsiella pneumoniae, Haemophilus influenzae).
  • Actinomyces species — Gram-positive, branching, filamentous bacteria (sulfur granules in actinomycosis).
  • Fungi — although special stains (PAS, GMS) are better, Gram stain can show yeast forms (Candida) and pseudohyphae.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498

❓ What is the purpose of Leishman staining in sputum examination?

Leishman stain (a Romanowsky-type stain similar to Giemsa) is used in sputum to identify:

  • Eosinophils — granules stain orange-red → suggest allergic conditions, bronchial asthma, parasitic infections, drug reactions.
  • Other leukocytes — neutrophils (polymorphonuclear cells) indicate bacterial infection.
  • Pus cells — confirms purulent sputum.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498

❓ What is demonstrated by an unstained cover-slip preparation of sputum?

An unstained wet-mount (cover-slip) preparation is used for demonstration of:

  • Entamoeba histolytica — motile trophozoites with ingested RBCs (if amoebic lung abscess / hepatic abscess has ruptured into bronchus).
  • Hydatid cyst — characteristic hooklets, scolices, or laminated membrane fragments.
  • Paragonimus (lung fluke) eggs — in parasitic pulmonary infections (endemic areas).

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498

❓ When is KOH preparation used in sputum examination? What does it demonstrate?

KOH (potassium hydroxide) preparation is done for demonstration of fungal elements, specifically:

  • Aspergillus species — the most clinically important fungal pathogen detected this way in sputum.
  • KOH dissolves host cells and debris but leaves fungal cell walls (chitin) intact → clear visualisation of hyphae.
  • Aspergillus appears as septate hyphae with regular, dichotomous (45° angle) branching.

Clinical settings: suspected aspergilloma, allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA), invasive aspergillosis in immunocompromised patients.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498

❓ How are malignant cells demonstrated in sputum? What stains are used?

Demonstration of malignant cells in sputum is done by sputum cytology:

  • Papanicolaou (Pap) stain — the standard cytological stain; provides excellent nuclear detail for identifying malignant cells.
  • Haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) stain — also used for sputum cytology smears; more familiar to histopathologists.

What is looked for: Cells with nuclear pleomorphism, high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic (N:C) ratio, hyperchromasia, irregular nuclear membranes, and mitotic figures → suggestive of bronchogenic carcinoma.

Clinical use: Particularly valuable for detecting squamous-cell carcinoma of the bronchus (exophytic endobronchial tumour → cells shed into sputum). Adenocarcinoma and small-cell carcinoma may also be detected but less reliably by sputum cytology alone.

Arif Guide 2025 Vol-2 p.498 · Robbins 10e, Lung tumours

❓ What is the role of culture in sputum examination?

Culture of the sputum specimen is essential for:

  • Identification of the causative organism in suspected respiratory infections — more sensitive & specific than Gram stain alone.
  • Antibiotic sensitivity testing — guides targeted antimicrobial therapy (reduces resistance).
  • Culture media used:
    • Blood agar & MacConkey agar — for common bacteria
    • Lowenstein-Jensen (LJ) medium — for Mycobacterium tuberculosis (ZN-positive cases)
    • Sabouraud dextrose agar — for fungi

4 · Indications of Sputum Examination

❓ Enumerate the indications of sputum examination.

Sputum examination is indicated in the following clinical situations:

A. Smear and Culture — identification of causative organism in suspected infection

InfectionKey Sputum Finding
PneumoniaGram stain + culture → identify bacterial pathogen (S. pneumoniae, K. pneumoniae, etc.)
Tuberculosis (TB)ZN stain for AFB + LJ culture → M. tuberculosis
Fungal infectionKOH mount + culture on Sabouraud agar → Aspergillus, Candida, etc.
Pneumocystis jirovecii (formerly P. carinii) in HIV/AIDSInduced sputum + GMS (Gomori methenamine silver) stain → cysts
BronchiectasisCulture (often polymicrobial: anaerobes + aerobes) for targeted antibiotic therapy

B. Cytological examination

IndicationKey Finding
Malignant cellsPap / H&E stain → bronchogenic carcinoma
Viral inclusionsIntranuclear or intracytoplasmic inclusions (e.g., CMV, herpes in immunocompromised patients)
AsbestosisAsbestos bodies (ferruginous bodies) — golden-brown, dumbbell-shaped, beaded structures in sputum → occupational asbestos exposure
🎯 "5 Infections + 3 Cytology" = 8 indications: Pneumonia, TB, Fungal, P. jirovecii/HIV, Bronchiectasis + Malignancy, Viral inclusions, Asbestosis.

🔁 Rapid-Fire MCQ & Viva Sweep

Sputum Examination — True/False & one-liners (test yourself, then reveal)
Best time to collect sputum for examination is in the evening (T/F)?
False. Best collected in the morning before any mouth wash — overnight secretions are maximal.
Rusty sputum is characteristic of bronchiectasis (T/F)?
False. Rusty sputum = lobar pneumonia. Bronchiectasis → foul-smelling, copious, blood-tinged or purulent sputum.
Pink frothy sputum is seen in pulmonary tuberculosis (T/F)?
False. Pink frothy sputum = pulmonary oedema. TB → blood-stained or haemoptysis.
Anchovy-sauce sputum is seen in amoebic liver abscess (T/F)?
True. Anchovy-sauce (brownish-red, thick) sputum = amoebic liver abscess that has ruptured into the lung/bronchus.
Ziehl-Neelsen stain is used for demonstration of tubercle bacilli (T/F)?
True. ZN stain = acid-fast stain → Mycobacterium tuberculosis appears as bright red acid-fast bacilli (AFB).
KOH preparation is done for demonstration of Aspergillus species (T/F)?
True. KOH dissolves host cells, leaving fungal chitin walls intact → Aspergillus septate hyphae with 45° dichotomous branching visible.
Leishman stain is used to demonstrate acid-fast bacilli in sputum (T/F)?
False. Leishman stain identifies eosinophils, leukocytes, and pus cells. ZN stain is for AFB.
Scanty sputum is seen in lung abscess (T/F)?
False. Lung abscess → copious sputum. Scanty sputum = bronchitis, bronchial asthma, early pneumonia.
Foul-smelling (foetid/putrid) sputum is seen in bronchiectasis and lung abscess (T/F)?
True. Bronchiectasis, lung abscess, and gangrene → offensive/putrid sputum due to anaerobic bacterial overgrowth.
Papanicolaou (Pap) stain in sputum is used for demonstration of malignant cells (T/F)?
True. Pap stain (and H&E) used for sputum cytology → detection of bronchogenic carcinoma cells.
Green/yellow sputum is seen in viral pneumonia (T/F)?
False. Green/yellow sputum = purulent conditions (bacterial infections) due to neutrophil-derived myeloperoxidase. Viral sputum is typically mucoid/white.
🎯 "Anchovy-sauce" sputum = ? · "Rusty" sputum = ? · "Pink frothy" sputum = ?
Anchovy-sauce = Amoebic liver abscess (ruptured into lung) · Rusty = Lobar pneumonia · Pink frothy = Pulmonary oedema.
🎯 The unstained cover-slip preparation in sputum demonstrates which organisms?
Entamoeba histolytica (motile trophozoites), hydatid cyst elements (hooklets, scolices), and Paragonimus eggs (lung fluke).
🎯 Sputum examination for P. jirovecii (P. carinii) is indicated in which patient group?
HIV/AIDS / immunocompromised patients with suspected Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP). Induced sputum + GMS stain → cysts.
🎯 Asbestos bodies (ferruginous bodies) in sputum indicate which condition?
Asbestosis — occupational asbestos exposure. Golden-brown, beaded, dumbbell-shaped structures.
🎯 Gram stain of sputum demonstrates which organisms besides common bacteria?
Actinomyces species (branching filamentous, Gram-positive) and fungi (yeast forms like Candida).
🎯 How many sputum specimens should be examined for best TB screening?
At least 3 early-morning specimens should be collected and examined by ZN stain for best sensitivity of AFB detection.
