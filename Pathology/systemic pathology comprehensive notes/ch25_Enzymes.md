# Ch 25 — Enzymes     <i>Full-detail flashcards (Q→A) · one short question per card · inline True/False practice · Robbins 4-pillar answers</i>
### 🧠 Big Picture — Enzymes of Clinical Importance (boss-level overview)
Clinical enzymes are <b>intracellular proteins released into the blood when cells are damaged</b>. Measuring their serum levels helps localise and quantify tissue injury. The key organ–enzyme map:
🔴 <b>Heart:</b> CK (Creatine Kinase) / CK-MB, AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase), LDH (Lactate Dehydrogenase), Troponins, Myoglobin.
🔴 <b>Liver:</b> ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase) = most liver-specific, AST, ALP (Alkaline Phosphatase), GGT (Gamma-Glutamyl Transferase), LDH.
🔴 <b>Muscle:</b> CK, AST, LDH, Aldolase.
🔴 <b>Bone:</b> ALP, ACP (Acid Phosphatase).
🔴 <b>Pancreas:</b> Amylase, Lipase.
🔴 <b>Prostate:</b> Prostatic Acid Phosphatase, PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen).
🔴 <b>Genetic:</b> Glucose-6-Phosphate Dehydrogenase (G6PD), Pyruvate Kinase.
<b>Two critical concepts:</b> (1) <b>Isoenzymes</b> — same enzyme, different tissue forms (e.g., CK-MM from muscle, CK-MB from heart, CK-BB from brain). (2) <b>Cardiac markers have a time-release curve</b> — CK-MB rises earliest (4–6 h), Troponins stay elevated longest (5–14 days) → guides diagnosis at different time points post-MI (Myocardial Infarction).

## 4 · SGPT & SGOT (Transaminases)
<b>📌 What are SGOT and SGPT? What do the abbreviations stand for?</b>

<b>SGOT = AST (Aspartate Aminotransferase)</b> · <b>SGPT = ALT (Alanine Aminotransferase)</b>.

Both are transaminases that transfer amino groups to a ketoacid: ALT from <b>alanine</b>, AST from <b>aspartate</b> (producing pyruvate and oxaloacetate respectively).

Arif 2025 p.483 · Davidson's 20th/940 · Exam tag: CU-09Ja

<b>📌 Which is more specific for liver disease — and why?</b>

Both SGPT & SGOT are located in the cytoplasm of the hepatocyte and widely distributed in other tissues.
ALT (SGPT) is more specific for hepatocellular damage because ALT activity outside the liver is low.
AST (SGOT) is also found in heart, muscle and RBCs, so it is less liver-specific.

🎯 <b>ALT = "A" for "Alone in liver" (most specific) · AST = "A" for "Anywhere" (heart, muscle, liver).</b>

Arif 2025 p.483

<b>📌 Give the normal values of AST and ALT.</b>

<b>AST (SGOT):</b> 10–45 IU/L · <b>ALT (SGPT):</b> 10–50 IU/L.

## 5 · Cardiac Markers (Enzymes + Proteins)
<b>🔴 What are cardiac markers?</b>

Intracellular enzymes or proteins <b>released into blood following cardiac muscle damage</b> due to ischaemia (e.g., MI) or other assaults. They show a serial change: <b>rise → peak → fall</b>.

Arif 2025 pp.483–484 · RU-15Ja, DU-13Ja, SU-19Nv

<b>🔴 Name the enzymes used as cardiac markers.</b>

A. Enzymes

Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK) / Creatine Kinase (CK) — especially CK-MB
Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST / SGOT)
Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH)
Gamma-Glutamyl Transpeptidase (γ-GTP)

B. Other proteins

Troponins (TnI and TnT)
Myoglobin

## 6 · Troponin — Best Indicator for MI
<b>🔴 What are troponins? Give their types.</b>

<b>Troponins</b> are regulatory proteins that control <b>calcium-mediated contraction of cardiac and skeletal muscle</b>.

Troponin I (TnI) — totally cardio-specific → called cardio-specific troponin-I (cTnI).
Troponin T (TnT) — not totally cardio-specific.
Troponin C (TnC) — not used as a cardiac marker.

Arif 2025 pp.484–485 · Exam tags: DU-08Ja, RU-15Ja, DU-06Ja

<b>🔴 Why are troponin T and I the best indicators for MI?</b>

They are structural cardiac muscle proteins released during <b>myocyte damage and necrosis</b>. They have <b>nearly complete cardiac tissue specificity + high sensitivity</b>, making them the gold standard for MI diagnosis.

Arif 2025 pp.484–485

<b>🔴 Give the kinetics of troponin after acute MI.</b>

Not normally detectable in circulation. After acute MI:

Rise: at 4–6 hours
Peak: at 24–48 hours
Remain elevated: for 5–14 days

<b>Other conditions where troponins may be elevated:</b> minor myocardial damage, pulmonary embolus, septic shock, pulmonary oedema.

Arif 2025 pp.484–485 · Robbins 10e/550 · Davidson's 23rd/450

## 7 · CK (Creatine Kinase / CPK)
<b>🔴 Write a short note on CK (Creatine Kinase) / CPK — what is it and where is it concentrated?</b>

An enzyme highly concentrated in <b>brain, myocardium, and skeletal muscle</b>. It is composed of two dimers designated <b>"M" (muscle)</b> and <b>"B" (brain)</b>.

<b>Normal values:</b> Male: 55–170 U/L · Female: 30–135 U/L.

<b>Alternative name:</b> Creatine Phosphokinase (CPK).

## 8 · CK-MB Isoenzyme
<b>🔴 What is the clinical importance of CK-MB?</b>

<b>CK-MB (Creatine Kinase MB isoenzyme):</b> the <b>cardiac-specific isoform</b> of creatine kinase. In MI, CK-MB is <b>disproportionately elevated compared with total CK</b>.

<b>Normal value:</b> <6% of total CK.

<table>
<tr><th>Enzyme</th><th>Normal Value</th><th>Rise Within</th><th>Peak At</th><th>Returns to Normal</th></tr>
<tr><td><strong>CK-MB</strong></td><td>Male: 55–170 IU/L<br/>Female: 30–135 IU/L</td><td>4–6 hours</td><td>12–24 hours</td><td>48–72 hours</td></tr>

## 9 · Clinical Case — Acute Myocardial Infarction
<b>🎯 A 56-year-old male has excruciating left-sided chest pain and sweating, not relieved by rest or sublingual nitroglycerine. What is your provisional diagnosis?</b>

<b>Provisional diagnosis:</b> <b>Acute Myocardial Infarction (MI)</b>.

Classic presentation — severe cardiac-type chest pain not relieved by rest/nitroglycerine, with sweating.

Arif 2025 p.485 · Exam tag: DU-11Ja

<b>🎯 What investigations and expected results confirm the MI?</b>

ECG: ST-segment elevation · Pathological Q-wave formation · T-wave inversion.
CK-MB: Markedly raised. Rises within 4–6 h · stays elevated up to 48–72 h.
Troponin-I: Markedly raised. Rises within 4–6 h · stays elevated up to 5–14 days.

Arif 2025 p.485 · Exam tag: DU-11Ja

## 10 · Enzymes as Tumour Markers
<b>🎯 Name two enzymes acting as tumour markers.</b>

Acid Phosphatase (ACP): marker for carcinoma of prostate and metastatic tumour of bone.
Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP): marker for metastatic tumour of bone.

## 11 · PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen)
<b>🔴 Write the clinical importance of PSA (Prostate-Specific Antigen).</b>

Measurement of PSA is the most important test in the diagnosis and management of prostate cancer.
20–40% of patients with serum PSA > 4.0 ng/mL will have prostate cancer on biopsy.
High serum PSA (>100 ng/mL) almost always indicates distant bone metastases.
PSA is most useful for monitoring response to treatment and disease progression.
It is also a tumour marker of some breast cancers.

Arif 2025 p.485 · Robbins 10e/981 · Davidson's 23rd/439, 1322, 1324 · Exam tag: DU-10/08Ja

## 12 · Clinical Case — Acute Pancreatitis
<b>🎯 A 51-year-old chronic alcoholic man has central abdominal pain radiating to the back; serum amylase 12,000 U/L (Normal <300 U/L). Provisional diagnosis?</b>

<b>Provisional diagnosis:</b> <b>Acute pancreatitis</b>.

Chronic alcoholism + severe radiating abdominal pain + markedly elevated serum amylase.

Arif 2025 p.485 · Exam tag: DU-13Ja

<b>🎯 What type of necrosis occurs in the peritoneum in this case?</b>

<b>Type of necrosis:</b> <b>Enzymatic fat necrosis</b>. Lipase released from the damaged pancreas hydrolyses peripancreatic fat → free fatty acids → combine with calcium → chalky-white deposits = <b>soap necrosis</b>.

Arif 2025 p.485 · Exam tag: DU-13Ja

## 13 · ADA (Adenosine Deaminase)
<b>📌 Write a short note on ADA (Adenosine Deaminase).</b>

<b>ADA (Adenosine Deaminase),</b> also known as adenosine aminohydrolase, is an enzyme involved in <b>purine metabolism</b> — needed for breakdown of adenosine from food and for turnover of nucleic acids in tissues.

Functions

Primary: development and maintenance of the immune system.
Other roles: epithelial cell differentiation, neurotransmission, gestation maintenance.

Clinical significance

ADA deficiency leads to <b>severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID)</b> and <b>pulmonary fibrosis</b>.

## 🔁 Rapid-Fire One-liners (test yourself, then reveal)
<b>Enzymes — one-liners & mnemonics</b>

🎯 ALT vs AST specificity for liver?

<b>ALT (SGPT) more liver-specific;</b> AST also in heart, muscle, RBCs.

🎯 CK-MB peak time after MI?

<b>12–24 hours</b> (rises 4–6 h, normal by 48–72 h).

🎯 Troponin returns to normal in how many days? LDH peak?

Troponin <b>5–14 days</b> · LDH peaks at <b>2–3 days</b>.

🎯 Two enzymes as tumour markers? Two for prostate cancer?

<b>ACP & ALP;</b> prostate: ACP + PSA.

🎯 Serum amylase is investigation of choice for? Type of necrosis?

<b>Acute pancreatitis;</b> enzymatic fat necrosis (soap).

🎯 ADA deficiency → ?

<b>Severe Combined Immunodeficiency (SCID)</b> + pulmonary fibrosis.

🎯 GGT raised in which conditions?

<b>Liver disease, obstructive jaundice, alcoholic cirrhosis, cholangitis/cholecystitis, alcohol excess.</b>
