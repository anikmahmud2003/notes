## ✍️ Answer Script — Group A

### Q1. Discuss the causes of cell injury with examples. Describe how hypoxia causes cell injury? 7(4+3)

### Causes of cell injury (4 marks)

| Cause | Mechanism | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Ischemia** | ATP depletion → energy failure | Myocardial infarction, cerebral infarction, ischemic hepatitis |
| 🔴 **Hypoxia** | ↓O₂ → ↓oxidative phosphorylation → ↓ATP | High altitude, respiratory failure, cyanotic lesions |
| 🔴 **Chemical / toxin** | Direct + lysosomal damage | CO, smoke inhalation, isoniazid, methotrexate, alcohol |
| 🔴 **Infection** | Cytokine release, phagocytosis, tissue necrosis | Pyogenic bacteria, tuberculosis, viruses |
| 🔴 **Physical agent** | Mechanical / thermal / radiation injury | Trauma, burns, radiation, cold |

### How hypoxia causes cell injury (3 marks)

```
Hypoxia (↓O₂)
   ↓
Mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation ↓ → ↓ ATP
   ↓
Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase failure → loss of ion gradients → cell swelling (cytoplasmic edema)
   ↓
Ca²⁺ influx (Na⁺/Ca²⁺ exchanger / damaged channels) → lysosomal enzyme activation, PLA₂, NO → membrane injury
   ↓
↓ ATP → mitochondrial destabilization + cytochrome C release → apoptosis / necrosis
   ↓
Lysosomal enzymes (cathepsins) leak → autolysis
```

📌 **Hypoxia vs ischemia:** both → ATP↓, but hypoxia = ATP↓ by **O₂ lack**, ischemia = ATP↓ by **blood flow stoppage**. Same downstream ionic/membranous injury.

> 🎯 "**Hypoxia = O₂ down → ATP down → pump fails → cell swells; Ischemia = flow down → ATP down → same downstream injury.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 2 (Cell Injury, Cell Death, and Adaptations) · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 01 (Cell Injury and Cell Death), pp. 23–51 & Ch 02 (Cellular Adaptation, Intracellular Accumulation and Cell Aging), pp. 52–72

### Q5. A person presented with a painful, red, edematous, warm swelling at arm following recent sting bite. What is the diagnosis? What is the mechanism of development of the signs? 1+2

#### Diagnosis
🔴 **Localized inflammatory reaction to the sting** (arthropod / mosquito bite) — with **localized cellulitis** if secondary bacterial infection develops.

#### Mechanism of signs
1. Sting → antigen → **mast cell degranulation** → **histamine** release.
2. Histamine → **vasodilation** → redness (rubor) + warmth (calor) · **↑vascular permeability** → edema (tumor) · **pain** via sensory C-fibers.

📌 Classic local reaction signs: **rubor, calor, tumor, dolor, functio laesa**.

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 3 (Inflammatory Response) · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 03 (Inflammation), pp. 73–118

## ✍️ Answer Script — Group B

### Q1. Define and classify shock with pathogenesis of septic shock. Shortly describe DIC. 7(5+2)

#### Definition of shock
🔴 **Shock = inadequate tissue perfusion with cellular dysfunction** (lactic acidosis, organ failure) despite adequate cardiac output in early stages.

#### Classification (5 marks)
| Type | Cause | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypovolemic** | ↓ blood volume | Hemorrhage, dehydration, burns |
| **Cardiogenic** | ↓ cardiac pump | MI, cardiomyopathy, valvular disease |
| **Septic** | Sepsis + vasodilation | Bacterial infection |
| **Anaphylactic** | IgE/mast cell → vasodilation | Allergy, anaphylaxis |
| **Neurogenic** | Loss of sympathetic tone | Spinal shock |

#### Pathogenesis of septic shock (2 marks)
```
Bacteria / endotoxin
   ↓
Kupffer cell activation → cytokine release (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6)
   ↓
NO-mediated arterial vasodilation → ↓ systemic vascular resistance
   ↓
↓ preload/CO → tissue hypoperfusion → lactic acidosis, organ dysfunction
   ↓
🔴 Septic shock = refractory/severe form with persisting hypotension
```

#### DIC — shortly (2 marks)
🔴 **DIC = sepsis + disseminated intravascular coagulation** → widespread microthrombi in small vessels + **consumptive coagulopathy** (fibrinogen + platelets consumed) → paradoxical bleeding (petechiae, oozing) + thrombosis (organ ischemia).

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 4 (Hemodynamic Disorders, Thromboembolic Disease, and Shock) · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 05 (Haemodynamic Disorders, Thromboembolism & Shock), pp. 137–175

### Q5. A 56 yrs old male presented with generalized edema which is pitting in nature. What may be the causes? How will you proceed to diagnose the cause of edema? 1+2

#### Causes of generalized pitting edema (1 mark)
- **Cardiac:** congestive heart failure (most common cause in elderly male).
- **Renal:** nephrotic syndrome / nephritic syndrome.
- **Hepatic:** cirrhosis → hypoalbuminemia.
- **Protein-losing enteropathy / malnutrition / cachexia**.

#### Diagnostic approach (2 marks)
1. **History:** onset, JVD, weight loss, diuretic use, leg swelling, orthopnea.
2. **Examination:** pitting edema + **hepatojugular reflux**, JVD, S³, pleural effusion.
3. **Laboratory:** serum **albumin**, total protein, **lipid profile**, renal function (BUN/Cr, urine protein/creatinine ratio, urine A/B ratio), LFTs.
4. **Imaging/other:** echocardiogram (cardiac), renal US/CT (renal vein thrombosis).

## ✍️ Answer Script — Group A (cont.) — Optional Questions to be answered from Q2, Q3, Q4 (since Q1 & Q5 are compulsory)

### Q2. Define and classify metaplasia with examples. What are the harmful effects of metaplasia? 3+2

- **Definition:** Reversible replacement of one **adult cell type** by another **adult cell type** (differentiation pathway shift).
- **Types:** squamous, glandular, columnar — e.g., **squamous metaplasia of bronchus** (smoker), **intestinal metaplasia of stomach** (duodenum).
- **Harmful effects:** irreversible; → dysplasia → carcinoma (intestinal metaplasia → colorectal cancer; squamous → lung SCC).

### Q3. Write shortly on beneficial and harmful effects of inflammation. What are the systemic effects of inflammation? 2+3

- **Beneficial:** defense, phagocytosis, wound healing, fever.
- **Harmful:** chronic inflammation → tissue destruction (hepatitis, IBD, silicosis, TB), autoimmunity, cancer promotion.
- **Systemic effects:** fever, ↑CRP/ESR, leukocytosis, cytokines → SIRS/sepsis.

### Q4. What are the hallmarks of chronic inflammation? Give the factors influencing wound healing? 1+4

- **Hallmarks:** IL-1, TNF-α, IL-6, prostaglandins, nitric oxide, lysosomal enzymes, macrophages (M1), granuloma (TB).
- **Wound healing factors:** infection, ischemia, foreign body, diabetes, malnutrition, smoking, debridement.

## ✍️ Answer Script — Group B (cont.) — Optional Questions to be answered from Q2, Q3, Q4 (since Q1 & Q5 are compulsory)

### Q2. Describe Virchow triad of thrombosis? Enumerate the fates of a thrombus. 3+2

- **Virchow triad:** endothelial injury, venous stasis, hypercoagulability (protein C/S, Leiden, cancer).
- **Fates of thrombus:** embolization, propagation → complete occlusion, regression (fibrinolysis), organization.

### Q3. Define and classify neoplasm. What are the features of a malignant neoplasm? 2+3

- **Neoplasm:** uncontrolled cell growth; **benign** vs **malignant**.
- **Malignant features:** invasion, metastasis, dedifferentiation, uncontrolled growth.

### Q4. How a tumor is diagnosed in a laboratory? What is grading and staging of tumor? 3+2

- **Diagnosis:** clinical → imaging → FNAC → biopsy (histopath + IHC) → molecular.
- **Grading:** degree of differentiation (G1 well → G3 poorly differentiated); CLL → Rai/Binet.
- **Staging:** TNM (T = tumor, N = nodes, M = metastasis).
