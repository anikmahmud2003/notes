## Q1. Classify diseases of the respiratory system. Name the Obstructive Lung Diseases.

### Classification of respiratory diseases

| **G. Pleura + mediastinum** | Effusions, pneumothorax, solitary fibrous tumor, mesothelioma |

### Obstructive Lung Diseases (⚠️ favourite viva question)
1. **Emphysema**
2. **Chronic bronchitis**

## Q2. Define COPD. Mention the causes/etiology of COPD. Enumerate the diseases of COPD with their clinical features.

### Definition (WHO)
**COPD = persistent respiratory symptoms + airflow limitation caused by abnormalities of the airways and/or alveoli due to significant exposure to noxious particles or gases** (i.e., tobacco smoke). It is a progressive but **treatable** disease. Currently the **4th leading cause of death** (projected 3rd).

### Etiology / causes of COPD
1. **Cigarette smoking (dominant cause, ~80% of COPD)** — 35–50% of heavy smokers develop COPD.
2. **α1-Antitrypsin deficiency** — genetic (Pi locus, chromosome 14); >80% of ZZ homozygotes develop panacinar emphysema.
3. **Environmental / occupational pollutants** — grain, cotton, silica dust; indoor biomass smoke.
4. **Poor early-life lung development** — low birth weight, childhood lung infections.
5. **Airway hyperresponsiveness** — even mild asthma increases risk.
6. **Genetic polymorphisms** — modifying susceptibility (not every smoker gets COPD).

### Diseases of COPD spectrum with clinical features
| Disease | Site | Main pathology | Clinical features |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chronic bronchitis** | Bronchus | Mucous gland hyperplasia + hypersecretion | Cough + copious sputum, cyanosis, cor pulmonale |
| **Emphysema** | Acinus | Airspace enlargement + wall destruction | Dyspnea, barrel chest, pursed-lip breathing |
| **Bronchiectasis** | Bronchus | Airway dilation + scarring | Cough, **foul purulent sputum**, fever, hemoptysis |
| **Asthma** | Bronchus | Smooth muscle hypertrophy, excess mucus, inflammation | **Episodic wheezing**, reversible obstruction |
| **Small-airway disease** | Bronchiole | Inflammatory scarring/obliteration | Cough, dyspnea |

> 💡 Emphysema + chronic bronchitis commonly coexist in the same patient (overlap syndrome).

## Q3. Define & Classify Emphysema. Discuss the pathogenesis of Emphysema.

### Definition
**Emphysema = irreversible enlargement of the airspaces distal to the terminal bronchiole WITH destruction of their walls** (with functionally important small-airway fibrosis). No visible scarring (unlike fibrosis).

### Classification (by anatomic distribution in the lobule)
| Type | Distribution | Association | Site |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Centriacinar (centrilobular)** | Proximal acinus (respiratory bronchioles); distal spared | **Heavy smoking — >95% of significant cases** | **Upper lobes, apical** |
| **2. Panacinar (panlobular)** | Entire acinus uniformly enlarged | **α1-Antitrypsin deficiency**; worsened by smoking | **Lower lobes / bases** |
| **3. Distal acinar (paraseptal)** | Distal acinus; proximal normal | **Spontaneous pneumothorax in young adults** | Adjacent to pleura/septa, upper half |
| **4. Irregular** | Acinus irregularly involved | Scarring / fibrosis | Focal, insignificant |

### Pathogenesis (3 intersecting mechanisms — Fig 15.8)
1. **Toxic injury + inflammation** — cigarette smoke damages epithelium → mediators (**LTB4, IL-8, TNF**) recruit inflammatory cells (T & B cells accumulate).
2. **Protease–antiprotease imbalance** — neutrophil proteases (**especially elastase**) digest connective tissue; protection lost in **α1-antitrypsin deficiency**.
3. **Oxidative stress** — tobacco smoke oxidants overwhelm **NRF2** (oxidant sensor); NRF2 knockout mice far more sensitive to smoke.

> 📌 **α1-Antitrypsin** (Pi locus, chr 14) = major inhibitor of neutrophil elastase. ZZ homozygotes (~0.012% of US) → **>80% develop panacinar emphysema**, earlier + worse if they smoke.

### Why obstruction occurs WITHOUT mechanical blockage
Loss of **elastic recoil** → ↓ **radial traction** on small airways → respiratory bronchioles **collapse during expiration** = functional obstruction. Young smokers also get small-airway inflammation → narrowing.

> 🎯 **One-liner:** Smoking → elastase/anti-elastase imbalance + oxidants → elastic destruction → loss of recoil → expiratory bronchiolar collapse.

## Q4. Define Chronic Bronchitis. Give its pathogenesis.

### Definition (clinical)
**Persistent cough with sputum production for ≥3 months in ≥2 consecutive years** (other causes excluded). **90% of patients are smokers.**

### Pathogenesis (initiator = noxious inhaled substances: tobacco smoke, grain/cotton/silica dust)
1. **Mucus hypersecretion (earliest change)** — submucosal gland enlargement + goblet cell hyperplasia (a protective reaction); mediated by histamine + IL-13.
2. **Acquired CFTR dysfunction** from smoking → **abnormally dehydrated mucus** → worse disease.
3. **Inflammation** — neutrophils, lymphocytes, macrophages → long-standing inflammation + **fibrosis of small airways (<2–3 mm)** → chronic obstruction.
4. **Infection** — does NOT initiate but **maintains disease + causes acute exacerbations**. Smoke also **paralyzes cilia** → poor mucociliary clearance.

### Morphology
- Hyperemia, swelling, edema of mucosa.
- **Mucous gland hypertrophy = Reid index** = thickness of mucous gland layer ÷ total bronchial wall thickness (**normal = 0.4**; ↑ in disease, proportional to severity).
- Goblet cell hyperplasia; smooth muscle hypertrophy + peribronchial fibrosis.
- Severe → **bronchiolitis obliterans**; squamous metaplasia/dysplasia (from smoke's mutagenic effect).

### Consequences
Progressive lung dysfunction → hypoxemia → **pulmonary hypertension → cor pulmonale**.

> 🎯 **One-liner:** Smoke → gland hypertrophy + goblet hyperplasia (Reid index ↑) → mucus hypersecretion → small-airway fibrosis → obstruction → cor pulmonale.

## Q5. Difference between Chronic Bronchitis & Emphysema

| Feature | **Chronic Bronchitis** | **Emphysema** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Cough + sputum ≥3 months × 2 years | Irreversible airspace enlargement + wall destruction |
| **Site** | Bronchus (large airways) | Acinus (beyond terminal bronchiole) |
| **Airway resistance** | **Increased** (mucus + fibrosis) | Normal or slightly ↑ (no mechanical block) |
| **Elastic recoil** | Normal | **Low (reduced)** |
| **Major pathology** | Mucous gland hypertrophy (Reid index ↑), goblet hyperplasia | Wall destruction, loss of alveolar attachments |
| **Age** | 40–45 yrs | 50–75 yrs |
| **Dyspnea** | Mild, late | **Severe, early** |
| **Cough** | **Early, copious sputum** | Late, scanty |
| **Cyanosis** | **Marked (blue)** | Late / mild (pink) |
| **Cor pulmonale** | **Common (early)** | Uncommon, end-stage |
| **CXR** | Prominent vessels, large heart | Hyperinflation, normal heart |
| **Clinical picture** | **Blue bloater** | **Pink puffer** |

> 🎯 **Pink puffer blows out; blue bloater drowns in his own mucus.**

## Q6. What is Pink Puffer & Blue Bloater? What are the restrictive lung diseases?

### Pink Puffer = Emphysema
- **Barrel chest**, hunched-over posture, **pursed-lip breathing**, severe early dyspnea.
- Weight loss (may suggest occult cancer).
- **Pink** because ventilation (O₂) is maintained initially — not cyanotic.
- Scanty late cough; elastic recoil low; cor pulmonale uncommon (end-stage).

### Blue Bloater = Chronic bronchitis
- **Persistent cough + copious sputum, hypercapnia, hypoxemia, mild cyanosis** → **blue**.
- Early dyspnea mild; **cor pulmonale common** (hypoxemia → pulmonary HTN).
- Airway resistance increased; CXR = prominent vessels + large heart.

### Restrictive lung diseases (can't fill up)
**A. Chronic interstitial / infiltrative diseases:**
1. **Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF/UIP)** — honeycomb lung, fibroblastic foci
2. **Nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP)**
3. **Cryptogenic organizing pneumonia (COP)** — Masson bodies
4. **Pneumoconioses** — silicosis, asbestosis, coal workers'
5. **Sarcoidosis**
6. **Hypersensitivity pneumonitis** (extrinsic allergic alveolitis)

**B. Chest-wall / extrapulmonary disorders:**
1. Severe **obesity**
2. **Pleural disease** (fibrosis, effusion)
3. **Kyphoscoliosis**
4. **Neuromuscular disease** (polio, muscular dystrophies)

> 🎯 Restrictive = **FEV1/FVC normal** (both FEV1 and FVC fall proportionally); obstructive = FEV1/FVC < 0.7.

## Q7. Define Bronchial Asthma. Give its Clinical Features.

### Definition
**Asthma = heterogeneous chronic airway inflammation with variable (reversible) expiratory airflow obstruction** — characterized by **wheezing, shortness of breath, chest tightness, and cough**, occurring typically at **night or early morning**. Bronchoconstriction is **at least partly reversible** (key difference from COPD).

### Clinical features
1. **Chest tightness, dyspnea, wheezing, cough** (cough may be the only symptom — "cough-variant asthma").
2. Attacks typically **at night / early morning**; episodic.
3. **Prolonged expiration** on examination + **widespread wheeze** (audible).
4. **Demonstrated increase in airflow obstruction** — spirometry: **FEV1/FVC < 0.7** that **improves with bronchodilator** (reversibility = hallmark).
5. Atopic asthmatics: **eosinophilia**, **Curschmann spirals** (mucus plugs) + **Charcot-Leyden crystals** (galectin-10) in sputum.
6. **Acute severe form (status asthmaticus)** → severe respiratory distress, cyanosis → death.
7. **Up to 50% of childhood asthma remits at adolescence**, but returns in many adults.

## Q8. Classification/Etiology of Bronchial Asthma. Difference between Asthma & COPD.

### Classification of asthma (by trigger)
| Type | Mechanism / trigger |
|---|---|
| **1. Atopic asthma (most common)** | Classic **type I IgE hypersensitivity**; childhood onset; allergens (dust, pollen, dander, food); positive skin wheal-and-flare test; high serum IgE; family history |
| **2. Non-atopic asthma** | No allergen sensitization, negative skin test; triggers = **viral infection** (rhinovirus, RSV), air pollutants (SO₂, ozone, NO₂), cold, exercise |
| **3. Drug-induced (aspirin-sensitive)** | Aspirin/NSAIDs inhibit **cyclooxygenase → ↓PGE2 → ↑ leukotrienes (LTC4/D4/E4)**; associated with **nasal polyps + recurrent rhinitis** |
| **4. Occupational asthma** | Fumes, organic/chemical dusts (wood, cotton, platinum), gases, chemicals; after repeated exposure |

### Difference between Asthma & COPD
| Feature | **Asthma** | **COPD** |
|---|---|---|
| **Age of onset** | Often childhood / young | Usually >40 yrs (smoker) |
| **Nature** | **Reversible** bronchoconstriction | **Irreversible** (progressive) airflow limitation |
| **Smoking link** | Not essential | **Major (80%)** |
| **Inflammation** | Th2/IgE, **eosinophils** | Neutrophils, macrophages, CD8 T cells |
| **Spirometry** | FEV1/FVC ↓, **improves with bronchodilator** | FEV1/FVC ↓, **poor reversibility** |
| **Airway remodeling** | Late, sub-basement membrane fibrosis | Present (small-airway fibrosis) |
| **Cor pulmonale** | Rare | Common (esp. bronchitic type) |
| **Mucous gland (Reid index)** | Mild ↑ | Prominent ↑ (chronic bronchitis) |

## Q9. Discuss the Pathogenesis of Bronchial Asthma. What is status asthmaticus?

### Pathogenesis (atopic asthma = Th2/IgE axis — Fig 15.10)
1. **Sensitization** — allergen presented to CD4+ T cells → **Th2 cells**.
2. **Th2 cytokines:** **IL-4** (IgE production + Th2), **IL-5** (eosinophil activation), **IL-13** (mucus + IgE). **Th17 (IL-17)** recruits neutrophils.
3. **IgE cross-links on mast cells** → degranulation.

**Early-phase reaction (minutes):** mast cell mediators → **bronchoconstriction, ↑ mucus, vasodilation, ↑ vascular permeability** (+ vagal reflex).
**Late-phase reaction (hours):** recruitment of **eosinophils, neutrophils, T cells** → eosinophil granule proteins (**major basic protein, eosinophil cationic protein**) damage epithelium.

**Key mediators (proven by drug response):**
- **Leukotrienes C4/D4/E4** — prolonged bronchoconstriction, mucus, permeability.
- **Acetylcholine** (parasympathetic/muscarinic).
- **IL-5** — anti-IL-5 biologics effective in severe eosinophilic asthma.
- **Galectin-10** → **Charcot-Leyden crystals**.

**Airway remodeling (chronic irreversible component):**
- Sub-basement membrane **fibrosis (type I + III collagen)**
- ↑ vascularity, ↑ submucosal glands + goblet cells
- **Smooth muscle hypertrophy/hyperplasia** + ↑ ECM
- Neutrophil-predominant (Th17) subset = **severe, steroid-refractory asthma**

**Genetics & environment:** 5q gene cluster (IL-13 strongest), IL-33/ST2, TSLP; **hygiene hypothesis** — early microbial exposure ↓ later atopy.

### Status asthmaticus
**= acute, severe, unremitting asthma attack that does not respond to standard bronchodilator therapy** and may be fatal.
- **Morphology (status asthmaticus):** overinflated lungs + atelectatic areas; **bronchi/bronchioles occluded by thick tenacious mucus plugs** containing shed epithelium; sputum shows **Curschmann spirals** + **Charcot-Leyden crystals**.
- Clinical: profound dyspnea, **cyanosis, death if untreated** (respiratory failure).

> 🎯 **One-liner:** Allergen → Th2 (IL-4/5/13) → IgE on mast cells → early bronchospasm + late eosinophilic inflammation → remodeling.

## Q10. Define & Enumerate the Causes of Bronchiectasis. Pathogenesis of Bronchiectasis.

### Definition
**Bronchiectasis = permanent (irreversible) dilation of bronchi and bronchioles** due to destruction of smooth muscle and elastic tissue by chronic/necrotizing inflammation. Now uncommon (better infection control).

### Causes of bronchiectasis
1. **Congenital/hereditary → infection:**
   - **Cystic fibrosis** (classic cause)
   - **Primary ciliary dyskinesia** (dynein defect)
   - **Kartagener syndrome** = situs inversus + bronchiectasis + sinusitis
   - Immunodeficiency states; intralobar sequestration
2. **Severe necrotizing pneumonia** — bacterial, viral, fungal (single or recurrent)
3. **Bronchial obstruction** — tumor, foreign body, mucus impaction → localized bronchiectasis
4. **Immune disorders** — RA, SLE, IBD, **post-transplant chronic rejection / chronic GVHD**
5. **Allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis (ABPA)** — in asthma/CF; Th2 → eosinophils → mucus plugs
6. **Idiopathic (up to 50%)** — dysfunctional host immunity to infection

### Pathogenesis — **"Obstruction + Infection"**
1. Defect in **airway clearance** (obstruction by mucus/secretions, ciliary dysfunction) → **distal pooling of secretions**.
2. Pooled secretions → **chronic bacterial infection** (H. influenzae ~50%, P. aeruginosa 12–30%).
3. Inflammation → **destruction of bronchial smooth muscle + elastic tissue** → **permanent dilation**.
4. Repeated cycles of obstruction + infection → progressive wall destruction, abscess, peribronchial fibrosis → **bronchiolitis obliterans**.

> 📌 CF = thick viscous secretions → obstruction → infection → destruction of wall (the model mechanism).

**Morphology:** lower lobes bilaterally, vertical/distal airways most severe; airways dilated up to **4× normal**, running almost to the pleura; cut surface = cystic, filled with mucopurulent secretions; acute + chronic inflammation, ulceration, squamous metaplasia.

**Clinical:** severe persistent cough, **foul-smelling, sometimes bloody sputum**, dyspnea, **hemoptysis (may be massive)**; morning paroxysms (positional drainage).

## Q11. Difference between Obstructive & Restrictive Lung Disease. Name the restrictive lung diseases.

### Difference
| Feature | **Obstructive** | **Restrictive** |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | ↑ resistance to airflow (diffuse airway disease) | ↓ expansion of lung parenchyma, ↓ total lung capacity |
| **Problem** | Can't blow out | Can't fill up |
| **Spirometry** | **FEV1/FVC < 0.7** (↓ maximal airflow) | FEV1 + FVC ↓ proportionally → **FEV1/FVC normal** |
| **Lung compliance** | ↑ (emphysema) or normal | **↓ (stiff lungs)** |
| **Residual volume / TLC** | **↑ (air trapping)** | ↓ |
| **Diffusion capacity** | ↓ (emphysema) | **↓** |
| **Crackles** | Usually absent (except chronic bronchitis) | **End-inspiratory crackles** |
| **Diseases** | COPD (emphysema, chronic bronchitis), asthma, bronchiectasis, bronchiolitis | Interstitial fibrosis, pneumoconioses, chest-wall disease |
| **Cor pulmonale** | Common (bronchitic type) | Advanced disease (honeycomb lung) |

### Restrictive lung diseases (name them)
1. **Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF / UIP)** — fibroblastic foci + honeycombing
2. **Nonspecific interstitial pneumonia (NSIP)**
3. **Cryptogenic organizing pneumonia (COP)** — Masson bodies
4. **Pneumoconioses** — silicosis, asbestosis, coal workers' pneumoconiosis
5. **Sarcoidosis**
6. **Hypersensitivity pneumonitis** (farmer's lung, bird breeder's lung)
7. **Chest-wall disorders** — obesity, kyphoscoliosis, pleural disease, neuromuscular disease
8. Also: acute → **ARDS**; **interstitial edema**

> 🎯 **One-liner:** Obstructive FEV1/FVC <0.7; restrictive FEV1/FVC normal.

## Q13. Outline the etiopathogenesis of Lung Cancer. How will you diagnose a case of bronchogenic carcinoma?

### Etiopathogenesis
**Risk factors:**
1. **Tobacco smoking (dominant carcinogen)** — ~**80%** of cases; near-linear relation to **pack-years**; heavy smoker (2 packs/day × 20 yrs) → **60× risk**; women more susceptible; secondhand smoke ↑ risk.
2. **Industrial / environmental** — **asbestos** (alone 5×, + smoking = **55×**), arsenic, chromium, uranium, nickel, vinyl chloride, mustard gas; **radon gas**; ionizing radiation.
3. **Genetic modifiers** — P-450 (CYP) activating variants, "mutagen sensitivity genotype," DNA-repair variants.
4. **Molecular genetics by subtype:**
   - **Adenocarcinoma:** **EGFR (10–15%, higher in non-smoking Asian women), ALK (3–5%), ROS1, MET, RET, BRAF, KRAS (~30%)**; most common in never-smokers.
   - **Squamous:** losses of **3p, 9p (CDKN2A/p16 65%), 17p (TP53)**; FGFR1 amplification; highly smoking-linked.
   - **Small cell:** virtually always smoking; **highest mutational burden; near-universal TP53 + RB inactivation; MYC amplification**.
   - **Never-smokers (25% worldwide):** women; adenocarcinoma; **EGFR common, KRAS almost never**.

**Pathogenesis sequence:** field effect (mutations persist in bronchial epithelium) → preinvasive lesions: **atypical adenomatous hyperplasia → adenocarcinoma in situ (lepidic)**; **squamous metaplasia → dysplasia → CIS** → invasive carcinoma.

### Diagnosis of bronchogenic carcinoma (work-up)
1. **Clinical suspicion** — smoker >50 yrs with **cough (75%), weight loss (40%), chest pain (40%), dyspnea (20%)**; hemoptysis, or metastatic presentation (bone pain, seizures).
2. **Imaging** — Chest X-ray (mass, hilar enlargement, atelectasis); **CT / low-dose CT screening**; CXR may show **Pancoast tumor** or cavity.
3. **Cytology** — **Sputum cytology** (squamous CIS is sputum-positive but radiologically invisible); **bronchial brushings/washings**; **FNAC**.
4. **Biopsy** — **Bronchoscopy + endobronchial/transbronchial biopsy** (central tumors); CT-guided percutaneous needle biopsy (peripheral); mediastinoscopy for nodal staging.
5. **Histology + Immunohistochemistry** — determine subtype:
   - **Adenocarcinoma: TTF-1 +, napsin A +**
   - **Squamous: p40/p63 +, CK5/6 +**
   - **Small cell: chromogranin, synaptophysin, CD56 +** (neuroendocrine markers)
   - Mesothelioma excluded with calretinin/WT-1/CK5/6 +, Claudin4 −
6. **Molecular testing** — **EGFR, ALK, ROS1, MET, RET, BRAF, KRAS, PD-L1** (guides targeted therapy/immunotherapy).
7. **Staging (TNM)** — T (tumor size/extension), N (nodes), M (metastasis); imaging: CT + PET + brain MRI.
8. **Screening for paraneoplastic syndromes** — serum Ca²⁺ (PTHrP), Na⁺ (SIADH), ACTH, etc.

## Q14. Write down the morphology of bronchogenic carcinoma.

### 1. Adenocarcinoma (50%)
- **Site:** **Peripheral**, usually smaller.
- **Microscopy:** glandular elements — **acinar, papillary, micropapillary, solid**; **TTF-1 positive** (also napsin A); peripheral **lepidic spread** ("crawling" along alveolar septa).
- **Minimally invasive** (≤3 cm, invasion ≤5 mm) → far better prognosis.
- **Mucinous adenocarcinomas** spread aerogenously → satellite tumors; can consolidate a whole lobe (mimics lobar pneumonia).

### 2. Squamous cell carcinoma (20%)
- **Site:** **Central / hilar** (peripheral form increasing).
- **Microscopy:** **keratinization + intercellular bridges + squamous pearls**; precursor = metaplasia → dysplasia → CIS over years.
- **Growth:** exophytic into lumen → **obstruction → distal atelectasis + infection**; or penetrates wall into peribronchial tissue/mediastinum; necrotic foci may cavitate.

### 3. Small cell carcinoma (15%)
- **Site:** major bronchi or periphery.
- **Most aggressive; metastasizes widely; virtually always fatal.**
- **Microscopy:** small cells, **scant cytoplasm, ill-defined borders, finely granular ("salt-and-pepper") chromatin, absent nucleoli, nuclear molding**; cells <3× small lymphocyte diameter; high mitoses; **extensive necrosis**; **Azzopardi effect** (basophilic DNA encrustation of vessel walls).
- **2/3 show dense-core neurosecretory granules (EM) + neuroendocrine markers (chromogranin, synaptophysin, CD56).** Secretes hormones → **PTHrP → hypercalcemia**.

### 4. Large cell carcinoma (2%)
- Undifferentiated; **diagnosis of exclusion** (negative TTF-1/napsin A and negative p40/p63); large nuclei, prominent nucleoli; LCNEC = molecularly small-cell-like with larger cells.

### Spread (common to all)
- Pleural + pericardial surfaces; **nodal mets >50%**.
- **Distant: adrenal >50% (most common), liver 30–50%, brain 20%, bone 20%.**
- Squamous metastasizes late outside thorax; metastasis may be first sign of occult primary.

### Local effects (secondary pathology)
Focal emphysema, atelectasis, suppurative bronchitis, **SVC syndrome**, pericarditis/pleuritis, **Pancoast tumor** (superior sulcus → ulnar pain + **Horner syndrome: enophthalmos, ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis**), hoarseness (recurrent laryngeal), dysphagia (esophagus), diaphragm paralysis (phrenic), rib destruction.

## Q15. Write down the morphology of small cell carcinoma of the Lung.

### Gross
- Central (major bronchi) or peripheral mass; bulky, soft, gray-white with **extensive necrosis**.
- Often appears as a **hilar/mediastinal mass** with airway compression.

### Microscopy (know these features — exam favourite)
1. **Small cells** — cells are <3× the diameter of a small lymphocyte.
2. **Scant cytoplasm + ill-defined cell borders** (closely packed, "bare" nuclei).
3. **Finely granular "salt-and-pepper" chromatin** (nuclear chromatin).
4. **Absent or inconspicuous nucleoli**.
5. **Nuclear molding** (nuclei mold against each other).
6. **Very high mitotic rate** (high proliferative index).
7. **Extensive necrosis** → basophilic appearance.
8. **Azzopardi effect** — basophilic DNA encrustation of vessel walls (from necrotic nuclei).
9. **Electron microscopy:** 2/3 show **dense-core neurosecretory granules**.
10. **IHC:** **chromogranin, synaptophysin, CD56 (NCAM)** positive; **TTF-1** often positive; **Ki-67 very high**.

### Molecular
- **Near-universal TP53 + RB inactivation**; 3p loss in nearly all; **MYC family amplification**; **highest mutational burden** of all lung cancers; virtually always smoking-related.

### Biological behaviour
- **Most aggressive lung cancer; metastasizes early + widely** (adrenal, brain, liver, bone); **paraneoplastic syndromes: SIADH, Cushing (ACTH), Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome**.
- Chemo/radiation sensitive but **rarely cured** (5-yr survival ~5%; extensive disease median ~10 months); **no known preinvasive phase**.

> 🎯 **One-liner:** Small cell = salt-and-pepper, molding, absent nucleoli, TP53+RB loss, neuroendocrine markers, paraneoplastic champion.

## Q16. Discuss the paraneoplastic syndromes / extrapulmonary manifestations related to bronchogenic carcinoma.


### Hormonal (endocrine) syndromes
| Hormone | Syndrome | Predominant tumor type |
|---|---|---|
| **ADH** | **SIADH / hyponatremia** | **Small cell** |
| **ACTH** | **Cushing syndrome** | **Small cell** |
| **PTHrP (+ PGE, cytokines)** | **Hypercalcemia** | **Mostly squamous** |

### Other systemic (non-hormonal) manifestations
1. **Lambert-Eaton myasthenic syndrome** — autoantibodies to **neuronal calcium channels** → muscle weakness (small cell).
2. **Pure sensory peripheral neuropathy.**
3. **Acanthosis nigricans** (skin).
4. **Leukemoid reactions** — ↑ WBC.
5. **Trousseau syndrome** — migratory **DVT/thromboembolism**.
6. **Hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy + digital clubbing.**

> 📌 Clinically significant syndromes occur in **1–10%** of cases, but serum hormone levels are elevated in many more. **Small cell = champion of paraneoplastic syndromes** (neuroendocrine origin).

> 🎯 **One-liner:** Hypercalcemia → squamous (PTHrP); SIADH + Cushing + Lambert-Eaton → small cell.

## 🩸 BONUS — SPUTUM EXAMINATION (topic in the title)

**What to examine in sputum:**
1. **Macroscopic:** amount, color (yellow-green = pus, rusty = pneumococcus, mucoid blood-tinged = **Klebsiella**), odor (**foul = anaerobic abscess/bronchiectasis**), consistency.
2. **Cytology:** **malignant cells** (sputum cytology for bronchogenic carcinoma — squamous CIS often sputum-positive), eosinophils (asthma), **Curschmann spirals + Charcot-Leyden crystals** (asthma), hemosiderin-laden macrophages ("heart failure cells" — chronic congestion).
3. **Microbiology:** Gram stain (lancet-shaped **pneumococcus**, Klebsiella), **AFB (acid-fast bacilli — TB)**, culture + sensitivity, Ziehl-Neelsen, KOH for fungi.
4. **Molecular/PCR** — TB, Legionella; urine antigen for Legionella/pneumococcus.
5. **Elastic fibers** (lung tissue destruction — abscess, TB).
