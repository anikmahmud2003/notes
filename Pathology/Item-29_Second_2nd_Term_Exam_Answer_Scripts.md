## ✍️ Answer Script — Group A

### Q1. Name the common ulcers of GIT. Give the characteristics of a malignant ulcer. Enumerate the diseases caused by H. pylori. 2+3+2

#### Common ulcers of GIT (2 marks)
- **Peptic ulcer:** gastric ulcer, duodenal ulcer (most common).
- **Other / infectious ulcers:** tuberculous ulcer, typhoid ulcer, amoebic ulcer, varus ulcer.

#### Characteristics of a malignant ulcer (3 marks)
- 🔴 **Indurated, hard, fixed, irregular base, rolling edges, non-healing** — **benign ulcer** is soft, friable, bleeding.

#### Diseases caused by H. pylori (2 marks)
- Peptic ulcer disease, chronic gastritis, **gastric carcinoma**, **MALT lymphoma**, increased risk of **intrapyloric/intraluminal gastric carcinoma (Type I IPC)**.

### Q2. A 25 year old woman is presented with a firm, mobile nodule in the outer quadrant of the right breast. Mention your D/D. How will you proceed to investigate the case? 2+1

#### Differential diagnosis (2 marks)
- **Fibroadenoma** (most common benign tumour in young women; mobile, rubbery, b/l) vs **fibrocystic change** vs **breast cyst** vs **carcinoma**.

#### Investigation (1 mark)
- Clinical breast **USG** → if complex/likely malignant → **FNAC** (or **triple assessment**: clinical + imaging + FNAC).

## ✍️ Answer Script — Group B

### Q1. Define and classify DM. Describe its pathogenesis and complications. 1+1+3+2

#### Definition and classification (2 marks)
- **DM:** chronic metabolic disorder characterized by **hyperglycemia**.
- **Types:** Type 1 (β-cell destruction/autoimmune → absolute insulin deficiency), Type 2 (insulin resistance + progressive β-cell failure), Gestational DM.

#### Pathogenesis (3 marks)
- **Type 1:** genetic susceptibility + environmental trigger (viral) → autoimmune β-cell destruction → absolute insulin deficiency.
- **Type 2:** obesity, insulin resistance → hyperinsulinemia → β-cell failure → hyperglycemia.

#### Complications (2 marks)
- **Acute:** DKA, HHS.
- **Chronic:** microvascular (diabetic retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy), macrovascular (CAD, stroke, PAD).

---

### Q2. The thyroid function tests show TSH: 11 mU/L, T3: 2.2 pmol/L, and ... at may be the causes of such condition? 1+2

- **Interpretation:** **TSH 11 mU/L** (normal ~0.4–4.0) → **elevated → primary hypothyroidism**; low/normal T3 (2.2 pmol/L) → consistent with hypothyroidism (subclinical/overt).
- **Causes:** iodine deficiency, **Hashimoto’s thyroiditis**, thyroid hormone resistance; secondary (pituitary) less likely.
- **Next step:** serum free T4, anti-TPO antibodies, pituitary workup if secondary.

## ✍️ Answer Script — Group A (cont.) — Optional Questions from Q3, Q4, Q5 (since Q1 & Q2 are compulsory)

### Q3. Classify salivary gland tumors. Name the polyps of small intestine. Give the pathogenesis of acute appendicitis. 2+1.5+1.5

- **Salivary gland tumors:** benign (pleomorphic adenoma, Warthin tumor) vs malignant (adenocarcinoma); WHO classification.
- **Small intestine polyps:** Peutz–Jeghers, Cronkhite–Canada.
- **Acute appendicitis:** luminal obstruction (fecalith) → mucosal ischemia → inflammation → acute appendicitis.

### Q4. Mention the causes of abnormal uterine bleeding in reproductive age group. What are the complications of leiomyoma? Give the pathogenesis of uterine cervical cancer? 1.5+1.5+2

- **Causes of AUB:** fibroids, hormonal imbalance, infection, malignancy, pregnancy.
- **Leiomyoma complications:** menorrhagia, anemia, pain, infertility, etc.
- **Cervical cancer pathogenesis:** HPV infection → cervical dysplasia → carcinoma.

### Q5. Short note (any two): a. Pleomorphic adenoma, b. Endometriosis, c. Dermoid cyst 2.5+2.5

- **Pleomorphic adenoma:** most common benign salivary gland tumor; mixed tissue.
- **Endometriosis:** endometrial tissue outside uterus.
- **Dermoid cyst:** benign teratoma (ovarian).

## ✍️ Answer Script — Group B (cont.) — Optional Questions from Q3, Q4, Q5 (since Q1 & Q2 are compulsory)

### Q3. Name the tests / fixatives related to fixation? Name the fixatives used in histopathology and cytopathology. 1+2+2

- **Histopathology fixatives:** 🔴 **10% neutral buffered formalin** (most common), carmine (iron hematoxylin), quick (chromium potassium sulfate), Ziehl–Neelsen (acid-fast), Gram, Giemsa/Methylene blue (B&B).
- **Cytopathology fixatives:** **Wet fixation** (95% ethanol / spray Carbowax), **air-drying** (Giemsa/MGG), liquid-based cytology (LBC).

### Q4. Short note on skin lesions. 2+3

- Inflammatory (eczema, psoriasis), neoplastic (BCC, SCC, melanoma, benign naevus).

### Q5. Short note (any two): a. FNAC, b. PAP smear, c. CSF examination 2.5+2.5

- **FNAC:** fine needle aspiration cytology (see Item-26).
- **PAP smear:** cervical exfoliative/abrasive cytology screening.
- **CSF examination:** meningitis diagnosis (cells, glucose, protein, culture).
