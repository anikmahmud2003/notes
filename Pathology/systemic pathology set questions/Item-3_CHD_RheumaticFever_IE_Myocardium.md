<summary><b>Q1. Classify congenital heart disease. Shortly describe the TOF.</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Congenital heart disease (CHD) = a structural abnormality of the heart or great vessels present at birth**, arising from faulty cardiac embryogenesis (~1% of live births; genes **GATA4, NKX2-5, TBX5, NOTCH1, JAG1**; associations — maternal rubella, diabetes, alcohol, Down/Turner syndromes).

### Classification

| Category | Hemodynamics | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **1. Left-to-right shunts (acyanotic, late cyanosis)** | Oxygenated blood re-circulates through lungs → volume overload → pulmonary HTN → **Eisenmenger reversal** | **ASD** (most common diagnosed in adults), **VSD** (most common CHD overall), **PDA** ("machinery" murmur), atrioventricular septal defect |
| 🔴 **2. Right-to-left shunts (cyanotic, early cyanosis)** | Venous blood bypasses lungs into systemic circulation — 🔴 **all begin with "T"** | **T**etralogy of Fallot, **T**ransposition of great arteries (d-TGA), persistent **T**runcus arteriosus, **T**ricuspid atresia, **T**otal anomalous pulmonary venous connection |
| **3. Obstructive lesions** | No shunt; pressure overload proximal to obstruction | **Coarctation of aorta** (Turner syndrome), aortic stenosis/atresia, pulmonary stenosis/atresia |

### 🔴 Tetralogy of Fallot (TOF) — the commonest CYANOTIC CHD

**The four cardinal features:**

| # | Feature |
|---|---|
| 1 | 🔴 **Subpulmonic (infundibular) stenosis** — the most important determinant of severity |
| 2 | 🔴 **Ventricular septal defect (VSD)** — large, membranous |
| 3 | 🔴 **Overriding aorta** — sits astride the VSD, receives blood from both ventricles |
| 4 | 🔴 **Right ventricular hypertrophy (RVH)** — secondary to the stenosis |

- **Embryology:** single defect — **anterosuperior displacement of the infundibular (outflow) septum**, which simultaneously produces stenosis, VSD and overriding aorta.
- **Morphology:** boot-shaped heart (RVH on CXR); right-sided aortic arch in ~25%.
- **Hemodynamics/clinical:** degree of shunt depends on stenosis severity — mild stenosis = "**pink tetralogy**" (L→R, acyanotic); severe = R→L shunt → **cyanosis from infancy**, dyspneic spells relieved by **squatting** (↑systemic resistance), clubbing, polycythemia, growth retardation.
- **Fate:** untreated — only 10% survive to 20 yr, 3% to 40 yr (hypoxic spells, endocarditis, brain abscess, stroke); treatment = total surgical repair.

> 🎯 **CHD: "L→R silent trio = ASD-VSD-PDA; Cyanotic all start with T"; TOF = PROVe — Pulmonic stenosis, Overriding aorta, RVH + VSD.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q2. What are cyanotic and acyanotic heart diseases?</b></summary>

🔴 **Cyanotic heart disease:** congenital lesions with a **right-to-left shunt or mixing**, so deoxygenated venous blood enters systemic circulation → **central cyanosis present from early life**.

🔴 **Acyanotic heart disease:** congenital lesions that are **left-to-right shunts or obstructive lesions** without early desaturation — patient appears pink; long-standing L→R shunt causes pulmonary vascular disease and **late cyanosis (Eisenmenger reaction)**.

| Feature | **Cyanotic (R→L shunt)** | **Acyanotic (L→R shunt / obstruction)** |
|---|---|---|
| Shunt direction | Right heart → left heart (bypasses lungs) | Left heart → right heart (recycles lungs) |
| Examples | 🔴 TOF, d-TGA, truncus arteriosus, tricuspid atresia, TAPVR ("5 Ts") | 🔴 ASD, VSD, PDA; coarctation, AS/PS |
| Onset of cyanosis | 🔴 Early (infancy) | Absent initially; 🔴 **late cyanosis when Eisenmenger develops** |
| Mechanism of symptoms | Poor oxygenation of systemic blood | Volume overload of pulmonary circuit → pulmonary HTN |
| Clinical features | Central cyanosis, **clubbing, polycythemia**, dyspneic spells, squatting (TOF), syncope | Recurrent chest infections, effort intolerance, CHF in infancy (large VSD), machinery murmur (PDA), wide fixed S2 split (ASD) |
| Complications | Brain abscess (paradoxical emboli), stroke, endocarditis | Eisenmenger syndrome (irreversible pulmonary vascular disease), endocarditis |

```text
Acyanotic L→R shunt for years → medial hypertrophy + intimal damage of
pulmonary arterioles → pulmonary vascular resistance ↑↑
→ resistance ≥ systemic → SHUNT REVERSES (R→L) = EISENMENGER SYNDROME
→ late cyanosis, clubbing, polycythemia — lesion now IRREPARABLE
```

> 🎯 **"Pink pipes pump Left→Right; Blue babies bypass lungs Right→Left"; every L→R shunt is one Eisenmenger away from turning blue.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q3. What is Rheumatic Fever? Describe the pathogenesis of Rheumatic fever.</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Rheumatic fever (RF) is an acute, immunologically mediated, multisystem inflammatory disease that occurs 2–3 weeks after an episode of group A β-hemolytic streptococcal (GAS) pharyngitis**, principally affecting children aged 5–15 years, and characteristically damaging the heart (rheumatic pancarditis) — chronic sequel being rheumatic heart disease.

📌 Only ~**3%** of infected individuals develop RF (genetic susceptibility; HLA-linked); poverty/crowding favour streptococcal spread. **Skin infection does NOT cause RF — only pharyngitis does.**

### Pathogenesis

🔴 **Autoimmune hypothesis — molecular mimicry between GAS M-protein antigens and cardiac self-antigens:**

```text
GROUP A β-HEMOLYTIC STREPTOCOCCAL PHARYNGITIS (M-protein types 1,3,5,6,14,18,24)
        ↓ (2–3 week latent period)
Immune response against streptococcal antigens:
ANTIBODIES (anti-M protein) + CD4+ T CELLS sensitized
        ↓
MOLECULAR MIMICRY / CROSS-REACTION — anti-streptococcal antibodies & T cells
react with CARDIAC ANTIGENS (myosin, tropomyosin, laminin, vimentin,
valvular glycoproteins) because of antigenic similarity
        ↓
┌─ Type II (humoral): antibody binding to valve/tissue antigens
│      → complement activation, inflammation → valvulitis (verrucae)
│
└─ Type IV (cell-mediated): CD4+ T-cell cytokines activate macrophages
       → granulomatous inflammation → ASCOFF BODIES in myocardium
        ↓
PANCARDITIS: pericarditis + myocarditis + endocarditis (all layers)
        ↓
Healing by fibrosis → repeated attacks → CHRONIC RHEUMATIC HEART DISEASE
(commissural fusion, leaflet/chordal scarring → mitral stenosis)
```

**Supporting evidence:** anti-streptolysin O titre rise precedes carditis; no streptococci found in lesions (sterile lesions); cross-reactive antibodies bind myocardium/valves (immunofluorescence); HLA class II association.

> 🎯 **"Strep throat today, rheumatism in 3 weeks": M-protein mimics heart protein → antibody + T cell crossfire → pancarditis.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q4. What are the Diagnostic criteria / Clinical Feature / Jones Criteria of rheumatic fever?</b></summary>

### 🔴 Revised Jones criteria — diagnosis requires **evidence of preceding GAS infection PLUS either 2 MAJOR or 1 MAJOR + 2 MINOR criteria**

**Evidence of preceding streptococcal infection:** rising/elevated **ASO titre** (or anti-DNase B), positive throat culture/streptococcal rapid antigen test, recent scarlet fever.

| Major criteria (JONES mnemonic) | Details |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **J — Joints: migratory polyarthritis** | Large joints (knee, ankle, elbow, wrist), one after another, extremely painful but leaves NO permanent deformity; most common feature in adults |
| 🔴 **O — Organ: Carditis (pancarditis)** | Endocardium (mitral/aortic verrucae + regurgitant murmur — **mitral valve always involved**), myocardium (Aschoff bodies → tachycardia, arrhythmia, failure), pericardium (friction rub); most serious manifestation |
| **N — Nodules: painless subcutaneous nodules** | Firm, 0.5–2 cm, over bony prominences (elbow, wrist, knee, shin); contain central fibrinoid necrosis with Aschoff-like histiocytic palisade |
| **E — Erythema marginatum** | Trunk/abdomen; erythematous serpiginous macules with **central clearing and ragged curved margins**, evanescent |
| **S — Sydenham chorea (St. Vitus dance)** | Rapid involuntary purposeless movements of face/hands, emotional lability; late manifestation (~6 months); self-limiting |

| Minor criteria | |
|---|---|
| **Fever** | Low-grade |
| **Arthralgia** (if arthritis not counted) | Pain without objective joint signs |
| **Raised acute-phase reactants** | ESR ↑, CRP ↑ |
| **Prolonged PR interval** | First-degree AV block (myocarditis effect) |
| (+ Previous history of RF/RHD) | Supportive |

### Clinical features summary

Onset 2–3 wk after sore throat; fever, migratory polyarthritis, chest pain/murmur/palpitation (carditis), subcutaneous nodules, rash, chorea; labs — ↑ESR/CRP, ↑ASO, leukocytosis.

> 🎯 **Jones major = JONES: Joints wander, Organ-heart burns, Nodules under skin, Erythema rings, St. Vitus dances; 2 Major OR 1 Major + 2 Minor + strep proof.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q5. Describe Etiopathogenesis of Rheumatic heart disease.### Etiology</b></summary>

1. 🔴 **Trigger organism: Group A β-hemolytic Streptococcus (Streptococcus pyogenes) pharyngitis** — virulent M-protein serotypes.
2. **Host factors:** genetic susceptibility (HLA-DR alleles, ~3% attack rate), age 5–15 yr, malnutrition/poverty/overcrowding.
3. **Recurrences:** each new streptococcal pharyngitis reactivates carditis and worsens chronic valve damage (hence secondary prophylaxis with penicillin).

### Pathogenesis (etiopathogenesis)

```text
GAS PHARYNGITIS (M-protein, streptolysin O/S, hyaluronic acid capsule)
        ↓
Humoral + cellular immune response (anti-M antibody, CD4+ T cells)
        ↓
MOLECULAR MIMICRY: cross-reactivity between streptococcal antigens and
cardiac antigens (cardiac myosin, laminin, tropomyosin, vimentin;
valve endothelium/VCAM-1 upregulation attracts T cells)
        ↓
Type II hypersensitivity (antibody-mediated tissue injury) +
Type IV hypersensitivity (CD4+ T-cell/macrophage granulomatous response)
        ↓
ACUTE RHEUMATIC PANCARDITIS:
• Myocardium — Aschoff bodies (fibrinoid necrosis + Anitschkow cells)
• Pericardium — fibrinous "bread-and-butter" exudate
• Endocardium/valves — edema → verrucae along lines of closure
        ↓
HEALING BY FIBROSIS after each attack (usually ≥10 yrs for full stenosis)
        ↓
CHRONIC RHD: leaflet thickening/calcification + COMMISSURAL FUSION +
chordal shortening/fusion → MITRAL STENOSIS (fish-mouth)/MR ± AS/AR
```

📌 **Valve preference: mitral virtually always (isolated ~⅔ of cases; mitral + aortic together ~25%); RHD is the cause of ~99% of acquired mitral stenosis.**

> 🎯 **RHD = "Rheumatic Repeats Ruin the Redundant mitral leaflets": mimicry → pancarditis → fibrosis → fused commissures → fish-mouth MS.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q6. Enumerate complication of Rheumatic fever.</b></summary>

### Complications during the acute attack

1. 🔴 **Cardiac (the only life-threatening one):**
   - Acute carditis → **arrhythmias** (AV block), **pericardial effusion**, acute **heart failure** (esp. children — "cor bovinum"), death (rare).
   - Myocarditis-related sudden death (uncommon).
2. **Joint involvement resolves completely — never deforms** (unlike RA).
3. Sydenham chorea — self-limiting (months), may relapse in pregnancy ("chorea gravidarum").
4. Skin lesions/nodules — heal without trace.

### Long-term complications (chronic RHD)

| # | Complication |
|---|---|
| 1 | 🔴 **Chronic valvular deformity — MITRAL STENOSIS (fish-mouth), mitral regurgitation, aortic stenosis/regurgitation** (combined lesions common) |
| 2 | **Atrial fibrillation** (from dilated left atrium in MS) → atrial mural thrombus |
| 3 | 🔴 **Systemic embolism** (LA thrombus → stroke, limb ischemia) |
| 4 | 🔴 **Infective endocarditis** on the damaged valve |
| 5 | **Pulmonary congestion → pulmonary hypertension → cor pulmonale (RVH/RVF)** |
| 6 | **Congestive cardiac failure** (valve dysfunction + atrial fibrillation) |
| 7 | **Recurrent rheumatic activity** worsening valves (need penicillin prophylaxis till age 40/long-term) |

> 🎯 **Acute RF spares joints but scars valves; chronic RHD = "MS → AF → Clot → Stroke", plus IE and cor pulmonale.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q7. Name the inflammatory diseases of heart. Mention the causes of pericarditis.</b></summary>

### Inflammatory diseases of the heart

| Layer involved | Diseases |
|---|---|
| **Pericardium** | Acute **pericarditis** (serous/fibrinous/purulent/hemorrhagic/caseous), constrictive pericarditis |
| **Myocardium** | **Myocarditis** (viral-Coxsackie, Chagas, autoimmune/giant-cell, drug hypersensitivity) |
| **Endocardium** | **Infective endocarditis** (acute/subacute), non-bacterial thrombotic (marantic) endocarditis, Libman-Sacks endocarditis (SLE), rheumatic endocarditis |
| **All three layers** | 🔴 **Rheumatic pancarditis** |

### Causes of pericarditis (by type)

| Type of pericarditis | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Serous** | Viral infection, **RF**, SLE, scleroderma, uremia, tumors — sparse lymphocytic infiltrate |
| 🔴 **Fibrinous/serofibrinous (MOST COMMON)** | 🔴 **Acute MI (day 2–4)**, **Dressler syndrome (post-MI, weeks later, autoimmune)**, uremia, RF, SLE, trauma/cardiac surgery (post-pericardiotomy), radiation — friction rub, "bread-and-butter" gross appearance |
| **Purulent/suppurative** | Direct extension from empyema/pneumonia/mediastinitis/**ring abscess**, hematogenous seeding, post-cardiac surgery; organisms — staphylococci, streptococci, pneumococci; heavy neutrophilic exudate |
| **Hemorrhagic** | 🔴 **Neoplastic infiltration (most common)**, TB, bleeding diathesis — blood mixed with exudate |
| **Caseous/granulomatous** | 🔴 **Tuberculosis** (pericardial caseation → calcification) — important cause of constrictive pericarditis |
| **Chronic fibrous/constrictive** | Healed any type (TB, purulent, post-surgery/radiation) — dense fibrocalcific shell encasing the heart |

> 🎯 **Heart inflammation by layer: "Painful Pouch (pericardium), Angry Muscle (myocardium), Bad Valves (endocarditis), All-three = Rheumatic"; Fibrinous rub loves MI and Uremia.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q8. Write/Describe in short about aschoff body.</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Aschoff body is the pathognomonic microscopic lesion of rheumatic fever — a focal interstitial myocardial granulomatous inflammatory nodule centred on fibrinoid necrosis of collagen, composed of Anitschkow cells (caterpillar macrophages), occasional multinucleated Aschoff giant cells and lymphocytes.**

### Structure & evolution (three stages)

| Stage | Histology |
|---|---|
| **1. Early/exudative-degenerative (1–2 wk)** | Swollen collagen fibers with 🔴 **fibrinoid necrosis** + lymphocytes/plasma cells around small vessels |
| **2. Granulomatous/proliferative (2–4 wk)** | Central fibrinoid necrosis surrounded by plump activated macrophages = 🔴 **Anitschkow cells** (some fuse into **Aschoff giant cells** — multinucleated, owl-eyed) + few T lymphocytes |
| **3. Healing/fibrous (2–4 months)** | Nodule shrinks to a spindle-shaped 🔴 **fibrous scar** (perivascular fusiform scar) |

### Sites

Myocardial **interstitium especially perivascular** near small vessels (left ventricle/posterior wall), subendocardium (left atrial endocardium → **MacCallum plaque**), and similar granulomas in valves/subcutaneous nodules.

### Significance

- Marker of **active rheumatic carditis**; correlates clinically with tachycardia, murmurs, arrhythmia.
- Repeated episodes leave increasing interstitial fibrosis → myocardial stiffness contributing to chronic dysfunction.

> 🎯 **Aschoff body = "fibrinoid fire ringed by caterpillar cells": early necrosis → caterpillar granuloma → spindle scar; pathognomonic of active RF.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q9. What is Anitschkow cell? What is Infective Endocarditis?</b></summary>

### Anitschkow cell

🔴 **Anitschkow cell = a large activated macrophage (histiocyte) characteristic of rheumatic inflammation, with abundant plump cytoplasm and a central serrated ribbon of chromatin that looks like a CATERPILLAR in longitudinal section ("caterpillar cell") and an OWL-EYE when cut transversely.**

- Found within Aschoff bodies; several may fuse to form **multinucleated Aschoff giant cells**.
- Also seen in other settings (normal pediatric hearts, post-injury states) but classic in RF.

### Infective endocarditis (IE)

🔴 **Infective endocarditis is a microbial infection of the endocardial surface — most commonly the cardiac valves — characterized by the formation of VEGETATIONS composed of platelets, fibrin, microorganisms and inflammatory cells, with destruction of underlying valve tissue.**

| Feature | **Acute IE** | **Subacute IE** |
|---|---|---|
| Organism | 🔴 **Staphylococcus aureus** (also β-hemolytic strep, pneumococcus) | 🔴 **Viridans streptococci (S. mutans, sanguis)** 50–60%; enterococci, HACEK group |
| Valve | Previously **NORMAL** valves | Previously **damaged/deformed** valves (RHD, MVP, bicuspid) |
| Course | Stormy, highly destructive, days–weeks, often needs surgery | Indolent, weeks–months, curable by antibiotics alone |
| Special setting | 🔴 IV drug users → **right-sided (tricuspid)** S. aureus IE | Dental procedures/sepsis seeding deformed valves |

(Prosthetic valve IE: early <2 mo — S. epidermidis/S. aureus; late >1 yr — streptococci/S. aureus.)

> 🎯 **Anitschkow = "caterpillar nucleus in the Aschoff nest"; IE = "vegetations = platelet-fibrin sandwich stuffed with germs" — Acute = Aureus on normal, Subacute = Streptococci on scared.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q10. Discuss the etiopathogenesis of Infective Endocarditis.</b></summary>

### Etiology (causative organisms)

| Setting | Organism |
|---|---|
| Subacute IE, native damaged valve | 🔴 **Viridans group streptococci** (*S. mutans*, *S. sanguis* — oral flora; dental procedures) |
| 🔴 Acute IE, normal valve / **IV drug users** | 🔴 **Staphylococcus aureus** (IVDU → tricuspid/right-sided) |
| After genitourinary/GI instrumentation | Enterococci (S. faecalis) |
| Prosthetic valve — early (<2 mo) | Coagulase-negative staphylococci (**S. epidermidis**) |
| Prosthetic valve — late (>1 yr) | Streptococci, S. aureus |
| Culture-negative (~10%) | Prior antibiotics, fastidious organisms — **Coxiella burnetii, Bartonella, HACEK** (Haemophilus, Actinobacillus, Cardiobacterium, Eikenella, Kingella), fungi (Candida/Aspergillus in immunosuppressed) |

### Pathogenesis (two prerequisites must meet)

🔴 **IE requires (a) BACTEREMIA + (b) ENDOCARDIAL SURFACE ABNORMALITY (or very virulent organism):**

```text
Sources of transient bacteremia:
dental extraction/scaling, GU/GI procedures, catheters/IV lines,
IVDU injections, skin sepsis, surgery
        ↓
Lands on predisposed endocardium:
deformed valve (RHD/MVP/bicuspid AoV), prosthetic valve,
septal defect, jet-damaged endocardium, OR nonbacterial
thrombotic vegetation (NBTE) on normal valve
        ↓
Platelet-fibrin microthrombus forms at high-turbulence site
(damaged endothelium → tissue factor)
        ↓
ORGANISM ADHERENCE via surface adhesins (fibronectin-binding
proteins of S. aureus; dextran of viridans strep; FimA)
        ↓
Colonization & proliferation INSIDE protective fibrin shield
(host defenses/phagocytes cannot penetrate)
        ↓
Bulky friable VEGETATION grows: platelet-fibrin + organisms
+ neutrophils; valve leaflets eroded/perforated; abscess extends
into annulus/conduction tissue
        ↓
Constant shedding: SEPTIC EMBOLI + IMMUNE COMPLEX formation
(type III hypersensitivity → glomerulonephritis, Osler nodes, Roth spots)
```

📌 Diagnosis uses **modified Duke criteria**: 2 major (typical positive blood cultures + echocardiographic oscillating intracardiac mass/abscess/new regurgitation) or 1 major + 3 minor or 5 minor (fever, predisposing condition, vascular phenomena — Janeway lesions; immunologic phenomena — Osler nodes/Roth spots/GN).

> 🎯 **IE formula: "Bug in blood lands on a battered valve → fibrin fort hides it → vegetate, embolize, immunize"; Duke: 2 majors or 1+3 or 5 minors.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q11. List Complications of Infective Endocarditis.</b></summary>

### A. Local (cardiac) complications

1. 🔴 **Valve destruction** — leaflet perforation/tearing → acute **regurgitation → acute heart failure** (leading direct cause of death).
2. **Ring (annular) abscess** → erosion into conduction system → 🔴 arrhythmia/heart block; may rupture into pericardium → suppurative pericarditis.
3. Myocardial abscess; fistulae; prosthetic valve dehiscence.

### B. Embolic complications

4. 🔴 **Septic embolization** of vegetation fragments → infarcts/abscesses in **brain (stroke), spleen, kidney, mesentery** (left-sided).
5. **Right-sided (IVDU) emboli → septic pulmonary infarcts/abscess.**
6. 🔴 **Mycotic (infective) aneurysm** of cerebral/systemic arteries — vessel-wall infection at embolic sites → rupture/hemorrhage.
7. Osteomyelitis (vertebral), splenic abscess.

### C. Immunologic (type III hypersensitivity) complications

8. 🔴 **Immune-complex glomerulonephritis** (hematuria, renal failure).
9. **Osler nodes** (tender digital pulp nodules), **Roth spots** (retinal hemorrhage with pale centre), positive rheumatoid factor.

### D. Systemic

10. Persistent bacteremia/sepsis, weight loss, anemia of chronic disease; embolic skin lesions (**Janeway lesions** — nontender palm/sole macules).
11. 🔴 **Death if untreated** (cure rates: viridans ~98%; S. aureus/enterococci 60–90%; gram-negative/fungi ~50%).

> 🎯 **IE complications in 4 baskets: "Destroy (valve), Travel (emboli/mycotic aneurysm), React (immune GN/Osler/Roth), Drain (abscess/sepsis)" — heart failure kills first.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q12. What is Vegetation? Name/causes of Vegetative Heart Diseases.</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Vegetation = an abnormal mass attached to a cardiac valve leaflet or mural endocardium, composed of platelets, fibrin, (microorganisms,) inflammatory cells ± red cells**, produced by endocardial injury/inflammation; may be sterile or infected.

### Vegetative heart diseases (causes/types of vegetation)

| Disease | Nature of vegetation | Cause/setting |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Infective endocarditis** | Large, bulky, **friable, destructive, INFECTED** (organisms inside); aortic + mitral most common; embolize → septic infarcts | S. aureus (acute)/viridans strep (subacute); IVDU → tricuspid |
| 🔴 **Rheumatic endocarditis (acute RF)** | Small (1–2 mm) **sterile warty VERRUCAL** vegetations arranged **along lines of closure** of leaflets (mitral posterior leaflet classic) | Autoimmune cross-reaction post-GAS pharyngitis |
| **Non-bacterial thrombotic endocarditis (NBTE/marantic)** | Small (1–5 mm) **sterile bland thrombi** on lines of closure, one/both sides of leaflet, non-destructive; embolize → sterile infarcts | Debilitated patients — 🔴 mucinous adenocarcinoma (pancreas/lung), sepsis, DIC, hyperestrogenic states; chronic disseminated coagulation |
| **Libman-Sacks endocarditis (verrucous)** | Sterile 1–4 mm warty vegetations **anywhere** on valve surfaces/chordae/endocardium (both sides), with fibrinoid necrosis | 🔴 **Systemic lupus erythematosus** + antiphospholipid syndrome; may heal with thickening |
| **Loeffler endomyocarditis** | Mural thrombus over eosinophil-damaged endocardium | Hypereosinophilia/eosinophilic leukemia (major basic protein injury) |

> 🎯 **Vegetation menu: "Infected & fat = IE; Warty rows = Rheumatic; Tiny bland = Marantic cancer; Anywhere-warty = Libman-Sacks (lupus)."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q13. Short Note: Pericarditis</b></summary>

- 🔴 **Definition:** inflammation of the pericardium (fibroserous sac around the heart); normally the sac contains <50 mL of straw-colored lubricating fluid.
- **Types & causes:**
  - **Serous** — viral, RF, SLE, scleroderma, uremia, tumors.
  - **Fibrinous/serofibrinous (commonest)** — 🔴 MI (day 2–4), Dressler syndrome (weeks later, autoimmune), uremia ("uremic friction"), RF, SLE, trauma/cardiac surgery, radiation; gross = shaggy "bread-and-butter" appearance; auscultation = **pericardial friction rub**.
  - **Purulent** — extension from pneumonia/empyema, ring abscess, postoperative; spiking fever, toxic state.
  - **Hemorrhagic** — malignancy, TB, bleeding diathesis.
  - **Caseous (TB)** → leads to calcific **constrictive pericarditis**.
- **Clinical:** sharp retrosternal pleuritic pain **worse lying flat, better sitting forward**, friction rub, ECG diffuse saddle-shaped ST elevation, pericardial knock.
- **Complications:** 🔴 **pericardial effusion → cardiac tamponade** (rapid 200–300 mL compresses atria/venae cavae — Beck triad: hypotension, muffled sounds, raised JVP; pulsus paradoxus), and **constrictive pericarditis** (fibrous shell up to 1 cm → diastolic restriction; treated by 🔴 **pericardiectomy**).

> 🎯 **"Rub the bread-and-butter sac": pain better forward + friction rub = pericarditis; beware tamponade (fast fill) and constriction (slow scar).**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q14. Short note: Cardiomyopathy</b></summary>

- 🔴 **Definition (WHO/expert consensus): cardiomyopathies are heterogeneous diseases of the myocardium causing mechanical and/or electrical dysfunction, usually with inappropriate hypertrophy or dilatation, and are either confined to the heart (primary) or part of a generalized systemic disorder (secondary).** Excluded: ischemic, valvular, hypertensive, congenital heart disease.
- **Classification (functional):**

| Type | Frequency | Functional defect | Morphology | Key causes/features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Dilated (DCM)** | **90%** | **Systolic failure** (↓EF) | 🔴 4-chamber dilation, flabby heart ×2–3 weight, mural thrombi | Genetic titin mutations (~20%), alcohol, viral myocarditis, peripartum, doxorubicin, hemochromatosis; transplant/death from HF or arrhythmia |
| 🔴 **Hypertrophic (HCM)** | 1:500, AD | **Diastolic failure**, outflow obstruction in 25% | 🔴 Massive asymmetric septal hypertrophy, banana cavity, **myofiber disarray**, SAM of mitral leaflet | Sarcomeric genes **MYH7/MYBPC3**; exertional angina/dyspnea, 🔴 sudden death in young athletes; β-blocker, septal myectomy |
| 🔴 **Restrictive (RCM)** | Least common | **Stiff ventricle** → poor filling, systole preserved | Normal/small cavities, biatrial dilation; infiltrate in wall | 🔴 Amyloidosis (Congo red, apple-green birefringence), sarcoidosis, radiation, endomyocardial fibrosis/Loeffler |
| Arrhythmogenic RV cardiomyopathy | Rare | Electrical instability → VT/SCD | RV wall replaced by fat + fibrosis | Desmosomal gene defects (Naxos syndrome) |

- **Clinical approach:** echo (chamber size/EF/wall thickness), MRI, ECG, family screening; management targets each physiology (DCM — HF drugs/VAD/transplant; HCM — β-blockers/ICD; RCM — treat underlying deposition).

> 🎯 **CMP trio: "Big floppy Bag (DCM-systolic), Bulky stiff Wall (HCM-diastolic), Hard packed Box (RCM-restrictive)" — DCM 90%, HCM kills athletes, amyloid stiffens.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q15. Short note: Myocarditis.</b></summary>

- 🔴 **Definition: myocarditis is inflammation of the myocardium associated with myocyte injury/necrosis and degeneration, caused by infectious agents, immune reactions or toxins.**
- **Causes:**
  - 🔴 **Viral (most common): Coxsackievirus A & B**, echovirus, influenza, HIV, CMV, parvovirus B19; post-viral immune phase also damages myocardium.
  - **Parasitic:** 🔴 **Chagas disease (Trypanosoma cruzi)** — South/Central America; trichinellosis (commonest helminthic).
  - **Bacterial/toxin-mediated:** diphtheria toxin; Lyme borreliosis (transient conduction block).
  - **Immune-mediated:** post-viral, **rheumatic fever**, SLE, drug hypersensitivity (methyldopa, sulfonamides → eosinophilic myocarditis), immune checkpoint inhibitors, 🔴 giant-cell myocarditis (fulminant, poor prognosis), transplant rejection.
- **Morphology:** interstitial inflammatory infiltrate (lymphocytes — commonest pattern) **surrounding individual degenerating/necrotic myocytes**; lesions patchy → endomyocardial biopsy may be falsely negative; eosinophils in drug cases; giant cells in giant-cell myocarditis.
- **Clinical:** spectrum — asymptomatic (with subsequent resolution) → fulminant HF with arrhythmias → sudden cardiac death; mimics MI (chest pain, enzyme rise with clean coronaries); some evolve into **dilated cardiomyopathy**.
- **Diagnosis:** clinical suspicion + troponin/ECG + cardiac MRI; endomyocardial biopsy is gold standard (Dallas criteria).

> 🎯 **"Virus attacks muscle between the beats": lymphocytes hugging dead myocytes = myocarditis; think Coxsackie, think Chagas, fear sudden death.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q16. Morphology of Rheumatic Heart disease (Acute & Chronic both)</b></summary>

### A. Acute rheumatic carditis (pancarditis — all three layers)

| Layer | Morphology |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Pericardium** | **Fibrinous/serofibrinous pericarditis** — shaggy "bread-and-butter" exudate; pericardial friction rub clinically |
| 🔴 **Myocardium** | 🔴 **ASCHOFF BODY** — pathognomonic granulomas: focal **fibrinoid necrosis of collagen** surrounded by Anitschkow (caterpillar) macrophages ± multinucleated Aschoff giant cells + lymphocytes, located perivascularly in interstitium; myocardial edema |
| 🔴 **Endocardium/valves** | 🔴 **Verrucal (warty) endocarditis** — small (1–2 mm) translucent sterile **verrucae along the lines of closure** of leaflets (classically posterior mitral leaflet; also aortic); valve cusp edema/fibrinoid change; chordae spared initially; **MacCallum plaque** — irregular endocardial thickening of posterior left atrial wall from regurgitant jet |

*(Mitral valve involved virtually always; mitral + aortic together in ~25%.)*

### B. Chronic rheumatic heart disease (years–decades after repeated attacks)

| Site | Morphology |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Mitral valve** | 🔴 Leaflet **thickening, rigidity, calcification**; **commissural fusion**; chordae tendineae shortened, thickened, fused; orifice narrowed to slit-like **"fish-mouth/buttonhole" stenosis**; ± combined regurgitation |
| **Aortic valve** | Similar — cuspal thickening/fusion (never isolated; accompanies mitral usually) |
| **Left atrium** | Dilated/hypertrophied (MS obstruction) → 🔴 mural thrombus in appendage → emboli; endocardial MacCallum thickening; atrial fibrillation |
| **Other chambers** | LV hypertrophy (if MR/AR dominant); pulmonary congestion → pulmonary HTN → 🔴 RV hypertrophy (cor pulmonale); chronic passive hepatic congestion |
| **Microscopy** | Fibrotic, hyalinized, neovascularized valve tissue with old granuloma-derived scars; active Aschoff bodies only if recent attack |

> 🎯 **Acute = "Bread-and-butter outside, Aschoff inside, warts on the closing line"; Chronic = "Fish-mouth mitral with fused chords and a clot-filled left atrium."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75
