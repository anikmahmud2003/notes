# Part A — Diabetes Mellitus: Definition & Diagnosis (Q1–7)

## Q1. Define DM. Classify it.

### Definition

🔴 **Diabetes mellitus (DM) = a group of metabolic disorders characterized by chronic hyperglycemia resulting from defects in insulin secretion, insulin action, or both.**

📌 Chronic hyperglycemia → long-term damage to **eyes, kidneys, nerves, heart and blood vessels**. DM is the **leading cause of end-stage renal disease, adult-onset blindness and nontraumatic lower-limb amputation**; myocardial infarction is the most common cause of death.

### Classification (WHO / ADA — etiologic classification)

| Class | Share | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Type 1 diabetes** | ~5–10% | **β-cell destruction → absolute insulin deficiency**; **1a = immune-mediated** (anti-GAD, anti-IA-2, anti-insulin, anti-ZnT8; HLA-DR3/DR4), **1b = idiopathic** |
| **Type 2 diabetes** | ~90–95% | **Insulin resistance + progressive insulin secretory defect** (β-cell failure) |
| **Gestational diabetes (GDM)** | 5–9% of pregnancies | Any degree of glucose intolerance **first recognized during pregnancy** |
| **Other specific types** | Rare | See below |

| Other specific types | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Monogenic β-cell defects** | **MODY 1–6** (HNF4A, **GCK**, HNF1A, PDX1, HNF1B, NEUROD1), neonatal diabetes (**KCNJ11, ABCC8**) |
| **Genetic insulin-action defects** | Type A insulin resistance (acanthosis nigricans), lipodystrophy |
| **Exocrine pancreas disease** | Pancreatitis, pancreatectomy, carcinoma, cystic fibrosis, **hemochromatosis**, fibrocalculous pancreatopathy |
| **Endocrinopathies** | Acromegaly, **Cushing syndrome**, hyperthyroidism, pheochromocytoma, glucagonoma, somatostatinoma |
| **Drug/chemical induced** | **Glucocorticoids**, thiazides, phenytoin, protease inhibitors, atypical antipsychotics, Vacor, pentamidine |
| **Infections** | Congenital rubella, CMV, Coxsackie B |
| **Genetic syndromes** | Down, Klinefelter, Turner, Prader-Willi, Werner |

> 🎯 "DM = chronic hyperglycemia from defective insulin secretion/action/both → **T1D (destruction, DKA)** · **T2D (resistance + failure, 90%)** · **GDM** · **other specific types (MODY, pancreatic, endocrine, drugs)**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q2. What are the differences between Type-I & Type-II diabetes mellitus?

### Type I vs Type II DM — comparison table

| Feature | **Type 1 DM** | **Type 2 DM** |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | **~5–10%** | **~90–95%** |
| Age of onset | Usually **childhood/adolescence** (<30 yr) | Usually **>40 yr** (now rising in obese children) |
| Onset | Rapid (weeks) — polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia, weight loss | **Insidious**, often asymptomatic for years |
| Body habitus | **Normal / thin** (weight loss) | **Obese (~80%)**, central obesity |
| Insulin level | **Absolute deficiency** (very low/absent) | Normal or ↑ early; **relative deficiency later** |
| C-peptide | **Low/absent** | Normal or ↑ (low only in advanced disease) |
| Autoimmunity | 🔴 **Positive islet autoantibodies** (anti-GAD65, anti-insulin, anti-IA-2/ICA512, anti-ZnT8) | **Negative** |
| Genetics | **HLA-DR3/DR4 (MHC class II)** + CTLA4, PTPN22, insulin VNTR; concordance 30–40% (MZ twins) | **No HLA linkage**; TCF7L2, PPARG, FTO; concordance **>90%** (MZ twins) |
| Pathogenesis | **Autoimmune T-cell–mediated β-cell destruction** (failure of self-tolerance) | **Insulin resistance** (FFAs, adipokines, inflammation) + **β-cell failure** |
| Islet morphology | **Insulitis**, β-cell depletion, islet atrophy | **No insulitis; islet amyloid (amylin)** deposition, mild β-cell loss |
| Ketosis | 🔴 **Prone to diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)** | Resistant to ketosis; 🔴 **hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS)** more typical |
| Associated autoimmune diseases | Hashimoto, Graves, Addison, vitiligo, pernicious anemia | Metabolic syndrome (obesity, dyslipidemia, hypertension) |
| Treatment | 🔴 **Insulin mandatory** (lifelong) | Diet + exercise → **oral hypoglycemics** (metformin) ± insulin late |
| Prevention | Not preventable (stage-based immune intervention trials) | Largely preventable by lifestyle (diet, exercise, weight loss) |

> 🎯 "T1D = Young, Thin, Autoantibody+, HLA-DR3/DR4, insulitis, absolute insulin → **DKA → must have insulin**; T2D = Older, Obese, Antibody−, no HLA, islet amyloid, resistant → **OHA → HHS**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q3. Give the lab diagnosis of DM.

### Diagnostic criteria (any ONE establishes DM)

| Test | Cutoff (venous plasma) | Remark |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Fasting plasma glucose (FPG)** | **≥7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL)** | No caloric intake ≥8 h (usually overnight 8–14 h) |
| 🔴 **2-h plasma glucose in 75-g OGTT** | **≥11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL)** | Performed as per standard protocol |
| 🔴 **HbA1c** | **≥6.5%** (48 mmol/mol) | NGSP-standardized assay; invalid in anemia/Hb disorders |
| 🔴 **Random plasma glucose** | **≥11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL)** | **With classic symptoms** (polyuria, polydipsia, unexplained weight loss) — no need to repeat |

📌 All tests except the random-symptomatic one must be **confirmed by repeat testing on a separate day** (unless unequivocal hyperglycemia with metabolic decompensation).

### Supporting / adjunct investigations

| Purpose | Tests |
|---|---|
| **Urine** | Glycosuria (appears when blood glucose exceeds renal threshold ~180 mg/dL), **ketonuria**, proteinuria/microalbuminuria |
| **Typing T1D vs T2D** | 🔴 Islet **autoantibodies** (GAD-65, IA-2, insulin, ZnT8) — positive in T1D; **serum C-peptide** — low in T1D, preserved in T2D |
| **Assessment of control/complications** | **HbA1c** (goal <7%), fasting lipid profile, serum urea/creatinine/electrolytes, urine albumin-creatinine ratio |
| **Screening for GDM** | 75-g OGTT at 24–28 weeks of gestation |

### Interpretation bands (non-pregnant adults)

| Parameter | Normal | **Prediabetes (IFG/IGT)** | **Diabetes** |
|---|---|---|---|
| FPG | <5.6 mmol/L (<100 mg/dL)* | 5.6–6.9 (100–125) ADA / **6.1–6.9 (110–125) WHO** | **≥7.0 (126)** |
| 2-h OGTT | <7.8 mmol/L (<140 mg/dL) | 7.8–11.0 (140–199) = **IGT** | **≥11.1 (200)** |
| HbA1c | <5.7% | **5.7–6.4%** | **≥6.5%** |

*WHO normal FPG upper limit is 6.0 mmol/L (110 mg/dL); ADA uses 5.6 mmol/L (100 mg/dL).

> 🎯 "Diagnose DM with **FPG ≥126 · 2-h OGTT ≥200 · HbA1c ≥6.5 · random ≥200 WITH symptoms** — repeat on another day except symptomatic random; add GAD antibody + C-peptide to type it."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q4. What is IFG and IGT? Tell their significance.

### Definitions

🔴 **Impaired fasting glucose (IFG)** = fasting plasma glucose **above normal but below the diabetic range** (a disorder of *basal* insulin secretion/hepatic glucose output).

🔴 **Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT)** = 2-hour plasma glucose **7.8–11.0 mmol/L (140–199 mg/dL)** after a 75-g OGTT with FPG <7.0 mmol/L (defect in *postprandial* glucose disposal — first-phase insulin release).

Together = 🔴 **"prediabetes" / intermediate hyperglycemia / dysglycemia**.

| Category | WHO | ADA |
|---|---|---|
| **IFG** | FPG **6.1–6.9 mmol/L (110–125 mg/dL)** | FPG 5.6–6.9 mmol/L (100–125 mg/dL) |
| **IGT** | 2-h PG **7.8–11.0 mmol/L (140–199 mg/dL)** | Same |
| **Combined IFG+IGT** | Highest progression risk | Same |
| **Prediabetic HbA1c** | 6.0–6.4% (WHO) | 5.7–6.4% |

### Significance

1. 🔴 **Harbinger of DM:** ~**25% progress to overt diabetes within 5 years**; majority eventually if lifestyle unchanged (combined IFG+IGT highest risk).
2. 🔴 **Macrovascular risk is already elevated** (atherosclerosis/MI/stroke begin in the prediabetic phase) even without microvascular disease.
3. Some microvascular injury may already appear in IGT (early retinopathy/neuropathy in a minority).
4. **Opportunity window:** intensive **lifestyle modification (DPP trial: diet + exercise ↓ incidence 58%)** or metformin delays/prevents progression.
5. **Label of caution:** IGT detected in pregnancy behaves as **gestational diabetes**; IFG/IGT patients need annual re-testing (FPG ± HbA1c).
6. Often clustered with **metabolic syndrome** (obesity, hypertension, ↑TG/↓HDL) → compound cardiovascular risk.

> 🎯 "IFG = fasting problem (110–125 WHO); IGT = post-load problem (140–199); both = prediabetes → **¼ become diabetic in 5 yr, CV risk already up, lifestyle now cuts risk by half**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q5. Tell WHO diagnostic criteria of DM.

### WHO criteria for diagnosis of DM (non-pregnant adults)

| Criterion | Value (venous plasma) |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Fasting plasma glucose** | **≥7.0 mmol/L (126 mg/dL)** *(fasting = no calorie intake for ≥8 h)* |
| 🔴 **2-h plasma glucose after 75-g oral glucose (OGTT)** | **≥11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL)** |
| 🔴 **HbA1c** | **≥6.5%** (assay aligned to international standards) |
| 🔴 **Random plasma glucose + classic hyperglycemic symptoms** | **≥11.1 mmol/L (200 mg/dL)** with polyuria/polydipsia/unexplained weight loss — no repeat required |

| Category | FPG (mmol/L) | 2-h OGTT (mmol/L) |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | <6.1 | <7.8 |
| **IFG** | 6.1–6.9 | <7.8 |
| **IGT** | <7.0 | 7.8–11.0 |
| 🔴 **Diabetes** | **≥7.0** | **≥11.1** |

### Rules of application

- Diagnosis should be **confirmed by a second test** on another day unless the patient presents with unequivocal hyperglycemia + symptoms or hyperglycemic crisis.
- Use **venous plasma**; capillary values differ slightly (whole-blood glucose ~12% lower than plasma).
- **Pregnancy:** separate WHO/IADPSG criteria (see GDM, Q6).
- Symptoms + random ≥11.1 mmol/L alone suffices — **do not wait for a second test before starting treatment** in a sick patient.

> 🎯 "**WHO: 7–11 rule** — FPG **7.0**, 2-h/random **11.1**, HbA1c **6.5**; confirm on a separate day unless symptomatic."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q6. What is GDM & IGT? Discuss about HbA1c.

### Gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM)

🔴 **GDM = any degree of glucose intolerance that is first recognized during pregnancy** (irrespective of whether insulin is needed or whether it persists after delivery).

- **Timing:** screened at **24–28 weeks** (placental hormones — human placental lactogen, progesterone, cortisol → insulin resistance peaks).
- **Diagnostic 75-g OGTT (IADPSG/WHO, one-step):** FPG **≥5.1 mmol/L (92 mg/dL)**, 1-h **≥10.0 mmol/L (180 mg/dL)**, or 2-h **≥8.5 mmol/L (153 mg/dL)** — any one value diagnoses GDM.

| Consequences | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Fetus/neonate** | **Macrosomia** (fetal hyperinsulinism) → birth injury/shoulder dystocia, neonatal **hypoglycemia**, respiratory distress syndrome, hyperbilirubinemia, hypocalcemia, **stillbirth**, later obesity/T2D |
| **Mother** | Pre-eclampsia, cesarean delivery, 🔴 **majority develop overt T2D within 10–20 years** — needs postpartum re-testing (OGTT at 6–12 weeks, then periodic) |
| **Management** | Diet → insulin (metformin used); resolves after delivery in most |

### Impaired glucose tolerance (IGT)

🔴 **IGT = 2-h plasma glucose 7.8–11.0 mmol/L (140–199 mg/dL) in the 75-g OGTT with FPG <7.0 mmol/L** — a state of post-prandial dysglycemia between normality and diabetes (prediabetes; see Q4 for significance).

### HbA1c (glycated hemoglobin)

🔴 **Formation:** glucose attaches **non-enzymatically and irreversibly** to the **N-terminal valine of the β-chain of hemoglobin A** (Ketoamine linkage) throughout the **~120-day red-cell lifespan** → **HbA1c reflects the average blood glucose of the preceding 8–12 weeks**.

| Aspect | Details |
|---|---|
| **Why superior to random/urine glucose** | No fasting needed; unaffected by day-to-day diet/exercise fluctuations and acute stress; objectively documents compliance |
| 🔴 **Diagnostic use** | **HbA1c ≥6.5%** diagnoses DM (NGSP-certified method); 5.7–6.4% = prediabetes |
| **Monitoring** | Every **3 months** (≈ RBC turnover); **target <7%** for most adults (individualized) |
| **Prognostic power** | Each 1% fall reduces microvascular complications (DCCT/UKPDS) — glycemia is a "memory" of complication risk |
| **Methods** | Cation-exchange chromatography/HPLC, boronate affinity, immunoassay, enzymatic — must be **NGSP/DCCT-aligned** |

### Conditions that make HbA1c unreliable (EXAM CAVEATS)

| Condition | Effect on HbA1c | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| **Hemolytic anemia, recent blood transfusion, malaria** | Falsely ↓ (short RBC survival) | Fructosamine/GA |
| **Iron-deficiency / megaloblastic anemia** | Falsely ↑ | Treat anemia, recheck |
| **Hemoglobinopathies (HbS, HbC, HbF ↑)** | Assay-dependent false ↑/↓ | Boronate-affinity/fructosamine |
| **Pregnancy** (↑ plasma volume, ↑RBC turnover) | Falsely ↓ | OGTT (mandatory for GDM) |
| **CKD/ESRD, erythropoietin therapy** | Variable (falsely ↓) | Glycated albumin |
| Recent-onset DM (short duration) | May underestimate | FPG/OGTT |

> 🎯 "GDM = glucose intolerance found in pregnancy (screen 24–28 wk; ≥5.1/≥10.0/≥8.5); IGT = 2-h 140–199; **HbA1c = 120-day glucose diary — diagnose ≥6.5, treat-to-target <7, beware anemia/Hbopathy/pregnancy**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q7. What is OGTT? Tell its procedure and interpretation.

### Definition

🔴 **Oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) = measurement of the body's ability to clear an oral glucose load**, by estimating blood glucose before and at defined times (standardly 2 h) after ingestion of 75 g glucose. It assesses the full physiologic loop — gut incretins, insulin secretion, insulin sensitivity and hepatic uptake.

**Indications:** borderline/equivocal FPG; **screening/diagnosis of GDM (24–28 wk)**; epidemiological studies; evaluation of reactive hypoglycemia, acromegaly (glucose-suppression test).

### Procedure (WHO standard 75-g test)

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1. Preparation (3 days) | Unrestricted diet containing **≥150 g carbohydrate/day** + normal physical activity (avoid carbohydrate depletion) |
| 2. Preparation (overnight) | **Fast 8–14 h**; water permitted; no smoking, caffeine or strenuous exercise |
| 3. Morning test | Conduct in the morning; patient remains **seated/resting** throughout |
| 4. Basal sample | Collect **fasting venous plasma glucose (FPG)** |
| 5. Glucose load | Dissolve **75 g anhydrous glucose monohydrate equivalent in 250–300 mL water** — drink within **5 minutes** (children: **1.75 g/kg, max 75 g**); flavored but non-carbonated preparations improve tolerability |
| 6. Post-load sample | Venous blood at **exactly 2 h** (plasma separated promptly) |
| 7. Pregnancy option | 100-g (Carpenter-Coustan, 4 samples: fasting, 1-h ≥180, 2-h ≥155, 3-h ≥140 mg/dL) or one-step 75-g as above |
| 8. Cautions | Postpone during acute illness; discontinue interfering drugs (steroids, thiazides, OCPs, niacin) if feasible; vomiting of the load → repeat on another day |

### Interpretation (venous plasma)

| Category | FPG (mg/dL) | 2-h PG (mg/dL) |
|---|---|---|
| **Normal** | <100 (WHO <110) | **<140** |
| **IFG** | 110–125 (ADA 100–125) | <140 |
| 🔴 **IGT** | <126 | **140–199** |
| 🔴 **Diabetes mellitus** | **≥126 (≥7.0 mmol/L)** | **≥200 (≥11.1 mmol/L)** |

- Both fasting and 2-h values should be interpreted together; either reaching the diabetic cutoff (with confirmation) diagnoses DM.
- **False-positive OGTT:** prolonged bed rest, acute illness/infection, hepatic disease, hyperthyroidism, drugs (glucocorticoids, thiazides, OCPs), post-gastrectomy (dumping-type curve), advanced age.
- **Flat/late curves** seen after gastric surgery (rapid absorption) or in hypothyroidism.

> 🎯 "**75 g in 250 mL within 5 min after an 8–14 h fast → 2-h value decides:** <140 normal · **140–199 IGT · ≥200 diabetes** (FPG ≥126 confirms)."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

# Part B — Complications & Hypoglycemia (Q8–13)

## Q8. What are the complications of DM?

### Overview table

| Group | Complication | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Acute metabolic** | **Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)** | T1D; insulin lack → ketosis; medical emergency |
| | **Hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state (HHS)** | T2D; glucose 600–1200 mg/dL, no ketosis, severe dehydration, coma |
| | **Hypoglycemia** | Most common acute complication *of therapy* (insulin/sulfonylurea) |
| | Lactic acidosis | Rare; metformin in renal failure/hypoxia |
| **Infections** | TB, pneumonia, UTI/pyelonephritis, mucormycosis, candidiasis, malignant (necrotizing) otitis externa | ↓ neutrophil chemotaxis/phagocytosis + vascular compromise |
| 🔴 **Chronic microvascular** | **Retinopathy** (background → proliferative) | Leading cause of adult-onset blindness; driven by VEGF |
| | **Nephropathy** | Microalbuminuria earliest → nodular glomerulosclerosis (**Kimmelstiel-Wilson**) → ESRD |
| | **Neuropathy** | Distal symmetric "glove-and-stocking" polyneuropathy, mononeuropathy, **autonomic** (postural hypotension, gastroparesis, erectile dysfunction) |
| 🔴 **Chronic macrovascular** | **Coronary artery disease** | 🔴 **Most common cause of death**; 2–4× risk; women lose premenopausal protection |
| | Cerebrovascular disease (stroke) | Accelerated atherosclerosis |
| | Peripheral arterial disease | **Gangrene ~100× more common**; diabetic foot ulcer → amputation |
| **Other chronic** | Diabetic foot (neuropathy + ischemia + infection), cataract (sorbitol), glaucoma, dermatopathy/necrobiosis lipoidica, stiff joints (cheiroarthropathy), frozen shoulder, periodontal disease, depression, dementia | Morbidity burden |

### Mechanisms of chronic hyperglycemic injury (4 pathways — EXAM FAVORITE)

| Mechanism | Consequence |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Advanced glycation end-products (AGEs)** | Bind **RAGE** → TGF-β (BM thickening), VEGF (retinopathy), ROS, procoagulant state; collagen cross-linking; trapped LDL |
| 🔴 **PKC activation** (via DAG) | ↑VEGF, TGF-β, **PAI-1** → vascular dysfunction, procoagulant |
| 🔴 **Polyol pathway** (aldose reductase → sorbitol, consumes NADPH → ↓GSH) | Oxidative stress; **sorbitol → cataract**; neuropathy |
| **Hexosamine pathway** | Fructose-6-P flux → cellular damage, enhanced oxidative stress |

📌 Chronic complications typically manifest **15–20 years** after onset and correlate with the degree and duration of hyperglycemia (good control delays them); basement-membrane thickening with **leaky** capillaries is the shared morphologic lesion.

> 🎯 "**Acute: DKA–HHS–Hypo–Lactic; Chronic: MICRO (Eye–Kidney–Nerve) + MACRO (Heart–Brain–Leg) + Foot + Infections — engines = AGE, PKC, Polyol, Hexosamine.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q9. Short note: Renal glycosuria.

### Definition

🔴 **Renal glycosuria = excretion of glucose in the urine despite normal blood glucose levels**, due to defective proximal tubular reabsorption of glucose (lowered renal threshold, usually **<10 mmol/L ≈ 180 mg/dL**) rather than hyperglycemia overwhelming a normal threshold.

### Mechanism

```
Glucose freely filtered at glomerulus (~100% filtered)
        ↓
Reabsorbed in proximal tubule by SGLT-2 (luminal) + GLUT-2 (basolateral)
        ↓
Mutation/defect (SLC5A2 encoding SGLT-2) → ↓ tubular maximum (Tm) for glucose
        ↓
Glucosuria appears at LOW plasma glucose (normal FPG, normal OGTT)
```

### Classification (Reubi; by severity)

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Type 0** | Most severe — no tubular reabsorption even at very low plasma glucose |
| **A (classic)** | Markedly ↓ Tm — glycosuria even in the fasting state |
| **B (mild)** | Moderately ↓ Tm — glycosuria mainly postprandial |

### Causes

| Cause | Comment |
|---|---|
| **Primary/familial (benign renal glucosuria)** | Autosomal recessive/dominant; **SLC5A2 (SGLT2) mutations**; asymptomatic, no treatment needed |
| **Generalized proximal tubular dysfunction — Fanconi syndrome** | Cystinosis, Wilson disease, heavy metals (lead, cadmium), outdated tetracycline, multiple myeloma → glycosuria + aminoaciduria + phosphaturia |
| **Physiologic** | **Pregnancy** (lowered threshold — common, benign) |
| **Pharmacologic** | **SGLT-2 inhibitors (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin)** deliberately produce glycosuria to lower glucose |

### Diagnosis & clinical importance

- Persistent **urine glucose positive with normal FPG, normal OGTT and normal HbA1c**; exclude DM before labeling.
- Benign familial type: no polyuria crisis beyond osmotic diuresis, normal life expectancy; counsel against mislabeling as diabetes.
- ⚠️ Viva point: **urinary glucose alone can NEVER diagnose DM** — always correlate with blood glucose.

> 🎯 "Renal glycosuria = **sugar in urine with normal blood sugar** — leaky SGLT-2 (familial, benign) or sick proximal tubule (Fanconi) or pregnancy; never diagnose diabetes from urine alone."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q10. What do you mean by hyperglycemia sine glycosuria? Tell the importance of HbA1c.

### Hyperglycemia sine glycosuria

🔴 **Hyperglycemia sine glycosuria = abnormally high blood glucose WITHOUT detectable glucose in the urine**, because the **renal threshold for glucose is elevated** above the prevailing blood glucose level.

```
Normally: renal threshold ≈ 180 mg/dL (10 mmol/L)
Blood glucose > threshold → glycosuria
        ↓
Raised threshold (elderly, long-standing DM with renal atherosclerosis/
nephrosclerosis, chronic kidney disease, ↓ GFR → smaller filtered load)
        ↓
Even marked hyperglycemia stays BELOW the (raised) threshold
        ↓
NO glycosuria despite hyperglycemia
```

📌 **Importance:** a **negative urine sugar test NEVER excludes diabetes**, especially in **elderly patients with renal vascular disease/impaired renal function** — diagnosis and monitoring must rely on **blood glucose and HbA1c**, not urine testing. (Converse situation: lowered threshold in pregnancy/renal glycosuria → glycosuria with normal blood glucose.)

### Importance of HbA1c

| # | Point |
|---|---|
| 1 | 🔴 **Integrated index of mean glycemia over preceding 8–12 weeks** (RBC lifespan ~120 days) — a "long-term memory" of glucose control |
| 2 | 🔴 **Unaffected by transient fluctuations** — meals, exercise, acute stress, day-to-day variability; no fasting required |
| 3 | 🔴 **Predicts chronic complications** (DCCT/UKPDS): each 1% reduction substantially lowers microvascular events |
| 4 | **Guides therapy:** measured every 3 months; target generally **<7%**; escalation/de-escalation of drugs based on trend |
| 5 | **Diagnostic:** HbA1c ≥6.5% diagnoses DM; 5.7–6.4% = prediabetes |
| 6 | **Objective compliance marker** in follow-up clinics (detects covert poor control despite reassuring home logs) |
| 7 | ⚠️ **Caveats:** unreliable in hemolytic anemia, recent transfusion, hemoglobinopathies, pregnancy, CKD/EPO therapy (use fructosamine/OGTT instead); assay must be NGSP-standardized |

> 🎯 "High sugar in blood but none in urine = **RAISED RENAL THRESHOLD** (old age, renal disease) → urine test can miss DM; hence trust **HbA1c — the 3-month glucose diary (≥6.5 dx, <7 target)**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q11. Define hypoglycemia. What are the causes of hypoglycemia?

### Definition

🔴 **Hypoglycemia = abnormally low plasma glucose concentration that produces symptoms/signs and is corrected by raising the glucose level** — formally documented by 🔴 **Whipple's triad**: (1) symptoms consistent with hypoglycemia, (2) low plasma glucose measured at the time of symptoms (**<70 mg/dL/3.9 mmol/L clinically significant; <54 mg/dL/3.0 mmol/L serious; <50 mg/dL/2.8 mmol/L classic biochemical cutoff**), (3) relief of symptoms when glucose is administered.

- **Severe hypoglycemia** = episode with altered mental/physical status requiring external assistance.
- Neonatal hypoglycemia: plasma glucose <40 mg/dL (2.2 mmol/L) in term babies.

### Causes of hypoglycemia (classified)

| Category | Causes |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Drugs** (most common) | **Insulin**, **sulfonylureas**, quinine, pentamidine, salicylates (massive), β-blockers (mask symptoms) |
| 🔴 **Alcohol** | Inhibits gluconeogenesis (esp. malnourished/fast drinkers — "holiday drinking" binge) |
| **Critical illness** | **Hepatic failure**, **renal failure** (↓ insulin clearance, ↓ gluconeogenesis), cardiac failure, sepsis, severe malaria, malnutrition |
| **Counter-regulatory hormone deficiency** | **Cortisol deficiency (Addison disease)**, growth hormone deficiency, hypopituitarism, hypothyroidism |
| 🔴 **Endogenous hyperinsulinism** | **Insulinoma** (β-cell tumor), β-cell hyperplasia/**nesidioblastosis** (incl. post-bariatric), anti-insulin/insulin-receptor antibodies, gain-of-function ABCC8/KCNJ11 (neonatal) |
| **Non-islet cell tumor** (paraneoplastic) | Large mesenchymal/hepatocellular/adrenocortical tumors secreting **"big" IGF-II** |
| **Reactive/postprandial** | Post-gastric-surgery (dumping/late hypoglycemia), early T2D (delayed insulin peak), hereditary fructose intolerance, galactosemia, leucine-sensitive hypoglycemia |
| **Neonatal** | Prematurity, maternal diabetes (transient hyperinsulinism), birth asphyxia, sepsis, Beckwith-Wiedemann |
| **Factitious** | Surreptitious self-administration of insulin/sulfonylurea (check **C-peptide**: suppressed with exogenous insulin, elevated with sulfonylurea) |

> 🎯 "**Whipple triad** defines it (<70 act, <50 classic); causes — **Drugs & Alcohol first**, then Sick organs, Hormone lack, **Insulinoma**, big-tumour IGF-II, Reactive, Neonatal, Factitious (C-peptide unmasks)."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q12. How does hypoglycemia occur in diabetic patients?

### Why diabetics develop hypoglycemia (the commonest acute complication of treatment)

| Mechanism | Explanation |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Missed / delayed / small meal after insulin or sulfonylurea** | Insulin acts with no absorbed substrate — the classic scenario (see Q15 case) |
| 🔴 **Relative insulin excess** | Wrong dose, wrong insulin type (rapid- vs long-acting mix-up), duplicated doses, injection into exercising limb/lipohypertrophy (erratic absorption) |
| 🔴 **Unaccustomed exercise** | Working muscle takes up glucose independently of insulin + increases injection-site absorption |
| 🔴 **Alcohol** | Blocks hepatic gluconeogenesis (fasting alcoholic diabetic at high risk, sometimes hours later at night) |
| **Renal failure** | ↓ insulin clearance + ↓ renal gluconeogenesis |
| **Weight loss / improved control** | Falling insulin requirement — dose not adjusted downward |
| **Gastroparesis** (autonomic neuropathy) | Erratic, unpredictable nutrient absorption |
| **Coexisting disease** | Liver failure, adrenal insufficiency (Addison), hypothyroidism, sepsis |
| 🔴 **Counter-regulatory failure — "hypoglycemia unawareness"** | Recurrent iatrogenic episodes blunt the adrenergic warning symptoms (defective glucagon + epinephrine responses) → severe recurrent attacks |
| **Somogyi phenomenon** | Undetected nocturnal hypoglycemia → counter-regulatory rebound → paradoxical fasting hyperglycemia next morning |

### Clinical features

| Stage | Plasma glucose | Manifestations |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Neurogenic (adrenergic)** | ~60–70 mg/dL | **Sweating, tremor, palpitations, tachycardia, anxiety, hunger, pallor** |
| 🔴 **Neuroglycopenic** | <50 mg/dL | Weakness, confusion, behavioral change, blurred vision, slurred speech, **seizures, unconsciousness, coma** |
| **Nocturnal** | Overnight | Night sweats, disturbed sleep, nightmares, morning headache |

### Immediate management (must know)

1. Stop activity; check capillary glucose if possible (**do not delay treatment** in a known diabetic with typical features).
2. 🔴 **Conscious & able to swallow:** **15–20 g rapidly absorbed carbohydrate** (3–4 teaspoons of sugar/glucose tablets/juice 150 mL) → **recheck after 15 minutes → repeat if still <70 mg/dL** (the **"15/20 rule"**) → follow with a complex-carbohydrate snack.
3. 🔴 **Unconscious / unable to swallow:** **IV 25–50 mL of 50% dextrose** (or 80–100 mL of 20%) bolus → **10% dextrose infusion** to hold glucose >100 mg/dL; hourly monitoring. **Never give oral fluid to an unconscious patient (aspiration).**
4. 🔴 **No IV access:** **glucagon 1 mg IM/SC** (less effective after alcohol, sulfonylureas or chronic hypoglycemia).
5. Identify the precipitant; adjust regimen; educate patient/family (carry sugar, medic-alert card, home glucose meter, standby glucagon); review for recurrent/unaware hypoglycemia.

> 🎯 "Hypo in a diabetic = **insulin/sulfonylurea meets NO food** (missed meal, exercise, alcohol, renal failure) → **adrenaline signs first (sweat+tremor+tachycardia), brain failure next** → **conscious: 20 g sugar/15-min rule · unconscious: IV 50% dextrose or IM glucagon**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q13. Discuss in short about the pathogenesis of diabetic ketoacidosis.

### Setting

🔴 **DKA = acute metabolic emergency of TYPE 1 diabetes** (absolute insulin deficiency; rare, stress-triggered in T2D). **Precipitants:** omission/underdosing of insulin, **infection** (most common), MI/stroke, pancreatitis, trauma, surgery, drugs (steroids; SGLT-2 inhibitors → euglycemic DKA).

### Pathogenesis (arrow-chain)

```
PRECIPITATING EVENT (infection / missed insulin / MI / trauma)
        ↓  Stress hormones: EPINEPHRINE, GLUCAGON, CORTISOL, GH rise
ABSOLUTE INSULIN DEFICIENCY + UNOPPOSED COUNTER-REGULATORY HORMONES
        ↓
┌──────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ GLUCOSE ARM          │ FAT ARM                                  │
│ ↓ uptake by muscle/  │ Hormone-sensitive LIPOPROTEIN LIPASE     │
│ fat; ↑ hepatic       │ inactive → hormone-sensitive LIPASE     │
│ gluconeogenesis &    │ active → massive LIPOLYSIS → FFAs       │
│ glycogenolysis       │   ↓                                      │
│   ↓                  │ Hepatic β-oxidation of FFAs              │
│ HYPERGLYCEMIA        │   ↓                                      │
│ (250–600 mg/dL)      │ KETONE BODIES: acetoacetic acid +        │
│   ↓                  │ β-hydroxybutyric acid + acetone          │
│ Exceeds renal        │   ↓                                      │
│ threshold →          │ KETONEMIA → ketonuria (buffered by       │
│ GLYCOSURIA           │ bicarbonate → HCO₃ consumed)             │
│   ↓                  │   ↓                                      │
│ OSMOTIC DIURESIS:    │ HIGH-ANION-GAP METABOLIC ACIDOSIS        │
│ loss of Na⁺/K⁺/      │   ↓                                      │
│ Mg²⁺/PO₄³⁻ + water   │ KUSSMAUL breathing (resp. compensation)  │
│   ↓                  │ + acetone = FRUITY BREATH                │
│ DEHYDRATION →        │ + nausea/vomiting/abdominal pain         │
│ hypovolemia → ↓renal │   ↓                                      │
│ perfusion → ↓glucose │ ACIDOSIS + HYPEROSMOLALITY + dehydration │
│ excretion (vicious   │   ↓                                      │
│ cycle) → SHOCK,      │ DEPRESSED CONSCIOUSNESS → COMA           │
│ AZOTEMIA, COMA       │                                          │
└──────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────┘
POTASSIUM: acidosis + insulin lack shift K⁺ out of cells;
total-body K⁺ is SEVERELY DEPLETED although serum K⁺ may be
normal/high — falls abruptly with insulin therapy → replace early.
```

### Laboratory profile

| Parameter | Finding |
|---|---|
| Blood glucose | **250–600 mg/dL** (may be near-normal in euglycemic DKA) |
| Arterial pH / HCO₃⁻ | **pH <7.30, HCO₃⁻ <15–18 mmol/L**, ↑ anion gap |
| Ketones | 🔴 **Ketonemia (β-hydroxybutyrate >3 mmol/L) + KETONURIA** (Rothera's test +) |
| Electrolytes | Hyponatremia (osmotic dilution), K⁺ variable but total-body deficit, prerenal azotemia |
| Others | Leukocytosis (stress), ↑ amylase, hypertriglyceridemia |

### Clinical picture & principles of treatment

- Polyuria/polydipsia → nausea, vomiting, **severe abdominal pain**, **deep sighing (Kussmaul) respiration**, **fruity acetone breath**, dehydration/hypotension → drowsiness → **coma**.
- Treatment pillars (in order): 🔴 **IV isotonic saline** (restore volume) → **IV regular insulin infusion (~0.1 unit/kg/h)** → **potassium replacement** → treat the precipitant (infection!) → bicarbonate only if pH <6.9. Mortality <1–5%.

> 🎯 "DKA = **no insulin + glucagon excess → glucose arm (hyperglycemia → osmotic diuresis → dehydration) + fat arm (FFA → ketones → high-anion-gap acidosis → Kussmaul + fruity breath → coma)**; treat **saline → insulin → K⁺ → trigger**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

# Part C — Benedict Test & Case (Q14–15)

## Q14. Tell the procedure and interpretations of Benedict's test.

### What it is

🔴 **Benedict's qualitative test = a simple chemical test that detects REDUCING SUGARS in urine** (chiefly **glucose**, but also fructose, galactose, lactose, maltose and pentoses) — the classical bedside/ward test for glycosuria.

### Principle

🔴 **In hot ALKALINE medium, the reducing sugars (through their free aldehyde/keto group, converted to the reactive ENEDIOL form) reduce the blue cupric ions (Cu²⁺) of copper sulfate to insoluble brick-red CUPROUS OXIDE (Cu₂O)**, forming a colored precipitate. **Sodium citrate** complexes the copper (prevents premature precipitation of copper hydroxide/carbonate) and **sodium carbonate** provides the alkalinity.

### Benedict's qualitative reagent composition

| Component | Amount per litre | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Copper sulfate (CuSO₄·5H₂O) | 17.3 g | Source of Cu²⁺ |
| Sodium citrate | 173 g | Chelating agent — keeps Cu²⁺ soluble |
| Sodium carbonate (anhydrous) | 100 g | Alkalinizes medium |
| Distilled water | to 1000 mL | Solvent |

### Procedure

1. Take **2 mL (≈5 mL) of Benedict's qualitative reagent** in a clean dry test tube.
2. Add **4–8 drops of well-mixed urine** (excess urine → false reduction).
3. **Boil directly over a flame for ~2 minutes** (or place in a boiling water bath for 3–5 minutes) while holding the tube safely.
4. Allow the tube to **cool spontaneously** (do not shake).
5. Observe the **color of the solution and the precipitate**.

### Interpretation table (semiquantitative)

| Colour / precipitate after cooling | Report | Approx. glucose |
|---|---|---|
| 🔵 Blue, no precipitate | **Negative** | Nil |
| Green solution, cloudy (no ppt) | Trace | <0.5% |
| 🟢 Green precipitate | **+** | **~0.5%** |
| Greenish-yellow / yellow-green precipitate | **++** | **~1.0%** |
| Yellow / orange precipitate | **+++** | **~1.5%** |
| 🔴 **Brick-red copious precipitate** | **++++** | **≥2.0%** |

### Sources of error / interpretation cautions (viva favorites)

- 🔴 **Non-specific:** detects **ALL reducing sugars** → a positive test is NOT proof of diabetes — **galactosemia (infants), lactosuria (pregnancy/nursing), fructosuria, pentosuria** all give positives; confirm glucose with **glucose oxidase (specific) dipstick**.
- **False positives:** **ascorbic acid (vitamin C)**, salicylates/aspirin, levodopa, cephalosporins, penicillins, isoniazid, uric acid, homogentisic acid (alkaptonuria), glucuronides.
- **Trace positivity** may occur normally after a large carbohydrate meal (alimentary glycosuria).
- False-negative Benedict's is uncommon (unlike glucose-oxidase strips, which vitamin C can inhibit).
- Modern practice: enzymatic dipsticks/Clinitest replaced Benedict's, but the principle (copper reduction) survives in Clinitest and in CSF/other-fluid sugar checks.

> 🎯 "**Alkali + heat: sugar's aldehyde pulls Cu²⁺ down to Cu₂O** — blue nil · green + (½%) · yellow-green ++ (1%) · yellow +++ (1½%) · **brick-red ++++ (≥2%)**; remember it catches ALL reducing sugars (vitamin C fools it)."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448

## Q15. A 25 year old type I diabetic patient was taking insulin regularly as treatment. He suddenly became unconscious and was brought to you. You examined and got the patient sweating with increased heart rate. His relatives gave a history of missed meal this morning after taking insulin. What is your diagnosis? How will you treat the patient?

### What is your diagnosis?

🔴 **Diagnosis: Severe HYPOGLYCEMIC COMA (insulin-induced hypoglycemia)** in a type 1 diabetic.

**Supporting logic (say these points in viva):**

| Clue from the stem | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Type 1 DM on **regular insulin** | Exogenous insulin on board |
| 🔴 **Took insulin but MISSED the meal** | Insulin acting with **no substrate** → relative insulin excess |
| 🔴 **Sudden** collapse | Hypoglycemia is abrupt (DKA evolves over hours–days) |
| 🔴 **Sweating + increased heart rate** | Sympathoadrenal (neurogenic) warning signs of hypoglycemia |
| **Unconsciousness** | Neuroglycopenic stage — brain deprived of glucose |

- Differential to exclude: **DKA** (but there insulin is omitted — here it was given; DKA shows dehydration, deep Kussmaul breathing, fruity breath, abdominal pain), HHS, stroke, head injury, meningitis, intoxication.

### How will you confirm?

- 🔴 **Immediate bedside capillary blood glucose** (glucometer): expected **<70 mg/dL (often <50 mg/dL)**; send venous plasma glucose simultaneously.
- Serum/urine **ketones negative** (rules against DKA); document findings after stabilization.
- ⚠️ If a glucose reading cannot be obtained quickly in an unconscious known diabetic — 🔴 **treat presumptively with glucose without waiting** (delay causes permanent neurological damage/death).

### Treatment (stepwise)

1. **ABCDE:** secure airway (unconscious patient), breathing, circulation; oxygen; **IV access**.
2. 🔴 **Never give anything orally while unconscious** (risk of aspiration).
3. 🔴 **IV 25–50 mL of 50% dextrose** slow bolus (alternative: 80–100 mL of 20% dextrose) → patient usually regains consciousness within minutes.
4. Follow with **10% dextrose infusion**, maintaining glucose **>100 mg/dL**; check capillary glucose every **15 minutes initially, then hourly**.
5. 🔴 **If IV access unavailable:** **glucagon 1 mg IM/SC** (family member may have been trained; less effective after alcohol/sulfonylurea).
6. Once **fully conscious and able to swallow safely** → **oral carbohydrates**: 15–20 g quick-acting (juice/glucose) followed by **complex carbohydrate + protein snack** (bread/biscuits/milk) to prevent recurrence — especially important with long-acting insulin still active.
7. **Observation:** monitor ≥24 h (long-acting insulins/sulfonylureas recur); admit if severe, recurrent or sulfonylurea-related.
8. **Prevent recurrence (discharge package):**
 - Explain the cause — **insulin must be matched with a meal**; never skip meals after dosing.
 - Review insulin regimen/dose and injection technique; teach **self-monitoring of blood glucose**.
 - Carry **quick sugar at all times** + identity/medic-alert card; train relatives in **glucagon injection**.
 - Screen for hypoglycemia **unawareness**, renal disease, adrenal insufficiency; consider regimen simplification.

> 🎯 "**Insulin + no meal + sweat/tachycardia + sudden coma = HYPOGLYCEMIC COMA** → glucometer <70 confirms → **IV 50% dextrose 50 mL** (or IM glucagon if no vein) → 10% drip → eat when awake → educate: 'needle goes in, food must go in.'"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 24 (Endocrine System), pp. 1065–1132 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II Blood Glucose and Diabetes Mellitus, pp. 433–448
