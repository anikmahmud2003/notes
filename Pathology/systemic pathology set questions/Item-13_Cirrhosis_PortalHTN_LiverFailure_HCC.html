Q1. Definition of cirrhosis of liver. What is chronic liver disease?

Definition of cirrhosis

🔴 Cirrhosis is an end-stage liver disease characterized by three cardinal features:

  1. 🔴 Bridging fibrous septa linking portal tracts with each other and with central veins;
  2. 🔴 Parenchymal regenerative nodules of hepatocytes surrounded by the fibrous septa;
  3. 🔴 Derangement/duplication of vascular architecture — portosystemic shunts and loss of normal sinusoidal perfusion.

📌 It is the common end-point of chronic hepatocellular injury of any cause; irreversible in practice; functional consequence = portal hypertension + hepatocellular failure.

Chronic liver disease

🔴 Chronic liver disease = persistent hepatic inflammation/necrosis/fibrosis lasting more than 6 months, demonstrated by clinical, biochemical or serological abnormality, irrespective of etiology — a broader umbrella that includes chronic hepatitis (viral/autoimmune/drug/metabolic), steatohepatitis, chronic cholestatic diseases (PBC/PSC) and their complications; 🔴 cirrhosis is its advanced/end stage.

🎯 "Cirrhosis triad = FIBROUS septa + REGENERATIVE nodules + VASCULAR shunting — 'FRV: Fibrosis-Regeneration-Vascular chaos'; chronic liver disease = >6 months of injury, cirrhosis = its final chapter."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

Q5. Describe the pathogenesis of cirrhosis of liver in chronic viral hepatitis.

Pathogenesis (chronic HBV/HCV → cirrhosis)

Persistent viral infection (HBV cccDNA / HCV quasispecies escape)
        ↓
Immune-mediated hepatocyte death (CD8+ CTL apoptosis/necrosis) — repeated cycles
        ↓
Chronic inflammation: Kupffer cell activation → cytokines
   (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, TGF-β, PDGF)
        ↓
🔴 QUIESCENT HEPATIC STELLATE CELLS (Ito cells, space of Disse) ACTIVATED
   → transform into myofibroblast-like cells
        ↓
Excess collagen types I & III deposited in perisinusoidal space
   → "capillarization" of sinusoids (loss of fenestrations)
        ↓
Fibrosis: portal → periportal → BRIDGING (portoportal/portocentral septa)
        ↓
Loss of hepatocyte mass stimulates compensatory REGENERATION
   → thick plates (>2 cells) forming NODULES encircled by septa
        ↓
Septa distort vasculature → intrahepatic shunts + increased sinusoidal resistance
        ↓
🔴 ESTABLISHED CIRRHOSIS (fibrosis + regenerative nodules + vascular derangement)

📌 Key points to say: (1) injury is immune-mediated, not directly cytopathic; (2) stellate cell activation by TGF-β/PDGF is the central fibrogenic event; (3) regression is possible if virus eradicated early (DAAs/antivirals), but advanced cirrhosis largely irreversible; (4) ongoing necrosis + regeneration = mutagenic milieu → HCC risk.

🎯 "Chain: CTL kills hepatocyte → cytokines wake STELLATE cells → collagen bridges → nodules regenerate → vessels derange = cirrhosis; remember 'TGF-β turns Ito into scar'."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405
Q6. What is NASH? How Non alcoholic fatty liver disease may give rise to cirrhosis?

NASH — definition

🔴 NASH (Non-alcoholic steatohepatitis) = the progressive form of NAFLD characterized histologically by the triad of hepatic steatosis (≥5% hepatocytes) + lobular inflammation + ballooned hepatocytes, occurring without significant alcohol intake and mimicking alcoholic hepatitis; it carries a real risk of fibrosis/cirrhosis. NAFLD = hepatic steatosis alone or with the above inflammation, associated with obesity/type 2 diabetes/hyperlipidemia (metabolic syndrome).

How NAFLD progresses to cirrhosis

Insulin resistance (obesity, metabolic syndrome, diabetes)
        ↓
Peripheral lipolysis ↑ + de novo lipogenesis ↑ → fatty acid influx to liver
        ↓
MACROVESICULAR STEATOSIS (simple NAFLD — ~80%, usually indolent)
        ↓ (in ~20%: second hit)
Lipotoxicity: oxidative stress + lipid peroxidation
   → mitochondrial dysfunction, TNF-α release,
     adipokine imbalance (↓adiponectin, ↑leptin)
        ↓
Kupffer cell + inflammatory recruitment → lobular inflammation
   → hepatocyte BALLOONING (cytokeratin injury, ± Mallory-Denk hyaline)
        ↓
Zone 3 pericellular "CHICKEN-WIRE" perisinusoidal fibrosis
   → stellate cell activation → bridging fibrosis
        ↓
🔴 NASH → MACRONODULAR CIRRHOSIS
        ↓
Steatosis/ballooning DISAPPEAR ("burned-out") → labelled CRYPTOGENIC cirrhosis
   (🔴 >90% of cryptogenic cirrhosis = burned-out NASH)
        ↓
Risk continues: HCC even in NAFLD-related cirrhosis (<1%/yr)

📌 Clinical: often asymptomatic (fatigue, RUQ dull ache); mildly raised AST/ALT (ALT>AST unless advanced); definitive diagnosis of NASH requires liver biopsy; treatment = weight loss/exercise/diabetes control.

🎯 "NASH triad = Steatosis + Ballooning + Lobular inflammation ('SBL'); road to cirrhosis = insulin resistance → fat → fire (inflammation) → wire (chicken-wire fibrosis) → burned-out cryptogenic cirrhosis."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

Gross morphology

Microscopic morphology

  1. 🔴 Fibrous septa bridging portal tracts to each other and to central veins; contain ductular reaction + inflammatory cells + proliferating vessels (shunts).
  2. 🔴 Regenerative nodules — hepatocytes in plates >2 cells thick; nodules LACK normal central veins and intact portal triads (architectural chaos).
  3. Sinusoidal capillarization — collagen deposition in space of Disse; loss of endothelial fenestrations.
  4. Ductular reaction at nodule-septa interface.
  5. 🔴 Etiologic fingerprints: Mallory-Denk hyaline + neutrophils (alcohol); ground-glass hepatocytes (HBV); lymphoid follicles + steatosis (HCV); iron (hemochromatosis, Prussian blue); copper/rhodanine (Wilson); PAS-positive diastase-resistant globules (α1AT); florid duct lesion (PBC)/onion-skin fibrosis (PSC).
  6. Late: hepatocyte dropout, cholestasis, features of decompensation (bile plugs, feathery degeneration).
🎯 "Gross = shrunken hobnail ball; Micro = SEPTA bridge + NODULES regenerate + plates >2-thick + no central veins inside nodules + hunt for the CAUSE (Mallory/ground-glass/iron/copper)."

Q12. What do you mean by acute liver failure? What are the causes of acute liver failure?

Definition

🔴 Acute liver failure (fulminant hepatic failure) = severe acute liver injury associated with encephalopathy and impaired synthetic function (INR ≥1.5) occurring WITHOUT pre-existing chronic liver disease, within 26 weeks of the initial onset of symptoms. Pathologically there is 🔴 massive/submassive hepatic necrosis; mortality high without transplantation.

Morphology of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

Epidemiology/context: 80–85% arise in cirrhotic liver; major risk factors = chronic HBV/HCV, alcohol, aflatoxin (Aspergillus), hemochromatosis, α1AT deficiency, NAFLD; male predominance up to 8:1 in endemic areas; molecular drivers include TERT promoter mutation, β-catenin activation, TP53 loss; HBx protein implicated in HBV-related carcinogenesis.

Gross morphology

  • Patterns: 🔴 (1) single large mass, (2) multifocal multinodular growth (often intrahepatic metastases), (3) diffuse infiltrative.
  • Cut surface: soft, pale yellow-white (fat) or GREEN tinted (bile production); hemorrhage/necrosis common; ± capsule.
  • 🔴 Vascular spread characteristic — invades portal/hepatic veins → IVC → right atrium; intrahepatic portal vein seeding → multifocal tumors; late lung metastasis.

Microscopic morphology

  • 🔴 Malignant hepatocytes arranged in thick trabeculae (>3–4 cells wide), pseudoglandular/acinar structures containing bile plugs, or solid sheets.
  • Cells resemble hepatocytes: polygonal, granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, round nuclei with prominent nucleoli; variable pleomorphism, giant cells; lipid/bile/Mallory bodies.
  • Sinusoid-like blood spaces surround trabeculae; reticulin framework lost.
  • 🔴 Fibrolamellar variant: young patients WITHOUT cirrhosis; large polygonal oncocytic cells (granular mitochondria-rich cytoplasm), vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli, separated by parallel lamellae of dense collagen; AFP NOT elevated; better prognosis.

Diagnosis pearls: raised AFP (advanced disease; insensitive early; negative in fibrolamellar); multiphasic CT/MRI shows 🔴 arterial-phase enhancement with rapid venous washout — diagnostic without biopsy in cirrhotics; USG surveillance of high-risk patients.

🎯 "Liver tumors: metastasis #1 overall, HCC #1 primary; HCC picture = cirrhotic man + green-yellow trabecular mass eating portal vein + AFP↑ + washout on CT; young noncirrhotic AFP-negative = fibrolamellar."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405
Q14. What is hepatorenal syndrome? What is hepatopulmonary syndrome?

Hepatorenal syndrome (HRS)

🔴 HRS = potentially reversible FUNCTIONAL renal failure occurring in advanced liver disease (cirrhosis/acute liver failure) with portal hypertension, characterized by intense renal vasoconstriction, oliguria, rising creatinine, in the ABSENCE of intrinsic renal pathology (normal tubules, no proteinuria/hematuria) or other cause of renal failure.

  • Pathogenesis: splanchnic vasodilation → effective arterial underfilling → RAAS/sympathetic activation + renal vasoconstrictors (endothelin) unopposed by reduced renal vasodilators (NO/prostaglandins) → falling GFR.
  • Types: Type 1 — rapid decline (triggered by SBP/variceal bleed; high short-term mortality); Type 2 — slowly progressive, refractory ascites.
  • Kidneys recover after successful liver transplant (proves functional nature).

Hepatopulmonary syndrome (HPS)

🔴 HPS = triad of (1) liver disease with portal hypertension, (2) arterial deoxygenation (PaO₂ <80 mmHg or elevated alveolar-arterial gradient), and (3) intrapulmonary vascular dilation/shunting (pulmonary arteriovenous communications).

  • Mechanism: failure of the diseased liver to clear pulmonary vasodilators (NO) → dilation/pre-capillary shunts → perfusion of poorly ventilated alveoli → hypoxemia.
  • Clinical: 🔴 platypnea (worse dyspnea upright) and orthodeoxia (desaturation standing), digital clubbing, cyanosis, spider naevi.
  • Resolves after liver transplantation.

        ↓
🔴 Gut-derived nitrogenous toxins — chiefly AMMONIA
   (colonic bacteria + glutaminase split urea/glutamine → NH₃)
        ↓
Ammonia escapes hepatic urea-cycle detoxification
        ↓
Enters systemic circulation → crosses blood-brain barrier
        ↓
Astrocyte ammonia metabolism → glutamine accumulation
        ↓
🔴 Astrocyte swelling (Alzheimer type II astrocytes) + low-grade cerebral edema
        ↓
Disturbed neurotransmission:
   • ↑ GABAergic tone (increased inhibitory transmission)
   • False neurotransmitters (octopamine, phenylethylamines)
   • Increased serotonin, altered dopaminergic signaling
        ↓
Neuronal inhibition dominates → confusion → asterixis → COMA

Aggravating factors: GIT hemorrhage (blood = protein load), constipation, high-protein diet, hypokalemic alkalosis (diuretics), infection, sedatives. Other contributing toxins: manganese deposition (basal ganglia), mercaptans, short/medium-chain fatty acids.

Pathogenesis of coagulopathy in liver failure

Massive hepatocyte loss
        ↓
🔴 Failure of synthesis of clotting proteins
   (fibrinogen, prothrombin II, V, VII, IX, X — all except factor VIII)
        ↓
Prolonged PT/INR (factor VII shortest half-life falls FIRST)
        +
Thrombocytopenia (hypersplenism in portal HTN + ↓ hepatic thrombopoietin)
        +
Impaired clearance of fibrinolytic activators + DIC-like consumption
        +
Cholestasis → bile salt lack → vitamin K malabsorption
        ↓
🔴 BLEEDING DIATHESIS (purpura, ecchymoses, variceal and mucosal bleeds)

📌 Vitamin K corrects coagulopathy only when cholestatic malabsorption dominates; in pure synthetic failure it does NOT — a favorite viva distinction.

Q17. Problem-based question: A male of 50 years has presented with weakness, tiredness and hepatomegaly for few months. He has got history of hepatitis B infection few years back. USG revealed Cirrhosis with a SOL in the right lobe of liver. (a) What is your diagnosis? (b) How will you proceed to confirm the diagnosis?

(a) Diagnosis

🔴 Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) arising in chronic hepatitis B-related cirrhosis.

Supporting points:

  • Chronic HBV infection — strongest global risk factor for HCC (HBV integration + HBx transactivator; even without cirrhosis).
  • Cirrhosis present — 80–85% of HCCs arise in cirrhotic liver.
  • Middle-aged male with constitutional symptoms (weakness/tiredness) + hepatomegaly — typical HCC presentation in endemic regions.
  • SOL in right lobe on USG in this setting = HCC until proven otherwise.
  • Differential diagnoses to mention: metastatic deposit, cholangiocarcinoma, hemangioma/focal nodular hyperplasia, hepatic abscess, regenerative/dysplastic nodule.

(b) Confirmation plan

StepInvestigationExpected findings
1. SerologyHBsAg, anti-HCV, HBV DNA PCRConfirms chronic HBV as underlying disease
2. Tumor marker🔴 Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)Elevated (>400 ng/mL strongly suggestive; normal does not exclude — negative in fibrolamellar)
3. 🔴 Dynamic imagingTriphasic (multiphasic contrast) CT scan / MRI of liver🔴 Arterial phase hyperenhancement with rapid venous/delayed washout — diagnostic of HCC in a cirrhotic liver without biopsy (LI-RADS criteria); assesses number, size, vascular invasion
4. Biochemical assessmentLFTs, PT/INR, albumin, bilirubin, creatinine; Child-Pugh/MELD scoreFunctional reserve — guides resection vs transplant
5. 🔴 Histology (if imaging atypical/non-LIRADS-5)USG-guided core needle biopsyThick trabeculae/pseudoglandular malignant hepatocytes, bile plugs, sinusoidal invasion; IHC: HepPar-1, glypican-3, arginase-1 positive
6. Staging workupChest X-ray/CT (lung mets), portal vein patency (Doppler)Extrahepatic spread, portal vein thrombus

📌 Note: biopsy avoided if lesion meets radiologic criteria (risk of needle-track seeding/bleeding in vascular tumor).

🎯 "PBQ recipe: HBV + cirrhosis + SOL = HCC until proven otherwise; confirm with AFP + triple-phase CT (washout!) + biopsy only if atypical — 'marker, scanner, needler'."
