## Q1. Classify the diseases of Blood Vessels. What is Berry aneurysm?

### Classification of diseases of blood vessels

| Group | Diseases |
|---|---|
| **1. Hypertensive vascular disease** | **Hyaline arteriolosclerosis** (benign HTN + diabetes), **Hyperplastic arteriolosclerosis** ("onion-skin", malignant HTN) |
| **2. Arteriosclerosis** | Atherosclerosis, arteriolosclerosis, Mönckeberg medial calcific sclerosis, fibromuscular intimal hyperplasia |
| **3. Aneurysms & dissection** | True aneurysm (**atherosclerotic AAA**, syphilitic, **berry**, mycotic), false (pseudo-) aneurysm, **aortic dissection** |
| **4. Vasculitis (arteritis)** | Large-vessel (**giant cell/temporal, Takayasu**), medium-vessel (**PAN, Kawasaki, Buerger**), small-vessel (**microscopic polyangiitis, GPA/Wegener, Churg-Strauss, Henoch-Schönlein IgA**, leukocytoclastic), infectious vasculitis |
| **5. Disorders of vessel hyperreactivity** | Raynaud phenomenon (primary/secondary), coronary vasospasm |
| **6. Diseases of veins & lymphatics** | Varicose veins, esophageal varices, hemorrhoids, **DVT/thrombophlebitis**, SVC/IVC syndrome, lymphangitis, lymphedema |
| **7. Vascular tumours** | Benign (**hemangioma**, lymphangioma, glomus tumour), intermediate (**Kaposi sarcoma**, hemangioendothelioma), malignant (**angiosarcoma**) |
| **8. Pathology of vascular intervention** | Stent restenosis, vein-graft atherosclerosis |

### 🔴 Berry aneurysm :

- **Definition:** 🔴 **a small (up to 1.5 cm), saccular, thin-walled congenital aneurysm occurring at branching points of the cerebral arteries of the Circle of Willis**, due to a developmental (congenital) defect/focal absence of the **tunica media (smooth muscle)** at the bifurcation.
- 📌 **Most common site: anterior communicating artery** (also middle cerebral and internal carotid bifurcation); **multiple in ~20–30%**.
- **Associations:** adult polycystic kidney disease (ADPKD), hypertension, cigarette smoking, Ehlers-Danlos syndrome, coarctation of aorta, family history.
- **Clinical importance:** silent until **rupture → subarachnoid haemorrhage (SAH)** — sudden "worst headache of life", meningism, unconsciousness; may compress cranial nerves before rupture (III nerve palsy).

```text
Congenital medial defect at arterial fork + HTN/shear stress → gradual outpouching
→ thin-walled saccular "berry" bulge → rupture → subarachnoid haemorrhage
```

> 🎯 **Berry = Birth defect at Branching points of Brain arteries → Bleeds (SAH).**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44

## Q2. Define and classify arteriosclerosis. Name of Congenital anomalies of Blood Vessel.

### Definition

🔴 **Arteriosclerosis** literally means **"hardening of the arteries"** — a generic term for **thickening and loss of elasticity of arterial walls**. Three principal patterns are recognised; a fourth (fibromuscular intimal hyperplasia) completes the group.

### Classification

| Type | Vessel affected | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Arteriolosclerosis** | Small arteries & arterioles | (a) **Hyaline** — homogeneous pink hyaline wall thickening; **benign hypertension, diabetes, aging** → narrow lumen, ischemic glomeruli. (b) **Hyperplastic** — concentric laminated **"onion-skin"** smooth muscle + reduplicated basement membrane ± **fibrinoid necrosis**; **malignant hypertension** |
| **2. Mönckeberg medial calcific sclerosis** | Muscular arteries of extremities >50 yr | Calcific deposits along the **internal elastic lamina/media**; 🔴 **does NOT narrow the lumen — clinically insignificant** (found on X-ray as "pipestem" calcification) |
| **3. Atherosclerosis** | Intima of elastic (aorta) + large/medium muscular arteries | Fibrofatty plaques; the most important pattern (see Q4–Q8) |
| **4. Fibromuscular intimal hyperplasia** | Muscular arteries & arterioles | Healing response to endothelial/catheter/stent injury; SMC + ECM proliferation → **in-stent restenosis, transplant arteriopathy** |

### Congenital anomalies of blood vessels

1. **Berry (congenital saccular) aneurysm** of Circle of Willis
2. **Arteriovenous fistula/communication** (congenital AV malformation)
3. **Coarctation of the aorta** (preductal/postductal)
4. **Anomalous origin of coronary artery** (e.g., LCA from pulmonary trunk)
5. **Aberrant right subclavian artery** (retro-esophageal)
6. **Fibromuscular dysplasia** (renal/carotid artery — "string of beads")
7. **Persistent truncus / vascular rings** encircling trachea-oesophagus
8. **Congenital lymphatic anomalies** (Milroy disease — hereditary lymphedema)

> 🎯 **Arteriosclerosis = "AAA-M": Arteriolosclerosis, Atherosclerosis, Mönckeberg (+ medial hyperplasia); Hyaline = Harmless-ish HTN, Onion = Ominous.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44

## Q3. Classify vascular tumours. Give the definition and classification of aneurysm.

### Classification of vascular tumours (all endothelial in origin; markers **CD31, CD34, von Willebrand factor**)

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Benign** | **Capillary hemangioma** (most common), juvenile/"strawberry" naevus (regresses by 7 yr), **cavernous hemangioma** (liver; von Hippel-Lindau), pyogenic granuloma (granuloma gravidarum in pregnancy), **lymphangioma/cystic hygroma** (Turner syndrome), **glomus tumour** (exquisitely painful, subungual), bacillary angiomatosis (Bartonella + HIV, Warthin-Starry stain) |
| **Malformations (non-neoplastic ectasias)** | Nevus flammeus/"port-wine stain" (Sturge-Weber syndrome), spider telangiectasia (hyperestrogenic states), Osler-Weber-Rendu hereditary hemorrhagic telangiectasia |
| **Intermediate (locally aggressive/low grade)** | **Kaposi sarcoma (HHV8)** — classic/endemic African/transplant-associated/AIDS-related; hemangioendothelioma |
| **Malignant** | **Angiosarcoma** (skin, breast, liver; associations: post-mastectomy lymphedema/Stewart-Treves, radiation, arsenic, Thorotrast, PVC); hemangiopericytoma (pericytic origin) |

### Definition of aneurysm

🔴 **Aneurysm = a localized abnormal permanent dilation of a blood vessel (or of the heart) to more than 1.5 times its normal diameter**, produced by structural weakening of the vessel wall.

### Classification of aneurysm

**A. According to wall composition (pathologic type):**

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **True aneurysm** | Wall contains **all 3 layers (intima, media, adventitia)** of an attenuated but intact vessel — atherosclerotic, syphilitic, congenital (berry), mycotic, post-stenotic aneurysms |
| **False (pseudo-) aneurysm** | Defect in the wall with **extravascular hematoma communicating with the lumen** — "pulsating hematoma"; e.g. post-angiography leak, contained ventricular rupture |
| **Dissecting aneurysm (dissection)** | Blood enters through an **intimal tear** and splits (dissects) the medial lamellae creating a blood-filled channel within the wall itself |

**B. According to shape:** **Saccular** (outpouching of one side, part of circumference — berry aneurysm) and **Fusiform** (circumferential, spindle-shaped, long segment — typical AAA).

**C. According to cause/etiology:** (1) **Atherosclerotic** — abdominal aorta, most common; (2) **Syphilitic** — ascending aorta (tertiary syphilis, vasa vasorum endarteritis); (3) **Congenital/berry**; (4) **Mycotic** (infected, e.g. bacterial endocarditis emboli); (5) **Dissecting** (hypertension, Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos); (6) Traumatic; (7) Post-stenotic and inflammatory (Takayasu, PAN, Kawasaki) aneurysms.

> 🎯 **"True keeps its coat, False loses the boat, Dissection cuts inside" — Saccular = Sac-side, Fusiform = Full-circle.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44

## Q4. Definition of Atherosclerosis, Atheroma/Atheromatous plaque. What is acute plaque change?

### Definitions

- 🔴 **Atherosclerosis:** a **chronic inflammatory disease of the intima of large and medium-sized arteries**, characterized by accumulation of lipids, smooth muscle cells and extracellular matrix forming **fibrofatty plaques (atheromas)** that narrow the lumen and weaken the underlying media. It is the **single most common cause of death worldwide** (via MI and stroke).
- 🔴 **Atheroma / Atheromatous (fibrofatty) plaque:** the characteristic lesion of atherosclerosis — a **raised, eccentric, white-to-yellow intimal lesion consisting of a central soft grumous yellow lipid core (cholesterol + cholesterol esters + debris) covered by a white firm fibrous cap** of smooth muscle cells and collagen.
- **Fatty streak:** the earliest lesion — flat/intimal yellow streak of **lipid-filled foam cells**; appears in children; precursor (not all progress) of plaques.

### Four components of an atheromatous plaque (histology)

1. **Cells** — smooth muscle cells, macrophages (foam cells), T lymphocytes.
2. **Extracellular matrix** — collagen, elastin, proteoglycans (fibrous cap).
3. **Lipids** — intracellular (foam cells) + extracellular **cholesterol clefts** (needle-shaped spaces after processing) in the necrotic core.
4. **Calcification**, plus **neovascularization** and surface thrombus in complicated lesions.

### 🔴 Acute plaque change (acute plaque disruption)

📌 **Definition:** sudden, unpredictable transformation of a stable plaque that precipitates an acute coronary event —

| Mechanism | Result |
|---|---|
| **Plaque rupture/fissuring** (thin fibrous cap tears) | Exposes highly **thrombogenic lipid core + collagen** → platelet-fibrin **occlusive thrombus** → **STEMI, unstable angina, stroke** |
| **Plaque erosion/ulceration** (endothelium denuded over plaque) | Subendothelial basement membrane exposed → less often occlusive thrombosis |
| **Intraplaque hemorrhage** (rupture of plaque neovessels) | Sudden plaque expansion → luminal compromise |

- 📌 Occurs in **"vulnerable/unstable plaques":** **large lipid core (>40%), thin fibrous cap (<65 µm), dense inflammation** (macrophages ↑MMPs, few SMCs) — often **<70% stenotic** before rupture (missed by angiography).
- **Triggers:** adrenergic surges (morning 6 AM–noon peak of MI), emotional stress, exercise, vasoconstriction (↓NO, ↑endothelin, cocaine), circadian platelet aggregability.

> 🎯 **"Thin cap, big fat core, angry macrophages = the ticking bomb; rupture → thrombus → MI."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44

## Q5. What are the common sites of atheroma. Describe the morphology of an atheromatous plaque.

### 🔴 Common sites of atheroma (in descending order of severity)

| Rank | Site |
|---|---|
| 1 | **Lower abdominal aorta** (worst; below renal arteries) + iliac arteries |
| 2 | **Coronary arteries** (proximal LAD > RCA > LCX) |
| 3 | **Popliteal arteries** (lower limbs) |
| 4 | **Internal carotid artery** (carotid sinus) |
| 5 | **Circle of Willis** (cerebral arteries) |
| Others | Renal artery **ostia**, mesenteric arteries, femoral; spares upper limbs, mesenteric/renal trunks (except ostia) |
| Pulmonary artery | Only in pulmonary hypertension/congenital shunts |

📌 Lesions are **focal and eccentric**, preferentially at **ostia, branch points and posterior wall of abdominal aorta** — areas of **turbulent/non-laminar flow** (laminar flow is atheroprotective via KLF2 transcription factor).

### Morphology of atheromatous plaque

**Gross (naked eye):**
- **Fatty streak** — flat or slightly raised, ≤1 mm wide yellow intimal streak (aortic arch, coronaries even in children).
- **Atheromatous plaque** — **raised, oval, white-white/yellow-white eccentric patch**, 0.3–1.5 cm, soft yellow core beneath a firm white fibrous cap; cut surface shows **yellow pultaceous (grumous) lipid material**; advanced lesions show ulceration, calcified grittiness, superimposed mural thrombus ("complicated plaque").

**Microscopic:**
1. **Fibrous cap** — inner luminal side: **smooth muscle cells + dense collagen + elastin + proteoglycans**, overlaid by endothelium.
2. **Necrotic lipid core** — deep side: **extracellular cholesterol crystals (cholesterol clefts), foam cells, cellular debris, fibrin**, surrounding dead cells.
3. **Cellular zone between the two** — **foam cells (macrophages + some SMCs laden with lipid via scavenger receptors)**, T lymphocytes, proliferating SMCs.
4. **Adventitia** — **neovascularization**, lymphocytes/macrophages; late **dystrophic calcification**; thinning/atrophy of underlying media; complicated lesions carry organizing thrombus.

**Progression:** plaques grow **outward (positive remodeling)** preserving the lumen until ~40% area stenosis; beyond this, lumen narrows progressively.

> 🎯 **Sites: "Abdomen → Coronary → Popliteal → Carotid → Willis" (bottom-up); Plaque = White cap over Yellow core, sitting at every Fork.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44

## Q6. Mention the risk factors of atherosclerosis. Short note on lipid profile

### 🔴 Risk factors of atherosclerosis

**A. Constitutional (non-modifiable):**

| Factor | Comment |
|---|---|
| **Family history** | 🔴 **Most important independent risk factor**; polygenic (+ rare Mendelian forms e.g. familial hypercholesterolemia) |
| **Age** | Progressive rise; MI incidence ×5 between 40–60 yr |
| **Male sex** | Premenopausal females protected by estrogen; after menopause risk equalises |
| Genetic abnormalities | e.g. LDL receptor mutations, ApoE4 allele |

**B. Modifiable (major, "big four"):**

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Hyperlipidemia (↑LDL)** | 🔴 Necessary initiator; statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) reduce events |
| **Hypertension** | ↑Ischemic heart disease risk ~60% |
| **Cigarette smoking** | **Doubles** IHD death rate (one pack/day ×2–3 risk) |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | MI ×2; **×100 risk of lower-limb gangrene** |

**C. Additional (lesser/unconfirmed) risk factors:** **C-reactive protein (hs-CRP)** elevation (inflammation marker), **hyperhomocysteinemia**, **metabolic syndrome** (obesity + insulin resistance + dyslipidemia + HTN), **lipoprotein(a)**, ↑PAI-1/fibrinogen (prothrombotic state), obesity, physical inactivity, stress/type-A personality, oral contraceptives + smoking, trans-fat diet, alcohol excess, **CHIP clonal hematopoiesis (TET2 mutation)** in elderly.

### Short note on Lipid Profile

- **Definition:** 🔴 **Lipid profile is a panel of fasting blood tests measuring plasma cholesterol fractions and triglycerides used to screen dyslipidemia and assess cardiovascular risk** (sample: venous blood after **9–12 hr overnight fast**; avoid alcohol/exercise 24 hr before).
- **Components:** total cholesterol, LDL-cholesterol, HDL-cholesterol, triglycerides, VLDL (= TG/5), non-HDL-C (= TC − HDL), TC:HDL ratio; optional — apolipoproteins B & A-1, lipoprotein(a).
- **Interpretation (adult, NCEP-ATP III):** desirable TC <200 mg/dL, LDL <100 mg/dL, HDL ≥40 (M)/≥50 (F) mg/dL, TG <150 mg/dL, TC:HDL <4.5. **High LDL + low HDL = highest atherogenic risk** (values detailed in Q10).
- **Uses:** screening healthy adults (every 5 yr from age 20), risk stratification of IHD patients, monitoring statin/lifestyle therapy, diagnosing primary & secondary hyperlipidemias.

> 🎯 **Risk factors: "Family-Age-Male cannot change; LDL-BP-Smoke-Diabetes must change"; Lipid report = "TC, LDL, HDL, TG — fast 12 hours to see".**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44 & Unit-II "Lipid and Cholesterol", pp. 487–492

## Q7. Describe the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis.

🔴 **Response-to-Injury Hypothesis (Ross):** atherosclerosis is a **chronic inflammatory and healing (fibroproliferative) response of the arterial wall to endothelial injury**, driven by lipids.

```text
ENDOTHELIAL INJURY / DYSFUNCTION (LDL excess, HTN, smoking toxins,
homocysteine, turbulent shear at branch points, viruses/immune injury)
        ↓
↑Endothelial permeability + expression of adhesion molecules
(VCAM-1, ICAM-1) + procoagulant surface
        ↓
LDL enters intima → becomes OXIDIZED (oxLDL) by ECs, macrophages, smoke radicals
        ↓
MONOCYTES adhere → migrate into intima → differentiate to MACROPHAGES
        ↓
Macrophages engulf oxLDL via SCAVENGER RECEPTORS (SR-A, CD36 — not regulated
by cholesterol feedback) → LIPID-FILLED FOAM CELLS  [fatty streak]
        ↓
PLATELET adhesion (focal endothelial denudation)
        ↓
Release of GROWTH FACTORS — PDGF, FGF, TGF-α (+ IFN-γ from recruited
CD4+ T cells activating macrophages/ECs)
        ↓
SMOOTH MUSCLE CELL migration (media → intima) + PROLIFERATION
+ ECM synthesis (collagen, proteoglycans)  → FIBROUS CAP formation
        ↓
Progressive INTRA- & EXTRACELLULAR LIPID ACCUMULATION → foam-cell death
→ NECROTIC LIPID CORE; CHOLESTEROL CRYSTALS activate NLRP3 INFLAMMASOME
→ IL-1β/IL-18 amplification of inflammation
        ↓
Neoangiogenesis, matrix remodeling, dystrophic CALCIFICATION
        ↓
MATURE FIBROFATTY ATHEROMA → complicated plaque (see Q8)
```

**Key supporting points (say in viva):**

- 📌 **Why focal?** Plaques localize at **branch points, ostia, posterior abdominal aorta** where flow is turbulent; **laminar flow → KLF2 transcription factor → atheroprotective gene programme** (↑NO synthase, ↓adhesion molecules). Turbulent flow reverses this; **statins partly act by raising KLF2**.
- 📌 **Foam cells** arise from **macrophages mainly + smooth muscle cells** (scavenger receptor pathway is unregulated → unlimited lipid uptake).
- 📌 **Chronicity:** repeated cycles of injury-healing-thrombosis (organizing mural thrombi incorporate into plaque) → progression over decades.
- 📌 Infection (CMV, *Chlamydophila pneumoniae*) is only circumstantially linked — **no proven causal role**.

> 🎯 **"Injure the pipe, let LDL in, eat by scavenger, call PDGF, grow the cap, crystallize the fire (NLRP3), calcify the tomb."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44

## Q8. Fates and complications of atherosclerosis.

### Fate

Plaque grows slowly by lipid accumulation + repeated organized mural thrombi → **stable fibrous plaque → complicated plaque**; many remain asymptomatic lifelong; clinical outcome depends on which artery and how fast lumen is lost.

### 🔴 Complications (with consequences)

| # | Complication | Mechanism → Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Critical chronic stenosis (≥70% cross-sectional area)** | Fixed narrowing → demand-supply mismatch on exertion → **stable angina, intermittent claudication, chronic mesenteric ischemia, ischemic encephalopathy** (>90% → rest ischemia) |
| 2 | **Acute plaque change → thrombosis** (rupture/fissure/erosion of vulnerable plaque) | Exposed thrombogenic core → platelet-fibrin occlusive thrombus → 🔴 **Acute MI, unstable angina, ischemic stroke, gangrene of leg** |
| 3 | **Intraplaque hemorrhage** | Rupture of plaque neovessels → sudden plaque expansion → worsening stenosis |
| 4 | **Atheroembolism** | Fragments of plaque debris + cholesterol crystals embolize → retinal/renal microinfarcts, **"blue toe" syndrome, cholesterol crystal emboli with needle-shaped clefts in small vessels** |
| 5 | **Aneurysm formation** | Plaque-induced ischemic atrophy/destruction of media → weakened wall dilates → **AAA, rupture** |
| 6 | **Calcification** | Dystrophic → rigid brittle artery (radiologically visible) |
| 7 | **Organizing mural thrombus incorporation** | Adds bulk → progressive plaque enlargement |
| 8 | Vascular insufficiency of end-organs | Chronic ischemic nephrosclerosis, bowel ischemia, dementia (vascular) |

📌 **Vulnerable (unstable) plaque = thin fibrous cap + large lipid core (>40%) + heavy inflammatory infiltrate + few SMCs** — ruptures even when stenosis is <70%.

> 🎯 **Complication chain: "Narrow (angina) → Burst (thrombus-MI) → Bleed in (hemorrhage) → Fly off (emboli) → Balloon out (aneurysm) → Stone over (calcification)."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44

## Q9. Classify the types of vasculitis. What is aortic dissection?

### 🔴 Classification of vasculitis

**A. By calibre of vessel involved (Chapel Hill/Robbins):**

| Calibre | Entities |
|---|---|
| **Large-vessel** | **Giant cell (temporal) arteritis** (>50 yr), **Takayasu arteritis** (<50 yr, "pulseless disease") |
| **Medium-vessel** | **Polyarteritis nodosa (PAN)**, **Kawasaki disease** (childhood, coronary), **Thromboangiitis obliterans (Buerger disease)** (young male smokers) |
| **Small-vessel** | **ANCA-associated:** microscopic polyangiitis (MPO-ANCA), **granulomatosis with polyangiitis/GPA-Wegener (PR3-ANCA)**, eosinophilic granulomatosis with polyangiitis (Churg-Strauss). **Immune-complex:** Henoch-Schönlein purpura (IgA), cryoglobulinemic vasculitis, lupus vasculitis, serum-sickness/drug-induced, leukocytoclastic cutaneous vasculitis |
| Variable-size | Behçet disease, infectious vasculitis (Aspergillus, Mucor, bacteria — direct invasion) |

**B. By pathogenic mechanism:** (1) **Immune-complex deposition** (HBsAg-antiHBs in ~30% PAN, DNA-antiDNA in SLE, IgA in HSP, drug haptens); (2) **Anti-neutrophil cytoplasmic antibodies (ANCAs)** — c-ANCA/PR3 → GPA; p-ANCA/MPO → microscopic polyangiitis & Churg-Strauss (pauci-immune lesions); (3) **Anti-endothelial cell antibodies** (Kawasaki); (4) **Autoreactive T-cell mediated** (giant cell & Takayasu arteritis); (5) **Direct microbial invasion** (needs antimicrobial, not steroid!).

### 🔴 Aortic dissection

- **Definition:** 🔴 **a catastrophic condition in which blood enters the aortic media through an intimal tear and splits (dissects) the laminar planes of the tunica media, forming a blood-filled channel within the aortic wall** (sometimes called dissecting aneurysm).
- **Risk groups:** men 40–60 yr with **hypertension (>90% cases)**; younger patients with **Marfan/Ehlers-Danlos/Loeys-Dietz**, pregnancy (3rd trimester), iatrogenic trauma. (Unusual in severe atherosclerosis/syphilis — medial fibrosis blocks propagation.)
- **Morphology:** longitudinal intimal tear usually in **ascending aorta within 10 cm of the aortic valve**; channel propagates distally in outer third of media; may re-enter lumen ("double-barreled aorta") or rupture externally.
- **Classification (Stanford):** 🔴 **Type A — involves ascending aorta (surgical emergency); Type B — distal to left subclavian (medical management).**
- **Clinical:** sudden excruciating **tearing chest pain radiating to the back between scapulae**, migrating downward; BP differs between arms; mimics MI.
- **Complications:** rupture → **haemopericardium/cardiac tamponade**, hemothorax; aortic insufficiency; occlusion of coronary (MI), carotid (stroke), renal/mesenteric/iliac branches (infarction).

> 🎯 **Vasculitis by size: "Large = Giant-Taka, Medium = PAN-Kawasaki-Buerger, Small = ANCA trio + IgA"; Dissection = "Type A Ascending = knife; Type B Below = pills."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "Blood Vessels", pp. 21–44

## Q10. Cause of Hyperlipidemia. Mention the constituents/components of Lipid profile with their normal values?

### 🔴 Causes of hyperlipidemia

**A. Primary (idiopathic/genetic):**

| Disorder | Defect |
|---|---|
| **Familial hypercholesterolemia** | LDL-receptor gene mutation (↑↑LDL; xanthomas, early CAD) |
| Familial defective ApoB-100 | ApoB ligand defect |
| **Familial combined hyperlipidemia** | ↑LDL ± ↑TG (multigene) |
| Familial dysbetalipoproteinemia (type III) | ApoE2/E2 homozygosity → ↑IDL, β-migrating VLDL |
| Lipoprotein lipase / ApoC-II deficiency (type I) | ↑↑Chylomicrons, eruptive xanthoma |
| Polygenic hypercholesterolemia; sporadic hypertriglyceridemia | Multifactorial |

**B. Secondary (underlying disease/drug):**

1. **Diabetes mellitus** (poor control → ↑TG/VLDL)
2. **Hypothyroidism** (↑LDL/cholesterol)
3. **Nephrotic syndrome** (↑LDL — lost oncotic pressure drives hepatic lipoprotein synthesis)
4. **Chronic alcoholism / liver disease** (↑TG)
5. **Obesity, high saturated-fat diet, sedentary life**
6. **Drugs:** oral contraceptives/estrogen, thiazides, corticosteroids, β-blockers, cyclosporine, protease inhibitors
7. Chronic kidney disease/uremia, biliary obstruction, Cushing syndrome, acromegaly, pregnancy

### 🔴 Constituents/components of lipid profile with normal values

| Component | Normal value (adult, fasting) | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Total cholesterol (TC)** | 🔴 **<200 mg/dL** (desirable) | 200–239 borderline; ≥240 high |
| **LDL-cholesterol ("bad")** | 🔴 **<100 mg/dL** (optimal) | 100–129 near-optimal; 130–159 borderline; ≥160 high |
| **HDL-cholesterol ("good")** | 🔴 **≥40 mg/dL (male), ≥50 mg/dL (female)**; ≥60 protective | Low = major CAD risk factor |
| **Triglycerides (TG)** | 🔴 **<150 mg/dL** | 150–199 borderline; 200–499 high; ≥500 very high (pancreatitis risk) |
| **VLDL-cholesterol** | 5–40 mg/dL (calculated ≈ TG ÷ 5) | Reflects endogenous TG transport |
| **Non-HDL cholesterol (TC−HDL)** | <130 mg/dL | Secondary target in hypertriglyceridemia/diabetes |
| **TC : HDL ratio** | <4.5 (<3.5 ideal) | Stronger predictor than TC alone |
| **Lipoprotein(a)** | <30 mg/dL | Independent genetic risk factor |
| **Apolipoprotein B-100** | <100 mg/dL (optimal <90) | Counts atherogenic particles |
| **Apolipoprotein A-I** | Male 110–180, Female 120–210 mg/dL | Structural protein of HDL |

📌 **Sample requirements:** 9–12 hr overnight fast (water allowed), no alcohol/exercise for 24 hr, sit 5 min before draw; EDTA plasma preferred for Lp(a). Report interpreted with risk category (diabetes, existing CAD set lower LDL goals <70 mg/dL).

> 🎯 **Causes: "Diabetics, Thyroids, Kidneys, Drinkers get Fat lipids"; Numbers: "200-100-40-150" = TC, LDL, HDL(M), TG.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II "Lipid and Cholesterol", pp. 487–492

## Q11. What is Lipoprotein? Classify lipoproteins. What is good cholesterol and bad cholesterol?

### Definition

🔴 **Lipoproteins are spherical macromolecular complexes of lipids (triglyceride + cholesteryl ester core) and proteins (apolipoproteins) with a polar surface coat of phospholipid, free cholesterol and apolipoproteins, synthesized to solubilize and transport water-insoluble lipids in plasma.**

- **Core (hydrophobic):** triglycerides + cholesteryl esters.
- **Shell (amphipathic):** free cholesterol + phospholipid + **apoproteins** (ligands for receptors + enzyme cofactors, e.g. ApoB-100, ApoA-I, ApoC-II, ApoE).

### Classification (by hydrated density on ultracentrifugation — inverse of size)

| Class | Density (g/mL) | Size (Å) | Main lipid carried | Apoproteins | Source → Destination / Function |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

(On electrophoresis: chylomicrons stay at origin; α-lipoprotein = HDL, pre-β = VLDL, β = LDL.)

### 🔴 Good cholesterol and bad cholesterol

- 🔴 **Good cholesterol = HDL-cholesterol** — transports cholesterol **away** from tissues/arteries back to the liver (reverse cholesterol transport) → **anti-atherogenic, protective**. Higher HDL = lower CAD risk.
- 🔴 **Bad cholesterol = LDL-cholesterol** — carries most plasma cholesterol **to** peripheral tissues including the arterial intima; penetrates injured endothelium, gets oxidized and deposited in plaques → **atherogenic**. Higher LDL = higher CAD risk. (VLDL, IDL and Lp(a) are also considered atherogenic/bad.)

> 🎯 **"HDL Hauls cholesterol Home (good); LDL Leaves it in Lumen walls (bad)."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II "Lipid and Cholesterol", pp. 487–492

## Q12. Why HDL is good cholesterol & Why LDL is bad cholesterol?

### 🔴 Why HDL is GOOD cholesterol

1. **Reverse cholesterol transport (the key job):**

```text
Peripheral tissue/foam-cell free cholesterol
   → ABCA1 transporter → accepted by nascent ApoA-I discoidal HDL
   → LCAT esterifies cholesterol → cholesteryl ester moves to core → mature HDL3 → HDL2
   → hepatic uptake via SR-B1 receptor (± CETP exchange to VLDL/LDL)
   → excreted in bile  ⇒ NET REMOVAL of cholesterol FROM artery wall
```

2. **Additional protective actions:** anti-inflammatory (inhibits VCAM-1/ICAM-1 expression on endothelium), antioxidant — enzyme **PON1 (paraoxonase)** destroys oxidized lipids of LDL, antithrombotic (inhibits platelet aggregation, stimulates NO/prostacyclin), promotes endothelial repair.
3. 📌 **Clinical proof:** HDL ≥60 mg/dL is a negative risk factor; low HDL predicts CAD independently; estrogen raises HDL (premenopausal protection); exercise, weight loss, niacin, gemfibrozil raise HDL.

### 🔴 Why LDL is BAD cholesterol

1. **It delivers cholesterol INTO the arterial wall:**

```text
↑LDL (esp. small-dense LDL) → crosses injured/dysfunctional endothelium
→ trapped in intima → OXIDIZED to oxLDL (ECs, macrophage lipoxygenase, smoking radicals)
→ oxLDL is cytotoxic to endothelium, chemoattractant to monocytes, inhibits NO (vasospasm)
→ engulfed UNREGULATED by macrophage SCAVENGER RECEPTORS (SR-A, CD36)
→ FOAM CELLS → fatty streak → atheroma (see Q7)
```

2. **Unregulated uptake:** unlike the LDL-receptor pathway, scavenger-receptor uptake has **no feedback inhibition** → unlimited lipid loading → foam-cell necrosis → necrotic lipid core.
3. 📌 **Clinical proof:** LDL <100 mg/dL desirable (<70 in diabetics/CAD); every 30 mg/dL fall in LDL cuts CAD risk ~30%; **statins work chiefly by lowering LDL** (HMG-CoA reductase inhibition + ↑hepatic LDL receptors); familial hypercholesterolemia (no LDL receptors) → MI in 2nd decade.

> 🎯 **"HDL = garbage truck (takes cholesterol OUT); LDL = delivery van (dumps cholesterol IN the wall) — and oxidized LDL is the arsonist."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II "Lipid and Cholesterol", pp. 487–492

## Q13. Difference between LDL & HDL.

| Feature | **LDL** | **HDL** |
|---|---|---|
| **Full form** | Low-density lipoprotein | High-density lipoprotein |
| **Density** | 1.019–1.063 g/mL | 1.063–1.21 g/mL (densest) |
| **Size** | Larger (~22 nm) | 🔴 Smallest lipoprotein (~8–11 nm) |
| **Origin** | Hepatic catabolism of VLDL → IDL | Directly secreted by **liver & intestine** (nascent discoidal) |
| **Main apoprotein** | 🔴 **ApoB-100** (single) | 🔴 **ApoA-I, ApoA-II** |
| **Electrophoresis** | β-position | α₁-position |
| **Major cargo** | **Cholesteryl esters (~60–70%)** | Protein-rich; collects cholesterol |
| **Receptor** | LDL receptor (apoB/E receptor) — regulated pathway | **SR-B1** scavenger receptor (hepatic) |
| **Direction of cholesterol transport** | 🔴 **Forward: liver → peripheral tissues/intima** | 🔴 **Reverse: tissues/arteries → liver → bile** |
| **Effect on artery** | **Atherogenic** — deposits cholesterol, forms foam cells | **Anti-atherogenic** — removes cholesterol; PON1 antioxidant; anti-inflammatory |
| **Nickname** | 🔴 **Bad cholesterol / "Lethal-Lousy"** | 🔴 **Good cholesterol / "Healthy"** |
| **Normal fasting level** | <100 mg/dL | ≥40 (M) / ≥50 (F) mg/dL; ≥60 protective |
| **When increased** | ↑CAD, MI, stroke, xanthoma risk | Protective against CAD |
| **Drug response** | ↓ by statins, ezetimibe, diet | ↑ by exercise, weight loss, niacin, fibrates, estrogen |
| **Genetic clue** | Absent LDL receptor (familial hypercholesterolemia) → sky-high LDL | ApoA-I variants (Tangier disease, ABCA1 defect) → almost no HDL, early CAD |

> 🎯 **"LDL = Lousy Delivery to Lumen; HDL = Healthy Drain back Home" — one floods the pipe, one cleans it.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 11 (Blood Vessels), pp. 491–526 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-II "Lipid and Cholesterol", pp. 487–492
