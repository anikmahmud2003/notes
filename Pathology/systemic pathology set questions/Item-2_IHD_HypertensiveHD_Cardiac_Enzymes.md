<summary><b>Q1. Define and classify Ischemic Heart Disease (IHD)/Coronary artery disease.</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Ischemic heart disease (IHD) is a group of related clinical syndromes resulting from myocardial ischemia — an imbalance between coronary blood supply and myocardial oxygen demand**, most commonly caused by **atherosclerotic narrowing/obstruction of the coronary arteries** (hence the synonym **coronary artery disease, CAD**).

- 📌 IHD = **single largest killer worldwide (>12% of all deaths)**; critical stenosis = obstruction of **>70% of coronary cross-sectional area** (>90% → ischemia even at rest).
- **Causes of ischemia:** ↓supply (atherothrombosis, spasm, embolus, hypotension/shock, anemia) and/or ↑demand (exertion, hypertrophy, thyrotoxicosis).

### Classification

| Type | Key features |
|---|---|
| **1. Angina pectoris** | Transient ischemia without necrosis — **(a) Stable/typical** (exertional, fixed >70% stenosis), **(b) Prinzmetal/variant** (rest pain, coronary vasospasm), **(c) Unstable/crescendo** (increasing frequency/at rest; plaque fissure + mural thrombus — pre-infarction state) |
| **2. Acute myocardial infarction (MI)** | Irreversible coagulative necrosis from sudden coronary occlusion (thrombus over ruptured/eroded plaque); transmural or subendocardial |
| **3. Chronic IHD (ischemic cardiomyopathy)** | Progressive heart failure after cumulative ischemic injury/multiple remote MIs → dilated hypertrophied scarred heart |
| **4. Sudden cardiac death (SCD)** | Unexpected cardiac death within ~1 h of symptoms, usually due to lethal arrhythmia (VF) on ischemic substrate |

> 🎯 **IHD family: "Angina cries but doesn't kill cells, MI kills, Chronic cripples, SCD strikes."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q2. Risk Factors of Ischemic Heart Disease. [Same as atherosclerosis]</b></summary>

🔴 The risk factors of IHD are **the same as those of atherosclerosis** because atherosclerotic coronary narrowing is its principal cause.

### A. Constitutional (non-modifiable)

| Factor | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Age** | Risk rises steadily; 45% of MIs occur <65 yr, 10% <40 yr |
| **Sex** | Male > female before menopause; estrogen protective |
| **Family history / genetics** | 🔴 Most important independent factor; familial hypercholesterolemia, ApoE4 |
| Race | Higher in South Asians at younger age |

### B. Modifiable (major four)

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Hyperlipidemia (↑LDL, ↓HDL)** | Initiates and drives plaque growth; statins reduce events ~30% |
| **Hypertension** | ↑IHD risk ~60%; also LVH ↑demand |
| **Smoking** | Doubles IHD death rate; promotes oxidation + vasospasm + thrombosis |
| **Diabetes mellitus** | MI risk ×2; silent (painless) MI common due to autonomic neuropathy |

### C. Additional risk factors

Obesity & metabolic syndrome, physical inactivity, stress/type-A personality, **hs-CRP elevation**, hyperhomocysteinemia, **lipoprotein(a)**, prothrombotic state (↑PAI-1, fibrinogen), oral contraceptives + smoking, high saturated/trans-fat diet, alcohol excess, cocaine use (vasospasm + thrombosis).

> 🎯 **Same as atheroma: "FAMS you can't change (Family-Age-Male-Sex); LBS-D you must (Lipid-BP-Smoke-Diabetes)."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q3. Give the pathogenesis of IHD.</b></summary>

🔴 **Central mechanism: fixed or dynamic coronary obstruction creates a supply–demand mismatch → myocardial ischemia → reversible then irreversible injury.**

```text
Risk factors (LDL↑, HTN, smoking, DM, family history)
        ↓
ENDOTHELIAL INJURY / DYSFUNCTION of coronary arteries
        ↓
Atherosclerotic plaque formation (response-to-injury hypothesis)
        ↓
┌─ SLOW GROWTH → CRITICAL STENOSIS (>70% area) ──→ STABLE ANGINA (on exertion)
│
└─ ACUTE PLAQUE CHANGE (rupture/fissure/erosion of vulnerable plaque:
   thin cap + large lipid core + inflammation ± adrenergic trigger)
        ↓
Collagen/tissue-factor exposure → PLATELET adhesion–aggregation (TXA2, ADP)
        ↓
FIBRINOUS THROMBUS → partial occlusion → UNSTABLE ANGINA
                     → total occlusion → TRANSMURAL MI
        ↓
MYOCARDIAL ISCHEMIA: ATP depletion (seconds) → loss of contractility (<2 min)
→ anaerobic glycolysis (lactate, pH↓) → membrane/sarcolemmal damage
        ↓
IRREVERSIBLE INJURY = COAGULATIVE NECROSIS in 20–40 min,
starting subendocardially, spreading as WAVEFRONT to transmural (3–6 hr)
        ↓
Sarcolemmal rupture → troponin & CK-MB leak into blood (biomarkers)
        ↓
Healing: neutrophils (1–3 d) → macrophages (3–7 d) → granulation tissue
→ FIBROUS SCAR by 6–8 wk  ⇒ CHRONIC IHD / complications
```

**Non-atherosclerotic causes (~10% of MIs):** coronary vasospasm (cocaine, Prinzmetal), emboli (AF mural thrombus, infective endocarditis, paradoxical via PFO), arteritis (Kawasaki/PAN), severe anemia/hypoxemia, hypotension-shock, amyloid deposition, aortic dissection involving coronaries.
📌 **Reperfusion within ~20–30 min salvages myocardium** ("time is muscle"); late reperfusion adds hemorrhage/reperfusion injury (ROS, Ca overload).

> 🎯 **"Blocked pipe → starving pump → dead muscle in half an hour; open it fast or bury the wavefront."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q4. List the Causes of precordial Chest Pain. Describe the clinical features of MI.</b></summary>

### Causes of precordial chest pain

| System | Causes |
|---|---|
| **Cardiac** | 🔴 **Ischemic: stable/unstable angina, acute MI**; pericarditis, myocarditis, aortic dissection, mitral valve prolapse |
| **Pulmonary** | Pulmonary embolism with infarction, spontaneous pneumothorax, pneumonia/pleurisy, pulmonary hypertension |
| **Gastrointestinal** | GERD/reflux esophagitis, peptic ulcer, esophageal spasm/rupture, biliary colic/cholecystitis, acute pancreatitis |
| **Musculoskeletal** | Costochondritis (Tietze syndrome), rib fracture, intercostal muscle strain, cervical/thoracic spondylosis |
| **Neurogenic** | Herpes zoster (before rash appears!), intercostal neuralgia |
| **Psychogenic** | Anxiety/panic attack, depression, somatization |

### 🔴 Clinical features of MI

**Symptoms:**
1. 🔴 **Severe retrosternal crushing/squeezing/heavy "vice-like" chest pain lasting >30 min** (often hours).
2. **Radiation** to left arm/forearm, jaw, neck, epigastrium, back.
3. 🔴 **NOT relieved by rest or nitroglycerin** (key differentiator from angina).
4. **Autonomic features:** profuse sweating (diaphoresis), nausea, vomiting, cold clammy skin.
5. **Dyspnea** (LV failure/pulmonary congestion), profound weakness, anxiety/"feeling of impending doom".
6. **Silent (painless) MI:** elderly and 🔴 **diabetic patients** (autonomic neuropathy) — may present only with dyspnea, syncope, epigastric discomfort or confusion.

**Signs:**
- Pallor, diaphoresis, restlessness; tachycardia or bradyarrhythmia.
- Low-grade fever after 24 hr (tissue necrosis, persists ~1 week).
- Cardiac: soft S1, **S4 gallop** (stiff ischemic LV), S3 if failure, new murmur (papillary dysfunction), pericardial friction rub (day 2–4).
- Lung crackles (pulmonary edema); hypotension/cardiogenic shock in large infarcts; JVD in RV infarct.

> 🎯 **MI pain: "CRUSHED >30 min, shoots to arm-jaw, sweat + vomit, nitro NO help"; Silent MI hides in Old + Diabetes.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q5. Define and classify angina pectoris. How angina can be differentiated from MI?</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Angina pectoris is paroxysmal substernal chest pain caused by transient myocardial ischemia WITHOUT myocyte death (necrosis)** — due to imbalance between coronary perfusion and demand; pain results from ischemia-induced release of **adenosine and bradykinin** stimulating sympathetic afferents.

### Classification

| Type | Trigger/duration | Mechanism | ECG |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Stable (typical/exertional)** | Exertion/emotion; 5–15 min; relieved by rest/nitroglycerin | Fixed **critical stenosis >70%**; demand exceeds supply | Transient ST depression during pain |
| **2. Prinzmetal (variant)** | 🔴 At rest, unrelated to exertion; responds promptly to nitrates/Ca-channel blockers | **Coronary vasospasm** near a plaque (± normal artery) | Transient ST elevation |
| **3. Unstable (crescendo/pre-infarction)** | Increasing frequency/severity, prolonged (>20 min), at rest | 🔴 **Plaque fissuring/rupture with non-occlusive mural thrombus**, embolization, vasospasm | ST depression/T inversion; troponin may rise slightly (NSTEMI border) |

📌 Silent ischemia: elderly + diabetics feel no pain despite objective ischemia.

### Differentiation of angina from MI

| Feature | **Angina pectoris** | **Myocardial infarction** |
|---|---|---|
| Pathology | Reversible ischemia only | 🔴 Irreversible **coagulative necrosis** |
| Duration of pain | **<15 min** | 🔴 **>30 min** (hours) |
| Severity | Pressure, moderate | Crushing, excruciating |
| Relief by rest/nitroglycerin | 🔴 Yes | 🔴 No |
| Associated features | Minimal | Sweating, nausea/vomiting, dyspnea, shock |
| Cardiac enzymes (troponin-I, CK-MB) | Normal | 🔴 Elevated |
| Leukocytosis/ESR/fever | Absent | Present (after 24 hr) |
| ECG | Transient ST-T changes, normalize between attacks | Persistent ST elevation → pathological Q waves evolve |
| Mortality | Low (but unstable type precedes MI) | Significant (arrhythmia, rupture, shock) |

> 🎯 **"Angina borrows, MI destroys": under 15 minutes + nitro relief = angina; over 30 minutes + enzyme rise = MI.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q6. What is Myocardial Infarction (MI)? Give the clinical features of MI.</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Myocardial infarction (MI) is irreversible necrosis (coagulative necrosis) of heart muscle produced by acute, sustained myocardial ischemia**, almost always following sudden total occlusion of a coronary artery by a **platelet-fibrin thrombus overlying a ruptured/eroded atherosclerotic plaque**.

- **Types by territory:** 🔴 **Transmural** (full wall thickness; total epicardial arterial occlusion) vs **Subendocardial/non-transmural** (inner ⅓; diffuse 3-vessel disease/hypotension without total occlusion).
- **Artery → territory (right-dominant 80%):** **LAD** — anterior LV wall, apex, anterior ⅔ septum (**most common, ~40–50%**); **RCA** — inferior/posterior wall, posterior ⅓ septum, RV; **LCX** — lateral wall.

### Clinical features of MI

1. 🔴 **Pain:** sudden severe retrosternal crushing/constricting pain **>30 min**, radiating to left arm/jaw/neck/epigastrium, **not relieved by rest or nitroglycerin**; maximal from onset.
2. 🔴 **Autonomic:** profuse sweating, nausea, vomiting, cold clammy extremities, pallor.
3. **Dyspnea** (acute LV failure), orthopnea; cough with frothy sputum if pulmonary edema.
4. **Weakness, anxiety, fear of impending death**; syncope (arrhythmia).
5. **Atypical/silent presentation:** diabetes, elderly, postoperative — painless dyspnea, epigastric "indigestion", confusion, syncope.
6. **Signs:** tachy-/bradycardia, low-grade fever (24 hr onward), S4/S3 gallop, soft S1, new MR murmur (papillary muscle dysfunction), pericardial rub (day 2–4), bilateral basal crackles, hypotension/cardiogenic shock, arrhythmias (VF = commonest early death).

> 🎯 **MI = "Dead muscle from a plugged pipe" — crushing pain >30 min + sweat + breathlessness + nitro-fail.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q7. Give the pathogenesis of MI.</b></summary>

```text
Vulnerable atherosclerotic plaque (thin fibrous cap <65 µm, lipid core >40%,
dense macrophage inflammation) ± trigger (morning adrenergic surge 6AM–noon,
exercise, emotional stress, cocaine, circadian platelet aggregability)
        ↓
PLAQUE RUPTURE / FISSURE / EROSION  (MMPs digest cap; endothelial erosion)
        ↓
Exposure of subendothelial COLLAGEN + TISSUE FACTOR + von Willebrand factor
        ↓
PLATELET ADHESION → ACTIVATION → AGGREGATION (TXA2, ADP, serotonin;
GpIIb/IIIa-fibrinogen bridges)
        ↓
Intrinsic/extrinsic COAGULATION CASCADE → FIBRIN mesh
        ↓
OCCLUSIVE RED THROMBUS (total occlusion; found angiographically in ~90%
of MIs within first 4 hr, 60% by 12–24 hr due to spontaneous lysis)
        ↓
CORONARY FLOW CEASES → SEVERE PROLONGED ISCHEMIA
        ↓
ATP depletion begins (seconds) → loss of contractility <2 min
→ ATP falls to 50% by 10 min, 10% by 40 min
        ↓
Anaerobic glycolysis → glycogen loss, lactate accumulation, intracellular pH↓
        ↓
Na⁺/K⁺ pump failure → cell swelling; Ca²⁺ influx; mitochondrial damage;
lysosomal activation; sarcolemmal rupture
        ↓
🔴 IRREVERSIBLE INJURY = COAGULATIVE NECROSIS in 20–40 min
(starting in SUBENDOCARDIAL zone — last perfused, highest pressure)
        ↓
WAVEFRONT progression outward → full-thickness TRANSMURAL infarct complete
by 3–6 hr (microvascular injury >1 hr)
        ↓
Necrotic cells leak TROPONIN-I/T + CK-MB into blood (diagnostic basis)
        ↓
REPERFUSION (thrombolysis/PCI): <20–30 min salvages; delayed reperfusion
→ hemorrhagic infarct + REPERFUSION INJURY (ROS burst, Ca overload)
```

**Non-atherothrombotic MI (~10%):** vasospasm (cocaine/ephedrine/Prinzmetal), coronary embolism (AF, IE, paradoxical PFO), vasculitis (Kawasaki/PAN), sickle crisis, amyloid in vessel walls, aortic dissection, shock/hypotension, severe aortic stenosis.

> 🎯 **"Crack → Clot → Cut-off → Ca²⁺ chaos → Coagulative necrosis in 20–40 min; Troponin tells the tale."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q8. Morphologic Changes of heart muscle in MI. Fates of MI.</b></summary>

### 🔴 Temporal morphology of MI (gross + microscopic)

| Time after occlusion | Gross appearance | Microscopic appearance |
|---|---|---|
| 0–30 min | None | Only EM: loss of glycogen, cell swelling, mitochondrial swelling |
| 2–4 hr | Dark mottling of myocardium | 🔴 **Wavy fibers** (stretching of injured bands), early coagulative necrosis begins |
| 4–12 hr | Dark mottling continues | 🔴 **Coagulative necrosis** — eosinophilic (hypereosinophilic) fibers, pyknosis, loss of nuclei/striping |
| 12–24 hr | Pale mottled area | Necrosis + 🔴 **neutrophil infiltration** starts; nuclear karyorrhexis |
| 1–3 days | Yellow-brown softening, hyperemic border | Neutrophil infiltrate **peaks**; myocyte karyolysis; muscle becomes pale yellow |
| 3–7 days | Yellow, soft, depressed centre | 🔴 **Macrophages clear debris; GRANULATION TISSUE** (fibroblasts + capillaries) appears at border (peak day 4); **danger period for rupture** |
| 7–14 days | Red-purple border, grey center | Granulation tissue matures; neovascularization prominent; collagen laid down |
| 2–8 weeks | 🔴 **Thin, firm, glistening WHITE FIBROUS SCAR** contracting progressively | Dense hyaline collagenous fibrosis; few vessels; scar is non-contractile |

📌 Infarct is **non-repairable by regeneration** — healing occurs by organization → fibrous scar (heart cannot replace myocytes).

### Fates of MI

1. 🔴 **Healing by organization → white fibrous scar** (6–8 wk) with permanent loss of contractility in that zone (most common fate with survival).
2. **Resolution (rare/minimal)** — very small infarcts replaced by thin scar.
3. **Extension/reinfarction** — peri-infarct ischemia or new plaque event enlarges the infarct.
4. 🔴 **Death** — from complications: arrhythmia (early), cardiogenic shock/CHF, cardiac rupture (day 3–7), embolism, ventricular aneurysm (late). *(Detailed list in Q12.)*
5. **Chronic evolution** — scarred akinetic segment → **ventricular aneurysm, mural thrombus, chronic IHD/ischemic cardiomyopathy.**

> 🎯 **Timeline: "Wavey 2hr → Dead 1d → Neutrophils 3d → Macro-clean 5d → Granulation 7d → White scar 8wk"; Fate = Scar or Die.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q9. What is chronic IHD? How it develops?</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Chronic IHD (ischemic cardiomyopathy) is progressive cardiac decompensation — manifesting as chronic congestive heart failure — that develops as the cumulative result of long-standing severe coronary atherosclerosis with multiple healed (silent or clinically apparent) myocardial infarctions.** It accounts for ~50% of cardiac transplant recipients.

### How it develops

```text
Long-standing severe multivessel atherosclerosis
        ↓
Repeated episodes of ischemia (silent MIs / known MIs / chronic angina)
        ↓
Progressive MYOCYTE LOSS (necrosis + apoptosis) — patchy, cumulative
        ↓
Replacement by DIFFUSE SUBENDOCARDIAL SCARRING (non-contractile tissue)
        ↓
Remodeling: compensatory hypertrophy of surviving myocytes
→ ventricular DILATION + HYPERTROPHY (eccentric) + interstitial fibrosis
        ↓
Progressive ↓EF → SYSTOLIC HEART FAILURE (chronic IHD established)
```

### Morphology

- **Heart enlarged (cardiomegaly, up to 900 g), flabby**, LV cavity dilated with **concentric + eccentric hypertrophy**.
- 🔴 **Diffuse subendocardial white fibrous scars** of varying ages + occasional old transmural scars; mural thrombi in ventricles/atria.
- Microscopy: myocyte hypertrophy, interstitial fibrosis, replacement scarring.

### Clinical

Stable angina + exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, fatigue, arrhythmias, murmurs; ends in refractory CHF, arrhythmic sudden death.

> 🎯 **"Many small deaths add up": repeated ischemia → lost myocytes → white patches → big floppy failing pump = ischemic cardiomyopathy.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q10. How will you Diagnose MI in Laboratory? / Lab Diagnosis of MI.</b></summary>

### A. Cardiac biochemical markers (the cornerstone)

| Marker | Rise | Peak | Normalize | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Cardiac Troponin-I (cTnI)** | 2–4 hr | 24–48 hr | **7–10 days** | 🔴 **Most specific & sensitive for myocardial necrosis** (not raised in skeletal muscle injury) |
| **Troponin-T (cTnT)** | 3–4 hr | 24–48 hr | 10–14 days | Equally specific; useful in renal failure interpretation |
| 🔴 **CK-MB** | 4–6 hr | ~24 hr | **48–72 hr** | Specific-ish for heart; 🔴 **best marker to detect REINFARCTION** (normalizes quickly); CK-MB fraction >6% of total CK suggests cardiac source |
| Total CK (CK-MM) | 6–12 hr | 24 hr | 48–72 hr | Non-specific (muscle, exercise) |
| LDH (LDH1>LDH2 "flip") | 24–48 hr | 72 hr | 10–14 days | Now obsolete; late marker |
| AST (SGOT) | 12–24 hr | 48 hr | 3–5 days | Non-specific (liver too) |
| Myoglobin | 1–3 hr (earliest) | 8–12 hr | 24 hr | Very early but non-specific |

📌 **Serial sampling:** diagnosis requires a characteristic rise-and-fall pattern; single normal troponin at ≥6 hr after symptom onset excludes MI.

### B. Electrocardiogram (immediate, bedside)

- **ST-segment elevation** (STEMI, transmural), ST depression/T-wave inversion (NSTEMI/subendocardial), 🔴 **pathological Q waves** (necrosis, appear 1–3 days, persist lifelong).
- **Localization:** V1–V4 = anteroseptal (LAD); II, III, aVF = inferior (RCA); I, aVL, V5–V6 = lateral (LCX); V4R = right ventricular.

### C. Other laboratory findings

- CBC: 🔴 **polymorphonuclear leukocytosis** (12–24 hr, subsides in ~1 week); ESR & CRP elevated (day 2 onward).
- Blood sugar ↑ (even non-diabetic), free fatty acids ↑.
- Urea, creatinine, electrolytes (K⁺, Mg²⁺ important for arrhythmia risk), lipid profile (risk assessment).

### D. Imaging

- **Echocardiography** — regional wall-motion abnormality (akinesia/dyskinesia), EF, mural thrombus, complications.
- **Coronary angiography** — identifies occluded vessel (gold standard for anatomy; basis for PCI).
- Chest X-ray — pulmonary congestion/cardomegaly.

> 🎯 **Lab trio: "ECG shows where, Troponin proves what died, CK-MB says if it's happening again."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75 & Unit-II "Enzymes", pp. 480–486

<summary><b>Q11. Cardiac Enzymes and proteins: Name with interpretation.</b></summary>

### Names with time-course and interpretation

| Enzyme/protein | Source | Rise / Peak / Fall | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Cardiac Troponin-I (cTnI)** | Actin filament regulatory protein of cardiac muscle | 2–4 hr / 24–48 hr / 7–10 d | 🔴 Gold standard for MI; specific for myocardium; stays high ~1 week → detects recent MI but NOT reinfarction timing |
| 🔴 **Cardiac Troponin-T (cTnT)** | Cardiac tropomyosin-binding protein | 3–4 hr / 24–48 hr / 10–14 d | Same utility; minor elevations in renal failure need trend interpretation |
| 🔴 **Creatine kinase-MB (CK-MB)** | Cytosolic enzyme of myocardium | 4–6 hr / 24 hr / 48–72 hr | MI diagnosis; 🔴 **rising values again after falling = reinfarction/infarct extension**; mass assay >5 ng/mL or MB fraction >6% total CK |
| **Total creatine kinase (CK-MM)** | Skeletal + cardiac muscle | 6–12 hr / 24 hr / 48–72 hr | Raised in IM injection, exercise, trauma, hypothyroidism → not specific alone |
| **LDH-1 > LDH-2 ("flipped pattern")** | Heart RBC | 24–48 hr / 72 hr / 10–14 d | Older late marker of MI; LDH-5 ↑ = liver |
| **AST (SGOT)** | Heart, liver, muscle | 12–24 hr / 48 hr / 3–5 d | Non-specific; ALT helps separate liver injury (ALT≫AST) |
| **Myoglobin** | All muscles | 1–3 hr / 8–12 hr / 24 hr | Earliest marker; negative value at 2–3 hr helps exclude; not specific |
| **BNP/NT-proBNP** | Stretched ventricular myocardium | Hours, tracks severity | Marker of heart FAILURE post-MI (not necrosis); low value rules out HF |
| **hs-CRP, fibrinogen, IL-6** | Inflammatory response | 24–48 hr | Risk stratification/prognosis; inflammation burden |

### Interpretation patterns (say in viva)

- 🔴 **Acute STEMI:** cTnI ↑ with typical curve + CK-MB ↑ + ECG ST elevation/Q waves.
- **Reinfarction/extension (day 3–7):** 🔴 **second rise of CK-MB** (troponin still high from first event — unhelpful).
- **Old MI (>10 days):** only troponin-T/I may remain positive; CK-MB/LDH normalized; Q waves persist on ECG.
- **Chest pain with normal troponin ×2 over 6 hr + normal ECG:** MI excluded (high NPV) → look for angina/GI causes.
- **Raised CK-MM + myoglobin but normal troponin:** skeletal muscle injury, not MI.

> 🎯 **"Troponin Tells (most specific, longest stay), CK-MB Marks re-MI (quick return), Myoglobin is first but never sure."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75 & Unit-II "Enzymes", pp. 480–486

<summary><b>Q12. Complications of MI.</b></summary>

| # | Complication | Time | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 🔴 **Arrhythmias** (VF, VT, heart block) | First hours–days | 🔴 **Commonest cause of early in-hospital death**; sudden death prehospital |
| 2 | 🔴 **Contractile dysfunction — cardiogenic shock / acute LV failure** | Immediately–days | 🔴 **Commonest cause of overall hospital death** in large infarcts (>40% LV lost) |
| 3 | 🔴 **Myocardial rupture** (soft necrotic muscle, days 3–7): (a) free-wall, (b) interventricular septum, (c) papillary muscle | 3–7 days | (a) **Haemopericardium → cardiac tamponade → sudden death**; (b) acquired VSD → left-to-right shunt; (c) **acute mitral regurgitation** → pulmonary edema |
| 4 | **Fibrinous (pericardial) pericarditis** | Day 2–4 | Friction rub, pleuritic pain (transmural MI) |
| 5 | **Dressler syndrome** (autoimmune pericarditis) | 2–10 weeks | Fever, rub, anti-myocardial antibodies |
| 6 | **Mural thrombus formation** on endocardial surface | Days–weeks | Systemic **emboli → stroke, renal/splenic infarct, limb ischemia** |
| 7 | **Ventricular (paradoxical) aneurysm** | Weeks–months | Thin scar bulges each systole → CHF, recurrent VT, mural thrombus/emboli (does NOT rupture) |
| 8 | Right ventricular infarction | With RCA occlusion | Raised JVP, hypotension without lung congestion |
| 9 | Ventricular remodeling/scarring → chronic IHD | Months–years | Dilated failing LV, secondary mitral regurgitation |

> 🎯 **Complication clock: "Arrhythmia first hour, Shock any time, Rupture day 3–7, Rub day 2–4, Thrombus anytime, Aneurysm later, Dressler weeks after."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q13. Hypertension : Definition , types, Cause.</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Hypertension is a persistent, sustained elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure ≥140 mm Hg systolic and/or ≥90 mm Hg diastolic recorded on two or more separate occasions** (2017 ACC/AHA stage 1 begins at ≥130/80 mm Hg).

### Types

| Basis | Types |
|---|---|
| **By cause** | 🔴 **Primary/essential/idiopathic (90–95%)** vs 🔴 **Secondary (5–10%)** |
| **By severity/course** | **Benign** (slow, chronic, uncomplicated for decades) vs 🔴 **Malignant/accelerated** (rapidly rising BP >200/120, papilledema, fibrinoid necrosis, renal failure — fatal in 1 yr untreated) |
| **By vessel side** | Systemic (left-sided) vs Pulmonary HTN → cor pulmonale (right-sided) |

### Causes

**A. Primary/essential (90–95%) — multifactorial:**
- Genetic predisposition (polygenic; genes affecting renal Na⁺ handling e.g. **EnaC, ACE polymorphisms**), 🔴 **"resetting of pressure natriuresis"** (kidney excretes salt only at higher pressure).
- Environment: **high salt intake**, obesity, stress, sedentary life, smoking, alcohol, aging, black race.

**B. Secondary (5–10%) — identify the culprit organ:**
1. 🔴 **Renal:** chronic renal parenchymal disease (commonest secondary cause); **renovascular — renal artery stenosis** (renin-driven).
2. 🔴 **Endocrine:** **primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn)**, **pheochromocytoma**, Cushing syndrome, acromegaly, hypo-/hyperthyroidism.
3. **Vascular:** coarctation of aorta, polyarteritis nodosa, aortic regurgitation.
4. **Drugs:** OCPs, steroids, NSAIDs, sympathomimetics, erythropoietin.
5. Sleep apnea, pregnancy (pre-eclampsia).
6. Single-gene forms (all increase distal Na⁺ reabsorption): **Liddle syndrome** (gain-of-function EnaC), glucocorticoid-remediable aldosteronism, 11β-/17α-hydroxylase deficiency.

📌 **Hypertensive heart disease (systemic):** chronic HTN → **concentric LV hypertrophy** (pressure overload) — heart weight **>500 g**, LV wall **>2 cm**, LA enlargement, interstitial fibrosis → diastolic/systolic failure, IHD, SCD.

> 🎯 **"140/90 twice = HTN"; Secondary hunt = "Kidney-Kidney artery-Hormone-Pipe-Pills". Hyaline = benign, Onion-skin = malignant.**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q14. A male of 50 years of age presented with sudden severe central chest pain & sweating for 1 hour. He is Hypertensive & Diabetic. What is your likely diagnosis? What are the immediate investigations you would suggest to confirm the diagnosis? (Answer: ECG, cardiac biochemical markers — most common Cardiac Troponin-I.)</b></summary>

### 🔴 Likely diagnosis: **Acute Myocardial Infarction (STEMI)**

### Justification (reasoning)

- **Typical pain profile:** sudden, severe, central/retrosternal chest pain of 1 hour duration — exceeds the 30-min threshold of MI; angina (<15 min, relieved by rest) excluded by duration.
- **Autonomic accompaniment:** sweating (diaphoresis) = classic sympathetic response of MI.
- 🔴 **Strong risk profile:** male sex, age 50, **hypertension and diabetes** — the two strongest modifiable IHD risk factors (DM also predisposes to larger, complicated infarcts).
- **Main differential to exclude:** **aortic dissection** (hypertensive, tearing pain radiating to back, BP asymmetry between arms) — must be considered before anticoagulation.

### Immediate investigations

| Investigation | Expected finding / purpose |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **ECG (within 10 min, serially)** | **ST-segment elevation** in contiguous leads (+ pathological Q waves later); localizes wall — V1–V4 anterior (LAD), II/III/aVF inferior (RCA); also detects arrhythmia |
| 🔴 **Cardiac biochemical markers — Cardiac Troponin-I (most commonly used)** | 🔴 **Rises 2–4 hr after onset, peaks 24–48 hr** — highly specific for myocardial necrosis; serial samples confirm the rise-fall pattern. Add **CK-MB** (peak ~24 hr, useful for reinfarction) |
| Bedside echocardiography | Regional wall-motion akinesia, EF, excludes tamponade/dissection flap |
| Chest X-ray | Widened mediastinum (dissection screen), pulmonary congestion |
| Baseline labs | CBC (leukocytosis), random blood glucose, urea/creatinine/electrolytes, lipid profile (risk factors) |
| Coronary angiography | Definitive localization → primary PCI (after stabilization) |

> 🎯 **"50-year-old diabetic hypertensive + crushing pain + sweat = MI until proven otherwise; ECG first, Troponin-I confirms."**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

<summary><b>Q15. A 35 years old smoker man presented with severe chest pain. His ECG shows ischemic change. What will be the pathologic change/effect/morphologic changes/types of necrosis occurred in myocardium? State the complication of MI.</b></summary>

### 🔴 Diagnosis: Acute Myocardial Infarction (premature CAD in a young smoker)

**Reasoning:** severe chest pain + ischemic ECG changes in a young man; smoking is a major modifiable risk factor causing endothelial injury, LDL oxidation, vasospasm and thrombosis → premature coronary atherosclerosis/plaque rupture (consider also cocaine use, hypercoagulable states, familial hypercholesterolemia).

### Pathologic change / effect in myocardium

```text
Plaque rupture → coronary thrombotic occlusion
        ↓
Severe ischemia → ATP depletion → membrane injury
        ↓
🔴 TYPE OF NECROSIS = COAGULATIVE NECROSIS
(cell outlines preserved, hypereosinophilic anucleate fibers,
later replaced by fibrous scar — heart cannot regenerate)
```

### Morphologic changes (timeline)

| Time | Change |
|---|---|
| 2–4 hr | Dark mottling; **wavy fibers** |
| 4–24 hr | Coagulative necrosis (hypereosinophilia, pyknosis), neutrophils begin |
| 1–3 days | Neutrophil peak, karyolysis |
| 3–7 days | Macrophages remove debris; granulation tissue; 🔴 **rupture-prone phase** |
| 1–2 wk | Fibroblasts + capillaries mature |
| 3–8 wk | 🔴 **White fibrous scar** (permanent, non-contractile) |

Pattern: usually **transmural** (total occlusion) starting subendocardially, spreading as wavefront.

### Complications of MI

1. 🔴 **Arrhythmias (ventricular fibrillation)** — commonest cause of early death.
2. **Cardiogenic shock / left ventricular failure.**
3. **Myocardial rupture** (free wall → tamponade; septum → VSD; papillary muscle → MR) — day 3–7.
4. **Pericarditis (day 2–4)** and later Dressler syndrome.
5. **Mural thrombus → systemic emboli** (stroke, renal infarct).
6. **Ventricular aneurysm** (late) → CHF, arrhythmia, embolism.

> 🎯 **Young smoker + blocked coronary = coagulative necrosis of myocardium; remember the danger window day 3–7 (soft heart = rupture).**

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75
