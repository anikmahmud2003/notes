<summary><b>Q1. Define cholelithiasis. Give the classification of gallstones.</b></summary>

### Definition

🔴 **Cholelithiasis = presence of one or more calculi (stones) within the gallbladder lumen** (when stones lodge in the biliary ducts the term is choledocholithiasis). Prevalence ~10–20% of adults; women affected 2–3× more than men; risk rises with age, estrogen states and obesity.

### Classification of gallstones

| Type | Frequency | Composition | Key associations |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Cholesterol stones** | **~80%** (Western series) | Pure cholesterol (crystalline cholesterol monohydrate) or cholesterol-dominant with calcium salts; yellow-white, round/oval, greasy, float in water | Estrogen (female/OCP/pregnancy), obesity, age ("Fat, Female, Forty"), rapid weight loss, ileal disease |
| **Pigment stones — BLACK** | 5–10% | Calcium bilirubinate + calcium carbonate/phosphate; small, black, brittle | 🔴 **UNCONJUGATED hyperbilirubinemia — hemolysis** (hereditary spherocytosis, sickle cell/thalassemia), cirrhosis, old age |
| **Pigment stones — BROWN** | 5–10% | Calcium bilirubinate + fatty acid soaps; soft, earthy, laminated | 🔴 **Bile stasis + INFECTION with β-glucuronidase-producing organisms** (*E. coli*), parasites (**Ascaris lumbricoides, Clonorchis sinensis**) — common in ASIA incl. Bangladesh |
| **Mixed stones** | 5–10% | Cholesterol + calcium bilirubinate/carbonate/palmitate; multiple, faceted | Overlap of above factors |

> 🎯 "**80% are cholesterol; pigment split = BLACK from blood (hemolysis), BROWN from bugs (infection/stasis); 'Black-Blood, Brown-Bug'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q2. Mention the etiology / risk factors of Cholesterol gall stone.</b></summary>

### Risk factors for cholesterol gallstones

| Risk factor group | Details |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Demographic — "3 F's": Female, Fat, Forty** | Women 2–3× (estrogen effect); obesity (increased cholesterol secretion); increasing age (peak 40–60 yr) |
| 🔴 **Estrogen-related** | Oral contraceptives/HRT, pregnancy/multiparity — estrogen ↑ hepatic cholesterol uptake & secretion, ↓ bile salt synthesis, suppresses gallbladder motility |
| **Diet/metabolic** | High-calorie/refined-carbohydrate diet, hyperlipidemia, diabetes mellitus, metabolic syndrome |
| 🔴 **Bile salt pool loss / ileal disease** | Crohn disease or surgical resection of terminal ileum → bile salt malabsorption → reduced enterohepatic circulation → lithogenic bile |
| **Gallbladder stasis** | Prolonged fasting/TPN, spinal cord injury, vagotomy, octreotide therapy — hypomotility allows crystal nidation/growth |
| **Rapid weight loss** | Very-low-calorie diets/bariatric surgery → mobilization of cholesterol into bile |
| **Genetic/ethnic** | Family history; Native Americans, Chilean women highest rates; LITH genes |
| **Drugs** | Clofibrate, octreotide, ceftriaxone (biliary sludging) |

> 🎯 "**Remember 4 F's + 2 losses: Female-Fat-Forty-Fertile(estrogen), plus loss of bile SALTS (ileum) and loss of MOTILITY (stasis) — 'make more cholesterol, keep less solvent, stir less'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q3. Mention the pathogenesis of Cholesterol gallstone formation.</b></summary>

### Pathogenesis of cholesterol gallstone formation

Cholesterol is kept soluble in bile as mixed micelles with **bile acids/salts + lecithin (phosphatidylcholine)**. Stones form when this balance breaks:

```
Normal bile: cholesterol solubilized in micelles
   (cholesterol : bile acids : lecithin in balance)
        ↓
🔴 THREE DEFECTS converge:
   1. BILE CHOLESTEROL HYPERSECRETION
      (obesity/age/estrogen/drugs → supersaturated "lithogenic" bile)
   2. HYPOSECRETION OF BILE SALTS / LECITHIN
      (ileal disease-resection, defective CYP7A1/bile-acid feedback)
   3. GALLBLADDER HYPOmotILITY (stasis)
      (pregnancy, fasting, TPN, octreotide)
        ↓
Supersaturated bile → cholesterol precipitates as vesicles/microcrystals
        ↓
NUCLEATION accelerated by mucin + glycoproteins secreted by gallbladder wall
        ↓
Crystals trapped by stagnant bile → grow + aggregate
   (gallbladder hypomotility also impairs clearance of crystals)
        ↓
🔴 CHOLESTEROL GALLSTONE
```

📌 Extra points: gallbladder absorptive changes and mucin hypersecretion promote nidation; estrogens both increase cholesterol secretion AND reduce motility; most people with supersaturated bile never form stones — nucleation + stasis complete the triad.

> 🎯 "**Stone recipe = supersaturated bile (too much cholesterol/too little solvents) + nucleation on mucin + stasis to let it cook — 'Mix-Nidate-Stagnate'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q4. Mention the complications of gallstones.</b></summary>

### Complications of gallstones

| Complication | Mechanism/key features |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Biliary colic** | Commonest presentation; transient cystic duct obstruction; RUQ pain radiating to right shoulder/scapula after fatty meals |
| 🔴 **Acute cholecystitis** (90% calculous) | Sustained cystic duct impaction → distension, chemical inflammation, ischemia, bacterial superinfection; Murphy sign positive |
| **Chronic cholecystitis** | Repeated low-grade obstruction/inflammation → fibrotic thick wall, Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses |
| **Mucocele/hydrops** | Chronic cystic duct obstruction → mucus-distended pale gallbladder |
| **Empyema of gallbladder** | Suppurative infection of obstructed gallbladder |
| 🔴 **Choledocholithiasis** | Stone migration into common bile duct → obstructive jaundice, cholangitic features |
| 🔴 **Ascending cholangitis** | Infected obstructed bile — Charcot triad (fever, jaundice, RUQ pain); life-threatening sepsis |
| 🔴 **Gallstone (acute biliary) pancreatitis** | Stone impacts at ampulla/common channel → reflux/obstruction of pancreatic duct |
| **Cholecystoenteric fistula → gallstone ileus** | Large stone erodes into duodenum/small bowel → mechanical obstruction (Rigler triad; Bouveret syndrome if gastric outlet) |
| **Porcelain gallbladder** | Dystrophic calcification of chronically inflamed wall — premalignant association |
| 🔴 **Gallbladder carcinoma** | Long-standing chronic cholecystitis/stones predispose (~90% adenocarcinoma) |
| 📌 Others | Pericholecystic abscess, perforation ± peritonitis, emphysematous cholecystitis (diabetics, gas-forming organisms) |

> 🎯 "**Stones cause trouble along their journey: stay = colic→cholecystitis→porcelain→cancer; travel = jaundice→cholangitis→pancreatitis; escape to gut = fistula→gallstone ileus — 'Stay, Travel, Escape'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q5. Mention the pathogenesis of pigment gallstones.</b></summary>

### Pathogenesis of pigment stone formation

```
A. BLACK pigment stones
Unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia
   (hemolytic anemia: spherocytosis/sickle/thalassemia;
    ineffective erythropoiesis; cirrhosis with hemolysis)
        ↓
Excess unconjugated bilirubin filtered/secreted into bile
   (bilirubin mono/diglucuronides deconjugated in bile)
        ↓
Unconjugated bilirubin (insoluble) combines with CALCIUM
        ↓
Precipitation as calcium bilirubinate + calcium carbonate/phosphate
        ↓
BLACK pigment stones (gallbladder; sterile bile; no infection needed)

B. BROWN pigment stones
Biliary STASIS + INFECTION
   (E. coli etc. — parasites Ascaris/Clonorchis as nidus; common in Asia)
        ↓
Bacterial β-GLUCURONIDASE hydrolyzes conjugated bilirubin
        ↓
Free unconjugated bilirubin released in stagnant bile
        ↓
Combines with calcium + fatty acids (phospholipase → fatty acid soaps)
        ↓
Soft earthy laminated BROWN stones (intra-/extrahepatic ducts > gallbladder)
        ↓
Recurrent cholangitis, hepatolithiasis → risk of cholangiocarcinoma
```

| Feature | Black stones | Brown stones |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Hemolysis/cirrhosis | Infection + stasis |
| Location | Gallbladder | Bile ducts (± gallbladder) |
| Geography | Worldwide | Asia (incl. Bangladesh) |
| Organisms | Sterile | E. coli/parasites present |

> 🎯 "**Both stones are calcium bilirubinate: BLACK needs no germs — just too much bilirubin from blood (hemolysis); BROWN needs BUGS — bacterial glucuronidase frees bilirubin in stagnant infected bile — 'Black-Blood, Brown-Bug'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q6. Short note: Acute cholecystitis.</b></summary>

### Acute cholecystitis

🔴 **Definition: acute inflammation of the gallbladder wall, almost always initiated by obstruction of the neck/cystic duct by a gallstone (calculous type, 90–95%).**

| Aspect | Features |
|---|---|
| Types | 🔴 **Calculous (90–95%)** vs **acalculous (5–10%)** — critically ill/postoperative/trauma/burns/sepsis patients, TPN, immunosuppression (ischemia + bile stasis; gangrene/perforation risk up to 40%) |
| Pathogenesis | Stone impaction → retained concentrated bile → 🔴 chemical injury by bile salts/lipids + distension pressure → ischemia of wall → bacterial overgrowth (*E. coli*, Klebsiella, Streptococcus, anaerobes) |
| Morphology | Enlarged tense reddish gallbladder; serosal fibrinopurulent exudate; edematous hemorrhagic thick wall; mucosa congested/ulcerated ± necrosis (gangrenous form); pus (empyema) |
| Clinical | 🔴 Acute RUQ pain **radiating to right shoulder/back**, fever, nausea/vomiting; 🔴 **positive Murphy sign** (inspiratory arrest during RUQ palpation); mild neutrophilic leukocytosis; USG — stones, thickened wall ≥4 mm, pericholecystic fluid, sonographic Murphy |
| Course | Attacks subside in days with treatment OR progress to complications |
| 🔴 Complications | Empyema · gangrene · 🔴 perforation (~10%) → peritonitis/pericholecystic abscess · cholecystoenteric fistula → gallstone ileus · emphysematous cholecystitis (gas-formers, diabetics) · ascending cholangitis · pancreatitis |
| Treatment | NBM, IV fluids, antibiotics, analgesia; early laparoscopic cholecystectomy |

> 🎯 "**Acute cholecystitis = stone blocks neck → chemical burn + ischemia + germs; remember MURPHY + shoulder pain + thick GB wall; dangers = gangrene-perforation-fistula.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q7. What are the morphological changes in chronic cholecystitis?</b></summary>

### Morphology of chronic cholecystitis

**Gross**
| Finding | Description |
|---|---|
| Wall | 🔴 Thickened, firm, gray-white (fibrosis); may be contracted or distended |
| Content | Clear greenish-brown mucinous fluid or bile ± stones (present in ~98%) |
| Mucosa | Flat/atrophic but usually INTACT (unlike acute ulceration); may be porcelain (calcified) |

**Microscopy**
| Finding | Description |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses** | 🔴 Herniation/pouching of mucosal epithelium deep into (or through) the muscular wall — hallmark feature |
| Chronic inflammatory infiltrate | Lymphocytes, plasma cells, macrophages throughout lamina propria and muscle |
| Fibrosis | Subserosal/subepithelial fibrous thickening of wall |
| Muscle hypertrophy | Smooth muscle hypertrophy ± mural fibrosis |
| Epithelial changes | Mucosal epithelium may atrophy/hypertrophy; 🔴 metaplasia — antral-type glands, goblet cells/Paneth cells (bile-induced intestinal metaplasia) |
| Associated lesions | Cholesterolosis ("strawberry gallbladder" — lipid-laden macrophages), impacted stone in neck, xanthogranulomatous change |

📌 Clinical note: chronic cholecystitis follows repeated attacks of acute/subacute inflammation; symptoms overlap with biliary colic.

> 🎯 "**One word wins viva = ROKITANSKY-ASCHOFF SINUSES (mucosa herniating through wall); add thick fibrotic wall + intact mucosa + stones + goblet-cell metaplasia.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q8. What type of carcinoma occurs in the gall bladder?</b></summary>

### Carcinoma of the gallbladder

| Aspect | Features |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Histologic type** | 🔴 **ADENOCARCINOMA in ~90% of cases** — variants: papillary, tubular, mucinous, signet-ring cell |
| Less common types | ~10%: squamous cell carcinoma/adenosquamous (from metaplastic epithelium), undifferentiated/anaplastic carcinoma, small-cell carcinoma |
| Epidemiology | Most common malignancy of extrahepatic biliary tract; elderly females; high-incidence areas include northern India, Chile, Bolivia |
| 🔴 Risk factors | **Gallstones (75–90% coexist)**, porcelain gallbladder (risk up to ~20–30%), chronic cholecystitis, anomalous pancreaticobiliary duct junction, choledochal cyst, chronic *Salmonella typhi* carrier state |
| Morphology | Infiltrating (diffuse wall thickening, leathery, often scirrhous/desmoplastic) or fungating/exophytic papillary mass; direct invasion of liver bed, porta hepatis; lymphatic spread to cystic/periportal nodes; peritoneal seeding |
| Clinical | Silent/mimics chronic cholecystitis (RUQ pain, weight loss, jaundice, palpable mass); frequently discovered incidentally in cholecystectomy specimens |
| Prognosis | 🔴 Very poor — 5-year survival <10%; early (mucosal) tumors cured by cholecystectomy |

> 🎯 "**Answer in one line: ADENOCARCINOMA (90%) in a stone-filled porcelain gallbladder of an old lady; squamous/adenosquamous make up the rest; prognosis dismal <10%.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q9. What are the common causes of acute pancreatitis?</b></summary>

### Common causes of acute pancreatitis

🔴 The unifying theme in ALL causes = inappropriate intrapancreatic activation of digestive enzymes (trypsin) → autodigestion.

| Category | Causes |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Biliary disease** | 🔴 **GALLSTONES (35–60% of cases)** — impaction at ampulla/common channel; also biliary sludge, parasites (*Ascaris, Clonorchis*) |
| 🔴 **Alcohol** | Chronic alcoholism (with gallstones accounts for 🔴 ~80% of all cases; male predominance 6:1) |
| **Metabolic** | Hypertriglyceridemia, hypercalcemia (hyperparathyroidism) |
| **Drugs/toxins** | Azathioprine, thiazides, statins, GLP-1 agonists/DPP-4 inhibitors, corticosteroids; scorpion sting venom |
| **Trauma/mechanical** | Blunt/penetrant abdominal trauma, iatrogenic operative injury, 🔴 ERCP |
| **Infections** | 🔴 Mumps virus, coxsackievirus, CMV, HIV |
| **Genetic** | 🔴 PRSS1 (gain-of-function trypsinogen, autosomal dominant), SPINK1 (loss of inhibitor), CFTR, CASR mutations |
| **Vascular/ischemic** | Shock, atheroembolism, polyarteritis nodosa |
| **Idiopathic** | ~10% |

📌 Mnemonic widely used: **I GET SMASHED** — Idiopathic, Gallstones, Ethanol, Trauma, Steroids, Mumps/Malignancy, Autoimmune, Scorpion sting, Hyperlipidemia/Hypercalcemia/Hypothermia, ERCP, Drugs.

> 🎯 "**Two causes do 80% of the job: GALLSTONES (women) + ALCOHOL (men) — then I GET SMASHED for the rest.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 19 (Pancreas), pp. 881–894 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q10. What are the clinical presentations of acute pancreatitis? How it is diagnosed in laboratory?</b></summary>

### Clinical presentations

| Feature | Details |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Cardinal symptom** | Constant, intense **upper abdominal/epigastric pain boring through to the BACK** (± left shoulder), partially relieved by leaning forward, aggravated by eating/alcohol |
| **GIT** | Anorexia, nausea, persistent vomiting; abdominal distension (adynamic ileus) |
| **General** | Fever, tachycardia, hypotension/shock in severe disease |
| **Signs** | Epigastric tenderness ± guarding; 🔴 Grey-Turner sign (flank ecchymosis) and 🔴 Cullen sign (periumbilical ecchymosis) in hemorrhagic pancreatitis |
| **Severe disease (SIRS)** | Acute abdomen, ARDS/respiratory distress, acute renal failure, DIC, hypocalcemic tetany, hyperglycemia/glycosuria (~10%) — medical emergency; ~5% die in first week |

### Laboratory diagnosis

| Test | Findings | Kinetics/significance |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Serum lipase** | Raised (>3× ULN) | 🔴 **MOST sensitive AND specific enzyme**; rises within 4–12 h; stays elevated **8–14 days** |
| 🔴 **Serum amylase** | Raised (>3× ULN) | Rises 4–12 h; short half-life → normalizes in **3–5 days**; less specific (also raised in parotitis/perforated ulcer/ectopic pregnancy) |
| **CBC** | Neutrophilic leukocytosis | Inflammatory response |
| 🔴 **Serum calcium** | 🔴 HYPOCALCEMIA | Saponification of necrotic fat by lipase (calcium soaps) — severity marker |
| **Glucose** | Hyperglycemia (~10% glycosuria) | Islet/endocrine injury |
| **Others** | ↑ LDH, ↑ AST, urea/creatinine rise, CRP ↑, hypoalbuminemia, methemalbumin | Severity/prognostic indicators (organ failure + pancreatic necrosis = adverse signs) |
| **Imaging (supportive)** | USG — swollen pancreas, gallstones; 🔴 Contrast CT — enlarged inflamed/necrotizing gland, peripancreatic stranding/fluid; MRCP for duct stones | Confirms diagnosis/complications (necrosis, pseudocyst, abscess) |

📌 Diagnosis = typical clinical picture + enzymes (lipase preferred) + imaging when severe or uncertain; serial lipase outlasts amylase.

> 🎯 "**Clinical = epigastric pain boring to back + vomiting + shock; lab = LIPISE king (rises 4–12h, lasts 8–14 days), amylase queen (gone in 3–5 days), calcium falls because fat drinks it (saponification) — 'Lipase late, Amylase away, Calcium down'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 19 (Pancreas), pp. 881–894 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224

<summary><b>Q11. Problem-based question: A woman presents with right upper quadrant abdominal pain for the past 6 hours. The pain suddenly starts after a heavy fatty meal and radiates to her right shoulder and back. She has associated nausea and vomiting. On examination, Murphy's sign is positive. Blood picture shows leucocytosis. Questions: (a) What is your clinical diagnosis? (b) Mention the pathogenesis. (c) Mention the complications.</b></summary>

### (a) Clinical diagnosis

🔴 **Acute calculous cholecystitis** (acute cholecystitis secondary to gallstone).

Supporting points:
- Middle-aged woman (classic demographic — female, fertile/fatty).
- 🔴 Sudden RUQ pain **after a heavy FATTY meal** (CCK-driven contraction against obstructed neck).
- 🔴 Pain **radiating to right shoulder/back** (diaphragmatic/visceral referral).
- Nausea, vomiting, fever implied.
- 🔴 **Positive Murphy sign** — inspiratory arrest on RUQ palpation (pathognomonic).
- Neutrophilic leukocytosis.
- Confirmatory workup: USG — gallstones, wall thickness ≥4 mm, pericholecystic fluid, sonographic Murphy sign; CBC; LFTs (to exclude choledocholithiasis/cholangitis).

### (b) Pathogenesis

```
Heavy fatty meal → duodenal CCK release → strong gallbladder CONTRACTION
        ↓
Gallstone impacts in cystic duct / Hartmann pouch (90–95% calculous)
        ↓
Obstruction → continued secretion → DISTENSION of gallbladder
        ↓
Concentrated retained bile → CHEMICAL INFLAMMATION
   (lysolecithin + bile salts injure mucosa; phospholipase A damage)
        ↓
Wall tension ↑ → venous/lymphatic compromise → ISCHEMIA of wall
        ↓
Mucosal barrier breakdown → bacterial proliferation
   (E. coli, Klebsiella, Streptococcus, anaerobes)
        ↓
ACUTE INFLAMMATION: edema, congestion, fibrinopurulent exudate,
neutrophilic infiltrate ± necrosis (gangrenous) — leukocytosis
```
📌 Acalculous variant (no stone): ischemia + bile stasis in critically ill patients — worth mentioning.

### (c) Complications

| Complication | Note |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Empyema (suppurative cholecystitis)** | Pus in obstructed gallbladder |
| 🔴 **Gangrenous cholecystitis** | Vascular compromise → wall necrosis (esp. diabetics/elderly) |
| 🔴 **Perforation (~10%)** | → localized pericholecystic abscess or generalized biliary peritonitis |
| 🔴 **Cholecystoenteric fistula → GALLSTONE ILEUS** | Stone erodes into duodenum/small bowel → mechanical obstruction (Rigler/Bouveret syndromes) |
| **Emphysematous cholecystitis** | Gas-forming organisms (Clostridia/E. coli) — diabetics, high gangrene risk |
| **Ascending cholangitis** | Spread to CBD with infection — Charcot triad |
| **Gallstone pancreatitis** | Migration of stone through ampulla/common channel |
| **Mirizzi syndrome** | Stone in cystic duct compressing common hepatic duct → jaundice |
| 📌 Recurrent/chronic cholecystitis | Fibrosis, Rokitansky-Aschoff sinuses, long-term carcinoma risk |

> 🎯 "**PBQ formula: fatty meal + RUQ pain to right shoulder + Murphy+ + leukocytosis = ACUTE CALCULOUS CHOLECYSTITIS; chain = CCK squeeze → stone stuck → chemical burn → ischemia → germs; watch out for gangrene-perforation-gallstone ileus.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224
