Q4. Name the inflammatory bowel diseases.

🔴 The two classic IBDs:

  1. Crohn disease (CD) — segmental transmural inflammation anywhere from mouth to anus.
  2. Ulcerative colitis (UC) — continuous mucosal inflammation starting at the rectum.
  3. Indeterminate colitis — features overlap; cannot be classified (~10% of chronic idiopathic IBD).

📌 Related but separate entities (mention for completeness): microscopic colitis (lymphocytic/collagenous), diversion colitis, collagenous colitis, Behçet, ischemic/infective colitides — these are NOT true IBD.

🎯 "Crohn + Ulcerative colitis (+ indeterminate colitis) — microscopic colitis is a mimic, not classic IBD."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 17 (Gastrointestinal Tract), pp. 753–822 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Gastrointestinal System, pp. 144–191

Q6. Mention important features of Crohn's disease.

Definition

Crohn disease = a chronic relapsing-remitting, TRANSMURAL, segmental (skip-lesion) granulomatous inflammatory bowel disease that may affect ANY part of the GIT from mouth to anus, most often the terminal ileum.

Q10. Short note on: GIST, Hirschsprung disease, Pseudomyxoma peritonei.

(a) GIST — Gastrointestinal Stromal Tumour

Definition: the most common MESENCHYMAL (stromal) neoplasm of the GI tract, arising from interstitial cells of Cajal (pacemaker cells), showing KIT (CD117) or DOG1 expression.

  • Sites: stomach (~60%) > small intestine > colon/rectum, esophagus; also omentum/mesentery.
  • Molecular: 🔴 activating KIT (c-KIT/CD117) mutation (~75–80%) or PDGFRA mutation (~8%) → constitutive tyrosine-kinase signalling; SDH-deficient subset in children/carney triad context.
  • Gross: submucosal/extrinsic mass covered by intact mucosa (central ulceration → bleeding); whorled fleshy tan cut surface ± hemorrhage/necrosis.
  • Micro: fascicles of spindle cells (or epithelioid variant); IHC: CD117/KIT+, DOG1+, CD34+, SMA variable; S100 negative.
  • 🔴 Behaviour graded (NOT benign/malignant binary): size + mitotic count predict metastasis (liver/peritoneum); lymphatics rare.
  • Treatment: surgical resection; unresectable/metastatic → 🔴 imatinib (tyrosine kinase inhibitor) — landmark targeted therapy.
🎯 "GIST = Cajal cell tumour, KIT/DOG1+, imatinib-responsive; behaviour = size + mitoses."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 17 (Gastrointestinal Tract), pp. 753–822 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Gastrointestinal System, pp. 144–191

(b) Hirschsprung disease

Definition: congenital aganglionic megacolon — absence of ganglion cells of BOTH the Meissner (submucosal) and Auerbach (myenteric) plexuses in a segment of colon due to arrest of neural-crest cell migration, causing functional obstruction.

  • Genetics: 🔴 loss-of-function RET mutations (majority familial cases); EDNRB/EDN3 ligand-receptor pair; ~10% associated with Down syndrome.
  • Pathogenesis chain:
Neural crest cells fail to migrate/die prematurely (craniocaudal)
   → aganglionic distal segment (RECTUM always; short vs long segment)
   → absent peristalsis + unrelaxed internal sphincter
   → functional obstruction
   → massive dilation + hypertrophy of PROXIMAL normally-innervated colon
  • Clinical: neonate fails to pass meconium within 48 h; abdominal distension, bilious vomiting; older children — chronic constipation; threats = 🔴 enterocolitis, perforation, fluid-electrolyte disturbance.
  • Diagnosis: suction rectal biopsy — 🔴 absence of ganglion cells + hypertrophied nerve trunks; acetylcholinesterase histochemistry highlights excess AChE fibres; frozen sections at pull-through.
  • Treatment: resection of aganglionic segment (pull-through procedures). Acquired megacolon mimics: Chagas disease, strictures, UC.
🎯 "No ganglion cells (Meissner+Auerbach) in RECTUM → no meconium; RET gene; AChE stain confirms."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 17 (Gastrointestinal Tract), pp. 753–822 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Gastrointestinal System, pp. 144–191

(c) Pseudomyxoma peritonei

Definition: accumulation of copious gelatinous/mucinous ascites with implants of mucinous epithelium on the peritoneal surfaces, producing progressive 'jelly belly' distension.

  • 🔴 Source: rupture (or transmural spread) of a low-grade appendiceal mucinous neoplasm (LAMN) in most cases; occasionally ovarian mucinous tumours (GI-type should raise suspicion of occult appendiceal primary).
  • Cells line the peritoneum as strips/nests floating in extracellular mucin; no true invasion required for the clinical picture.
  • Behaviour: slowly progressive, recurs relentlessly; causes bowel obstruction, malnutrition; rarely distant metastasis.
  • Treatment: cytoreductive surgery + 🔴 hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC).
🎯 "Appendiceal mucinous neoplasm ruptures → jelly belly → debulk + HIPEC."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 17 (Gastrointestinal Tract), pp. 753–822 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Gastrointestinal System, pp. 144–191
Q11. Etiopathogenesis of acute appendicitis.

Definition

Acute appendicitis = acute transmural inflammation of the appendix, almost always initiated by luminal OBSTRUCTION, culminating in bacterial invasion, ischemic necrosis and possible perforation — the most common cause of the acute abdomen requiring surgery.

Etiopathogenesis

LUMINAL OBSTRUCTION
   • FECALITH (inspissated stool) — most common in adults
   • LYMPHOID HYPERPLASIA (viral infection/Yersinia) — most common in children
   • Foreign body, fruit seeds, parasites (Enterobius vermicularis)
   • Tumour (carcinoid/mucinous neoplasm) — rare lead point
        ↓
Continued mucus secretion into closed lumen
        ↓
↑ Intraluminal pressure → venous congestion → mural ISCHEMIA
        ↓
Loss of mucosal integrity → BACTERIAL INVASION
(E. coli, Bacteroides fragilis, Peptostreptococcus, Pseudomonas)
        ↓
ACUTE TRANSWALL INFLAMMATION (neutrophils throughout wall)
        ↓
Suppurative → gangrenous (necrosis) → PERFORATION
        ↓
Localized periappendicular abscess OR generalized PERITONITIS

📌 Supporting factors: low-fiber diet (fecalith formation), family history; peak age 10–30 yr.

🎯 "Obstruction (fecalith/lymphoid hyperplasia) → pressure → ischemia → bacteria → necrosis → perforation."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 17 (Gastrointestinal Tract), pp. 753–822 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Gastrointestinal System, pp. 144–191

Q14. What do you mean by IBS & IBD? Give the difference between IBS & IBD.

Definitions

  • 🔴 IBS (irritable bowel syndrome) = a chronic FUNCTIONAL disorder of gut-brain interaction characterized by recurrent abdominal pain related to defecation with altered bowel frequency/form (Rome criteria, ≥3 months) without any demonstrable structural, inflammatory or biochemical abnormality.
  • 🔴 IBD (inflammatory bowel disease) = a group of chronic idiopathic IMMUNE-mediated INFLAMMATORY diseases of the GIT — principally Crohn disease and ulcerative colitis — with documented structural pathology (transmural/mucosal inflammation, ulcers, granulomas, crypt abscesses).

Differences

Q15. What are risk factor /predisposing factor for Colorectal carcinoma? Give the pathogenesis of colorectal carcinoma.

Risk factors

CategoryFactors
Age/demography>50 yr (rising in younger adults); male slightly > female; developed-world diet

Protective factorsFiber, fruits/vegetables, calcium, vitamin D, aspirin/NSAIDs (COX inhibition)

Pathogenesis — two molecular pathways

A. APC/β-catenin (chromosomal instability) pathway — adenoma-carcinoma sequence (classic):

NORMAL epithelium
   → APC loss (5q21) [initiating event; FAP = germline]
      → β-catenin accumulates → ↑MYC, cyclin D1 → proliferation → EARLY ADENOMA
   → KRAS activation (12p) → growth autonomy → LATE/LARGE ADENOMA
   → loss of SMAD2/SMAD4 (18q) (TGF-β pathway) → LATE ADENOMA with high-grade dysplasia
   → TP53 loss (17p) → CARCINOMA
   → further changes → METASTASIS

(Left colon, napkin-ring/annular stenosing tumours.)

B. MSI (microsatellite instability) pathway — mismatch repair defect:

Defective DNA mismatch repair
   • LYNCH: germline MLH1/MSH2/MSH6/PMS2 + second-hit loss
   • Sporadic: MLH1 promoter METHYLATION (CIMP-high) ± BRAF V600E
        → frameshift mutations accumulate (TGFBR2, BAX) → MSI-HIGH carcinoma

(Right/proximal colon, bulky mucinous tumours, better prognosis.)

Morphology correlation

Right colon — fungating/bulky exophytic masses (wide lumen, liquid stool → late obstruction, iron-deficiency anemia); left colon — annular constricting "napkin-ring" lesions (solid stool → early obstruction, altered habit, bleeding); microscopy: adenocarcinoma forming glands ± mucin/signet-ring.

🎯 "APC → KRAS → SMAD2/4 → TP53 (left, chromosomal) vs MMR-loss/MSI-H ± BRAF+CIMP (right, Lynch-like) — plus FAP/Lynch/IBD/red-meat risks."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 17 (Gastrointestinal Tract), pp. 753–822 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Gastrointestinal System, pp. 144–191
Q16. Problem-based question: A man presented with acute abdominal pain, vomiting, and fever. On examination, tenderness was observed in the right iliac fossa. Blood picture shows neutrophilic leukocytosis. (a) What is your diagnosis? (b) What are the possible etiologies and complications? (c) After operation, what do you expect in the histopathology?

(a) Diagnosis

🔴 Acute appendicitis (acute suppurative appendicitis). Supporting points:

  • Periumbilical pain shifting to RIGHT ILIAC FOSSA (McBurney point) — classic migratory pain (visceral → parietal).
  • Anorexia, nausea/vomiting, fever (low grade).
  • 🔴 Neutrophilic leukocytosis (12,000–20,000/µL) with left shift.
  • Differential to mention briefly: mesenteric adenitis (Yersinia), Meckel diverticulitis, Crohn disease, ectopic pregnancy/ovarian torsion (female), right ureteric calculus, perforated peptic ulcer, typhoid ileitis.

(b) Possible etiologies

  1. 🔴 Obstruction of the appendiceal lumen:
  • Fecolith/appendlith (most common in adults);
  • Lymphoid follicular hyperplasia (children — post-viral/Yersinia);
  • Foreign body, fruit seeds, Enterobius vermicularis;
  • Neoplasm (carcinoid, LAMN) as lead point;
  • Kinking/adhesions, low-fiber diet predisposing to fecalith.
  1. Obstruction → mucus retention → ↑intraluminal pressure → venous compromise → ischemia → bacterial proliferation (E. coli, Bacteroides, Peptostreptococcus) → acute transmural suppurative inflammation.

(c) Expected histopathology of the resected appendix

  • 🔴 Acute suppurative (transmural) appendicitis:
  • Neutrophilic infiltration — dense PMNs throughout all coats: mucosa (with cryptitis/crypt microabscesses) → lamina propria → muscularis → serosa.
  • Mucosal ulceration/fibrino-purulent exudate on the luminal surface.
  • Congestion, edema, dilated engorged vessels; focal hemorrhage.
  • Fecolith/inspissated faecal material ± Enterobius in the lumen; lumen may contain pus.
  • If advanced: gangrenous appendicitis — coagulative necrosis of wall, thrombosed vessels, serosal fibrino-purulent (acute) serositis; perforation site with acute peritonitic exudate.
  • Chronic changes (recurrent attacks): fibrosis, chronic inflammatory cells, obliteration of lumen.
  • 🔴 Report should also note absence of neoplasia (exclude incidental carcinoid at tip).
