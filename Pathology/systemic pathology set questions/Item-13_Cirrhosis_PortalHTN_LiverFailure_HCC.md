## Q1. Definition of cirrhosis of liver. What is chronic liver disease?

### Definition of cirrhosis

🔴 **Cirrhosis is an end-stage liver disease characterized by three cardinal features:**
1. 🔴 **Bridging fibrous septa** linking portal tracts with each other and with central veins;
2. 🔴 **Parenchymal regenerative nodules** of hepatocytes surrounded by the fibrous septa;
3. 🔴 **Derangement/duplication of vascular architecture** — portosystemic shunts and loss of normal sinusoidal perfusion.

📌 It is the common end-point of chronic hepatocellular injury of any cause; irreversible in practice; functional consequence = portal hypertension + hepatocellular failure.

### Chronic liver disease

🔴 **Chronic liver disease = persistent hepatic inflammation/necrosis/fibrosis lasting more than 6 months**, demonstrated by clinical, biochemical or serological abnormality, irrespective of etiology — a broader umbrella that includes chronic hepatitis (viral/autoimmune/drug/metabolic), steatohepatitis, chronic cholestatic diseases (PBC/PSC) and their complications; 🔴 **cirrhosis is its advanced/end stage**.

> 🎯 "**Cirrhosis triad = FIBROUS septa + REGENERATIVE nodules + VASCULAR shunting — 'FRV: Fibrosis-Regeneration-Vascular chaos'; chronic liver disease = >6 months of injury, cirrhosis = its final chapter.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q2. List the causes of cirrhosis. Mention the causes common in Bangladesh.

### Causes of cirrhosis

| Group | Causes |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Viral** | Chronic hepatitis B (±HDV), hepatitis C |
| 🔴 **Alcohol** | Alcoholic liver disease → micronodular (Laennec) cirrhosis |
| 🔴 **NAFLD/NASH** | Metabolic syndrome, obesity, type 2 diabetes — most common cause in Western countries; >90% of "cryptogenic" cirrhosis is burned-out NASH |
| **Autoimmune** | Autoimmune hepatitis type 1/2 |
| **Cholestatic/biliary** | PBC, PSC, extrahepatic biliary obstruction → secondary biliary cirrhosis, biliary atresia (children) |

| **Drug/toxin** | Methotrexate, amiodarone |

| **Parasitic** | Schistosomiasis (worldwide) |

### Causes common in Bangladesh

1. 🔴 **Chronic hepatitis B infection — single most common cause**
2. 🔴 **Hepatitis C infection**
3. **NAFLD/NASH** (rising with obesity/diabetes epidemic)
4. **Alcoholic liver disease** (increasing)
5. **Biliary atresia/cholestatic disorders** in children
6. Wilson disease, hemochromatosis — less frequent but reported

> 🎯 "**World list: Virus + Alcohol + NASH + Autoimmune + Biliary + Metabolic + Vascular + Cryptogenic; BD podium = HBV first, HCV second, NASH climbing, alcohol rising — 'B for Bangladesh, B for HBV'.**"

## Q3. Classify cirrhosis of liver according to etiology / What are the causes (etiology) of cirrhosis of liver.

### Etiologic classification of cirrhosis

| Etiologic class | Specific causes | Morphologic clue |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Viral** | Chronic HBV ± HDV, HCV | Macronodular; ground-glass cells (HBV); lymphoid follicles + steatosis (HCV) |

| **Drug/toxin** | Methotrexate, amiodarone | Steatohepatitic or fibrotic pattern |
| **Vascular/outflow** | Budd-Chiari, cardiac cirrhosis (chronic CHF), sinusoidal obstruction syndrome | Centrilobular congestion/fibrosis |
| **Infective-parasitic** | Schistosomiasis | Pipe-stem portal fibrosis (non-cirrhotic portal HTN) |
| 🔴 **Cryptogenic** | Cause undetectable at diagnosis | >90% represent burned-out NASH |

## Q4. Classify cirrhosis morphologically. How cirrhosis is graded histologically/clinically?

### Morphological classification

| Type | Nodule size | Features |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Micronodular** | <3 mm, uniform | Classic alcoholic/Laennec cirrhosis, also hemochromatosis, obstructive biliary |
| 🔴 **Macronodular** | >3 mm (up to several cm), irregular, may contain portal tracts within large nodules | Post-viral (HBV/HCV) cirrhosis; follows massive necrosis |
| **Mixed** | Both patterns coexist | Common transitional state |
| **Incomplete septal (fine scarring)** | Slender, often incomplete septa branching from portal tracts | Partially regressed cirrhosis |

📌 Note: micronodular can evolve into macronodular over time (nodules enlarge by regeneration/coalescence).

### Histological grading/staging

| Tool | What it measures |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **GRADE = necroinflammatory activity** (interface/piecemeal necrosis, lobular inflammation) | Scored by **Knodell-HAI, Ishak, METAVIR (A0–A3)** on biopsy |
| 🔴 **STAGE = fibrosis extent** | **METAVIR F0 (none) → F1 (portal) → F2 (periportal/septa) → F3 (bridging) → 🔴 F4 = CIRRHOSIS** |
| Biopsy findings | Fibrous septa bridging portal tracts/central veins, regenerative plates >2 cells thick, absence of portal tracts/hepatic venules inside nodules, ductular reaction |

### Clinical grading/scoring

| Score | Parameters | Classes |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Child-Pugh score** | Serum bilirubin, serum albumin, PT/INR prolongation, ascites, hepatic encephalopathy | 🔴 **A (well-compensated, 5–6 pts) / B (7–9) / C (decompensated, 10–15)** |
| 🔴 **MELD score** | Bilirubin, INR, creatinine (+ sodium) | Predicts 90-day mortality; used for transplant allocation |
| 📌 Imaging/supportive | USG/CT nodular shrunken liver, elastography (FibroScan kPa) | Non-invasive staging adjuncts |

> 🎯 "**Morphology = micro (<3 mm) vs macro (>3 mm) vs mixed; histology = GRADE activity + STAGE fibrosis (METAVIR F4 = cirrhosis); clinic = Child-Pugh A/B/C + MELD — 'Grade the fire, Stage the scars'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q5. Describe the pathogenesis of cirrhosis of liver in chronic viral hepatitis.

### Pathogenesis (chronic HBV/HCV → cirrhosis)

```
Persistent viral infection (HBV cccDNA / HCV quasispecies escape)
        ↓
Immune-mediated hepatocyte death (CD8+ CTL apoptosis/necrosis) — repeated cycles
        ↓
Chronic inflammation: Kupffer cell activation → cytokines
   (TNF-α, IL-1, IL-6, TGF-β, PDGF)
        ↓
🔴 QUIESCENT HEPATIC STELLATE CELLS (Ito cells, space of Disse) ACTIVATED
   → transform into myofibroblast-like cells
        ↓
Excess collagen types I & III deposited in perisinusoidal space
   → "capillarization" of sinusoids (loss of fenestrations)
        ↓
Fibrosis: portal → periportal → BRIDGING (portoportal/portocentral septa)
        ↓
Loss of hepatocyte mass stimulates compensatory REGENERATION
   → thick plates (>2 cells) forming NODULES encircled by septa
        ↓
Septa distort vasculature → intrahepatic shunts + increased sinusoidal resistance
        ↓
🔴 ESTABLISHED CIRRHOSIS (fibrosis + regenerative nodules + vascular derangement)
```

📌 Key points to say: (1) injury is immune-mediated, not directly cytopathic; (2) **stellate cell activation by TGF-β/PDGF is the central fibrogenic event**; (3) regression is possible if virus eradicated early (DAAs/antivirals), but advanced cirrhosis largely irreversible; (4) ongoing necrosis + regeneration = mutagenic milieu → **HCC risk**.

> 🎯 "**Chain: CTL kills hepatocyte → cytokines wake STELLATE cells → collagen bridges → nodules regenerate → vessels derange = cirrhosis; remember 'TGF-β turns Ito into scar'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q6. What is NASH? How Non alcoholic fatty liver disease may give rise to cirrhosis?

### NASH — definition

🔴 **NASH (Non-alcoholic steatohepatitis) = the progressive form of NAFLD characterized histologically by the triad of hepatic steatosis (≥5% hepatocytes) + lobular inflammation + ballooned hepatocytes**, occurring without significant alcohol intake and mimicking alcoholic hepatitis; it carries a real risk of fibrosis/cirrhosis. **NAFLD** = hepatic steatosis alone or with the above inflammation, associated with obesity/type 2 diabetes/hyperlipidemia (metabolic syndrome).

### How NAFLD progresses to cirrhosis

```
Insulin resistance (obesity, metabolic syndrome, diabetes)
        ↓
Peripheral lipolysis ↑ + de novo lipogenesis ↑ → fatty acid influx to liver
        ↓
MACROVESICULAR STEATOSIS (simple NAFLD — ~80%, usually indolent)
        ↓ (in ~20%: second hit)
Lipotoxicity: oxidative stress + lipid peroxidation
   → mitochondrial dysfunction, TNF-α release,
     adipokine imbalance (↓adiponectin, ↑leptin)
        ↓
Kupffer cell + inflammatory recruitment → lobular inflammation
   → hepatocyte BALLOONING (cytokeratin injury, ± Mallory-Denk hyaline)
        ↓
Zone 3 pericellular "CHICKEN-WIRE" perisinusoidal fibrosis
   → stellate cell activation → bridging fibrosis
        ↓
🔴 NASH → MACRONODULAR CIRRHOSIS
        ↓
Steatosis/ballooning DISAPPEAR ("burned-out") → labelled CRYPTOGENIC cirrhosis
   (🔴 >90% of cryptogenic cirrhosis = burned-out NASH)
        ↓
Risk continues: HCC even in NAFLD-related cirrhosis (<1%/yr)
```

📌 Clinical: often asymptomatic (fatigue, RUQ dull ache); mildly raised AST/ALT (ALT>AST unless advanced); definitive diagnosis of NASH requires **liver biopsy**; treatment = weight loss/exercise/diabetes control.

> 🎯 "**NASH triad = Steatosis + Ballooning + Lobular inflammation ('SBL'); road to cirrhosis = insulin resistance → fat → fire (inflammation) → wire (chicken-wire fibrosis) → burned-out cryptogenic cirrhosis.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q7. Give the pathogenesis of ascites in cirrhosis.

### Pathogenesis of ascites in cirrhosis

```
Cirrhotic architectural distortion
        ↓
Increased sinusoidal resistance + splanchnic vasodilation (↑NO)
        ↓
🔴 PORTAL SINUSOIDAL HYPERTENSION (portal HTN)
        ↓                                    ↓
Hydrostatic pressure ↑              Lymph overproduction
        │                           → weeping of lymph from liver surface
        ↓
Splanchnic vasodilation → effective arterial blood volume ↓
        ↓
RAAS + sympathetic activation + ADH secretion (non-osmotic)
        ↓
Renal Na⁺ and water RETENTION
        ↓                                ↓
🔴 HYPOALBUMINEMIA               Secondary hyperaldosteronism
(hepatocellular failure →          (↓ hepatic aldosterone breakdown)
 ↓ plasma oncotic pressure)
        ↓
Fluid transudation into peritoneal cavity
        ↓
🔴 ASCITES (transudate: protein <2.5 g/dL, SAAG ≥1.1 g/dL)
```

Contributing factors to enumerate:
1. 🔴 **Portal/sinusoidal hypertension** — primary driver.
2. 🔴 **Hypoalbuminemia** — reduced oncotic pressure.
3. 🔴 **Secondary hyperaldosteronism + RAAS/ADH** — sodium/water retention.
4. 🔴 **Splanchnic arterial vasodilation** (nitric oxide excess) — effective hypovolemia.
5. Hepatic lymph leakage from capsular surface.
6. Reduced renal perfusion → functional renal impairment perpetuates retention.

> 🎯 "**Ascites formula: BACKWARD (portal pressure pushes fluid out) + FORWARD (vasodilation tricks kidney into retaining salt) + ONCOTIC (no albumin to hold it in) — 'Pressure-Pull-Retain'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q8. Discuss the pathology / morphology of cirrhosis of liver.

### Gross morphology

| Feature | Description |
|---|---|
| Size/shape | 🔴 Early enlarged, later **shrunken, hard, diffusely nodular** organ (may be <1000 g) |
| Surface/cut section | 🔴 Diffuse nodularity — nodules of parenchyma encircled by gray-white fibrous septa throughout the ENTIRE liver |
| 🔴 Micronodular | Uniform nodules ~<3 mm ("hobnail"); classic alcohol/Laennec; greenish tint if cholestatic |
| 🔴 Macronodular | Irregular larger nodules (>3 mm–several cm), post-viral/post-necrotic |
| Biliary cirrhosis | Deep green (bile staining), jigsaw-puzzle/garland-shaped elongated nodules |
| Advanced disease | Fibrous bands broad; parenchyma nearly replaced (end-stage) |

### Microscopic morphology

1. 🔴 **Fibrous septa** bridging portal tracts to each other and to central veins; contain ductular reaction + inflammatory cells + proliferating vessels (shunts).
2. 🔴 **Regenerative nodules** — hepatocytes in plates **>2 cells thick**; nodules LACK normal central veins and intact portal triads (architectural chaos).
3. **Sinusoidal capillarization** — collagen deposition in space of Disse; loss of endothelial fenestrations.
4. Ductular reaction at nodule-septa interface.
5. 🔴 Etiologic fingerprints: Mallory-Denk hyaline + neutrophils (alcohol); ground-glass hepatocytes (HBV); lymphoid follicles + steatosis (HCV); iron (hemochromatosis, Prussian blue); copper/rhodanine (Wilson); PAS-positive diastase-resistant globules (α1AT); florid duct lesion (PBC)/onion-skin fibrosis (PSC).
6. Late: hepatocyte dropout, cholestasis, features of decompensation (bile plugs, feathery degeneration).

> 🎯 "**Gross = shrunken hobnail ball; Micro = SEPTA bridge + NODULES regenerate + plates >2-thick + no central veins inside nodules + hunt for the CAUSE (Mallory/ground-glass/iron/copper).**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q9. Enumerate the clinical features of cirrhosis of liver.

### Clinical features grouped by mechanism

| Mechanism | Features |
|---|---|
| **General/nonspecific** | Weakness, fatigue, weight loss, anorexia, low-grade fever |
| 🔴 **Hepatocellular failure (loss of function)** | Jaundice (mixed), coagulopathy/easy bruising (↓clotting factors), hypoalbuminemia → edema, gynecomastia/testicular atrophy/loss of libido, palmar erythema, spider angiomas (hyperestrogenemia), fetor hepaticus, hypoglycemia |
| 🔴 **Portal hypertension** | **Ascites**, **esophageal/gastric varices (hematemesis/melena)**, splenomegaly ± hypersplenism (pancytopenia), caput medusae/periumbilical collaterals, hemorrhoids |
| **Decompensation signs** | Hepatic encephalopathy (asterixis, drowsiness → coma), abdominal distension, shifting dullness |
| 📌 Signs on examination | Firm nodular hepatomegaly (or small end-stage liver), dilated abdominal wall veins, Dupuytren contracture/alcohol stigmata (if alcoholic), Kayser-Fleischer ring (Wilson), bronze skin + diabetes (hemochromatosis) |

> 🎯 "**Split the answer in two: CELL failure (jaundice, bleeding, estrogens, fetor, coma) vs PIPE failure (ascites, varices, spleen, caput medusae) — 'the factory fails AND the drain blocks'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

| Complication | Key points |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Portal hypertension consequences** | Varices (esophageal bleeding = major cause of death), ascites, spontaneous bacterial peritonitis, splenomegaly/hypersplenism |
| 🔴 **Hepatic encephalopathy/coma** | Ammonia-mediated neuropsychiatric deterioration |
| 🔴 **Acute-on-chronic liver failure** | Precipitated by infection/GI bleed/drugs |
| 🔴 **Hepatorenal syndrome** | Functional renal failure — intense renal vasoconstriction; poor prognosis |
| 🔴 **HEPATOCELLULAR CARCINOMA** | Risk 1–6%/yr depending on etiology (highest hemochromatosis/HBV) |

| 📌 Death causes | Hepatic coma, massive GI hemorrhage (variceal), intercurrent infection, hepatorenal syndrome, HCC |

> 🎯 "**Complications rhyme with portal HTN + failure: VARICES bleed, ASCITES infects (SBP), SPLEEN eats cells, BRAIN fogs, KIDNEY shuts (HRS), LUNG shunts (HPS), CANCER grows (HCC).**"

## Q11. Pathogenesis of edema in cirrhosis of liver.

### Pathogenesis of peripheral edema in cirrhosis

```
Hepatocellular failure (cirrhosis)
        ↓
🔴 FALL IN SERUM ALBUMIN SYNTHESIS → HYPOALBUMINEMIA
        ↓
Reduced plasma oncotic (colloid osmotic) pressure
        ↓
Transudation of fluid from intravascular → interstitial compartment
        ↓
Effective circulating volume falls
        ↓
Activation of RAAS + ADH + sympathetic system
        ↓
Renal retention of sodium and water
        ↓
More fluid leaves capillaries (same low oncotic pressure)
        ↓
🔴 DEPENDENT PITTING EDEMA (legs/sacrum) — aggravated by ascites
```

Contributing factors:
1. 🔴 Hypoalbuminemia (dominant mechanism).
2. Sodium/water retention (secondary hyperaldosteronism).
3. Portal hypertension + ascites → pooling of fluid in peritoneum worsens effective hypovolemia.
4. 📌 Edema is gravity-dependent — ankles first in ambulatory patients, sacral in bedridden.

> 🎯 "**Edema chain: sick liver → no albumin → oncotic suction fails → fluid leaks → kidney hoards salt → more leaks; 'Albumin down, ankles up'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q12. What do you mean by acute liver failure? What are the causes of acute liver failure?

### Definition

🔴 **Acute liver failure (fulminant hepatic failure) = severe acute liver injury associated with encephalopathy and impaired synthetic function (INR ≥1.5) occurring WITHOUT pre-existing chronic liver disease, within 26 weeks of the initial onset of symptoms.** Pathologically there is 🔴 **massive/submassive hepatic necrosis**; mortality high without transplantation.

### Causes of acute liver failure

| Category | Causes |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Drugs/toxins (leading group)** | 🔴 **Acetaminophen/paracetamol (#1 in USA)**, idiosyncratic drug reactions (isoniazid, halothane, NSAIDs, antibiotics), mushroom (*Amanita phalloides*) |
| 🔴 **Viral hepatitis (~10%)** | **HAV, HEV** (commonest worldwide), **HBV ± HDV** (commonest in Asia/Mediterranean), rarely HCV, HSV, EBV, CMV, yellow fever |
| **Autoimmune** | Acute presentation of autoimmune hepatitis |
| **Metabolic/hereditary** | Wilson disease (acute decompensation), acute fatty liver of pregnancy/HELLP, Reye syndrome |
| **Vascular/ischemic** | Ischemic hepatitis ("shock liver"), Budd-Chiari syndrome, sinusoidal obstruction syndrome |
| **Others/indefinite** | Malignant infiltration, sepsis; up to ~15% remain "indeterminate" |

> 🎯 "**ALF definition = encephalopathy + INR≥1.5 + NO prior liver disease + ≤26 weeks; causes = PARACETAMOL first, viruses A/E/B next — 'drug then bug'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q13. List the neoplasms of liver. Give the morphology of hepatocellular carcinoma.

### Neoplasms of the liver

| Group | Tumors |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Benign epithelial** | Hepatocellular adenoma (subtypes: HNF1α-inactivated, inflammatory, β-catenin-activated — rupture/bleed risk), focal nodular hyperplasia (central stellate scar) |
| **Benign mesenchymal** | 🔴 **Cavernous hemangioma (most common benign tumor)**, infantile hemangioendothelioma, lipoma |
| 🔴 **Primary malignant** | 🔴 **Hepatocellular carcinoma (most common PRIMARY malignant tumor)** incl. fibrolamellar variant · cholangiocarcinoma (+ Klatskin tumor) · combined hepatocellular-cholangiocarcinoma · hepatoblastoma (children) · angiosarcoma (vinyl chloride/thorotrast) |
| 🔴 **Metastatic (most common overall)** | Colon, breast, lung, pancreas >> others — multiple nodules, hepatomegaly with preserved LFTs |

### Morphology of hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)

**Epidemiology/context:** 80–85% arise in cirrhotic liver; major risk factors = chronic HBV/HCV, alcohol, aflatoxin (Aspergillus), hemochromatosis, α1AT deficiency, NAFLD; male predominance up to 8:1 in endemic areas; molecular drivers include TERT promoter mutation, β-catenin activation, TP53 loss; **HBx protein implicated in HBV-related carcinogenesis**.

**Gross morphology**
- Patterns: 🔴 (1) **single large mass**, (2) **multifocal multinodular** growth (often intrahepatic metastases), (3) diffuse infiltrative.
- Cut surface: soft, pale yellow-white (fat) or GREEN tinted (bile production); hemorrhage/necrosis common; ± capsule.
- 🔴 **Vascular spread characteristic — invades portal/hepatic veins → IVC → right atrium; intrahepatic portal vein seeding → multifocal tumors; late lung metastasis.**

**Microscopic morphology**
- 🔴 Malignant hepatocytes arranged in **thick trabeculae (>3–4 cells wide), pseudoglandular/acinar structures containing bile plugs, or solid sheets**.
- Cells resemble hepatocytes: polygonal, granular eosinophilic cytoplasm, round nuclei with prominent nucleoli; variable pleomorphism, giant cells; lipid/bile/Mallory bodies.
- Sinusoid-like blood spaces surround trabeculae; reticulin framework lost.
- 🔴 **Fibrolamellar variant:** young patients WITHOUT cirrhosis; large polygonal oncocytic cells (granular mitochondria-rich cytoplasm), vesicular nuclei with prominent nucleoli, separated by parallel lamellae of dense collagen; **AFP NOT elevated**; better prognosis.

**Diagnosis pearls:** raised **AFP** (advanced disease; insensitive early; negative in fibrolamellar); multiphasic CT/MRI shows 🔴 **arterial-phase enhancement with rapid venous washout** — diagnostic without biopsy in cirrhotics; USG surveillance of high-risk patients.

> 🎯 "**Liver tumors: metastasis #1 overall, HCC #1 primary; HCC picture = cirrhotic man + green-yellow trabecular mass eating portal vein + AFP↑ + washout on CT; young noncirrhotic AFP-negative = fibrolamellar.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q14. What is hepatorenal syndrome? What is hepatopulmonary syndrome?

### Hepatorenal syndrome (HRS)

🔴 **HRS = potentially reversible FUNCTIONAL renal failure occurring in advanced liver disease (cirrhosis/acute liver failure) with portal hypertension, characterized by intense renal vasoconstriction, oliguria, rising creatinine, in the ABSENCE of intrinsic renal pathology (normal tubules, no proteinuria/hematuria) or other cause of renal failure.**

- Pathogenesis: splanchnic vasodilation → effective arterial underfilling → RAAS/sympathetic activation + renal vasoconstrictors (endothelin) unopposed by reduced renal vasodilators (NO/prostaglandins) → falling GFR.
- Types: **Type 1** — rapid decline (triggered by SBP/variceal bleed; high short-term mortality); **Type 2** — slowly progressive, refractory ascites.
- Kidneys recover after successful liver transplant (proves functional nature).

### Hepatopulmonary syndrome (HPS)

🔴 **HPS = triad of (1) liver disease with portal hypertension, (2) arterial deoxygenation (PaO₂ <80 mmHg or elevated alveolar-arterial gradient), and (3) intrapulmonary vascular dilation/shunting (pulmonary arteriovenous communications).**

- Mechanism: failure of the diseased liver to clear pulmonary vasodilators (NO) → dilation/pre-capillary shunts → perfusion of poorly ventilated alveoli → hypoxemia.
- Clinical: 🔴 **platypnea (worse dyspnea upright) and orthodeoxia (desaturation standing)**, digital clubbing, cyanosis, spider naevi.
- Resolves after liver transplantation.

| Feature | HRS | HPS |
|---|---|---|
| Organ involved | Kidney (functional) | Lung (vascular dilation) |
| Core lesion | Renal vasoconstriction | Pulmonary vasodilation/shunt |
| Hallmark | Oliguria + rising creatinine, urine Na <10 mEq/L | Hypoxemia + orthodeoxia/platypnea |
| Reversal | Liver transplant | Liver transplant |

> 🎯 "**Both start in the failing liver but travel opposite ways: HRS = KIDNEY vessels CONSTRICT; HPS = LUNG vessels DILATE; both cured by transplant — 'kidney squeezes, lungs stretch'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q15. What are features of liver failure? How liver failure can be diagnosed in laboratory?

### Clinical features of liver failure

| System | Features |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Neuropsychiatric (encephalopathy)** | Drowsiness/confusion → coma; 🔴 asterixis (flapping tremor); fetor hepaticus; cerebral edema (acute form) |
| 🔴 **Coagulopathy** | Purpura, ecchymoses, GI bleeding (↓II,V,VII,IX,X; ↑PT/INR) |
| 🔴 **Jaundice** | Progressive deep icterus |
| **Metabolic** | 🔴 Hypoglycemia, electrolyte disturbances (hyponatremia/hypokalemia), acid-base disorder (respiratory alkalosis early, metabolic acidosis late) |
| **Renal** | Hepatorenal syndrome (oliguria, azotemia) |
| **Cardiorespiratory** | Hypotension, hypoxemia, pulmonary edema |
| **Infection** | Sepsis due to impaired immunity |
| 📌 Chronic failure extras | Ascites, edema, spider naevi, palmar erythema, gynecomastia |

### Laboratory diagnosis of liver failure

| Test | Finding | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **PT/INR** | 🔴 Markedly prolonged, unresponsive to vitamin K | **Best single indicator of severity/synthetic failure** |
| 🔴 **Serum bilirubin** | Rapidly rising (mixed/conjugated) | Excretory failure |
| **ALT/AST** | Very high initially; 🔴 FALLING transaminase with RISING bilirubin ("bili-transaminase dissociation") = massive necrosis, ominous | Necrosis pattern |
| 🔴 **Serum albumin** | Low | Synthetic failure (chronic) |
| **Serum ammonia** | Raised | Encephalopathy correlation |
| **Glucose** | Hypoglycemia | Loss of gluconeogenesis — prognostic |
| **Creatinine/urea** | Rising | Hepatorenal syndrome |
| **ABG/electrolytes** | Alkalosis→acidosis, hyponatremia | Metabolic derangement |
| **Serology/virology** | HBsAg/IgM anti-HAV/anti-HCV/anti-HEV, paracetamol level, autoantibodies, ceruloplasmin | Identify cause |
| 📌 Others | Factor V level, lactate, alpha-fetoprotein (baseline) | Prognostic scoring (King's College criteria, MELD) |

> 🎯 "**Failure labs tell one story: the FACTORY stops making (albumin↓, factors↓/INR↑) and stops cleaning (bilirubin↑, ammonia↑) while glucose drops — 'Make nothing, clean nothing'.**"

## Q16. Give the pathogenesis of hepatic encephalopathy and coagulopathy in liver failure.

### Pathogenesis of hepatic encephalopathy

```
Hepatocellular failure (± portosystemic shunting in cirrhosis)
        ↓
🔴 Gut-derived nitrogenous toxins — chiefly AMMONIA
   (colonic bacteria + glutaminase split urea/glutamine → NH₃)
        ↓
Ammonia escapes hepatic urea-cycle detoxification
        ↓
Enters systemic circulation → crosses blood-brain barrier
        ↓
Astrocyte ammonia metabolism → glutamine accumulation
        ↓
🔴 Astrocyte swelling (Alzheimer type II astrocytes) + low-grade cerebral edema
        ↓
Disturbed neurotransmission:
   • ↑ GABAergic tone (increased inhibitory transmission)
   • False neurotransmitters (octopamine, phenylethylamines)
   • Increased serotonin, altered dopaminergic signaling
        ↓
Neuronal inhibition dominates → confusion → asterixis → COMA
```
Aggravating factors: GIT hemorrhage (blood = protein load), constipation, high-protein diet, hypokalemic alkalosis (diuretics), infection, sedatives. Other contributing toxins: manganese deposition (basal ganglia), mercaptans, short/medium-chain fatty acids.

### Pathogenesis of coagulopathy in liver failure

```
Massive hepatocyte loss
        ↓
🔴 Failure of synthesis of clotting proteins
   (fibrinogen, prothrombin II, V, VII, IX, X — all except factor VIII)
        ↓
Prolonged PT/INR (factor VII shortest half-life falls FIRST)
        +
Thrombocytopenia (hypersplenism in portal HTN + ↓ hepatic thrombopoietin)
        +
Impaired clearance of fibrinolytic activators + DIC-like consumption
        +
Cholestasis → bile salt lack → vitamin K malabsorption
        ↓
🔴 BLEEDING DIATHESIS (purpura, ecchymoses, variceal and mucosal bleeds)
```
📌 Vitamin K corrects coagulopathy only when cholestatic malabsorption dominates; in pure synthetic failure it does NOT — a favorite viva distinction.

> 🎯 "**Brain: ammonia made by gut, detoxed by liver — liver fails, brain bathes in NH₃ (glutamine-swollen astrocytes, GABA up); Blood: liver makes every factor but VIII — so INR climbs, platelets drop, vitamin K won't fix what the factory forgot.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405

## Q17. Problem-based question: A male of 50 years has presented with weakness, tiredness and hepatomegaly for few months. He has got history of hepatitis B infection few years back. USG revealed Cirrhosis with a SOL in the right lobe of liver. (a) What is your diagnosis? (b) How will you proceed to confirm the diagnosis?

### (a) Diagnosis

🔴 **Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) arising in chronic hepatitis B-related cirrhosis.**

Supporting points:
- Chronic HBV infection — strongest global risk factor for HCC (HBV integration + HBx transactivator; even without cirrhosis).
- Cirrhosis present — 80–85% of HCCs arise in cirrhotic liver.
- Middle-aged male with constitutional symptoms (weakness/tiredness) + hepatomegaly — typical HCC presentation in endemic regions.
- SOL in right lobe on USG in this setting = HCC until proven otherwise.
- Differential diagnoses to mention: metastatic deposit, cholangiocarcinoma, hemangioma/focal nodular hyperplasia, hepatic abscess, regenerative/dysplastic nodule.

### (b) Confirmation plan

| Step | Investigation | Expected findings |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Serology | HBsAg, anti-HCV, HBV DNA PCR | Confirms chronic HBV as underlying disease |
| 2. Tumor marker | 🔴 **Serum alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)** | Elevated (>400 ng/mL strongly suggestive; normal does not exclude — negative in fibrolamellar) |
| 3. 🔴 Dynamic imaging | **Triphasic (multiphasic contrast) CT scan / MRI of liver** | 🔴 **Arterial phase hyperenhancement with rapid venous/delayed washout** — diagnostic of HCC in a cirrhotic liver without biopsy (LI-RADS criteria); assesses number, size, vascular invasion |
| 4. Biochemical assessment | LFTs, PT/INR, albumin, bilirubin, creatinine; Child-Pugh/MELD score | Functional reserve — guides resection vs transplant |
| 5. 🔴 Histology (if imaging atypical/non-LIRADS-5) | **USG-guided core needle biopsy** | Thick trabeculae/pseudoglandular malignant hepatocytes, bile plugs, sinusoidal invasion; IHC: HepPar-1, glypican-3, arginase-1 positive |
| 6. Staging workup | Chest X-ray/CT (lung mets), portal vein patency (Doppler) | Extrahepatic spread, portal vein thrombus |

📌 Note: biopsy avoided if lesion meets radiologic criteria (risk of needle-track seeding/bleeding in vascular tumor).

> 🎯 "**PBQ recipe: HBV + cirrhosis + SOL = HCC until proven otherwise; confirm with AFP + triple-phase CT (washout!) + biopsy only if atypical — 'marker, scanner, needler'.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 18 (Liver and Gallbladder), pp. 823–880 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Liver and Biliary Tract, pp. 192–224 · Unit-II Liver Function Test and Jaundice, pp. 392–405
