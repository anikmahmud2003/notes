# Part A — Eye: Retinoblastoma (Q1)

## Q1. Short note: Retinoblastoma.

### Definition

🔴 **Retinoblastoma = the most common primary intraocular malignancy of CHILDREN** (~3% of childhood cancers; ~1 in 15,000–20,000 live births). Cell of origin = a **neuronal progenitor** cell (not a bipotential "retinoblast"). **~40% are heritable** (germline RB1 mutation); peak age **<3 yr**.

### Pathogenesis — Knudson TWO-HIT hypothesis

🔴 **RB1 tumor-suppressor gene (chromosome 13q14) — the first discovered tumor suppressor gene; both alleles must be inactivated.**

| Feature | Heritable (germline) | Non-heritable (sporadic) |
|---|---|---|
| First hit | **Germline RB1 mutation** (inherited or de novo) | Somatic mutation in one retinal cell |
| Second hit | Somatic loss of the remaining allele (LOH) in a retinal cell | Second somatic hit in the same cell |
| Laterality / multiplicity | Often **bilateral + multifocal** | Usually **unilateral, unifocal** |
| Age at onset | Earlier (<1–2 yr) | Later (2–4 yr) |
| 🔴 Trilateral retinoblastoma | Bilateral RB + **pinealoblastoma** (dismal) | Absent |
| 🔴 Second primary cancers | **Osteosarcoma, soft-tissue sarcomas**, melanoma (radiation-induced in field) | Not increased |

### Morphology

- **Gross:** white mass filling the globe; endophytic (into vitreous) or exophytic (subretinal) growth; 🔴 **focal dystrophic calcification is characteristic** (radio-opaque on CT/ultrasound).
- **Microscopy:** small round blue cells, hyperchromatic nuclei, scant cytoplasm; viable cells **encircle blood vessels with sleeves, beyond which lie zones of necrosis**;
 - 🔴 **Flexner–Wintersteiner rosettes** = tumor cells arranged around a central lumen (photoreceptor differentiation) — characteristic;
 - **Fleurettes** = better-differentiated photoreceptor clusters;
 - Homer Wright rosettes may occur (less specific).

### Clinical features & spread

- 🔴 **Leukocoria ("cat's eye" white pupillary reflex)** — most common presentation; strabismus second; advanced → glaucoma, proptosis.
- Spread: **optic nerve → brain (subarachnoid space)**; choroid → hematogenous to **bone/bone marrow** (lungs rare). 🔴 **Optic-nerve invasion beyond lamina cribrosa = worst prognostic sign**; extraocular extension poor prognosis.
- Treatment: enucleation for large tumors; chemoreduction + laser/cryotherapy preserves vision in smaller tumors. **Survival >90%** with modern therapy.

> 🎯 "Child white pupil (leukocoria) = retinoblastoma → **RB1 two-hit (13q14)** · Flexner-Wintersteiner rosettes + dystrophic calcification · bilateral/pineal = germline · optic nerve invasion = bad."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 29 (The Eye), pp. 1305–1323 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I The Eye, pp. 373–375

# Part B — ENT: Sinusitis & Otitis Media (Q2–6)

## Q2. Describe the pathogenesis of acute sinusitis.

### Definition

🔴 **Acute sinusitis = acute inflammation (<4 weeks) of the mucosa of paranasal sinuses** (maxillary most commonly involved), almost always following viral upper-respiratory infection or allergic rhinitis.

### Pathogenesis (arrow-chain)

```
VIRAL URI / ALLERGIC RHINITIS (rhinovirus, influenza, etc.)
        ↓
MUCOSAL EDEMA + inflammation around the SINUS OSTIUM
(ostia are narrow — esp. maxillary & anterior ethmoid)
        ↓
OSTIAL OBSTRUCTION (blockage of drainage pathway)
        ↓
IMPAIRMENT OF MUCOCILIARY CLEARANCE + stagnant mucus
↓ oxygen tension in closed cavity
        ↓
BACTERIAL SUPERINFECTION of retained secretions
🔴 S. pneumoniae · nontypeable H. influenzae · M. catarrhalis
        ↓
ACUTE PURULENT INFLAMMATION — neutrophils, mucopurulent exudate
→ facial pain/fullness (worse on bending), nasal obstruction,
purulent discharge, fever
```

### Predisposing factors

- Viral URIs, **allergic rhinitis**; nasal polyps, deviated nasal septum, turbinate hypertrophy.
- Dental root infection (maxillary odontogenic sinusitis).
- Impaired ciliary clearance — 🔴 **primary ciliary dyskinesia/Kartagener syndrome** (sinusitis + bronchiectasis + situs inversus), cystic fibrosis.
- Swimming/diving, barotrauma; immunodeficiency; nasogastric tubes.

### Complications (must mention)

- Orbital cellulitis/abscess (via thin lamina papyracea — ethmoid).
- **Osteomyelitis of frontal bone ("Pott's puffy tumor").**
- 🔴 **Septic thrombophlebitis of cavernous/dural venous sinuses**, meningitis, brain abscess.
- ⚠️ In **diabetics**: rhinocerebral **mucormycosis** — angioinvasive fungal infection, emergency.

> 🎯 "**Viral URI → swollen ostium blocked → cilia paralysed + stagnant mucus → pneumococcus/H. flu/Moraxella move in → pus**; diabetics beware Mucor; watch out for orbit and cavernous sinus."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 16 (Head and Neck), pp. 731–751 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Head, Neck and Oral Cavity, pp. 136–143

## Q3. Describe the pathogenesis of chronic sinusitis.

### Definition

🔴 **Chronic sinusitis = inflammation of paranasal sinus mucosa persisting >12 weeks** (some classifications ≥8–12 weeks), with irreversible mucosal changes.

### Pathogenesis (arrow-chain)

```
REPEATED ACUTE EPISODES / UNRESOLVED ACUTE SINUSITIS
(persistent ostial blockage: polyp, deviated septum, allergy)
        ↓
CONTINUED impaired drainage + defective mucociliary clearance
        ↓
Chronic inflammatory infiltrate (lymphocytes, plasma cells,
eosinophils), mucosal thickening, glandular hyperplasia,
FIBROSIS of lamina propria ± polypoid change (edematous stroma
with eosinophils — "allergic" nasal polyps)
        ↓
🔴 POLYMICROBIAL flora incl. anaerobes + BACTERIAL BIOFILMS
on the mucosal surface (antibiotic resistance, persistence)
        ↓
ALLERGIC FUNGAL RHINOSINUSITIS in atopic hosts
(noninvasive fungal hyphae + eosinophilic "allergic mucin")
        ↓
Irreversible changes: polyps, osteitic new bone, mucocele/pyocele
```

### Key points

- **Polymicrobial** — aerobic + **anaerobes** (Peptostreptococcus, Bacteroides); fungi may colonize (**fungal ball/mycetoma**; invasive disease only if immunosuppressed).
- **Biofilms** explain chronicity and failure of standard antibiotics.
- Component of 🔴 **Kartagener syndrome** (ciliary defect); cystic fibrosis (viscid secretions).
- Complications: mucocele formation, bone erosion, orbital/intracranial extension (less common than acute).
- Management logic: treat cause (surgery FESS to restore ostial drainage) + long antibiotics/intranasal steroids.

> 🎯 "**Acute repeats itself until it becomes permanent:** blocked ostium + broken cilia → thickened polypoid fibrotic mucosa + biofilm polymicrobial anaerobes (+ fungus in atopics) → drain it surgically."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 16 (Head and Neck), pp. 731–751 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Head, Neck and Oral Cavity, pp. 136–143

## Q4. Define otitis media. Mention its types.

### Definition

🔴 **Otitis media = inflammation of the middle ear cleft (tympanic cavity, mastoid air cells, eustachian tube)**, predominantly an **infants' and children's disease** because their short, horizontal eustachian tube permits reflux of nasopharyngeal secretions.

### Types

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Serous / secretory otitis media** ("otitis media with effusion", glue ear) | Sterile, thin watery/mucoid effusion behind an intact tympanic membrane after viral URI/allergy/barotrauma/eustachian-tube dysfunction; conductive deafness |
| 🔴 **Acute suppurative otitis media** | Rapid-onset bacterial infection — severe earache, fever, bulging red drum → perforation with otorrhea |
| 🔴 **Chronic suppurative otitis media (CSOM)** | Long-standing (>2 weeks) discharge through a **perforated tympanic membrane**; two varieties: |
| – **Tubotympanic (safe type)** | Central perforation of pars tensa, mucosal disease only, no bone destruction |
| – **Atticoantral (unsafe type)** | Attic/marginal perforation, involves attic-antrum, associated with **cholesteatoma** + ossicle erosion — danger of intracranial complications |
| **Adhesive otitis media** | Fibrous adhesions fixing drum to medial wall after healed effusions |
| **Specific (uncommon)** | Tuberculous otitis media |

📌 **Cholesteatoma** = keratinizing squamous epithelium growing invasively within the middle ear, accumulating keratin debris that erodes bone (ossicles, labyrinth, facial canal) — hallmark of unsafe CSOM.

> 🎯 "**Middle-ear inflammation in kids (horizontal eustachian tube): Serous (sterile glue) → Acute suppurative (pneumo/H.flu/Moraxella) → Chronic suppurative = safe tubotympanic vs UNSAFE atticoantral with cholesteatoma**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 16 (Head and Neck), pp. 731–751 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Head, Neck and Oral Cavity, pp. 136–143

## Q5. Describe the pathogenesis of otitis media.

### A. Acute suppurative otitis media (arrow-chain)

```
VIRAL UPPER RESPIRATORY INFECTION (children)
        ↓
EUSTACHIAN TUBE DYSFUNCTION/OBSTRUCTION (mucosal edema;
short horizontal tube in infants allows direct reflux)
        ↓
NEGATIVE MIDDLE-EAR PRESSURE + aspiration/reflux of
nasopharyngeal secretions into the tympanic cavity
        ↓
Impaired clearance of the trapped secretions
        ↓
BACTERIAL PROLIFERATION → ACUTE SUPPURATIVE INFLAMMATION
🔴 S. pneumoniae · nontypeable H. influenzae · M. catarrhalis
        ↓
Hyperemic bulging drum → severe earache, fever
        ↓
Purulent exudate under pressure → TM RUPTURE (otorrhea),
or spread to mastoid air cells (mastoiditis), rarely inward —
ossicles/labyrinth, temporal cerebritis/abscess, meningitis
```

### B. Serous otitis media

```
Eustachian tube obstruction (viral URI, allergy, adenoid hypertrophy,
barotrauma, tumour in nasopharynx in adults!)
        ↓
Resorption of trapped air → NEGATIVE pressure → transudation
of sterile serous/mucoid fluid ("glue ear") → conductive hearing loss
```

### C. Chronic suppurative otitis media

```
Untreated/recurrent acute attacks OR persistent perforation
        ↓
Chronic mucosal inflammation + discharge through perforated TM
        ↓
Unsafe (atticoantral) variety: retraction pocket/attic perforation
→ ingrowth of keratinizing squamous epithelium = CHOLESTEATOMA
        ↓
Keratin debris + inflammation erode ossicles/bone
→ mastoiditis, facial palsy, labyrinthitis, intracranial spread
```

📌 Organisms shift in chronic disease to **Pseudomonas aeruginosa, S. aureus, fungi**. ⚠️ In diabetics, **Pseudomonas causes aggressive necrotizing ("malignant") external otitis** extending to skull base.

> 🎯 "**Blocked baby eustachian tube sucks in nose bugs** (pneumo > H. flu > Moraxella) → pus → burst drum; sterile negative-pressure version = glue ear; chronicity breeds cholesteatoma (unsafe) and Pseudomonas."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 16 (Head and Neck), pp. 731–751 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Head, Neck and Oral Cavity, pp. 136–143

## Q6. Short note: Chronic suppurative otitis media.

### Definition

🔴 **CSOM = persistent (>2 weeks) purulent discharge from the middle ear through a non-intact (perforated) tympanic membrane**, with chronic inflammation of mucosa and/or bone; sequel of inadequately treated acute otitis media, usually beginning in childhood.

### Two clinicopathological types

| Feature | **Tubotympanic — SAFE type** | **Atticoantral — UNSAFE type** |
|---|---|---|
| Perforation | **Central**, pars tensa | **Attic (marginal)**, pars flaccida/tensa edge |
| Pathology | Mucosa only — congestion, granulations, polyp | Bone-invading: **cholesteatoma** + granulation tissue |
| Discharge | Mucopurulent, intermittent, odorless | Purulent, foul-smelling (keratin debris), continuous |
| Deafness | Mild–moderate conductive | Moderate–severe conductive (ossicular erosion) |
| Complications | Rare | 🔴 Mastoiditis, facial nerve palsy, labyrinthitis, extradural/subdural abscess, **brain abscess, meningitis** |

### Organisms

Mixed: **Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Staphylococcus aureus, Proteus, E. coli, anaerobes** ± fungi.

### Cholesteatoma (key concept)

- Keratinizing squamous epithelium abnormally present in the middle ear (retraction pocket/immigration through perforation/congenital rest).
- Layers: matrix (epithelium) → keratin debris accumulation → **bone erosion by pressure + enzymatic (collagenase) activity**.
- Grossly pearly-white mass; microscopically keratin flakes beneath squamous lining.

### Management principles

Safe type: aural toilet, antibiotics, dry-ear closure (myringoplasty ± cortical mastoidectomy).
Unsafe type: **mastoidectomy (modified radical/radical) to exteriorize/remove cholesteatoma** — prevent intracranial catastrophe.

> 🎯 "**Discharging ear >2 weeks through a hole: SAFE central hole = mucosa only; UNSAFE attic hole + smelly cholesteatoma eats bone → facial palsy & brain abscess → surgery.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 16 (Head and Neck), pp. 731–751 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Head, Neck and Oral Cavity, pp. 136–143

# Part C — CNS Tumours & Degenerative Diseases (Q7–13)

## Q7. Classify brain tumours

### Classification (WHO-based)

| Category | Tumours |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Glial (gliomas)** — most common PRIMARY group | **Astrocytic:** pilocytic astrocytoma (WHO I), diffuse astrocytoma (II), anaplastic astrocytoma (III), **glioblastoma multiforme (IV)**; **Oligodendroglioma** (II/III); **Ependymoma** (II/III; myxopapillary I); subependymoma |
| **Embryonal (primitive neuroectodermal)** | **Medulloblastoma** (cerebellum, children), atypical teratoid/rhabdoid tumor |
| **Neuronal & mixed neuronal-glial** | Ganglioglioma, gangliocytoma, central neurocytoma, DNET (dysembryoplastic neuroepithelial tumor) |
| **Choroid plexus** | Papilloma (I), carcinoma (III) |
| **Pineal parenchymal** | Pineocytoma, pineoblastoma |
| 🔴 **Tumours of meninges** | **Meningioma** (benign, WHO I; atypical II; anaplastic III), hemangiopericytoma |
| **Tumours of cranial/spinal nerves** | 🔴 **Schwannoma** (CN VIII vestibular), neurofibroma, malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumor |
| **Lymphomas/haematopoietic** | Primary CNS lymphoma (EBV+ in immunosuppressed) |
| **Germ cell tumours** | Germinoma (pineal/suprasellar), teratoma |
| **Sellar region** | Pituitary adenoma, craniopharyngioma |
| 🔴 **Metastatic tumours** | 🔴 **The MOST COMMON overall** — lung, breast, melanoma, kidney, GI |

### Adults vs children (EXAM FAVORITE)

| | Adults | Children |
|---|---|---|
| Commonest overall | **Metastases** | Gliomas/pilocytic astrocytoma, medulloblastoma |
| Common sites | Supratentorial | 🔴 **70% infratentorial (posterior fossa)** |
| Typical tumours | GBM, meningioma, schwannoma | Medulloblastoma, pilocytic astrocytoma, ependymoma |

> 🎯 "**Gliomas rule the primary world, metastases rule the whole world** — remember GLIOMAS (astro/oligo/ependy) + MEDULLO + MENINGIOMA + SCHWANNOMA + LYMPHOMA + GERM CELL + METASTASES; kids posterior fossa, adults above."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q8. Discuss the special characteristics of the CNS tumours.

1. 🔴 **No TNM staging** — behaviour is governed by anatomical LOCATION and grade, not by stage; even histologically **BENIGN tumours can kill** by compressing vital medullary centres in the confined, non-expandable skull (a benign posterior-fossa meningioma can be fatal).
2. 🔴 **Location matters more than histology** — a "benign" tumour in an eloquent/inaccessible site (brainstem, motor cortex) may be untreatable, while a malignant one in resectable frontal pole may do well.
3. 🔴 **Infiltrative growth:** diffuse gliomas interdigitate with functioning brain — surgical excision can never be complete → recurrence inevitable; no true capsule.
4. 🔴 **Metastasis outside the CNS is extraordinarily rare** — gliomas practically never leave the neuraxis (short survival + barriers); but spread **within CSF pathways** occurs (medulloblastoma "drop metastases" to cauda equina; "icing" over surfaces), rarely via ventriculoperitoneal shunts.
5. Same histological type varies in prognosis by **age and site** (cerebellar pilocytic astrocytoma curable vs diffuse midline glioma fatal).
6. **Molecular markers now define entities:** IDH mutation, **1p/19q codeletion** (oligodendroglioma), MGMT-promoter methylation (temozolomide response) — incorporated into WHO grading.
7. Incidence ~10–17/100,000; ~20% of childhood cancers; adult tumours mostly supratentorial, childhood mostly posterior fossa.
8. 🔴 **Most common intracranial tumour overall = metastatic carcinoma** (¼–½ of hospitalized cancer patients; lung, breast, melanoma, kidney, GI).

> 🎯 "**No TNM, no capsules, no distant mets** — benign kills by compression, gliomas kill by infiltration, children seed via CSF; location > histology; metastases outnumber everyone."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q9. Short note: Glioma.

### Definition

🔴 **Glioma = the umbrella term for primary brain tumours arising from glial cells** — astrocytes, oligodendrocytes and ependymal cells; together they constitute **the most common group of primary brain neoplasms**.

### Types table

| Tumour | Grade | Molecular | Morphology / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pilocytic astrocytoma** | I | KIAA1549-BRAF fusion | Children; cerebellum/optic nerve/hypothalamus; cyst + mural nodule; **Rosenthal fibers**, eosinophilic granular bodies; resection curative |
| **Diffuse astrocytoma** | II | **IDH-mutant**, TP53, ATRX | Nuclear atypia; infiltrates beyond margin |
| **Anaplastic astrocytoma** | III | same | + mitoses, cellular |
| 🔴 **Glioblastoma multiforme (GBM)** | IV | IDH-wildtype (primary: EGFR amp, TERT, +7/−10) or IDH-mutant (secondary) | **Palisading necrosis + microvascular proliferation**; ring-enhancing; **most common primary malignant brain tumour**; median survival <2 yr |
| 🔴 **Oligodendroglioma** | II/III | 🔴 **IDH-mutant + 1p/19q CODELETION** (diagnostic) | "Fried-egg" clear cells, **chicken-wire capillaries**, calcification; **best-prognosis glioma** (10–20 yr) |
| 🔴 **Ependymoma** | II/III | NF2 (spinal) | **Perivascular pseudorosettes + true rosettes**; 4th ventricle in children (→ hydrocephalus), spinal cord in adults |

📌 Survival ladder: oligodendroglioma II/III (10–20 yr) > astrocytoma II/III (5–15 yr) > GBM IDH-mut (2–4 yr) > GBM IDH-wt (6 mo–2 yr).

> 🎯 "Glioma = glial family — **pilocytic (kidney-shaped Rosenthal fibers, kid's cerebellum), diffuse/anaplastic astrocytoma, GBM (palisading necrosis), oligo (fried egg + chicken-wire + 1p/19q), ependymoma (pseudorosettes)**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q10. Short note: Schwannoma.

### Definition

🔴 **Schwannoma (neurilemmoma) = benign, encapsulated tumour of SCHWANN cells** of cranial/spinal/peripheral nerves; sporadic in 90%; the commonest intracranial site is CN VIII at the cerebellopontine angle (**vestibular/acoustic schwannoma** — tinnitus + sensorineural hearing loss).

### Morphology

| Aspect | Findings |
|---|---|
| Gross | Well-**encapsulated**, firm, yellowish fusiform mass attached to the nerve **eccentrically** — nerve fibers splayed over capsule; separable from nerve at surgery |
| 🔴 **Antoni A pattern** | Compact spindle cells with nuclear palisading → 🔴 **Verocay bodies** (two parallel rows of nuclei surrounding an acellular eosinophilic zone) |
| 🔴 **Antoni B pattern** | Hypocellular loose myxoid tissue with microcysts |
| Other | Thick-walled **hyalinized vessels**; "ancient change" (degenerative nuclear atypia — not malignancy) |
| IHC | Diffuse strong **S-100 protein** (+ SOX10) |

### Genetics & associations

- 🔴 Biallelic loss of **NF2 gene (merlin, chromosome 22q12)**.
- 🔴 **Bilateral vestibular schwannomas = hallmark of NEUROFIBROMATOSIS TYPE 2** (NF2 also → multiple meningiomas, spinal ependymomas).
- Schwannomatosis: multiple NON-vestibular schwannomas (SMARCB1/LZTR1).

### Behaviour

Benign; recurs rarely after complete excision; 🔴 **malignant transformation exceedingly rare (<1%)**; treatment = surgical enucleation preserving the parent nerve.

> 🎯 "**Schwann cell tumour in a capsule sitting beside the nerve: Antoni A palisades make Verocay bodies, Antoni B is myxoid mush, S-100 lights up, NF2/merlin gone — bilateral acoustic = NF2.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q11. Short note: Neurofibroma.

### Definition

🔴 **Neurofibroma = benign peripheral-nerve-sheath tumour containing a MIXED population of Schwann cells, perineurial-like cells, fibroblasts and mast cells, with entrapped axons** — often solitary in normal individuals, multiple when part of **NEUROFIBROMATOSIS TYPE 1 (NF1, neurofibromin, 17q11.2)**.

### Variants

| Variant | Features |
|---|---|
| **Localized cutaneous** (most common) | Solitary nodular skin lesion; not NF1-linked when solitary |
| **Diffuse** | Plaque-like subcutaneous expansion, children, head & neck |
| 🔴 **Plexiform** | Along large deep nerves — nerve expanded into a tortuous rope/"bag of worms"; 🔴 **PATHOGNOMONIC of NF1**; congenital; risk of malignant change |

### Morphology

- Gross: unencapsulated, fusiform **expansion of the whole nerve segment** — cannot be separated from the nerve.
- Microscopy: haphazardly arranged spindle cells in myxoid matrix; 🔴 **"shredded-carrot" collagen** (wispy strands); mast cells; axons run through the lesion; S-100 positive but **patchy** (vs diffuse in schwannoma).

### Behaviour

- Benign and slow-growing; recurrence if incompletely excised.
- 🔴 **Plexiform neurofibroma undergoes malignant transformation to malignant peripheral nerve sheath tumour (MPNST) in 3–5%** — sudden growth/pain = alarm.

> 🎯 "Neurofibroma = **mixed-cell tangle inside the nerve with shredded-carrot collagen** — cutaneous ones are common; **plexiform 'bag-of-worms' screams NF1** and may turn sarcoma."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q12. Difference between Schwannoma and Neurofibroma.

| Feature | **Schwannoma** | **Neurofibroma** |
|---|---|---|
| Cell composition | Pure **Schwann cells** | **Mixed** — Schwann + perineurial-like + fibroblasts + mast cells + axons |
| Relation to nerve | **Eccentric** mass displacing fibers; encapsulated; easily shelled out | Fusiform **expansion incorporating the whole nerve**; unencapsulated; cannot separate |
| Histology | 🔴 **Antoni A (Verocay bodies) + Antoni B**; hyalinized vessels | Haphazard spindles + myxoid stroma + 🔴 **shredded-carrot collagen** |
| S-100 staining | **Diffuse strong** | Patchy/weak |
| Plexiform variant | Absent | 🔴 Present — pathognomonic of NF1 |
| Genetic association | 🔴 **NF2 (merlin, 22q12)**; bilateral vestibular schwannomas | 🔴 **NF1 (neurofibromin, 17q11.2)**; café-au-lait spots, Lisch nodules |
| Multiple lesions | Schwannomatosis (nonvestibular) | Multiple cutaneous in NF1 |
| Malignant change | 🔴 Exceedingly rare (<1%) | **3–5% of plexiform → MPNST** |
| Recurrence | Very low | Higher if incompletely excised |

> 🎯 "**Schwannoma sits BESIDE the nerve (capsule, Verocay, S-100 bright, NF2); neurofibroma SWELLS the whole nerve (mixed cells, shredded carrot, patchy S-100, NF1, plexiform → sarcoma).**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q13. What are the degenerative diseases of CNS?

### Concept

Progressive, age-related loss of **specific neuronal populations** with **accumulation of misfolded protein aggregates (proteinopathies)**; no inflammation; symmetrical atrophy.

### The list (grouped)

| Group | Disease | Protein/inclusion | Key feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dementias | 🔴 **Alzheimer disease** | Aβ plaques + tau tangles | Most common dementia; hippocampal atrophy |
| | Frontotemporal lobar degeneration / **Pick disease** | tau (Pick bodies), TDP-43, C9orf72 | Personality/language first |
| | Dementia with Lewy bodies | α-synuclein | Fluctuating cognition, visual hallucinations |
| Movement disorders | 🔴 **Parkinson disease** | α-synuclein **Lewy bodies** | Loss of substantia nigra neurons; tremor-rigidity-bradykinesia |
| | 🔴 **Huntington disease** | Polyglutamine huntingtin (CAG, chr 4) | AD; caudate-putamen atrophy; chorea + dementia |
| Cerebellar/spinal | Spinocerebellar ataxias (SCA1–17), Friedreich ataxia (frataxin GAA), ataxia-telangiectasia (ATM) | Polyglutamine etc. | Progressive ataxia |
| Motor neuron | 🔴 **Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)** | TDP-43/SOD1/C9orf72 | UMN + LMN loss, fasciculations |
| Prion diseases | 🔴 **Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD)**, variant CJD, kuru, fatal familial insomnia | **PrP^Sc** (protease-resistant prion protein) | Spongiform change; rapidly progressive dementia + myoclonus; transmissible |

> 🎯 "**Degeneratives die of their own proteins:** AD (Aβ+tau) · Pick (tau) · Parkinson/DLB (α-synuclein Lewy) · Huntington (CAG huntingtin) · SCAs/Friedreich (ataxia) · ALS (TDP-43) · CJD (prion sponge)."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

# Part D — Meningitis & CSF Examination (Q14–23)

## Q14. Mention the indications of CSF examination.

### Diagnostic indications

| System | Indication |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Infections** | Suspected **meningitis (pyogenic/TB/fungal)**, encephalitis, neurosyphilis — identify organism, cells, chemistry |
| 🔴 **Subarachnoid hemorrhage** | CT-negative but clinically suspected SAH → xanthochromia, RBCs |
| **Demyelinating disease** | **Multiple sclerosis** — oligoclonal bands, ↑IgG index |
| **Peripheral neuropathy** | **Guillain-Barré** — albuminocytologic dissociation (↑protein, normal cells) |
| **Malignancy** | Leptomeningeal metastasis/primary CNS lymphoma — **cytology**, flow cytometry |
| **Dementia work-up** | Alzheimer biomarkers (↓Aβ42, ↑total/ phosphorylated tau) |
| **Raised ICP / hydrocephalus** | Measure **opening pressure**; idiopathic intracranial hypertension; normal-pressure hydrocephalus assessment |
| **Autoimmune/paraneoplastic** | Anti-NMDAR/paraneoplastic antibody panels |

### Therapeutic indications

- 🔴 **Intrathecal drug administration** — methotrexate/cytarabine (leukemia CNS prophylaxis), amphotericin B (fungal meningitis), spinal anesthesia, analgesics.
- **Therapeutic removal of CSF** — IIH (pseudotumor cerebri) serial taps, NPH tap test.
- Instillation of contrast medium (myelography/cisternography).

> 🎯 "**LP for diagnosis (meningitis, SAH, MS, GBS, cytology, pressure) and for treatment (intrathecal drugs, therapeutic taps).**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q15. What is meningitis? What are the types?

### Definition

🔴 **Meningitis = inflammation of the meninges (leptomeninges — pia and arachnoid — and the CSF within the subarachnoid space)**, clinically marked by fever, headache, neck stiffness and photophobia; meningeal irritation gives **Kernig and Brudzinski signs**.

### Types

| Type | Cause/character |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Acute pyogenic (bacterial) meningitis** | Neutrophilic purulent exudate; organisms vary by age; medical emergency |
| 🔴 **Acute aseptic (viral) meningitis** | Lymphocytic; usually enteroviruses; self-limited; glucose NORMAL |
| 🔴 **Chronic bacterial meningitis** | **Tuberculous** (basal gelatinous exudate), syphilitic, brucellar |
| **Fungal meningitis** | Cryptococcus (esp. AIDS), Candida, Coccidioides |
| **Parasitic/amoebic** | Naegleria fowleri (primary amoebic meningoencephalitis), eosinophilic (Angiostrongylus) |
| **Non-infectious (chemical/carcinomatous)** | Ruptured epidermoid/dermoid, NSAIDs, IVIG; leptomeningeal carcinomatosis; SLE, Behçet, sarcoid |

> 🎯 "Meninges inflamed → **PYOGENIC (pus, PMN, sugar ↓↓) · ASEPTIC/viral (lymphocytes, sugar NORMAL) · CHRONIC-TB (basal exudate, spider-web) · FUNGAL (crypto in AIDS) · chemical/carcinomatous**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q16. What are the causes of meningitis?

### Bacterial — by age (Robbins Table, EXAM FAVORITE)

| Age group | Organisms |
|---|---|
| **Neonates (0–2 mo)** | 🔴 **E. coli, group B Streptococcus**, Listeria monocytogenes |
| Infants/children | **Haemophilus influenzae type b** (↓ by vaccination), S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis |
| **Adolescents/young adults** | 🔴 **Neisseria meningitidis** (meningococcus — epidemics, petechial rash, Waterhouse-Friderichsen) |
| **Older adults/elderly** | 🔴 **Streptococcus pneumoniae, Listeria monocytogenes** |
| Special settings | Post-neurosurgery/shunt: staphylococci, gram-negatives; asplenia → pneumococcus; complement deficiency → meningococcus; immunocompromised → Listeria, TB, Cryptococcus |

### Viruses (aseptic)

- 🔴 **Enteroviruses (~80%** — echovirus, coxsackievirus), mumps, **HSV-2** (adult meningitis)/HSV-1, VZV, arboviruses (West Nile), HIV (acute), lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus.

### Others

- **Fungi:** 🔴 *Cryptococcus neoformans* (immunosuppressed), Candida, Aspergillus, Histoplasma, Coccidioides.
- **Parasites:** Naegleria fowleri, Angiostrongylus cantonensis (eosinophilic), Toxoplasma, cysticerci.
- **Non-infectious:** SLE, Behçet, sarcoidosis, drugs (NSAIDs, trimethoprim, IVIG, antibiotics), ruptured craniopharyngioma/dermoid (chemical), carcinomatous meningitis.

> 🎯 "**By AGE: newborn = E. coli + Group B strep; teen = MENINGOCOCCUS; old = PNEUMOCOCCUS + Listeria; infant = H. flu** — viruses = enterovirus; AIDS = cryptococcus."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q17. What are the normal findings of CSF? When spider clot web is formed in CSF?

### Normal CSF findings (lumbar, adult)

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Appearance | 🔴 Clear, colorless, does NOT clot |
| Pressure (lateral decubitus) | **60–180 mm H₂O** |
| Total cells | 🔴 **0–5 cells/µL — ALL lymphocytes** (no neutrophils, no RBCs) |
| Total protein | **15–45 mg/dL** |
| Glucose | 🔴 **50–80 mg/dL ≈ ⅔ of simultaneous blood glucose** (ratio 0.5–0.6) |
| Chloride | 120–130 mEq/L |
| Other | No fibrinogen → no coagulation; IgG index low; LDH low |

### Spider-web (cobweb) clot

🔴 **A fine cobweb/spider-web coagulum forms when CSF protein (fibrinogen) is markedly elevated** — the meningeal inflammation lets plasma proteins including fibrinogen leak in; on standing, fibrin strands weave a delicate web (classically over hours).

- 🔴 **Classic setting = TUBERCULOUS meningitis** (protein often >100–500 mg/dL) — the web may trap AFB, raising smear yield.
- Also seen whenever CSF protein is very high: Froin syndrome (meningeal block — xanthochromia + high protein + spontaneous coagulation), complete spinal block, pyogenic meningitis (heavier clot).

> 🎯 "**Normal CSF = crystal-clear, 60–180 mm water pressure, 0–5 lymphocytes, protein 15–45, sugar ⅔ of blood**; SPIDER-WEB = leaked fibrinogen from HIGH PROTEIN → think TB meningitis."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q18. State the CSF findings of different types of meningitis.

### Comparison table (EXAM MUST-KNOW)

| Parameter | 🔴 Pyogenic (bacterial) | 🔴 TB (chronic) | Viral (aseptic) | Fungal (cryptococcal) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Appearance | 🔴 **Turbid/purulent**; heavy clot | Clear/opalescent, 🔴 **spider-web clot** | 🔴 Clear | Clear; viscous stringy (capsule) |
| Pressure | ↑↑ (often >250 mm H₂O) | ↑ | Normal/slightly ↑ | ↑ |
| Cells/µL | 🔴 **100–90,000 NEUTROPHILS** (early may be lympho.) | **10–500 LYMPHOCYTES** (persistently raised) | **10–300 lymphocytes** | **10–500 lymphocytes** |
| Protein mg/dL | 🔴 ↑↑ (100–1000) | 🔴 ↑↑ (100–500+) | Normal/mildly ↑ (45–100) | Moderately ↑ |
| Glucose mg/dL | 🔴 **↓↓ <40 (<40% of serum)** | 🔴 **↓ (often <50% serum)** | 🔴 **NORMAL (>60% serum)** | ↓↓ (markedly low in crypto) |
| Chloride | Normal/slight ↓ | 🔴 ↓↓ (<110 mEq/L) | Normal | ↓ |
| Direct microscopy/culture | **Gram stain + culture** — organism seen/grown | **ZN stain for AFB** (low yield), culture LJ/MGIT, GeneXpert | PCR (enterovirus), serology | 🔴 **India ink**, CrAg, culture |

📌 Additional discriminators: lactate ↑ in bacterial; adenosine deaminase ↑ in TB; neutrophilia + CRP/procalcitonin in blood support bacterial etiology.

> 🎯 "**PUS: turbid, thousands of polys, sugar nearly zero; TB: clear + spider web, lymphocytes, protein sky-high, sugar & chloride down; VIRUS: everything mild except sugar stays NORMAL; FUNGUS: India ink + low sugar.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q19. Give the laboratory diagnosis of tubercular meningitis.

### Step 1 — CSF analysis (3 consecutive tubes collected at LP)

| Test | Finding in TBM |
|---|---|
| Macroscopy | Clear/xanthochromic; 🔴 **fine spider-web coagulum on standing** |
| Cell count | **Lymphocytic pleocytosis 10–500/µL** (early neutrophils possible) |
| Protein | 🔴 Markedly elevated **100–500 mg/dL** (higher if block) |
| Glucose | 🔴 Reduced (<50% of paired blood glucose); chloride ↓ |
| Adenosine deaminase (ADA) | Elevated (>10 U/L) — supportive, not specific |

### Step 2 — Microbiology (definitive)

| Method | Yield/time |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Ziehl-Neelsen stain for AFB** on centrifuged deposit (esp. from the spider-web clot; large volume/repeated LPs improve yield) | Smear positivity only **~10–20%** — low yield |
| 🔴 **Culture on Löwenstein-Jensen medium (gold standard)** | Positive in weeks — **6–8 weeks** incubation; allows drug sensitivity |
| Liquid culture **MGIT/BACTEC** | Faster (1–3 weeks) + DST |
| 🔴 **PCR / GeneXpert MTB-RIF Ultra** | Detects MTB DNA + **rifampicin resistance in ~2 h**; WHO-recommended first-line on CSF |

### Step 3 — Supportive work-up

- **Chest X-ray** — primary complex/miliary shadows (source focus); sputum/gastric aspirate AFB (especially children).
- **Mantoux test** (48–72 h induration), ESR/CRP raised, hyponatremia (SIADH).
- **HIV testing** (TBM risk group); fundus for choroid tubercles.
- CT/MRI: basal enhancement, hydrocephalus, infarcts.
- Exclude mimics: India ink/CrAg for cryptococcus; cytology for malignancy.

> 🎯 "**TB-CSF triad: lymphocytes + protein ↑↑ + sugar/chloride ↓ + spider web** → confirm with **ZN smear (poor yield), LJ culture 6–8 wk, and GeneXpert (fast + RIF resistance)**; ADA, Mantoux and chest X-ray back you up."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q20. Give the laboratory diagnosis of pyogenic meningitis.

### Blood investigations

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| CBC | Leukocytosis with **neutrophilia**, toxic granules |
| 🔴 **Blood culture** | Positive in 50–75% — drawn BEFORE antibiotics |
| CRP/procalcitonin | Markedly raised (bacterial marker) |
| Paired serum glucose | Needed to interpret CSF:blood ratio |

### CSF findings (the diagnostic core)

| Parameter | Finding |
|---|---|
| Appearance | 🔴 **Turbid/purulent**; ↑ opening pressure |
| Cells | 🔴 **Neutrophilic pleocytosis — hundreds to thousands/mm³ (may reach 90,000)** |
| Protein | 🔴 Elevated (100–1000 mg/dL) |
| Glucose | 🔴 **Markedly reduced — <40 mg/dL, less than 40% of paired serum glucose** |
| Lactate | Raised |

### Specific identification of the organism

| Method | Detail |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Gram stain** | Gram-negative diplococci (N. meningitidis), gram-positive diplococci (S. pneumoniae), gram-positive cocci in chains/clusters — positive in 60–90% before antibiotics |
| 🔴 **Culture & sensitivity** (CSF + blood) | Gold standard — identifies organism + antibiotic susceptibilities |
| **Latex agglutination** antigen detection (pneumococcal, meningococcal A/C/Y/W135, Hib) | Useful after partial antibiotic pretreatment |
| **PCR (16S rRNA/multiplex)** | Rapid, high sensitivity post-antibiotics |
| Skin scrapings/biopsy of petechiae | May show meningococci (meningococcemia) |

> 🎯 "**Cloudy CSF + thousands of neutrophils + protein up + sugar crashed (<40% of blood) + GRAM STAIN & CULTURE name the killer**; add latex agglutination/PCR if antibiotics already started; always take blood cultures first."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q21. Write the indications, contraindications and complications of lumbar puncture.

### Indications

| Diagnostic | Therapeutic |
|---|---|
| 🔴 CSF study for suspected **meningitis/encephalitis** | 🔴 Intrathecal drugs — chemotherapy (methotrexate), amphotericin, anesthesia |
| SAH (CT-negative), demyelination (oligoclonal bands), GBS, malignancy (cytology), dementia biomarkers | Removal of CSF — IIH, NPH tap test |
| Measure opening pressure | Myelographic contrast instillation |

### Contraindications

| Type | Details |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Raised intracranial tension with risk of coning** | Space-occupying lesion with papilledema, focal neurological deficits or altered sensorium → 🔴 **CT head BEFORE LP** (avoid herniation); obstructive hydrocephalus |
| **Infection at puncture site** | Local skin/septic focus → risk of carrying infection into the canal (epidural abscess) |
| 🔴 **Coagulopathy** | Thrombocytopenia (<50,000), anticoagulants, hemophilia, DIC → spinal hematoma |
| **Suspected spinal cord compression/epidural abscess below the needle site** | Image first |
| Cardiorespiratory compromise preventing safe positioning | Relative |

### Complications

1. 🔴 **Post-LP (post–dural-puncture) headache** — most COMMON (10–30%); low-pressure headache worse upright, relieved lying flat; managed by bed rest, fluids, analgesics, caffeine, epidural blood patch.
2. 🔴 **Cerebral/tonsillar herniation (coning)** — most FEARED; follows LP in raised ICT with focal lesion.
3. **Infection** — local sepsis, arachnoiditis, discitis, iatrogenic meningitis.
4. **Bleeding** — spinal subdural/epidural hematoma → cord compression emergency.
5. **Nerve root injury/radiculopathy, transient leg pain/back pain**; late **epidermoid tumor** if stylet technique not used.

> 🎯 "**Do it for diagnosis & intrathecal drugs; DON'T do it if the brain is full (CT first!), skin is dirty, or blood won't clot** — fear CONING most, headache most often."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q22. Tell the sites of CSF fluid aspiration.

### Principal site — lumbar cistern

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Interspace** | **L3–L4 or L4–L5 interspinous space** — BELOW the conus medullaris (ends at L1–L2 in adults, L3 in infants) so the cord is not injured; cauda equina nerve roots float aside |
| Surface landmark | Line joining highest points of iliac crests (intercristal/**Tuffier line**) crosses L4 spinous process |
| Position | **Left lateral decubitus, knee-chest flexion** (or sitting, flexed) — opens interspinous spaces; lateral position needed for accurate opening pressure |

### Alternative/additional sites

- 🔴 **Cisternal puncture (cisterna magna, suboccipital C1–C2 region)** — when lumbar access impossible (blockage, infection) or myelography; more hazardous.
- 🔴 **Ventricular tap through the anterior fontanelle** — in INFANTS with open fontanelles (open sutures allow direct ventricular puncture); also via burr hole/Ommaya reservoir in shunted patients.
- From a **ventriculoperitoneal shunt reservoir** for sampling.
- (Historically: atlanto-occipital route.)

> 🎯 "**Needle between L3–L4/L4–L5 below the cord's end — patient curled in left lateral position; babies get fontanelle taps; stuck cases use the cisterna magna.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

## Q23. A male of 27 years presented to you with high fever and unconsciousness. His neck rigidity and Kernig's sign are positive. The spinal tap yielded turbid CSF with increased pressure. What is the likely diagnosis? How to confirm it?

### Likely diagnosis

🔴 **Acute PYOGENIC (bacterial) meningitis** — a medical emergency.

**Reasoning from the stem (say these in viva):**

| Clue | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Young adult + high fever | Acute infection |
| Unconsciousness | Cerebral involvement/toxic encephalopathy |
| 🔴 **Neck rigidity + Kernig sign positive** | Meningeal irritation (meningism) |
| 🔴 **TURBID CSF with increased pressure** | Purulent (bacterial) exudate — clear fluid would suggest viral/TB |

Likely organisms at this age: **Neisseria meningitidis** (look for petechial rash) or **Streptococcus pneumoniae**.

### How to confirm

1. 🔴 **CSF analysis:** turbid appearance, opening pressure ↑, **neutrophilic pleocytosis** (hundreds–thousands/µL), **protein ↑, glucose ↓↓ (<40 mg/dL, <40% of paired blood glucose)** — confirms bacterial meningitis.
2. 🔴 **Gram stain of centrifuged CSF deposit** — shows the organism (gram-negative diplococci / gram-positive diplococci) in 60–90% before antibiotics.
3. 🔴 **CSF culture & sensitivity** (± blood cultures taken before antibiotics) — gold standard; latex agglutination antigen tests and multiplex PCR help if antibiotics were already given.
4. Supporting: CBC neutrophilic leukocytosis, ↑CRP/procalcitonin, CT head if focal signs/impaired consciousness before LP.

⚠️ **Do not delay treatment:** after drawing blood/CSF cultures, start **empirical IV third-generation cephalosporin (ceftriaxone) ± vancomycin + dexamethasone immediately**.

> 🎯 "**Fever + stiff neck + Kernig + TURBID CSF = pyogenic meningitis → prove it: neutrophils↑↑, protein↑, glucose↓↓, GRAM STAIN + CULTURE (and blood cultures) → antibiotics without waiting.**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469

# Part E — Cerebrovascular Disease (Q24)

## Q24. Define CVD. Mention the types with short pathogenesis.

### Definition

🔴 **Cerebrovascular disease (CVD) = any abnormality of the brain caused by a pathological process involving its blood vessels** — chiefly thrombotic or embolic arterial occlusion, systemic hypoperfusion, or vessel rupture with hemorrhage.

📌 **Stroke** = sudden onset of focal/global neurologic deficit of vascular origin persisting **>24 h**; resolving within 24 h = **transient ischemic attack (TIA)**.

### Types with pathogenesis

| Type | Subtype | Short pathogenesis |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **ISCHEMIC stroke (~85%)** | **Thrombotic** | Atherosclerotic plaque (HTN/diabetes) ulcerates at 🔴 carotid bifurcation, MCA origin, ends of basilar → platelet-fibrin thrombus → occlusion → pale infarct |
| | **Embolic** | 🔴 Emboli from cardiac mural thrombi (**atrial fibrillation**, MI, valves), carotid plaques, paradoxical/fat/air → lodge at branch points, 🔴 **MCA territory most affected**; often hemorrhagic on reperfusion |
| | **Systemic hypoperfusion / watershed** | Shock/cardiac arrest → global ischemia → 🔴 bilateral **watershed (ACA-MCA borderzone)** infarcts; selective vulnerability CA1, Purkinje cells |
| | **Lacunar (small-vessel)** | 🔴 Hypertension → lipohyalinosis/hyaline arteriolosclerosis of 40–900 µm vessels → thrombosis → small cavitated infarcts **<15 mm** in putamen, thalamus, internal capsule, pons |
| | Venous sinus thrombosis | Hypercoagulable states → superior sagittal/deep vein thrombosis → hemorrhagic venous infarct |
| 🔴 **HEMORRHAGIC (~15%)** | **Intraparenchymal (intracerebral)** | 🔴 Chronic hypertension → hyaline arteriolosclerosis + **Charcot-Bouchard microaneurysms** rupture (putamen/thalamus 50–60%, pons, cerebellum) → clotted hematoma destroying brain; lobar bleeds from **cerebral amyloid angiopathy** |
| | **Subarachnoid hemorrhage** | 🔴 Ruptured **saccular (berry) aneurysm** of circle of Willis (90% anterior circulation branch points; wall lacks media + elastic lamina; assoc. ADPKD, Ehlers-Danlos, HTN, smoking) → sudden "worst headache", blood in SAS → vasospasm, hydrocephalus; 25–50% die with first bleed |
| | Vascular malformations | **Arteriovenous malformation** (young males, seizure/ICH) rupture |

> 🎯 "CVD = brain trouble from vessels — **BLOCKED (thrombus on plaque, embolus from AF heart, shock watershed, HTN lacunes) or BURST (Charcot-Bouchard hypertensive bleed into basal ganglia; berry aneurysm SAH — worst headache ever).**"

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 28 (Central Nervous System), pp. 1241–1304 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Central Nervous System, pp. 363–372 · Unit-II Body Fluids (CSF), pp. 456–469
