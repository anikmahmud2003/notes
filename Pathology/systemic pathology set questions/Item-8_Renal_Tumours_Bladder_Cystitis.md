## Q1. Classify kidney tumours.

### Definition

🔴 **Tumours of the kidney are neoplasms arising from renal parenchymal or pelvic urothelial tissues, classified broadly into BENIGN and MALIGNANT groups** — in adults **renal cell carcinoma (85% of adult renal cancers)** dominates, while in children **Wilms tumour (nephroblastoma)** is the most important.

### Classification of kidney tumours

| Group | Tumours |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Benign epithelial tumours** | **Renal papillary adenoma** (<1.5 cm, cortical, trisomies 7 & 17), **Oncocytoma** (intercalated-cell origin, mahogany-brown, packed mitochondria, central scar in ⅓), **Metanephric adenoma** (rare) |
| 🔴 **Benign mesenchymal tumours** | **Angiomyolipoma** (vessels + smooth muscle + fat; 25–50% associated with **tuberous sclerosis**, TSC1/TSC2; risk of spontaneous haemorrhage), leiomyoma, fibroma, haemangioma |
| 🔴 **Malignant epithelial — Renal cell carcinoma subtypes** | 🔴 **Clear cell (conventional) RCC — 70–80%** · **Papillary RCC — 10–15%** · **Chromophobe RCC — ~5% (best prognosis)** · **Collecting duct (Bellini) carcinoma ≤1%** · **Xp11 translocation (TFE3) carcinoma** · Renal **medullary carcinoma** (sickle-cell trait) · Sarcomatoid change (dedifferentiation of any subtype — ominous) |
| **Malignant — embryonal (childhood)** | 🔴 **Wilms tumour (nephroblastoma)** — most common primary renal tumour of childhood |
| **Malignant — urothelial** | 🔴 **Transitional cell (urothelial) carcinoma of renal pelvis/calyces** (5–10% of primary renal tumours; 50% have a concomitant bladder tumour; ↑ in Lynch syndrome) |
| **Malignant mesenchymal (rare)** | Leiomyosarcoma, angiosarcoma, rhabdomyosarcoma, synovial sarcoma |
| **Others / secondary** | Primary renal lymphoma, plasma cell neoplasm; **metastases to kidney** (lung, breast, melanoma) |

### Renal cell carcinoma subtypes at a glance (exam gold)

| Subtype | % | Origin | Genetics / marker | Morphology clue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Clear cell** | **70–80%** | Proximal tubule | **Loss of 3p (VHL gene, 3p25.3) in 98%** → HIF pathway | Bright **yellow** cut surface, glycogen/lipid-rich clear cytoplasm, delicate vascular network; **invades renal vein/IVC** |
| **Papillary** | 10–15% | Distal convoluted tubule | **Trisomies 7 & 17, loss of Y; activating MET mutations** (hereditary + sporadic) | Papillae with **foamy macrophages in cores**, psammoma bodies; **multifocal/bilateral** |
| **Chromophobe** | ~5% | **Intercalated cells** of collecting duct | **Multiple chromosome losses, extreme hypodiploidy** | Pale eosinophilic cells, prominent cell borders, **perinuclear halo**; **best prognosis** |
| **Collecting duct (Bellini)** | ≤1% | Medullary collecting duct | No distinct cytogenetics | Tubular/glandular structures in **desmoplastic stroma, hobnail cells**; aggressive |
| **Xp11 translocation** | Rare | — | **TFE3 (Xp11.2) fusion** → nuclear TFE3 | Young patients; clear cytoplasm + papillae |

### Pathogenesis (molecular chains)

```text
CLEAR CELL RCC:
Germline or somatic VHL inactivation (3p25.3, two hits)
   → failure of ubiquitin-ligase-mediated degradation of HIF-1α/HIF-2α
   → HIF active even in normoxia
   → ↑ VEGF (angiogenesis — richly vascular tumour) + ↑ TGF-α/IGF (autocrine growth)
   + MYC-driven metabolic shift (glycolysis, lipid accumulation)
   → clear cytoplasm → clear cell RCC

PAPILLARY RCC:
Activating MET mutation (chr 7) / trisomies 7 & 17 + loss of Y
   → hepatocyte growth factor (scatter factor) signalling
   → tubulopapillary proliferation with foamy macrophage cores → papillary RCC

CHROMOPHOBE RCC:
Accumulating losses of many chromosomes (extreme hypodiploidy)
   → altered mitochondrial/vesicular content → pale cells with perinuclear halos
```

### Clinical significance

- 🔴 **Clear cell RCC is the prototype** — vascular, yellow, vein-invading, paraneoplastic ("the great mimic").
- **Chromophobe carries the best prognosis; collecting duct and sarcomatoid variants the worst.**

> 🎯 **Remember:** "Benign = **AOA** (**A**denoma, **O**ncocytoma, **A**ngiomyolipoma); Malignant adult = **CPC** — **C**lear (VHL/yellow), **P**apillary (MET/foam), **C**hromophobe (halo/best prognosis); Child = Wilms; Pelvis = Urothelial."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 20 (Kidney), pp. 895–952 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Renal System, pp. 225–249

## Q2. Short Note: Wilms tumour.

### Definition

🔴 **Wilms tumour (nephroblastoma) is a malignant embryonal neoplasm derived from nephrogenic blastemal cells that recapitulates stages of normal renal development (metanephric blastema), characterised histologically by a TRIPHASIC pattern of blastema, stroma and epithelium.** It is the **most common primary renal tumour of childhood** (incidence ~1 in 10,000; **peak age 2–5 years**; 95% before 10 yr; 5–10% bilateral → germline predisposition).

### Genetics / associations

| Syndrome | Features | Genetics |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **WAGR syndrome** | **W**ilms + **A**niridia + **G**U anomalies + intellectual **R**etardation (~33% risk of Wilms) | Constitutional **deletion 11p13** → loss of **WT1** + **PAX6** (PAX6 alone → aniridia only) |
| 🔴 **Denys-Drash syndrome** | Gonadal dysgenesis (male pseudohermaphroditism) + early nephropathy (**diffuse mesangial sclerosis**) | **Dominant-negative WT1 missense** mutation; risk up to **~90%**; also gonadoblastoma |
| 🔴 **Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome** | Organomegaly, **macroglossia**, hemihypertrophy, omphalocele, adrenal cytomegaly | **11p15.5 (WT2 locus)** — **IGF2 loss of imprinting / paternal uniparental disomy** |
| **Sporadic (~90%)** | — | **CTNNB1 (β-catenin)** gain-of-function (~10%); **DROSHA/DGCR8/DICER1** miRNA-processing genes (15–20%); **TP53** mutation marks **anaplasia** |

### Pathogenesis

```text
Two-hit (Knudson) model of tumour-suppressor genes:
Germline WT1 deletion/mutation (first hit)
   → somatic second hit in a nephrogenic rest cell
   → loss of transcription-factor control of renal development genes
   → persistence of undifferentiated blastema → clonal proliferation
   → triphasic tumour (blastema + stroma + epithelium)
β-catenin (WNT pathway) activation and miRNA-processing gene defects cooperate;
TP53 mutation drives ANAPLASIA (chemoresistance).
```

📌 **Nephrogenic rests** = precursor lesions of Wilms tumour (present in 25–40% of unilateral cases, virtually 100% of bilateral cases) → increased risk of contralateral tumour.

### Morphology

- **Gross:** large, solitary, well-circumscribed, **tan-grey** mass replacing the pole of the kidney, with areas of haemorrhage, necrosis and cysts; compresses adjacent parenchyma.
- **Microscopic (triphasic — must say in viva):**
  - 🔴 **Blastema** — sheets of small round blue cells;
  - **Stroma** — spindle cells ± heterologous differentiation (**skeletal muscle, cartilage, bone, fat**);
  - **Epithelial** — abortive **tubules and primitive glomeruli**.
  - 🔴 **Anaplasia (~5%)**: enlarged hyperchromatic pleomorphic nuclei + multipolar abnormal mitoses → **TP53 mutated, chemoresistant, worst prognosis**.

### Clinical features & lab diagnosis

- Mother notices a **large abdominal/flank mass** (may cross midline); **haematuria, abdominal pain, hypertension** (renin secretion); pulmonary metastases at diagnosis.
- **Lab/imaging:** urinalysis (haematuria), ultrasound/CT abdomen (solid intrarenal mass), chest imaging for mets; definitive diagnosis = **histopathology** (biopsy/nephrectomy specimen).

### Prognosis

- 🔴 Cure rate **~90%** (surgery + chemotherapy; historically 30%). Adverse factors: **anaplasia**, LOH of **1p and 16q**, gain of 1q; late second malignancies after radiotherapy.

> 🎯 **Remember:** "Wilms = **childhood kidney mass, 2–5 yr** — **BSE triphasic** (**B**lastema, **S**troma, **E**pithelium); **WAGR = 11p13 WT1+PAX6**, Denys-Drash = WT1 missense, Beckwith = 11p15 IGF2; **Anaplasia = TP53 = bad**; still ~90% curable."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 20 (Kidney), pp. 895–952 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Renal System, pp. 225–249

## Q3. Renal cell carcinoma: Risk factors, Classification, Morphology / Pathology, C/F, Lab Diagnosis.

### Definition

🔴 **Renal cell carcinoma (RCC, hypernephroma/Grawitz tumour) is a primary malignant epithelial neoplasm of the kidney arising from the renal tubular epithelium, accounting for ~85% of adult renal cancers, characterised by a yellow cut surface, rich vascularity, propensity to invade the renal vein, and diverse paraneoplastic manifestations ("the great mimic").** Peak incidence **6th–7th decade; male : female = 2 : 1**.

### Risk factors

| Category | Factors |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Chemical/lifestyle** | **Cigarette smoking (most important — doubles the risk)**, asbestos, petroleum products, heavy metals (cadmium, lead) |
| 🔴 **Host factors** | **Obesity (especially in women)**, **hypertension**, unopposed oestrogen therapy, age >50 yr, male sex |
| 🔴 **Acquired renal disease** | **End-stage renal disease with acquired (dialysis-associated) cystic disease** (up to 100-fold ↑ risk) |
| **Hereditary syndromes (~4%)** | 🔴 **von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) syndrome** — bilateral/multiple clear cell RCC + retinal/cerebellar haemangioblastoma; **Hereditary papillary RCC** (activated **MET**); **Hereditary leiomyomatosis & RCC** (**fumarate hydratase**, aggressive papillary); **Birt-Hogg-Dubé** (**folliculin/BHD**, pulmonary cysts + skin lesions); **Tuberous sclerosis** (angiomyolipoma ± RCC) |

### Classification (WHO-based, by histological subtype)

*(See Q1 table — clear cell 70–80% > papillary 10–15% > chromophobe ~5% > collecting duct ≤1%, Xp11/TFE3 translocation carcinoma, renal medullary carcinoma; any subtype may show **sarcomatoid dedifferentiation** — an ominous sign.)*

### Pathogenesis

```text
CLEAR CELL (prototype):
VHL tumour suppressor inactivation — somatic biallelic loss (98% have loss of 3p)
   → no degradation of HIF-1α/HIF-2α (ubiquitin-ligase function lost)
   → constitutive hypoxia response even at normal O2
   → ↑VEGF → intense neoangiogenesis (delicate "chicken-wire" vessels)
   → ↑TGF-α, IGF, GLUT1, glycolytic switch + lipid/glycogen storage
   → clear-cell phenotype

PAPILLARY:
MET (hepatocyte growth factor receptor) activation / trisomy 7 & 17, −Y
   → papillary proliferation with foamy macrophages

CHROMOPHOBE:
Multiple chromosome losses (extreme hypodiploidy) → perinuclear halo, best prognosis
```

### Morphology

**Gross:**
- Usually solitary, unilateral, spherical mass, **commonly at the upper pole**; size variable (often >5 cm when symptomatic).
- Cut surface: 🔴 **bright yellow-tan, variegated with areas of haemorrhage, necrosis, cystic degeneration**; margins push into surrounding parenchyma.
- 🔴 Characteristically **invades the renal vein** — a solid column of tumour may extend along the **inferior vena cava, sometimes reaching the right heart**.

**Microscopic:**
| Subtype | Microscopy |
|---|---|
| **Clear cell** | Nests/trabeculae/acini of polygonal cells with **clear (glycogen + lipid-rich) cytoplasm**, small central nuclei, surrounded by **delicate thin-walled sinusoidal vessels** |
| **Papillary** | Papillary fronds with fibrovascular cores containing **foamy lipid-laden macrophages** ± psammoma bodies; cuboidal/columnar cells |
| **Chromophobe** | Large pale cells, **prominent "plant-like" cell membranes, perinuclear halos**; solid sheets around vessels |
| **Collecting duct** | Irregular glands/tubules in **desmoplastic stroma**, hobnailing, medullary location |

**Spread:**
- 🔴 **Haematogenous (dominant)** — renal vein → IVC → **lung (>50% of metastatic sites)**, **bone (33%, lytic)**, liver, adrenal, brain.
- **Lymphatic** — hilar/paraaortic nodes.
- **Direct** — perinephric fat, renal pelvis.

### Clinical features (C/F)

1. 🔴 **Classic triad — painless haematuria + loin (costovertebral angle) pain + palpable lumbar mass — present in only ~10%** of patients.
2. 🔴 **Haematuria is the single most reliable clue** (gross or microscopic, intermittent).
3. Flank/loin dull aching pain; palpable irregular mass in the lumbar region; varicocele (left renal vein invasion).
4. 🔴 **Paraneoplastic syndromes ("great mimic"):** **polycythaemia** (ectopic erythropoietin), **hypercalcaemia** (PTHrP), **hypertension**, **Stauffer syndrome** (hepatic dysfunction without jaundice), Cushing syndrome, feminisation/masculinisation, eosinophilia, leukemoid reaction, amyloidosis.
5. ~15% present **with metastases first** (bone pain, cough, weight loss, fever of unknown origin).

### Lab diagnosis

| Step | Findings |
|---|---|
| **Urine examination** | Microscopic haematuria (RBCs); malignant cells rarely on cytology |
| 🔴 **Imaging — Ultrasound abdomen** | Solid hypoechoic renal mass; distinguishes simple cyst from tumour |
| 🔴 **Contrast-enhanced CT / MRI abdomen** | Investigation of choice — enhances avidly, shows extent, renal vein/IVC extension, nodes; CT chest for staging |
| **IV urography** | Displacement/deformity of calyces ("spider-leg" deformity) |
| **Serum biochemistry** | Raised ESR, serum calcium, ALP; polycythaemia (↑Hb); LFT derangement (Stauffer); FBC, RFT |
| 🔴 **Histopathology (biopsy/nephrectomy)** | Definitive — subtype identification (clear/papillary/chromophobe); immunohistochemistry as needed (e.g., CAIX/PAX8 in clear cell, AMACR/CK7 in papillary, CD117/CK7 in chromophobe) |
| **TNM staging** | T1–T4, nodal status, distant metastases — guides radical nephrectomy ± targeted therapy (VEGF/tyrosine kinase inhibitors for metastatic disease); 5-year survival ~70% overall, falls sharply with renal vein invasion/metastases |

### Complications

Renal vein thrombosis with IVC extension · haemorrhage · metastatic disease (lung, bone — pathological fracture) · paraneoplastic sequelae · local recurrence.

> 🎯 **Remember:** "RCC = **yellow, bloody, vein-climbing mimic** — **Smoker + Obese + Hypertensive man 60**; triad **Haematuria-Loin pain-Lump (only 10%)**; **VHL → HIF → VEGF** makes it yellow and vascular; **Lungs & bones** take the metastatic bus."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 20 (Kidney), pp. 895–952 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Renal System, pp. 225–249

## Q4. A male of 55 years presented with hematuria, loin pain, palpable mass in the lumbar region. What is the likely diagnosis? What are the histological types of the lesion?

### Likely diagnosis

🔴 **The likely diagnosis is RENAL CELL CARCINOMA (RCC)** — a 55-year-old male presenting with the **classic triad: painless haematuria + loin pain + palpable mass in the lumbar region**.

**Justification of findings:**

| Finding | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Haematuria** | Tumour erodes into the pelvicalyceal system — intermittent, gross or microscopic (most reliable symptom of RCC) |
| **Loin pain** | Capsular/distension stretch or invasion of surrounding tissue; dull persistent ache |
| **Palpable lumbar mass** | Enlarged kidney replaced by tumour; mobile with respiration |
| **Age 55, male** | Peak incidence 6th–7th decade, M:F = 2:1; smoking/obesity/hypertension are typical cofactors |

📌 Supporting clues to ask/look for: smoking history, weight loss, fever, varicocele, polycythaemia/hypercalcaemia (paraneoplastic). Confirm by **ultrasound + contrast CT abdomen** and **histopathology**.

### Histological types of the lesion

| Type | Frequency | Key microscopic features |
|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Clear cell (conventional) carcinoma** — most likely here | **70–80%** | Polygonal cells with **clear cytoplasm (glycogen/lipid)** in alveolar nests, **delicate thin-walled blood vessels**; bright-yellow gross appearance; **VHL gene (3p25) loss** |
| **Papillary (chromophile) carcinoma** | 10–15% | Papillae with fibrovascular cores, **foamy macrophages**, psammoma bodies; trisomies 7 & 17; **MET** mutations; often multifocal |
| **Chromophobe carcinoma** | ~5% | Large pale granular cells, **perinuclear halo**, thick cell membranes; **best prognosis** |
| **Collecting duct (Bellini) carcinoma** | ≤1% | Aggressive; irregular tubules in desmoplastic medullary stroma, hobnail cells |
| **Xp11.2 translocation (TFE3) carcinoma** | Rare, young patients | Clear cytoplasm + papillary architecture; nuclear TFE3 positivity |
| **Renal medullary carcinoma** | Rare | Occurs in **sickle cell trait** patients; very aggressive |
| **Sarcomatoid change** | Any subtype | Spindle-cell dedifferentiation — **ominous prognostic feature** |

📌 Differential diagnoses to mention in viva: **urothelial (transitional cell) carcinoma of the renal pelvis** (earlier haematuria), xanthogranulomatous pyelonephritis (grossly mimics RCC), hydronephrosis/polycystic kidney (mass), transitional cell carcinoma of bladder (haematuria without mass).

> 🎯 **Remember:** "**Male 55 + Haematuria + Loin pain + Lumbar lump = Classic triad of RCC** — and **80% will be CLEAR CELL** (VHL → yellow → vein-invading)."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 20 (Kidney), pp. 895–952 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Renal System, pp. 225–249

## Q5. Cystitis: Cause, Predisposing Factor.

### Definition

🔴 **Cystitis is inflammation of the urinary bladder, most commonly infective (bacterial) in origin, presenting with frequency, urgency, dysuria and suprapubic pain; it is the commonest form of lower urinary tract infection (uncomplicated cystitis occurs chiefly in women of reproductive age).**

### Causes (aetiology)

| Category | Agents |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Bacterial (most common)** | 🔴 **E. coli (#1, >80%)**, Proteus, Klebsiella, Enterobacter; **Staphylococcus saprophyticus** (young sexually active women); Enterococcus faecalis, Streptococcus agalactiae; Pseudomonas (catheter-associated) |
| **Mycobacterial** | Mycobacterium tuberculosis (sterile pyuria) |
| **Viral** | 🔴 **Adenovirus, BK polyomavirus** → haemorrhagic cystitis (boys, immunosuppressed) |
| **Fungal** | Candida species (diabetes, catheters, immunocompromise) |
| **Parasitic** | 🔴 **Schistosoma haematobium** — chronic cystitis → squamous metaplasia → squamous cell carcinoma |
| **Non-infectious (chemical/radiation/drug)** | 🔴 **Radiation cystitis**, **cyclophosphamide (haemorrhagic cystitis)**, catheter trauma; systemic diseases — lupus; idiopathic — **interstitial cystitis**, eosinophilic cystitis, malakoplakia (Michaelis-Gutmann bodies) |

### Predisposing factors

| Category | Factors |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Female anatomy** | Short urethra, proximity to vagina/anus; sexual intercourse ("honeymoon cystitis") |
| 🔴 **Urinary outflow obstruction / incomplete emptying** | **BPH**, urethral stricture, bladder neck obstruction, **bladder calculi/tumour**, neurogenic bladder, diverticula → residual urine acts as culture medium |
| 🔴 **Instrumentation** | Indwelling/long-term **catheterisation**, cystoscopy, stents |
| 🔴 **Pregnancy** | Hormonal ureteric relaxation + pressure → stasis |
| 🔴 **Diabetes mellitus** | Glycosuria favours bacterial growth |
| **Immunosuppression** | Steroids, chemotherapy, HIV, transplant |
| **Others** | Menopause (oestrogen lack), faecal incontinence, vesicoenteric fistula, prior UTIs |

### Pathogenesis

```text
Periurethral/intestinal flora colonise the distal urethra
   → ascend (short female urethra / instrumentation / obstruction-stasis)
   → attach to bladder urothelium (fimbrial adhesins, e.g., type 1 pili of E. coli)
   → evade flushing + innate defences (uroplakin binding, TLR4 blunting)
   → mucosal invasion → acute inflammation (hyperaemia → neutrophils → ulceration)
Unrelieved stasis/obstruction or virulent organism
   → chronic cystitis / ascending pyelonephritis
```

### Morphology

- **Acute:** reddened, hyperaemic, oedematous mucosa ± petechiae; wall infiltrated with **neutrophils**, sometimes ulceration/exudate.
- **Chronic:** lymphocyte/plasma cell infiltration, fibrosis, mucosal fissures and bridges; **follicular cystitis** (lymphoid aggregates); **polypoid cystitis**; schistosomal eggs with terminal spines ± squamous metaplasia.

### Clinical features

Sudden onset **frequency, urgency, dysuria (burning micturition), suprapubic pain/tenderness**; turbid, foul-smelling urine ± haematuria; **usually NO fever** (fever suggests pyelonephritis).

### Lab diagnosis

- 🔴 **Urine routine examination:** pyuria (pus cells >10/HPF), bacteriuria, RBCs.
- 🔴 **Midstream urine culture:** significant bacteriuria **≥10⁵ CFU/mL** (≥10³ in symptomatic male/catheter specimen) + antibiotic sensitivity.
- Dipstick: positive **nitrite** (gram-negatives reduce nitrate) + leukocyte esterase.
- Sterile pyuria → think TB, chlamydia, partially treated UTI.

### Complications

Ascending infection → **acute pyelonephritis** · chronic cystitis with fibrosis/bladder contracture · **squamous metaplasia → squamous cell carcinoma** (especially schistosomiasis) · emphysematous cystitis (diabetics) · haemorrhagic cystitis.

> 🎯 **Remember:** "Cystitis = **FUDS**: **F**requency, **U**rgency, **D**ysuria, **S**uprapubic pain — **E. coli climbs a short tube through stasis** (obstruction/catheter/pregnancy/diabetes open the door)."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 21 (Lower Urinary Tract), pp. 953–984 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Renal System, pp. 225–249

## Q6. Urinary Bladder Tumour: Types, risk factors.

### Definition

🔴 **Urinary bladder tumours are neoplasms arising from the bladder urothelium or its supporting tissues; more than 90% are malignant EPITHELIAL tumours — urothelial (transitional cell) carcinoma being by far the most common — classically presenting as PAINLESS GROSS HAEMATURIA in an older patient.**

### Types of urinary bladder tumour

**A. Epithelial tumours (>90–95% of bladder neoplasms):**

| Type | % / note |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Urothelial (transitional cell) carcinoma** | **~90%** — the dominant bladder cancer; spectrum: **papilloma → low-grade papillary → high-grade/invasive → carcinoma in situ (flat high-grade)** |
| **Squamous cell carcinoma** | ~5% (much higher in endemic areas) — follows **Schistosoma haematobium** infection or **chronic irritation** (stones, long-term catheter, bladder exstrophy) |
| **Adenocarcinoma** | Rare (~2%) — arises in **urachal remnants**, **bladder exstrophy**, intestinal metaplasia |
| **Small cell (neuroendocrine) carcinoma** | Rare, highly aggressive, paraneoplastic syndromes |
| **Mixed/other** | Sarcomatoid carcinoma, adenosquamous carcinoma |
| **Benign** | 🔴 **Urothelial papilloma**, inverted papilloma, condyloma |

**B. Nonepithelial tumours (rare):**

| Type | Note |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Rhabdomyosarcoma** | Most common bladder malignancy of **children/infants**; grossly grape-like — **"sarcoma botryoides"** |
| **Leiomyosarcoma** | Adults; rare |
| **Others** | Lymphoma, melanoma, metastases (secondary involvement from prostate/cervix carcinoma) |

### Biological behaviour of urothelial carcinoma (low vs high grade vs CIS)

| Feature | Low-grade papillary | High-grade | Carcinoma in situ (CIS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Growth | **Exophytic papillary (Ta)** | Infiltrative | 🔴 **Flat**, non-papillary |
| Cytology | Mild atypia | Marked pleomorphism | Severe atypia, high grade |
| Invasion risk | Low (but **recurs frequently**) | High — muscle invasion | High — precursor of invasive cancer |
| Field change | — | Multifocal recurrences | Frequently multifocal |

### Risk factors

| Factor | Comment |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Cigarette smoking** | 🔴 **Single most important risk factor** (≈50% of cases; aromatic amine/carcinogen load) |
| 🔴 **Occupational carcinogens — aromatic amines/aniline dyes** | Rubber, cable, dye, textile, leather, paint industries (2-naphthylamine, benzidine) — latency 15–40 yr |
| 🔴 **Schistosoma haematobium** | Chronic cystitis → squamous metaplasia → 🔴 **SCC** (endemic Africa/Egypt) |
| **Chronic bladder irritation** | Calculi, long-term indwelling catheter, bladder exstrophy, recurrent UTI → SCC |
| **Drugs/radiation** | 🔴 **Cyclophosphamide** (haemorrhagic cystitis → bladder cancer), **pelvic irradiation**; phenacetin analgesic abuse; aristolochic acid (Chinese herb nephropathy) |
| **Host factors** | Male sex, age >50–60 yr, white race; family history; **Lynch syndrome** (also upper-tract urothelial cancer) |

### Pathogenesis

```text
CARCINOGEN PATHWAY (smoke / aniline dyes):
Aromatic amines absorbed → hepatic N-hydroxylation (CYP1A2) → circulating electrophiles
   → excreted into urine → prolonged contact with urothelium
   → DNA adduct formation (field effect — whole urothelium exposed)
   → LOW-GRADE PAPILLARY TRACK: activating FGFR3 mutations (+ PIK3CA)
        → exophytic papillary tumour (Ta), recurs but rarely invades
   → HIGH-GRADE TRACK: TP53 + RB inactivation (17p/13q loss)
        → severe atypia → CIS (flat) → muscle-invasive carcinoma
```

### Morphology

- **Site:** trigone and lateral/posterior walls most common.
- **Gross:** solitary or multifocal **papillary (cauliflower-like, friable)** or **sessile/flat-ulcerated invasive** masses; papillomas are delicate, pink, non-invasive.
- **Micro:** papillae with fibrovascular cores lined by multilayered urothelium showing graded atypia; invasive tumours breach basement membrane → lamina propria (T1) → muscularis propria (T2) → perivesical fat (T3) → adjacent organs (T4).

### Clinical features & lab diagnosis

1. 🔴 **Painless gross (or microscopic) haematuria** — the hallmark presentation.
2. Frequency, urgency, dysuria (irritative symptoms — suggest CIS); flank pain/hydronephrosis if ureteric orifice obstructed; palpable mass late.
3. 🔴 **Urine cytology** — malignant urothelial cells (good for high grade/CIS surveillance).
4. 🔴 **Cystoscopy + biopsy (gold standard)** — visualises lesion, defines grade/stage; TURBT resects/stages.
5. Imaging: CT urogram/IVU (filling defect, upper-tract synchronicity — 50% of renal-pelvis urothelial tumours coexist with bladder tumours).
6. Recurrence is frequent (multifocal field effect) → lifelong surveillance; muscle-invasive disease → cystectomy ± chemotherapy.

> 🎯 **Remember:** "**Smoke → Transitional; Schisto/Stones/Stent (chronic irritation) → Squamous; Exstrophy/Urachus → Adenocarcinoma; Children → Botryoid rhabdomyosarcoma.** Presentation = **painless haematuria**; work-up = **cytology + cystoscopy-biopsy**; FGFR3 = low-grade, TP53 = high-grade."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 21 (Lower Urinary Tract), pp. 953–984 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Renal System, pp. 225–249

## Q7. Mention the types of Urinary stone with the pathogenesis. Mention the complications of urinary stones.

### Definition

🔴 **Urinary stones (urolithiasis) are hard crystalline concretions formed by precipitation of solutes from supersaturated urine, most commonly within the renal calyces and pelvis; they affect 5–10% of the population, men more than women, with peak onset at 20–30 years.**

### Types of urinary stones

| Type | % | Composition / appearance | Radiopacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| 🔴 **Calcium oxalate ± calcium phosphate** | **70–80% (most common)** | Hard, spiky/envelope oxalate crystals; whitish | 🔴 **Radio-opaque** |
| 🔴 **Magnesium ammonium phosphate (struvite, "infection/triple-phosphate stone")** | 5–10% | Coffin-lid crystals; **largest stones — STAGHORN calculus casting the pelvicalyceal system** | Radio-opaque |
| 🔴 **Uric acid stone** | 5–10% | Smooth, yellow-brown, small | 🔴 **RADIOLUCENT — invisible on plain X-ray** |
| **Cystine stone** | 1–2% | Hexagonal crystals; familial | Faintly radio-opaque |
| **Rare** | <1% | Xanthine, drug stones (indinavir, triamterene) | Variable |

### Pathogenesis (stone-by-stone chains)

```text
GENERAL REQUIREMENT:
Supersaturation of urine with the stone constituent
(↓urine volume, ↑excretion, abnormal pH) ± DEFICIENCY OF CRYSTAL INHIBITORS
(citrate, pyrophosphate, diphosphonate, magnesium, osteopontin)
   → nucleation → crystal growth & aggregation → retention in calyx → stone

CALCIUM OXALATE/PHOSPHATE:
Idiopathic hypercalciuria (≈50%) / hypercalcaemia-hypercalciuria
(hyperparathyroidism, sarcoidosis, bone resorption) / hyperoxaluria
(enteric — chronic diarrhoea & fat malabsorption bind Ca, or primary
oxalosis) / hyperuricosuria (uric acid crystals NUCLEATE calcium oxalate)
/ hypocitraturia (citrate normally complexes Ca)
   → calcium supersaturation → oxalate/phosphate precipitation → stone

STRUVITE (infection stone):
Urea-splitting bacteria (Proteus most typical, Klebsiella,
Ureaplasma, some Staph) hydrolyse urea via UREASE
   → NH3 + CO2 → ammonium ↑ + urine alkalinised (pH ↑)
   → magnesium ammonium phosphate + carbonate apatite precipitate
   in alkaline urine → rapidly growing STAGHORN calculus filling pelvis + calyces
   (obstruction ↔ infection vicious cycle)

URIC ACID:
Hyperuricosuria (gout, leukaemia/lymphoma, tumour-lysis, high purine diet)
± ACIDIC CONCENTRATED URINE (pH <5.5 — uric acid insoluble in acid;
>half of formers have NEITHER hyperuricaemia nor hyperuricosuria)
   → uric acid crystallises → smooth radiolucent stones

CYSTINE:
Hereditary defect of renal tubular reabsorption of CYSTINE +
dibasic amino acids (ornithine, arginine, lysine) — "COAL" — cystinuria
   → cystine poorly soluble especially at acidic pH
   → hexagonal cystine stones (recur throughout life)
```

### Morphology

Unilateral in ~80%; favoured sites — **renal calyces and pelves** (then bladder); usually small (few mm) and multiple; progressive accretion → **staghorn calculi** (classically struvite).

### Clinical features

Often asymptomatic; 🔴 **renal colic** — excruciating loin-to-groin pain when the stone impacts at the **ureteropelvic junction, where the ureter crosses the iliac vessels, or the ureterovesical junction**; haematuria, dysuria/frequency, nausea/vomiting; sterile pyuria or secondary infection.

### Complications of urinary stones

| Complication | Mechanism/effect |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Hydronephrosis & hydroureter** | Chronic partial obstruction → pelvicalyceal dilation → **pressure atrophy of renal parenchyma** → progressive loss of function |
| 🔴 **Renal failure** | Bilateral stones or obstruction of a solitary functioning kidney → post-renal azotaemia (complete bilateral obstruction → anuria) |
| 🔴 **Recurrent urinary tract infection** | Stasis + stone nidus → **acute/chronic pyelonephritis** |
| 🔴 **Pyonephrosis / perinephric abscess / urosepsis** | Infection superimposed on an obstructed system — pus fills the pelvis; gram-negative septicaemia risk |
| **Papillary necrosis** | Ischaemia/infection of papillae; sloughed papilla → further obstruction |
| **Renal colic attacks & haematuria** | Mucosal abrasion by moving stones |
| **Squamous metaplasia → squamous cell carcinoma** | Long-standing chronic irritation by stone |
| **Recurrence** | Metabolic abnormality persists → repeated stone episodes |

> 🎯 **Remember:** "Stone types = **C-U-S-C** — **C**alcium (70%, opaque), **U**ric acid (acid urine, **radiolucent**), **S**truvite (Splitting-urea bugs → alkaline → **Staghorn**), **C**ystine (genetic, hexagonal). Rule: **Supersaturation + lost citrate = crystal factory**; obstructed + infected kidney = **pyonephrosis waiting to happen**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 21 (Lower Urinary Tract), pp. 953–984 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Renal System, pp. 225–249

## Q8. What are the common causes of urinary tract obstruction?

### Definition

🔴 **Urinary tract obstruction (obstructive uropathy) is partial or complete blockage of urine flow anywhere from the renal pelvis/ureter to the urethral meatus; unrelieved obstruction leads to HYDRONEPHROSIS (dilation of pelvis and calyces) with renal parenchymal atrophy, and predisposes to INFECTION and STONE FORMATION.**

### Common causes of urinary tract obstruction

| Level | Causes |
|---|---|
| 🔴 **Congenital anomalies** | **Posterior urethral valves (commonest cause in male infants)**, urethral stricture/meatal stenosis, **ureteropelvic junction (UPJ) narrowing**, uretero-vesical junction obstruction, severe vesicoureteral reflux, bladder neck contracture |
| 🔴 **Urinary calculi** | Renal/ureteric/bladder stones (commonest cause of acute obstruction in adults); sloughed papillae (papillary necrosis), **blood clots** (haemorrhagic cystitis, tumour) |
| 🔴 **Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)** | 🔴 **Commonest cause of bladder outlet obstruction in elderly men** (prostate carcinoma likewise) |
| 🔴 **Tumours** | Bladder cancer (trigone/ureteric orifice), carcinoma cervix/uterus, ovarian masses, colorectal cancer, **retroperitoneal tumours/lymphoma**, metastatic nodes |
| **Inflammation/fibrosis** | Urethritis/prostatitis/ureteritis oedema, **retroperitoneal fibrosis** (idiopathic, methysergide), post-surgical/irradiation strictures, urinary tuberculosis, schistosomiasis |
| 🔴 **Functional (neurogenic) bladder** | Spinal cord injury, diabetic neuropathy, multiple sclerosis, Parkinson disease → incomplete emptying without mechanical block |
| **External compression/miscellaneous** | **Pregnancy** (right ureter compressed by gravid uterus), uterine prolapse/cystocele, aberrant renal vessels crossing the UPJ, phimosis |

### Classification shortcuts

- By site: **intraluminal** (stones, clots, papillae) · **intramural** (strictures, valves, neurogenic detrusor) · **extramural** (prostate, tumours, fibrosis, pregnancy).
- By side/duration: **unilateral vs bilateral** (bilateral → renal failure) · **acute vs chronic** · **complete vs incomplete**.

### Consequences (pathophysiology chain)

```text
Obstruction → urine stasis → residual urine (culture medium)
   → INFECTION + STONE FORMATION
Continued glomerular filtration → pelvis/calyces dilate (HYDRONEPHROSIS)
   → pressure transmitted back → ischaemia + tubular atrophy
   → FIRST functional loss = impaired concentrating ability (polyuria/nocturia)
   → later ↓GFR, interstitial inflammation/fibrosis
Far advanced → thin-walled sac (up to 15–20 cm), shrunken kidney
Complete bilateral obstruction → oliguria/anuria → POST-RENAL FAILURE
Relief of obstruction → massive POST-OBSTRUCTIVE DIURESIS (salt-water rich)
```

### Clinical clues

Acute: renal colic ± anuria. Chronic partial: nocturia/polyuria, recurrent UTI/stones, hypertension; bilateral chronic obstruction presents as **chronic kidney disease**.

> 🎯 **Remember:** "Causes = **STONES-VIT-P**: **S**tones, **T**umours, **O**utflow (BPH), **N**eurogenic bladder, **E**mbryonic (valves/UPJ), **S**tricture-fibrosis, **V**essels/pregnancy compress, **I**nflammation (TB/schisto), **T**raffic-clots-papillae, **P**ressure from outside. Bottom line: **BPH in old men, valves in baby boys, stones everywhere else**; obstruction invites **infection + stones + hydronephrosis**."

> 📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 21 (Lower Urinary Tract), pp. 953–984 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I Renal System, pp. 225–249
