Q1. Define and classify Ischemic Heart Disease (IHD)/Coronary artery disease.

Definition

🔴 Ischemic heart disease (IHD) is a group of related clinical syndromes resulting from myocardial ischemia — an imbalance between coronary blood supply and myocardial oxygen demand, most commonly caused by atherosclerotic narrowing/obstruction of the coronary arteries (hence the synonym coronary artery disease, CAD).

  • 📌 IHD = single largest killer worldwide (>12% of all deaths); critical stenosis = obstruction of >70% of coronary cross-sectional area (>90% → ischemia even at rest).
  • Causes of ischemia: ↓supply (atherothrombosis, spasm, embolus, hypotension/shock, anemia) and/or ↑demand (exertion, hypertrophy, thyrotoxicosis).

Classification

TypeKey features
1. Angina pectorisTransient ischemia without necrosis — (a) Stable/typical (exertional, fixed >70% stenosis), (b) Prinzmetal/variant (rest pain, coronary vasospasm), (c) Unstable/crescendo (increasing frequency/at rest; plaque fissure + mural thrombus — pre-infarction state)
2. Acute myocardial infarction (MI)Irreversible coagulative necrosis from sudden coronary occlusion (thrombus over ruptured/eroded plaque); transmural or subendocardial
3. Chronic IHD (ischemic cardiomyopathy)Progressive heart failure after cumulative ischemic injury/multiple remote MIs → dilated hypertrophied scarred heart
4. Sudden cardiac death (SCD)Unexpected cardiac death within ~1 h of symptoms, usually due to lethal arrhythmia (VF) on ischemic substrate
🎯 IHD family: "Angina cries but doesn't kill cells, MI kills, Chronic cripples, SCD strikes."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

🔴 Clinical features of MI

Symptoms:

  1. 🔴 Severe retrosternal crushing/squeezing/heavy "vice-like" chest pain lasting >30 min (often hours).
  2. Radiation to left arm/forearm, jaw, neck, epigastrium, back.
  3. 🔴 NOT relieved by rest or nitroglycerin (key differentiator from angina).
  4. Autonomic features: profuse sweating (diaphoresis), nausea, vomiting, cold clammy skin.
  5. Dyspnea (LV failure/pulmonary congestion), profound weakness, anxiety/"feeling of impending doom".
  6. Silent (painless) MI: elderly and 🔴 diabetic patients (autonomic neuropathy) — may present only with dyspnea, syncope, epigastric discomfort or confusion.

Signs:

  • Pallor, diaphoresis, restlessness; tachycardia or bradyarrhythmia.
  • Low-grade fever after 24 hr (tissue necrosis, persists ~1 week).
  • Cardiac: soft S1, S4 gallop (stiff ischemic LV), S3 if failure, new murmur (papillary dysfunction), pericardial friction rub (day 2–4).
  • Lung crackles (pulmonary edema); hypotension/cardiogenic shock in large infarcts; JVD in RV infarct.
🎯 MI pain: "CRUSHED >30 min, shoots to arm-jaw, sweat + vomit, nitro NO help"; Silent MI hides in Old + Diabetes.
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75
Q5. Define and classify angina pectoris. How angina can be differentiated from MI?

Definition

🔴 Angina pectoris is paroxysmal substernal chest pain caused by transient myocardial ischemia WITHOUT myocyte death (necrosis) — due to imbalance between coronary perfusion and demand; pain results from ischemia-induced release of adenosine and bradykinin stimulating sympathetic afferents.

Classification

Q6. What is Myocardial Infarction (MI)? Give the clinical features of MI.

Definition

🔴 Myocardial infarction (MI) is irreversible necrosis (coagulative necrosis) of heart muscle produced by acute, sustained myocardial ischemia, almost always following sudden total occlusion of a coronary artery by a platelet-fibrin thrombus overlying a ruptured/eroded atherosclerotic plaque.

  • Types by territory: 🔴 Transmural (full wall thickness; total epicardial arterial occlusion) vs Subendocardial/non-transmural (inner ⅓; diffuse 3-vessel disease/hypotension without total occlusion).
  • Artery → territory (right-dominant 80%): LAD — anterior LV wall, apex, anterior ⅔ septum (most common, ~40–50%); RCA — inferior/posterior wall, posterior ⅓ septum, RV; LCX — lateral wall.

Clinical features of MI

  1. 🔴 Pain: sudden severe retrosternal crushing/constricting pain >30 min, radiating to left arm/jaw/neck/epigastrium, not relieved by rest or nitroglycerin; maximal from onset.
  2. 🔴 Autonomic: profuse sweating, nausea, vomiting, cold clammy extremities, pallor.
  3. Dyspnea (acute LV failure), orthopnea; cough with frothy sputum if pulmonary edema.
  4. Weakness, anxiety, fear of impending death; syncope (arrhythmia).
  5. Atypical/silent presentation: diabetes, elderly, postoperative — painless dyspnea, epigastric "indigestion", confusion, syncope.
  6. Signs: tachy-/bradycardia, low-grade fever (24 hr onward), S4/S3 gallop, soft S1, new MR murmur (papillary muscle dysfunction), pericardial rub (day 2–4), bilateral basal crackles, hypotension/cardiogenic shock, arrhythmias (VF = commonest early death).
🎯 MI = "Dead muscle from a plugged pipe" — crushing pain >30 min + sweat + breathlessness + nitro-fail.
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

📌 Infarct is non-repairable by regeneration — healing occurs by organization → fibrous scar (heart cannot replace myocytes).

Fates of MI

  1. 🔴 Healing by organization → white fibrous scar (6–8 wk) with permanent loss of contractility in that zone (most common fate with survival).
  2. Resolution (rare/minimal) — very small infarcts replaced by thin scar.
  3. Extension/reinfarction — peri-infarct ischemia or new plaque event enlarges the infarct.
  4. 🔴 Death — from complications: arrhythmia (early), cardiogenic shock/CHF, cardiac rupture (day 3–7), embolism, ventricular aneurysm (late). (Detailed list in Q12.)
  5. Chronic evolution — scarred akinetic segment → ventricular aneurysm, mural thrombus, chronic IHD/ischemic cardiomyopathy.
🎯 Timeline: "Wavey 2hr → Dead 1d → Neutrophils 3d → Macro-clean 5d → Granulation 7d → White scar 8wk"; Fate = Scar or Die.
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75
Q9. What is chronic IHD? How it develops?

Definition

🔴 Chronic IHD (ischemic cardiomyopathy) is progressive cardiac decompensation — manifesting as chronic congestive heart failure — that develops as the cumulative result of long-standing severe coronary atherosclerosis with multiple healed (silent or clinically apparent) myocardial infarctions. It accounts for ~50% of cardiac transplant recipients.

How it develops

Long-standing severe multivessel atherosclerosis
        ↓
Repeated episodes of ischemia (silent MIs / known MIs / chronic angina)
        ↓
Progressive MYOCYTE LOSS (necrosis + apoptosis) — patchy, cumulative
        ↓
Replacement by DIFFUSE SUBENDOCARDIAL SCARRING (non-contractile tissue)
        ↓
Remodeling: compensatory hypertrophy of surviving myocytes
→ ventricular DILATION + HYPERTROPHY (eccentric) + interstitial fibrosis
        ↓
Progressive ↓EF → SYSTOLIC HEART FAILURE (chronic IHD established)

Morphology

  • Heart enlarged (cardiomegaly, up to 900 g), flabby, LV cavity dilated with concentric + eccentric hypertrophy.
  • 🔴 Diffuse subendocardial white fibrous scars of varying ages + occasional old transmural scars; mural thrombi in ventricles/atria.
  • Microscopy: myocyte hypertrophy, interstitial fibrosis, replacement scarring.

Clinical

Stable angina + exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, fatigue, arrhythmias, murmurs; ends in refractory CHF, arrhythmic sudden death.

🎯 "Many small deaths add up": repeated ischemia → lost myocytes → white patches → big floppy failing pump = ischemic cardiomyopathy.
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75

📌 Serial sampling: diagnosis requires a characteristic rise-and-fall pattern; single normal troponin at ≥6 hr after symptom onset excludes MI.

B. Electrocardiogram (immediate, bedside)

  • ST-segment elevation (STEMI, transmural), ST depression/T-wave inversion (NSTEMI/subendocardial), 🔴 pathological Q waves (necrosis, appear 1–3 days, persist lifelong).
  • Localization: V1–V4 = anteroseptal (LAD); II, III, aVF = inferior (RCA); I, aVL, V5–V6 = lateral (LCX); V4R = right ventricular.

C. Other laboratory findings

  • CBC: 🔴 polymorphonuclear leukocytosis (12–24 hr, subsides in ~1 week); ESR & CRP elevated (day 2 onward).
  • Blood sugar ↑ (even non-diabetic), free fatty acids ↑.
  • Urea, creatinine, electrolytes (K⁺, Mg²⁺ important for arrhythmia risk), lipid profile (risk assessment).

D. Imaging

  • Echocardiography — regional wall-motion abnormality (akinesia/dyskinesia), EF, mural thrombus, complications.
  • Coronary angiography — identifies occluded vessel (gold standard for anatomy; basis for PCI).
  • Chest X-ray — pulmonary congestion/cardomegaly.
🎯 Lab trio: "ECG shows where, Troponin proves what died, CK-MB says if it's happening again."
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75 & Unit-II "Enzymes", pp. 480–486

Q13. Hypertension : Definition , types, Cause.

Definition

🔴 Hypertension is a persistent, sustained elevation of systemic arterial blood pressure ≥140 mm Hg systolic and/or ≥90 mm Hg diastolic recorded on two or more separate occasions (2017 ACC/AHA stage 1 begins at ≥130/80 mm Hg).

Types

BasisTypes
By cause🔴 Primary/essential/idiopathic (90–95%) vs 🔴 Secondary (5–10%)
By severity/courseBenign (slow, chronic, uncomplicated for decades) vs 🔴 Malignant/accelerated (rapidly rising BP >200/120, papilledema, fibrinoid necrosis, renal failure — fatal in 1 yr untreated)
By vessel sideSystemic (left-sided) vs Pulmonary HTN → cor pulmonale (right-sided)

Causes

A. Primary/essential (90–95%) — multifactorial:

  • Genetic predisposition (polygenic; genes affecting renal Na⁺ handling e.g. EnaC, ACE polymorphisms), 🔴 "resetting of pressure natriuresis" (kidney excretes salt only at higher pressure).
  • Environment: high salt intake, obesity, stress, sedentary life, smoking, alcohol, aging, black race.

B. Secondary (5–10%) — identify the culprit organ:

  1. 🔴 Renal: chronic renal parenchymal disease (commonest secondary cause); renovascular — renal artery stenosis (renin-driven).
  2. 🔴 Endocrine: primary hyperaldosteronism (Conn), pheochromocytoma, Cushing syndrome, acromegaly, hypo-/hyperthyroidism.
  3. Vascular: coarctation of aorta, polyarteritis nodosa, aortic regurgitation.
  4. Drugs: OCPs, steroids, NSAIDs, sympathomimetics, erythropoietin.
  5. Sleep apnea, pregnancy (pre-eclampsia).
  6. Single-gene forms (all increase distal Na⁺ reabsorption): Liddle syndrome (gain-of-function EnaC), glucocorticoid-remediable aldosteronism, 11β-/17α-hydroxylase deficiency.

📌 Hypertensive heart disease (systemic): chronic HTN → concentric LV hypertrophy (pressure overload) — heart weight >500 g, LV wall >2 cm, LA enlargement, interstitial fibrosis → diastolic/systolic failure, IHD, SCD.

🎯 "140/90 twice = HTN"; Secondary hunt = "Kidney-Kidney artery-Hormone-Pipe-Pills". Hyaline = benign, Onion-skin = malignant.
📖 Robbins 10e, Ch 12 (The Heart), pp. 497–548 · 📗 Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Unit-I "The Heart", pp. 45–75
Q14. A male of 50 years of age presented with sudden severe central chest pain & sweating for 1 hour. He is Hypertensive & Diabetic. What is your likely diagnosis? What are the immediate investigations you would suggest to confirm the diagnosis? (Answer: ECG, cardiac biochemical markers — most common Cardiac Troponin-I.)

🔴 Likely diagnosis: Acute Myocardial Infarction (STEMI)

Justification (reasoning)

  • Typical pain profile: sudden, severe, central/retrosternal chest pain of 1 hour duration — exceeds the 30-min threshold of MI; angina (<15 min, relieved by rest) excluded by duration.
  • Autonomic accompaniment: sweating (diaphoresis) = classic sympathetic response of MI.
  • 🔴 Strong risk profile: male sex, age 50, hypertension and diabetes — the two strongest modifiable IHD risk factors (DM also predisposes to larger, complicated infarcts).
  • Main differential to exclude: aortic dissection (hypertensive, tearing pain radiating to back, BP asymmetry between arms) — must be considered before anticoagulation.

Q15. A 35 years old smoker man presented with severe chest pain. His ECG shows ischemic change. What will be the pathologic change/effect/morphologic changes/types of necrosis occurred in myocardium? State the complication of MI.

🔴 Diagnosis: Acute Myocardial Infarction (premature CAD in a young smoker)

Reasoning: severe chest pain + ischemic ECG changes in a young man; smoking is a major modifiable risk factor causing endothelial injury, LDL oxidation, vasospasm and thrombosis → premature coronary atherosclerosis/plaque rupture (consider also cocaine use, hypercoagulable states, familial hypercholesterolemia).

Pathologic change / effect in myocardium

Plaque rupture → coronary thrombotic occlusion
        ↓
Severe ischemia → ATP depletion → membrane injury
        ↓
🔴 TYPE OF NECROSIS = COAGULATIVE NECROSIS
(cell outlines preserved, hypereosinophilic anucleate fibers,
later replaced by fibrous scar — heart cannot regenerate)

Morphologic changes (timeline)

TimeChange
2–4 hrDark mottling; wavy fibers
4–24 hrCoagulative necrosis (hypereosinophilia, pyknosis), neutrophils begin
1–3 daysNeutrophil peak, karyolysis
3–7 daysMacrophages remove debris; granulation tissue; 🔴 rupture-prone phase
1–2 wkFibroblasts + capillaries mature
3–8 wk🔴 White fibrous scar (permanent, non-contractile)

Pattern: usually transmural (total occlusion) starting subendocardially, spreading as wavefront.

Complications of MI

  1. 🔴 Arrhythmias (ventricular fibrillation) — commonest cause of early death.
  2. Cardiogenic shock / left ventricular failure.
  3. Myocardial rupture (free wall → tamponade; septum → VSD; papillary muscle → MR) — day 3–7.
  4. Pericarditis (day 2–4) and later Dressler syndrome.
  5. Mural thrombus → systemic emboli (stroke, renal infarct).
  6. Ventricular aneurysm (late) → CHF, arrhythmia, embolism.
🎯 Young smoker + blocked coronary = coagulative necrosis of myocardium; remember the danger window day 3–7 (soft heart = rupture).
