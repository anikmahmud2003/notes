📄 PAPER 1 — 1st TERM (SUPPLY) EXAMINATION (WRITTEN) — [BATCH Z-32]
Total marks: 80 · Time: 2 hours 40 min · Answer four (4) from each group including Q1(SEQ) & Q5(PBQ).
✍️ GROUP A — Answer Script
1 Discuss the causes of cell injury with examples. Describe how hypoxia causes cell injury? 7(4+3)

Causes (4): Ischemia, Hypoxia, Chemicals/toxins, Infection, Physical agents.

Hypoxia mechanism (3): ↓O₂ → ↓ATP → Na/K pump fails → cell swell; Ca²⁺ influx → lysosomal enzymes/PLA₂/NO → membrane injury → apoptosis/necrosis.

High yield: Hypoxia = O₂ down → ATP down → pump fails → swell; Ischemia = flow down → ATP down → same downstream.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 2 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 01–02

2 Define and classify metaplasia with examples. Harmful effects? 3+2

Metaplasia: One adult cell type replaced by another adult cell type (differentiation shift).

Types: Squamous, Glandular — e.g., squamous metaplasia of bronchus (smoker); intestinal metaplasia of stomach (duodenum).

Harmful: Irreversible → dysplasia → carcinoma (intestinal metaplasia → colorectal cancer).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 2 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 02

3 Beneficial and harmful effects of inflammation. Systemic effects? 2+3

Beneficial: Defense (phagocytosis), wound healing, fever.

Harmful: Chronic inflammation → tissue destruction (hepatitis, IBD, silicosis, TB), autoimmunity, cancer promotion.

Systemic: Fever, ↑CRP/ESR, leukocytosis, cytokines → SIRS/sepsis.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 3 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 03

4 Hallmarks of chronic inflammation? Factors influencing wound healing? 1+4

Hallmarks: IL-1, TNFα, IL-6, prostaglandins, nitric oxide, lysosomal enzymes, macrophages (M1), granuloma (TB).

Wound healing factors: Infection, ischemia, foreign body, diabetes, malnutrition, smoking, debridement.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 3 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 03

5 Painful, red, edematous, warm swelling at arm following recent sting bite. Diagnosis? Mechanism? 1+2

Diagnosis: Localized inflammatory reaction to sting (secondary cellulitis if infected).

Mechanism: Mast cell degranulation → histamine → vasodilation (red/warm) + ↑permeability (edema) + C-fiber pain.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 3 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 03

✍️ GROUP B — Answer Script
1 Define and classify shock with pathogenesis of septic shock. Shortly describe DIC. 7(5+2)

Shock: Inadequate tissue perfusion → cellular dysfunction.

Types: Hypovolemic, Cardiogenic, Septic, Anaphylactic, Neurogenic.

Septic shock: Bacteria/endotoxin → cytokines → NO → vasodilation → ↓SVR → ↓CO → tissue hypoperfusion.

DIC: Microthrombi + consumptive coagulopathy → bleeding + thrombosis.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 4 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 05

2 Describe Virchow triad? Fates of thrombus? 3+2

Virchow triad: Endothelial injury, Venous stasis, Hypercoagulability.

Fates: Embolization, Propagation, Regression (fibrinolysis), Organization.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 4 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 05

3 Define and classify neoplasm. Malignant features? 2+3

Neoplasm: Uncontrolled cell growth; Benign vs Malignant.

Malignant: Invasion, metastasis, dedifferentiation, uncontrolled growth.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 7 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 08

4 Tumor diagnosis in lab? Grading and staging? 3+2

Dx: Clinical → imaging → FNAC → biopsy (IHC) → molecular.

Grading: Differentiation (G1 well → G3 poorly); CLL → Rai/Binet.

Staging: TNM (T, N, M).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 7 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 08

5 56 yrs old male, generalized edema which is pitting in nature. Causes? How diagnose cause? 1+2

Causes: Cardiac (CHF), Renal (nephrotic), Hepatic (hypoalbuminemia), Protein-losing enteropathy, Malnutrition.

Diagnosis: History → exam (JVD, hepatojugular reflux) → labs (albumin, lipids, renal fn, urine protein/A ratio) → imaging (echo).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 4 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-1, Ch 05

📄 PAPER 2 — 2nd TERM (SUPPLY) EXAMINATION (SAQ, SEQ, PBQ) — [BATCH Z-32]
Total marks: 80 · Time: 3 hours · Answer FOUR(4) from each group including Q1 & Q2.
✍️ GROUP A — Answer Script
1 Risk factors of atherosclerosis? Plaque → MI? Lab diagnose MI? 2+3+2

Risk factors: Modifiable — HTN, DM, smoking, dyslipidemia, obesity; Non-modifiable — age, sex, genetics. + chronic inflammation.

Plaque → MI: Plaque rupture/erosion → thrombus → coronary occlusion → ischemia → necrosis.

Lab dx: Troponin T/I, CK-MB, myoglobin + ECG (ST elevation/depression).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 11, 12 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 01–02, Ch 25

2 56 yrs smoker male, firm fixed LN at R supraclavicular; FNAC anaplastic SCC; CXR coin-like hilar lesion. Dx? Confirm? 1+2

Dx: Squamous cell carcinoma of lung (central type; fixed LN = metastatic).

Confirm: Bronchoscopy biopsy → histopath + IHC (CK5/6, p63/p40).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 15 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 04, Ch 03

3 Enumerate causes of generalized lymphadenopathy. Hodgkin vs Non-Hodgkin differences? R-S cell variants? 1.5+2+1.5

Causes: Infections (EBV, TB, HIV, bacterial), Malignancies (lymphoma), Autoimmune.

Hodgkin vs NHL: Hodgkin = Reed–Sternberg cells (CD15+, CD30+, MUM1+, EMA+); NHL = B-cell (most), T-cell, NK-cell.

R-S variants: Lacunar (classical Hodgkin), Binucleated (Burkitt), Multinucleated.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 13 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 03

4 Obstructive lung diseases with etiology? What is COPD? 4+1

Obstructive diseases: COPD (emphysema, chronic bronchitis), Asthma, Bronchiectasis, Cystic fibrosis, Hypersensitivity pneumonitis.

COPD: Chronic obstructive airflow limitation (emphysema: α1-antitrypsin deficiency; chronic bronchitis: smoking).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 15 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 04

5 Classify glomerular diseases. Differentiate nephrotic vs nephritic syndrome from urine? 4+1

Nephrotic: Proteinuria >3.5 g/day (hypoalbuminemia, edema, lipiduria, low urine A/B ratio).

Nephritic: Hematuria + RBC casts + oliguria, urine protein moderate, urine A/B ratio > 1.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 20 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 08

✍️ GROUP B — Answer Script
1 Classify hepatic neoplasms. Hepatic mass dx? Pathogenesis of HCC from HBV? 2+2+3

Classify: Benign (HCA, FNH, hemangioma); Primary malignant (HCC, cholangiocarcinoma); Metastatic.

Hepatic mass dx: USG/CT (arterial enhancement + washout), AFP, biopsy.

HCC from HBV: Chronic HBV → injury/inflammation → cirrhosis → dysplasia → HCC.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 18 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 07

2 A testicular mass. Causes? How will you investigate? 1+2

Causes: Hydrocele, seminoma, germ cell tumour, torsion, epididymitis.

Investigate: Scrotal US, FNAC/biopsy, tumour markers (AFP/hCG/LDH/inhibin).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 22 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 09

3 Give the pathogenesis of BPH. 2+3

BPH pathogenesis: Androgens (DHT) → prostate stromal/epithelial hyperplasia → BPH → urinary symptoms.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 22 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 09

4 What may be the cause of...? 3+2

Cause (renal): Renal impairment / renal stone — obstructive, infectious, stone-related causes.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 20 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 08

5 Short note (any two): a. Gall bladder stone, b. Cirrhosis, c. Renal stone 2.5+2.5

Gall bladder stone: Cholesterol/pigment stones; bile stasis → precipitation.

Cirrhosis: End-stage liver fibrosis (bridging septa, regenerative nodules, vascular shunting).

Renal stone: Calcium oxalate/pH-related; infection/obstruction causes.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 18, 20 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 07, Ch 08

📄 PAPER 3 — 2nd TERM (SUPPLY) EXAMINATION (SAQ, SEQ, PBQ) — PART 2 — [BATCH Z-32]
Total marks: 80 · Time: 3 hours · Answer FOUR(4) from each group including Q1 & Q2.
✍️ GROUP A — Answer Script
1 Name the common ulcers of GIT. Characteristics of malignant ulcer? Diseases caused by H. pylori? 2+3+2

Common ulcers: Peptic ulcer (gastric, duodenal); TB, typhoid, amoebic ulcers.

Malignant ulcer: Indurated, hard, fixed, irregular base, rolling edges, non-healing.

H. pylori: Peptic ulcer, gastritis, gastric cancer, MALT lymphoma, Type I IPC.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 17 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 06

2 25 yrs woman, firm mobile nodule in outer quadrant of right breast. D/D? How investigate? 2+1

D/D: Fibroadenoma vs cyst vs carcinoma vs fibrocystic change.

Investigate: Clinical breast US → FNAC (triple assessment).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 23 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 12

3 Classify salivary gland tumors. Small intestine polyps? Pathogenesis of acute appendicitis? 2+1.5+1.5

Salivary tumors: Benign (pleomorphic adenoma, Warthin); Malignant (adenocarcinoma).

Small intestine polyps: Peutz–Jeghers, Cronkhite–Canada.

Acute appendicitis: Luminal obstruction (fecalith) → mucosal ischemia → inflammation.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 17 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 06

4 Causes of abnormal uterine bleeding in reproductive age group? Complications of leiomyoma? Pathogenesis of uterine cervical cancer? 1.5+1.5+2

Causes of AUB: Fibroids, hormonal imbalance, infection, malignancy, pregnancy.

Leiomyoma complications: Menorrhagia, anemia, pain, infertility.

Cervical cancer pathogenesis: HPV → dysplasia → carcinoma.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 22 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 10, Ch 12

5 Short note (any two): a. Pleomorphic adenoma, b. Endometriosis, c. Dermoid cyst 2.5+2.5

Pleomorphic adenoma: Most common benign salivary gland tumor; mixed tissue.

Endometriosis: Endometrial tissue outside uterus.

Dermoid cyst: Benign teratoma (ovarian).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 23 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 12

✍️ GROUP B — Answer Script
1 Define and classify DM. Describe its pathogenesis and complications. 1+1+3+2

DM: Chronic metabolic disorder with hyperglycemia.

Types: Type 1 (autoimmune β-cell destruction), Type 2 (insulin resistance + β-cell failure).

Pathogenesis: T1 = autoimmune; T2 = obesity/insulin resistance.

Complications: DKA/HHS, microvascular (retinopathy/nephropathy/neuropathy), macrovascular (CAD/stroke).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 24 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 21

2 Thyroid TFTs: TSH 11 mU/L, T3 2.2 pmol/L. Causes? 1+2

Interpretation: TSH 11 (elevated, normal ~0.4–4.0) + low T3 → primary hypothyroidism (iodine deficiency / Hashimoto's).

Next: Serum free T4, anti-TPO; pituitary workup if secondary.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 24 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 13

3 Name the fixatives used in histopathology and cytopathology. 1+2+2

Histopathology: 10% neutral buffered formalin, carmine, quick, Ziehl–Neelsen, Gram, Giemsa.

Cytopathology: Wet fix (95% ethanol / spray Carbowax), air-dry (Giemsa/MGG), LBC.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 24 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 23

4 Short note on skin lesions. 2+3

Inflammatory: Eczema, psoriasis.

Neoplastic: BCC, SCC, melanoma, benign naevus.

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 25 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 14

5 Short note (any two): a. FNAC, b. PAP smear, c. CSF examination 2.5+2.5

FNAC: Fine needle aspiration cytology (cells from lesion; screening/triage).

PAP smear: Cervical cytology (exfoliative/abrasive).

CSF examination: Meningitis diagnosis (cells, glucose, protein, culture).

Ref: Robbins 10e, Ch 21 · Arif 15e (2025), Vol-2, Ch 23
