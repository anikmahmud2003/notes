Introduction to Pathology

Pathology literally translates as the study of suffering (Greek pathos = suffering, logos = study); more
prosaically, and as applied to modern medicine, it is the study of disease.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/1)

Q. Define pathology. What are the divisions of pathology?
Ans

Pathology: Pathology is the study of the structural, biochemical, and functional changes in cells, tissues, and
organs that underlie disease.

Division of pathology: Traditionally the study of pathology is divided into general pathology and systemic pathology.

* General pathology is concemed with the common reactions of cells and tissues to injurious stimuli.

Such reactions are often not tissue specific: thus acute inflammation in response to bacterial infections
produces a very similar reaction in most tissues.

Systemic pathology (on the other hand), examines the alterations and underlying mechanisms in
diseases of particular organ systems, such as ischemic heart disease.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/33)

Cell Injury

Q. Define cell injury. (DU-08Ja, BUP-22Nv,21Nv/M, RU-20Nv,19M, SU-20Nv/M, CU-05Ja)
(Very Important for VIVA)

Ans.

Cell injury: If the limits of adaptive responses are exceeded or if cells are exposed to damaging insults,
deprived of critical nutrients, or compromised by mutations that affect essential cellular functions, a
sequence of events follows that is termed cell injury.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/35)
Or,
When cells are stressed so severely that they are no longer able to adapt or when cells are exposed to
inherently damaging agents, cell injury results.

(Ref: Lecture SSMC)

Q. What are the causes of cell injury? (DU-10Ja, RU-20Nv,17M,12Ja, SU-22Nv,20M,15Ja,12Ju,
CU-11Ja) (Very Important for VIVA)
Ans.
Causes of cell injury: The major causes of cell injury can be grouped into the following categories:
1. Oxygen deprivation:

i. Hypoxia: Hypoxia is a deficiency of O, to the tissue, which causes cell injury by reducing aerobic
oxidative respiration. Causes include inadequate oxygenation of the blood due to cardio-
respiratory failure; and decreased oxygen-carrying capacity of the blood, as in anaemia or CO
poisoning and severe blood loss.

ii. Ischemia: It means loss of blood supply from impeded arterial flow or reduced venous drainage ina
tissue.
2. Physical agents: It includes mechanical trauma, extremes of temperature (burn and deep cold), sudden
changes in atmospheric pressure, radiation, and electric shock etc.

3. Chemical agents and drugs: Glucose or salt in hypertonic concentrations; oxygen in high
concentration; trace amount of poisons e.g. arsenic, cyanide, or mercury; environmental pollutants,
insecticides, and herbicides; industrial and occupational hazards, such as carbon monoxide and
asbestos; recreational drugs such as alcohol; variety of therapeutic drugs (toxic side effects).

4. Infectious agents: Virus, rickettsiae, bacteria, fungi, and higher forms of parasites.

5. Immunologic reactions: Immune reactions to foreign bodies (such as viruses and environmental
substances), and autoimmune diseases (injurious reactions to endogenous self-antigens).

Q. How reversible cell injuries become irreversible? (Important for VIVA)

Ans.

Transformation of reversible cell injury to irreversible cell injury: Although the “point of no return,” at

lysosomal membranes results in the enzymatic dissolution of the injured cell that is characteristic of
necrosis or cell death.

Q. How ATP is produced?
Ans.
ATP production: ATP is produced in two ways:
1) Oxidative phosphorylation (the major pathway in mammalian cells, particularly those that are non-
dividing e.g. brain and liver).

2) Glycolytic pathway (which can generate ATP in the absence of oxygen using glucose derived either
from body fluids or from the hydrolysis of glycogen).
(Ref: Robbins/10"/49)

(N.B: ATP is required for virtually all synthetic and degradative processes within the cell. These include
membrane transport, protein synthesis, lipogenesis, and the deacylation-reacylation reactions necessary for
phospholipid turnover. Hence, depletion of ATP to 5% to 10% of normal levels has widespread effects on many
critical cellular systems.)

(Ref: Robbins/10"/50)

Q. Describe the biochemical mechanisms leading to cell membrane damage. (DU-10Ju, RU-10Ju)
Ans.
Biochemical_mechanisms leading to_cell_ membrane damage: Several biochemical mechanisms may

contribute to membrane damage-

1. Reactive oxygen species: Oxygen free radicals cause injury to cell membranes by lipid peroxidation,
oxidative modification of proteins and lesions in DNA.

2. Decreased phospholipid synthesis: Decreased phospholipid synthesis as a consequence of defective
mitochondnial function or hypoxia may affect all cellular membranes, including those of mitochondria.

3. Increased phospholipid breakdown: Severe cell injury is associated with increased degradation of
membrane phopholipids, probably due to activation of endogenous phospholipases by increased levels
of cytosolic and mitochondrial Ca’. Phospholipid breakdown leads to accumulation of /ipid
breakdown products including unesterified free fatty acids, acyl carnitine, and lysophospholipids,

membrane or exchange with membrane phospholipids, potentially causing changes in permeability and
electrophysiologic alterations.

4. Cytoskeletal abnormalities: Activation of proteases by increased cytosolic Ca** may cause damage
to elements of the cytoskeleton. In the presence of cell swelling, particularly in myocardial cells, this
damage leads to detachment of the cell membrane from the cytoskeleton, rendering it susceptible to

Q. What is reperfusion injury? Discuss the mechanism of reperfusion injury. (DU-05Ja)
Q. Short note: Ischemia-reperfusion injury. (Important for VIVA for Honours Gainer)

Ans.

Reperfusion injury / Ischemia-reperfusion injury: Restoration of blood flow to ischemic tissues can
promote recovery of cells if they are reversibly injured, but can also paradoxically exacerbate the injury and
cause cell death.

Depending on the intensity and duration of the ischemic insult, in some cases, after restoration of blood flow to

Mechanisms of reperfusion injury:

Q. Give the pathogenesis of dry & wet gangrene. (CU-04Ja)
Ans.
Dry gangrene: Dry gangrene occurs due to lack of arterial blood supply, which may occur in:
1. Changes in arterial wall, e.g. atherosclerosis, arteriolosclerosis and thromboangitis obliterans
(Buerger’s disease). Commonest example is diabetic gangrene
2. Spasm of artery, e.g. Raynaud’s disease, ergot poisoning
3. Obstruction of the lumen of artery, e.g. thrombosis, embolism
4. Pressure from outside by tumour, tight plaster.

Pathogenesis and pathology: Dry gangrene occurs due to lack of arterial blood supply in the extremities,
particularly lower extremity. Usually gangrene starts at the great toe. The area is cold, dry, shriveled,
mummified, black due to iron sulphide, and foul smelling. Iron is derived from haemolysed red blood cells and
hydrogen sulphide is produced due to putrefaction by invaded saprophytes. Iron and hydrogen sulphide react
and form black iron sulphide. Dry gangrene usually occurred in distal part of extremity but the area of

gangrene may gradually extend proximally.

Wet gangrene: When bacterial infection is superimposed there is more liquefactive necrosis, and then it is
called as wet gangrene.

Sites:
1. Intestine is the commonest site. In strangulated hernia, intussusception, volvulus & thrombosis in

mesenteric artery

2. Bed sores
3. Lungs
4. Other sites: Vulva, scrotum, anterior abdominal wall, thigh etc.
