Myeloproliferative Disorders
(V.V.I for VIVA)

Q. Define and classify myeloproliferative disorders. (DU-05Ja, SU-22Nv,20M, BUP-21Nv/M,
CU-19M)

Q. What are the myeloproliferative disorders? (CU-16Ju,09Ja)

Q. Short note: Myeloproliferative disorder.

Ans.

Myeloproliferative disorder: Myeloproliferative disorders are proliferative neoplastic disorders due to
uncontrolled proliferation of abnormal progenitor cells which are capable of producing erythroid, myeloid, and
megakaryocytic series.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6""/311)

Or,
Myeloproliferative disorders are a group of conditions arising from marrow stem cells and characterized by
clonal proliferation of one or more haemopoietic components in the bone marrow and, in many cases, the liver

and spleen.
(Ref: Hoffbrand/6"/201)

Classification: Four disorders are included in this classification-
1. Polycythaemia rubra vera (proliferation predominantly involves erythropoiesis)
2. Essential thrombocythaemia (proliferation predominantly involves megakaryopoiesis)
3. Myelofibrosis with myeloid metaplasia (idiopathic / primary myelofibrosis)
4. Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML).
(Ref: Basic Hematology, Dr. Mainuddin/90; de Gruchy/6"/311)

(Note: Chronic myeloid leukaemia (CML), which used to be classified as a myeloproliferative disorder but is
now recognized as a separate entity.)
(Ref: Hoffbrand/S"/232)

Q Define and classify myclofibrosis with clinical features.

Q Short note: Myclofibrosis.
Ans.
Myclofibrosis: The term myelofibrosis is used to describe fibrosis and collagen formation in the marrow.
(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/311)
Or,
Myelofibrosis is a disorder charactererized by progressive generalized reactive fibrosis of the bone marrow in
association with the development of haemopoiesis in the spleen and liver (known as myeloid metaplasia).
(Ref: Hoffbrand/5"/238)
Or,
Myelofibrosis is a disorder of unknown etiology characterized by bone marrow fibrosis, extramedullary
haemopoiesis and leucoerythroblastic blood picture.
(Ref: Short Case in Clinical Medicine/5"/252)

Synonyms:
® Idiopathic myelofibrosis
* Myelosclerosis
= Agnogenic myeloid metaplasia
s

Myelofibrosis with myeloid metaplasia (MMM).
(Ref: Hoffbrand/5"/238)
Types:
1. Primary myelofibrosis: Myelofibrosis developing in polycythaemia vera.
2. Secondary myelofibrosis: Tuberculosis, secondary carcinoma, Hodgkin’s disease, leukaemia, and a
variety of other haematological disorders.

1. It is a disease of adult life, occurring most commonly between the ages of 40 and 70 years.
2. Symptoms of anaemia- weakness, lassitude, fatigue, dyspnoea on exertion, palpitation, and signs of
CCF (in severe case).
3. Massive splenomegaly (main physical sign) and hepatomegaly.
Weight loss, anorexia, fever, night sweats are common.
5. Bleeding problems, bone pain, gout, acute abdominal pain, leg cramps, or jaundice are presenting
manifestations.
6. Radiological bone changes occur in about 30% of cases.

>

(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/311)

Q. Give the laboratory diagnosis of myelofibrosis.

Q. Highlights the peripheral blood film of myelofibrosis.
Ans

Laboratory diagnosis of myelofibrosis:
A) Blood picture:
1. Hb% - Reduced (normal or increased in some patients).

2. Peripheral blood film:
RBC: Normocytic, normochromic with marked anisocytosis and poikilocytosis. Characteristic
‘tear-drop’ cells and also oval or elliptical cells are common. Polychromasia and
basophilia are often prominent. Numerous normoblasts and nucleated red cells are present.

WBC: Normal, reduced or raised.
Platelet: Normal, reduced or raised.

Reticulocytes: Moderately raised.

Polycythaemia
(V.V.1. for VIVA & MCQ)

Q. Define polycythaemia. (CMU-22Nv) What are the causes of polycythaemia? (DU-13Ju, CU-
16/12Ju,10Ja)

Q. Short note: Polycythaemia. (SMU-23M, CU-15/11Ju, RU-09Ju)
Ans.

Polycythaemia: Polycythaemia means elevated levels of all cellular components of blood.

It is usually used when there is elevation of red cell count alone, or in combination with elevation of
granulocyte or platelet numbers.

An increase in red cell count is typically accompanied by an increase in the haemoglobin concentration and
haematocnit (PCV).

(Ref: de Gruchy/5""/318)
Or,

Polycythaemia is defined as an increase in the haemoglobin concentration above the upper limit of normal for
the patient’s age and sex.

(Ref: Hoffbrand/6""/203)
Causes / types of polycythaemia:

A. True polycythaemia: Increase in the total number of red cells in the body (due to alteration of
erythropoietin).

1. Primary polycythaemia / Polycythaemia rubra vera: Idiopathic (decreased erythropoietin).
2. Secondary polycythaemia (erythrocytosis): Increased erythropoietin.

a. Secondary to tissue hypoxia (appropriate cause):
High altitude

Congenital heart disease

Chronic pulmonary disease (e.g. COPD)

Hypoventilation syndromes (obesity, obstructive sleep apnoea)
Smoking

Renal transplantation

b. Secondary to inappropriately increased erythropoietin production:
= Non-neoplastic kidney disease — cysts, hydronephrosis
= Tumours of kidney and liver — renal cell cancer (RCC), hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC)
= Cerebellar haemangioblastoma, phaeochromocytoma, adrenal adenoma etc.

3. Benign familial polycythaemia.

B. Relative polycythaemia: A reduction in plasma volume relative to the volume of red cells
(erythropoietin is normal).

a. Dehydration

b. Bums

c. Reduced fluid intake

d. Redistribution of body fluids

e. Spurious polycythaemia (polycytheaemia of stress).

(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/295; Hoffbrand/6"/204)

Q. Write the blood picture of polycythaemia rubra vera. (CU-10Ja)
Q. Short note on: Polycythacmia vera / Polycythaemia rubra vera. (DU-06M, CU-12/1 1Ja,06S)

Ans.
Polycythaemia vera: It is a chronic, progressive, and ultimately fatal disease, in which the fundamental
abnormality is an excess production of the formed elements of the blood by a hyperplastic bone marrow.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/295)

Clinical features:
Common:

1. Cerebral symptoms, especially headache and dizzines

2. Cardiovascular symptoms

3. Development of red face or bloodshot eyes

4. Weakness, lassitude, and tiredness

5. Gastrointestinal symptoms, especially dyspepsia

6. Visual disturbances

7. Pruritus

8. Thrombotic complications

9. Haemorrhagic manifestations

10. Peripheral vascular disease

11. Accidental discovery on routine examination.
Occasional:

1. Splenomegaly

2. Gout

3. Incidental discovery in investigation of other medical problems

4. Psychiatric manifestations.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/297)

Laboratory diagnosis:

A. Blood picture:
= Hb% - Increased (18 - 24 gm /dl, my be higher)

PCV - Raised (60-70%, and sometimes higher)
ESR - Decreased (1 mm/hour)
MCV, MCH & MCHC - Reduced.

Peripheral blood film:

= RBC count - Increased

= WBC - Leukocytosis

= Platelet - Thrombocytosis

= Reticulocytes - /.5% or above.

B. Bone marrow examination: Trephine biopsy my be needed.
= Cellularity - Increased

MEE ratio - Normal or reduced

Erythropoiesis - Increased

Granulopoiesis - Increased

Megakaryopoiesis - Increased.

C. Others:
a. Serum uric acid level - Increased
b. Serum lactate dehydrogenase level - Increased.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/299)

(N.B: The JAK2 mutation is present in the bone marrow and peripheral blood granulocytes in over 95% of

patients.)
(Ref: Hoffbrand/6"/295)

Lymphoproliferative Disorders

Q. Classify lymphoproliferative disorders.
Q. Short note on: Lymphoproliferative disorder. (RU-03Ja)
Lymphoproliferative disorders: These are heterogenous disorders of neoplastic proliferation of mature
lymphoid cells.

Or,
Lymphoproliferative disorders (LPDs) refer to several conditions in which lymphocytes are produced in
excessive quantities. They typically occur in patients who have compromised immune systems.

Types:
1. B-lymphoproliferative disorders:
i) Chronic lymphocytic leukaemia (CLL)
ii) Plasma cell leukaemia
ill) Hairy cell leukaemia

2. T-lymphoproliferative disorders:
i) T-cell large granulocyte leukaemia
ii) Adult T-cell leukaemia.

Nice to Know:

Hairv cell leukaemia: It is a chronic low grade hematological malignancy of abnormally shaped B

ivmphoeytes (hairy cells). The disease is marked by pancytopenia & splenomegaly
