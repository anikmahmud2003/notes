Q. Short note: Retinoblastoma. (SU-17M)
Ans.
Retinoblastoma: It is the most common primary intraocular malignancy of children.

Age: Diagnosed usually prior to the 4 years of age.

Pathogenesis of retinoblastoma: Two mutations of the RB gene, located on chromosome 13q14 lead to
neoplastic proliferation of the retinal cells. (Important for VIVA)

Morphology:
1. The pathology of retinoblastoma of both hereditary and sporadic type is identical.

2. Tumours may contain both undifferentiated and differentiated elements.
3. Undifferentiated elements appear as collections of small round cells with hyperchromatic nuclei.
4. Viable tumour cells are found encircling tumour blood vessels with zones of necrosis typically found
in relatively avscular areas.
5. Focal zones of dystrophic calcification are characteristic of retinoblastoma.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/1324)

Q. Short note: Glucoma.

Ans.

Glucoma: The term glucoma refers to a collection of diseases characterized by distinctive changes in the
visual field and in the cup of the optic nerve. Most of the glucomas are associated with elevated intra-occular
pressure.

Classification of glucoma:
1. Primary:
= Primary open angle glucoma (most common)
* Primary angle closure glucoma

2. Secondary:
® Secondary open angle glucoma

* Secondary angle closure glucoma.
