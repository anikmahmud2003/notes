Diagnostic criteria for diabetes mellitus (as per ADA & WHO): (Very Important for VIVA)

1. A single fasting plasma glucose > 7.0 mmol/L (=126 mg/dl) in symptomatic patients and on two
separate occasions in asymptomatic patients.

2. A random plasma glucose > 11.1 mmol/L (= 200 mg/dl) in an individual with classic sign/ symptoms
of diabetes (polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia & weight loss) and on two separate occasions in
asymptomatic patients.

3. A2 hours OGTT > 11.1 mmol/L (> 200 mg/dl) after a 75 gm load of glucose.

An HbAIc of = 48 mmol/mol (> 6.5%) is also diagnostic of diabetes.
(Ref: Lecture SSMC; Davidson’s/23"'/727)

(N.B: As HbAlIc reflects the last 2-3 months of glycaemia, it should not be used to diagnose diabetes where

Confirmatory biochemical tests for diabetes mellitus:

« Either fasting plasma glucose (FPG) or random plasma glucose (RPG) can be used for the
confirmation of diagnosis. In symptomatic individuals one abnormal plasma glucose measurement in
the diabetic range confirms the diagnosis.

* Asymptomatic individuals with a positive screening test (FPG or RPG) need another abnormal plasma
glucose measurement on another day for the confirmation of the diagnosis.

* OGTT is not recommended for routine use as a confirmatory test.

(N.B: Fasting is defined as no caloric intake for at least 8 h. Random is defined as any time of day without
regard to time since last meal.)
(Ref: Manual of diabetes mellitus)

Nice to Know:

Screening tests for DM:

* Screening is recommended for all individuals with one or more major risk factors for
developing diabetes mellitus

* FPG (fasting plasma glucose) is the laboratory test recommended for screening of individuals for
diabetes mellitus

* RPG (random plasma glucose) is an alternative test for screening.

* When facilities to do FPG or RPG are not available, CBG (capillary blood glucose) and UG (urinary
glucose) are alternatives screening for patients with classical symptoms of DM.

(Ref: Manual of diabetes mellitus)

blood glucose values are elevated but are not diagnostic of diabetes, glucose tolerance is usually assessed by
the glycemic response to oral ingestion of a glucose load.

Impaired fasting glucose (IFG): Impaired fasting glucose (IFG) refers to a condition in which the fasting
blood glucose level is consistently elevated above what is considered normal levels; however, it is not high
enough to be diagnosed as diabetes mellitus.

Or,
IFG is defined as the fasting glucose level between 6.1 to 6.9 mmol/L (110 to 125 mg/dL) according to WHO.

Diagnostic criteria: The WHO criteria for impaired fasting glucose (IFG) -

* Fasting plasma glucose = 6.1 mmol/L (110 mg/dL) to 6.9 mmol/L (125 mg/dL)
* 2hrs after OGTT glucose is normal, i.e. <7.8 mmol/L (<140 mg/dl).

Importance:
1. These patients are prone to develop frank diabetes mellitus and cardiovascular disease.

2. There is a 50% risk over 10 years of progressing to overt diabetes.
3. IFG is a risk factor for mortality due to the serious complications of type-2 diabetes mellitus.

Advice to patients with IFG: The patients are advised for weight reduction about 5 to 10% of their body
weight, regular exercise and follow up. Usually no drug therapy is recommended.
(Ref: Lecture SSMC; Long Cases in Clinical Medicine/1*/610)

Q. Define ketonuria. How it is formed?

Ans.

Ketonuria: Acetoacetate, B-hydroxybutyrate, and acetone are collectively called ketone bodies or ketones.
Excretion of more than a trace of these substances in the urine is called ketonuria.

Mechanism of formation of ketonuria: Glucose provides energy to our body. If intake is insufficient or
metabolism defective due to lack of insulin as in untreated or uncontrolled diabetes, body obtains energy by
breaking down fats & ketones. Ketones are toxic to brain and can cause coma found in diabetic ketoacidosis.
In ketoacidosis, ketonuria is always present. Urine ketone test detect acetoacetate and acetone only.
