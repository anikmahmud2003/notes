Introduction

Immunity: The classic definition of immunity is protection from infectious pathogens.
| (Ref: Robbins/10"/190)

Types of immunity:
1. Innate immunity (also called natural, or native, immunity)

2. Adaptive immunity (also called acquired, or specific, immunity).
(Ref: Robbins/10'/190)

Components of immunity:
1. Innate immunity: The major components of innate immunity are-
* Epithelial barriers
* Dendritic cells
* Natural killer (NK) cells
* Several plasma proteins (including the proteins of the complement system).

2. Adaptive immunity: The adaptive immune system consists of lymphocytes and their products,
including antibodies.

Types of adaptive immunity: There are two types of adaptive immunity:

1. Humoral immunity, which protects against extracellular microbes and their toxins, and
2. Cell-mediated (or cellular) immunity, which is responsible for defense against intracellular
microbes.

Humoral immunity is mediated by B (bone marrow-derived) lymphocytes and their secreted products,
antibodies (also called immunoglobulins, Ig), and cellular immunity is mediated by T (thymus-derived)

lymphocytes.

Both classes of lymphocytes express highly specific receptors for a wide variety of substances, which are

called antigens.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/190)

Tissues of the immune system: The tissues of the immune system consist of-

Q. What are the common disorders of immune system?

Immunological disorders:

= Hypersensitivity reactions: Here, there is altered exaggerated immune reaction to an antigen, e.g.

Q. Short note: Type-I hypersensitivity. (BUP-21Nv/M)

Ans.

Type I hypersensitivity: Immediate, or type I, hypersensitivity is a rapid immunologic reaction occurring in
a previously sensitized individual that is triggered by the binding of an antigen to IgE antibody on the surface
of mast cells.

These reactions are often called allergy, and the antigens that elicit them are allergens.

Anaphylaxis: Anaphylaxis may be defined as an immediate hypersensitivity response triggered by IgE-
mediated mast cell degranulation, leading to anaphylactic shock due to vasodilation and smooth muscle
contraction. It may be caused by drugs, bee sting, food etc.

Anaphylactic shock: Systemic anaphylaxis (anaphylactic shock) is a potentially fatal reaction characterized
by rapid onset of urticaria, bronchospasm, laryngeal edema, and shock after exposure to an offending antigen.

(Ref: BRS Pathology/5"/70)

Q. What is transplant rejection? What are the types? How prolonged survival of a transplant
can be achieved?
Q. Short note: Transplant rejection.

Ans.
Transplant_rejection: A major barrier to transplantation is the process of rejection, which is an
immunological reaction in which the recipient’s immune system recognizes the graft as being foreign and
attacks it.
Or,

Rejection is a process in which T lymphocytes and antibodies produced against graft antigens react against and
destroy tissue grafts.

(Ref: Robbins/10'/234)

Classification of transplant rejection: On the basis of the morphology and the underlying mechanism,
rejection reactions are classified as-
1. Hyperacute: Occurs within minutes or hours after transplantation.
2. Acute: Occurs within days after transplantation but suddenly.
3. Chronic: Progressive over a period of 4 to 6 months.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/235)

Measures to be taken to prevent transplant rejection:
1. Tissue matching-
= HLA typing and matching
* Blood grouping and cross matching
* Cross matching of patients serum with donors leukocytes

2. Immune suppression of the host by-
* Total body irradiation by x-ray
* Cytotoxic drug therapy e.g. corticosteroids, azathioprine, cyclosporine

3. Others: Such as injection of excessive amounts of antigens like those antigens shed from the grafted
tissue in the circulation. These injected antigens will neutralize the host antibody or T-cell.
(Ref: Prof. Akram/3"/193,194)
