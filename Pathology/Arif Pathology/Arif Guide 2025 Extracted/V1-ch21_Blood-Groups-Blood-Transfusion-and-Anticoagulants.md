Q. What is cross matching? Write down the aim and necessity of cross matching. (DU-08Ja)
Ans.
Cross matching: It is the antigenic matching of donor’s and recipient’s blood group antigens (RBC) and
antibodies (plasma).
Or,

The only sure safeguard against transfusion complications is to match the serum of the recipient directly
against the cells of the donor. This is called cross matching.

(Ref: S. Wright’s/13*/49)

Once the ABO & Rh blood groups of donor and recipient are known it is necessary to proceed with cross
matching before transfusion can be performed.

Necessity of cross matching:
1. There may be error in sample identification
2. The recipient’s serum may contain antibodies other than Anti-A, Anti-B & Anti-D. About 1% of
hospital patients possess antibodies outside the ABO and Rh system
3. Non cross matched O group blood is not free of risk
4. Non cross matched Rh -ve blood to Rh +ve recipient is not entirely innocuous.

Aim: To detect antibody in the recipient serum against the Donor’s red cells.
(Ref: Lecture DMC)

Q. Enumerate in brief the Rh-incompatibility. (SU-17M)
Q. Short note: Rh-incompatibility. (DU-13/11Ju, CU-15Ja,12Ju,09/07Ja,08Ju)
Ans.

Rh-incompatibility: It is a condition occurs when there is anti-Rh antibody in the serum of Rh (+ve) person
followed by antigen-antibody reaction.

Occurrence: Rh-incompatibility may develop in one of the following conditions-

1. When an Rh (-ve) person is transfused with Rh (+ve) blood, he/she will develop anti-D antibody. After
subsequent transfusion with Rh (+ve) blood, he/she suffers from hemolytic transfusion reaction, in
which anti-D antibody binds with Rh-antigen on the RBC membrane causing destruction of the RBC
(hemolysis).

2. In case of a Rh (-ve) mother pregnant with a Rh (+ve) fetus may develop erythroblastosis fetalis
usually from second to third pregnancy.

Explanation: When an Rh (-ve) mother carries an Rh (+) fetus, some fetal red blood cells enter the maternal
circulation at the time of delivery. Fetal RBC stimulates anti-D antibody production in the mother. During the
next pregnancies, anti-D antibody from mother crosses the placenta to the Rh (+ve) fetus & causes hemolytic
transfusion reaction in the fetus. Rh-incompatibility in a Rh (+ve) fetus growing in an Rh (-ve) mother results
in two clinical syndromes-

= Hydrops fetalis
= Hemolytic disease of newborn (erythroblastosis fetalis).

Prevention:
1. Administration of anti-Rh (anti-D) globulin after child birth or abortion within 72 hours or earlier
2. Prevent or minimize foeto-maternal bleeding during manual removal of placenta
3. No prophylactic ergometrine
4. Avoid Rh (+ve) blood transfusion to Rh (-ve) person.

Treatment: Replacement of the newborn infant’s blood with Rh (-ve) blood. Approximately 400 ml of Rh
(-ve) blood is infused over a period of 1.5 or more hours while the baby’s own Rh (+ve) blood is being
removed after a regular interval during first few weeks of life.

(Ref: Hoffbrand/5"/356-357; de Gruchy/5"/477)

Q. Mention the metabolic changes in stored blood.
Q. Mention 4 important haematological changes of stored blood. (DU-08Ja)

Ans.
Metabolic changes in stored blood: (V.V./ for VIVA)
1. Loss of CO,
2. Hyperkalaemia
3. Hypernatraemia
4. Hypocalcemia
5. Hypophosphataemia
6. Metabolic acidosis (Anerobic glycolysis: Conversion of pyruvate to lactate)
7. Iron overload
8. Low concentration of 2,3-DPG
9. Formation of ammonia from nitrogenous substance

10. Passage of substances through the RBC
11. Rarely citrate toxicity due to rapid metabolism of citrate in liver.
(Ref: Basic Hematology, Dr. Mainuddin/125; Lecture SSMC)

Q. What do you mean by hypersplenism? What are the causes of hypersplenism?

Ans.
Hypersplenism: Hypersplenism is a syndrome which is characterized by the triad of-

1. Splenomegaly
2. Anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, or any combination of these, in association with hyperplasia

of the marrow precursors of the deficient cell types

3. Correction of the blood cytopenia(s) by splenectomy.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/629)

Common causes of hypersplenism:

1. Kala-azar and malaria
2. Chronic hemolytic anemia

3. CLL, CML, myelofibrosis and lymphoma.
