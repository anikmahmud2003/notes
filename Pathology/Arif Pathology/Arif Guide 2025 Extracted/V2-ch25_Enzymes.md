Q. Short note: SGPT & SGOT. (CU-09Ja)
Ans.

SGPT & SGOT: Aspartate transaminase (AST / SGOT) & alanine transaminase (ALT / SGPT) function
normally to transfer the amino group from an amino acid, alanine in the case of ALT and aspartate in the case
of AST, to a ketoacid, producing pyruvate and oxaloacetate respectively.

Both SGOT & SGPT arc located in the cytoplasm of the hepatocyte. Although both transaminase enzymes are
widely distributed in other tissues of the body, the activities of ALT outside the liver are low, and therefore this
enzyme is considered more specific for hepatocellular damage.

(Ref: Davidson’s/20"/940)

Q. Short note: Troponin. (DU-06Ja)

Ans.

Troponin: Troponins are proteins that regulate calcium-mediated contraction of cardiac and skeletal muscle.

Types:
* Troponin I (Tn): It is totally cardio-specific, hence it is called cardio-specific troponin-I (cTn-I).
* Troponin T (TnT): It is not totally cardio-specific.
* Troponin C (TnC): It is not used as a cardiac marker.

Clinical importance: Troponin I (TnI) and troponin T (TnT) are structural cardiac muscle proteins that are
released during myocyte damage and necrosis, and represent the best indicator for diagnosis of acute MI,
because these markers have nearly complete cardiac tissue specificity and high sensitivity.

Troponin I (TnI) and Troponin T are not normally detectable in the circulation, but following acute MI, levels
of both cardiac troponins rise at 4 to 6 hours and peak at 24-48 hours. Troponin levels remain elevated for 5
to 14 days after the acute event.

(Ref: Robbin’s/10"/550; Davidson’s/23"/450)

(N.B: The troponins level may also be elevated in minor degrees of myocardial damage, pulmonary embolus,
septic shock and pulmonary oedema).
(Ref: Davidson’s/23"/450)

Q. A male of 56 years of age presented to you with excruciating left sided chest pain and
sweating, not relieved by taking rest and nitroglycerine sublingually. What is your
provisional diagnosis? What are the investigations you should advice and their expected
clinical results? (DU-11Ja)

Ans,

Provisional diagnosis: Acute myocardial infarction.

Investigations with expected results:
i) ECG —ST segment elevation, Pathological Q-wave formation, T-wave inversion.
ii) CK-MB - Markedly raised. It starts to rise within 4-6 hours and remains elevated up to 48 to 72
hours.
iii) Troponin-I — Markedly raised. It starts to rise within 4-6 hours and remains elevated up to 5 to 14
days.

Q. Name two enzymes acting as tumour marker. (RU-13Ja)
Ans.
Two enzymes acting as tumour marker:

1. Acid phosphatase: Marker for carcinoma of prostate and metastatic tumour of bone.
2. Alkaline phosphatase (ALP): Marker for metastatic tumour of bone.

Q. Write the clinical importance of PSA. (DU-10/08Ja)
Ans.
Clinical importance of PSA:

1. Measurement of PSA is the most important test used in the diagnosis and management of prostate
cancer.
20-40% of patients with a serum PSA > 4.0 ng/ml will have prostate cancer on biopsy.
High levels of serum PSA (> 100 ng/ml) almost always indicate distant bone metastases.
PSA is most useful for monitoring response to treatment and disease progression.
It is also a tumour marker of some breast cancers.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/981; Davidson’s/23"¢/439,1322,1324)

Q. Short note: ADA. (SU-17M)
Ans.

ADA: Adenosine deaminase (also known as adenosine aminohydrolase, or ADA) is an enzyme involved
in purine metabolism. It is needed for the breakdown of adenosine from food and for the turnover of nucleic
acids in tissues.

Importance: Its primary function in humans is the development and maintenance of the immune system. —
However, ADA association has also been observed with epithelial cell differentiation, neurotransmission, —
and gestation maintenance.

Adenosine deaminase deficiency leads to pulmonary fibrosis.
(Ref: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenosine_deaminase) _
