Q. What do you mean by the term ‘polychromasia’? (For VIVA)

Ans.

Polychromasia: When red cells take light blue diffused tint (for nucleus) in the stained blood film, it is
termed as polychromasia. It indicates young red cells (reticulocytes) and appears in increased number in
accelerated / exaggerated erythropoiesis, e.g. in haemolysis or in acute haemorthage.

(Ref: Lecture SSMC)

Q. Short note: Poikilocytosis. (CU-15Ja)

Ans.

Poikilocytosis: It means a marked variation in shape of the RBC (e.g. oval cell, pencil cell, spherocyte,
stomatocyte, elliptocyte, sickle cell, tear drop cell, fragmented cell / schistocyte, acanthocyte, burr cell, basket cell etc.).

Q. How will you prepare and stain a peripheral blood film?
Q. Name the stains used for blood film study. (RU-16/14Ju)

Ans.

Preparation of blood film:
Requirements:

= Glass slide

= Spreader slide (specially designed glass slide with a smooth edge)

* A disposable blood lancet

= Sterile gauze / cotton swabs

= 70% alcohol / spirit.

Procedure:

1. A drop of blood by finger prick is taken on a grease-free clear slide. The drop should be on the right
hand side.

2. The slide is fixed with the left hand by holding the left side between thumb and fore finger.

3. Another slide having smooth, even edge is taken as a spreader.

4. The spreader should be held with the right hand and placed on the slide in front of the blood drop at an
angle of 45° on the right.

5. The spreader is spread towards the blood till it touches the drop and the blood drop is allowed to run
along the spreader.

6. A film is drawn before the blood has reached the ends by light, quick and smooth gliding movement of
the spreader (head of film) towards the other end making the tail of the film.

7. Then dried the blood film in the air.

Q. Why Leishman’s stain is widely used?
Ans.
Leishman’s stain is widely used: Because-
1. It is very easy stain to prepare & takes only few minutes for staining
2. Granules of WBC, RBC & platelet take this stain well showing their characteristics.

Q. What is the composition of Leishman’s stain?
Ans.
Composition of Leishman’s stain:

1. Eosin methylene blue

Q. Why fixative is not used in the procedure of Leishman’s staining?
Ans.

Fixative is not used in the procedure of Leishman’s staining: Leishman’s stain contains methyl alcohol
which can fix as well as stain the PBF. Therefore in Leishman’s staining no fixative is used.

Q. Name the possible errors that can lead to excessive blue coloration of PBF.
Ans.
Possible errors that can lead to excessive blue coloration of PBF:

1. Over alkalinity of either the stain or the buffer

2. Insufficient washing with the buffer

3. Overtly thick smear.

Q. Name the possible errors that can lead to excessive red coloration of PBF.
Ans.
Possible errors that can lead to excessive red coloration of PBF:
1. Too short staining time
2. Excessive washing
3. Acidic pH of the buffer
4. Acidic ph of the diluting distilled water if the distilled water is used instead of buffer.

Q. Short note: ESR. (DU-21Oct/M,17/16/14Ja, SU-22/21Nv,19M,15Ja, 10Ju, RU-20Nv,18M,17/13/
07Ja, CU-16Ju)

Ans.

ESR (Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate): When a volume of blood is mixed with a specific amount of
suitable anticoagulant and is allowed to stand in a vertical position in a narrow tube, the blood cells (/ formed

elements) settle down leaving the clear plasma above. The sedimentation of red cells that takes place in first
hour is called ESR.

(N.B: Anticoagulant used: Trisodium citrate)

Methods of ESR estimation:
1. Westergren method
2. Wintrobe method

Normal value of ESR:
1. Westergren method 0 - 10 mm in first hour 0 - 20 mm in first hour
2. Wintrobe method 0 - 8 mm in first hour 0 - 10 mm in first hour

Clinical importance of ESR:
1. It is a measure of biochemical changes of blood. It has no specific diagnostic value. Thus, ESR is
non-specific, but it is useful as a screening test
2. Although high ESR always indicates presence of active disease but normal ESR does not exclude the
active disease
3. It also monitors the prognosis of disease process.

Conditions with high ESR: (Very Important for VIVA)

A) Physiological conditions:
1. Old age (over the age of 60 years, ESR of 30 mm in first hour may be normal)
2. Pregnancy
3. Vaccination
4. High altitude

B) Pathological conditions:

Q. Mention the factors that influence ESR determination.

Factors modifying ESR:
1. Red cell factors:

Weight of cells: ESR © weight of cells

Surface area

Roleaux formation: Roleaux sediment more rapidly than single cell as the weight of the cell
increases in roleaux & surface area decreases.

Number of RBC: Increased number of red cell reduces ESR and decreased number increases it.
Erythrocyte / Plasma ratio: Ratio increased (tPCV) = | ESR & vice versa.

Anemia: In anemia, ESR increases because change in erythrocyte / plasma ratio favours roleaux
formation. Microcytes sediment slower than macrocytes as macrocytes have decreased surface
area-volume ratio.

Shape: Red cells with an abnormal shape such as sickle cells or spherocytes hinder rouleaux
formation and thus lower ESR.

2. Plasma factors:

ESR is increased by elevated level of fibrimogen and to a lesser extent by elevated level of
globulin (a2, B, y globulin). These proteins favour roleaux formation by decreasing negative
charge of erythrocytes that tends to keep them apart.

ESR increased with increased acute phase protein, such as C-reactive protein, serum amyloid A
(SAA) protein etc.

Albumin and lecithin reduces ESR & cholesterol increases ESR
