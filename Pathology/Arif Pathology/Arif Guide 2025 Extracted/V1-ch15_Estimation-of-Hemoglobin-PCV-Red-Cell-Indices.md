Q. Give the basic structure of Hb in short.
Ans.
Basic structure of Hb:
* 4sub-units
* Each sub-unit consists of a globin chain and a haem moiety
* Total 4 globin chain (polypeptide chain) - 2 a chains and 2 f chains, and 4 ferrous iron.

Each globin chain combines with a heam group whose central iron atom is the site at which oxygen attaches to Hb.
(Ref: Lecture SSMC & ShSMC)

Q. Short note: Fetal hemoglobin (HbF). (RU-08Ju)

Ans.

Fetal hemoglobin (HbF): HbF or fetal Hb is normally present in the blood of the human fetus. It is normally
replaced by adult Hb (HbA) soon after birth. In certain individuals, it fails to disappear and persists throughout
life.

Structure: It consists of 2a chains and 2y chains (272).

Importance: HbF has a greater affinity for O2 than HbA because it dose not bind with 2,3 DPG (Dihydroxy
phosphoglycerate). This facilitates fetal RBCs to pick up O) at lower Po? available in the placenta. Moreover, it
is more resistant to alkali than HbA.

(N.B: 2,3 DPG only bind with B chain).
(Ref: Ganong/22""/534,535)

Conditions in which Hb-F is elevated:
Thalassaemia

Sideroblastic anemia
Haemoglobinopathies
Megaloblastic anemia

Aplastic anemia

Leukaemia

Early pregnancy.

Q What are the disadvantages of acid haematin method?
Ans.
Disadvantages of Acid haematin method:
* Acid haematin is unstable. The colour fades away immediately after reaching the peak concentration.
The colour intensity has to be matched with the brown colour of comparator box visually.
* There is no international standard for this brown colour comparator.
* There is more chance of personal error as it is a visual method.
* Not all type of Hb is converted to acid haematin e.g. sulphhaemoglobin, methaemoglobin.
(Ref: Lecture DMC)

Q. Short note on: Packed cell volume (PCV) (RU-17/12Ju) / Haematocrit. (RU-11/08Ja)
Ans.

Packed cell volume (PCV): Packed cell volume or haematocrit is the volume of red blood corpuscles in
relation to that of whole blood.

Or,
It is the proportion of volume of red cell to the total volume of blood.

(Ref: Lecture SSMC)

It is expressed as the volume of RBC per litre of whole blood. It is also expressed as percentage. Such as, 45%,
i.e. 45 ml RBC is present in 100 ml of blood or, 0.45 L of RBC is present in | L of blood.

Normal level:
1. Male: 0.40 - 0.54 L/ L (40% - 54%)
2. Female: 0.37 - 0.47 L/L (37% - 47%)

Uses of PCV:
1. Determination of red cell indices
2. Assessment of anemia by the relationship 1 mm of PCV = 0.34 gm of Hb per 100 ml of blood
3. Assessment of polycythemia
4. Number of white cells can also be assessed by assuming the following relationship i.e. | mm of buffy
coat = 10000 WBC /mm’ of blood
