Q. Write down the role of hydrostatic pressure in edema formation. (RU-1 7Ju, 1 lJa)

Ans.

Role of hydrostatic pressure in edema formation: Hydrostatic pressure of blood always tends to push the
fluid outside the capillary. On the other hand colloidal osmotic pressure always opposes to extravasate fluid.
When hydrostatic pressure rises in such extent that it exceeds the colloidal osmotic pressure, then extravasation
of fluid occurs. As a result fluid is accumulated in interstitial space, causing edema.

(Ref: Lecture RMC)

Hyperaemia & Congestion

Q. Define hyperaemia. (CU-03Ja)
Ans.

Hyperaemia: Hyperemia is an active process in which arteriolar dilation leads to increased blood flow.

Affected tissues turn red (erythema) because of increased delivery of oxygenated blood.

Example: At sites of inflammation or in skeletal muscle during exercise.

Q. Define congestion. (CU-03Ja)

Q. Short note: Congestion.
Ans.

Congestion: Congestion is a passive process in which there is a local increased blood volume resulting from

reduced outflow of blood from a tissue.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/117)

Example: It can be systemic as in cardiac failure, or localized, as in isolated venous obstruction.

Types:
1. According to site of involvement:

* Localized congestion: Isolated venous obstruction

* Systemic congestion: Cardiac failure

2. According to duration of onset:
a. Acute congestion
* Lung (Acute left ventricular failure)
= Liver (Acute right sided heart failure)

b. Chronic congestion
= Lung (Rheumatic mitral stenosis)
= Liver (Chronic right sided heart failure)

Effects of congestion:

1. Congested tissues take on a dusky reddish-blue colour (cyanosis) due to red cell stasis and the

accumulation of deoxygenated Hb.

2. Development of edema as a result of the increased volumes and pressure.

3. In long-standing chronic passive congestion, the lack of blood flow causes chronic hypoxia,

potentially resulting in ischemic tissue injury and scarring.
4. Capillary rupture in chronic congestion can also cause small haemorrhagic foci.

5. Subsequent breakdown and phagocytosis of the extravasated red cell debris can eventually result in

small clusters of haemosiderin-laden macrophages.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/117,118; Prof. Khaleque/40)

Role of platelet in normal hemostasis: Platelets play important role in every aspects of hemostasis-

1. Vasoconstriction: When a blood vessel is cut or ruptured, platelets secrete serotonin and
thromboxane A, which cause vasoconstriction and thus reduce flow of blood outside the vessel.

2. Platelet plug formation: Platelets adhere to extracellular matrix collagen at sites of endothelial injury
by vWF. On activation, they secrete ADP & TxA; — Further platelet recruitment — Formation of
primary hemostatic plug.

3. Coagulation: Platelets also express phospholipid complexes on which clotting pathway activation
occur — Formation and deposition of fibrin + Secondary hemostatic plug.

Q. Short note: Decompression sickness. (RMU-23M)

Ans.

Decompression sickness: It is a particular form of gas embolism which occurs when individuals are exposed

solution in the tissues and the blood.
