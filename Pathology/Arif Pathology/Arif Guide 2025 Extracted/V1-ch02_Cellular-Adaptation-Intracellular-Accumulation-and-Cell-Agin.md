Q. Explain the mechanism of left ventricular hypertrophy.

Ans.

Mechanism of left _ventricular hypertrophy: Increased mechanical work due to pressure or volume
overload (c.g. systemic hypertension or aortic stenosis), or trophic signals (e.g. those mediated through the
activation of B-adrenergic receptors) causes myocytes to increase in size (hypertrophy). Hypertrophy is
dependent upon increased cellular protein synthesis, which enables the assembly of additional sarcomeres.

The pattern of hypertrophy reflects the nature of the stimulus. In response to increases in pressure (c.g.
hypertension or aortic stenosis), ventricles develop pressure overload hypertrophy, which usually causes a
concentric increase in wall thickness. In pressure overload, new sarcomeres are predominantly assembled in

parallel to the long axes of cells, expanding the cross-sectional area of myocytes. In contrast, volume overload
hypertrophy is characterized by ventricular dilatation.

Types of atrophy: (Very Important for VIVA)
1. Physiologic atrophy:
a) Atrophy during early fetal development e.g. atrophy of notochord or thyroglossal duct.
b) The uterus decreases in size shortly after parturition (delivery).

2. Pathologic atrophy:
a) Localized: e.g. denervation atrophy, disuse atrophy, pressure atrophy.
b) Generalized: e.g. due to starvation, senile atrophy etc.

Common causes of atrophy:

1. Decreased workload (disuse atrophy) e.g. skeletal muscle atrophy in a patient restricted to complete
bed rest or when a broken limb is immobilized in a plaster cast.

2. Loss of innervations (denervation atrophy) e.g. Atrophy to the muscle fibers due to damage of the
supplying nerves.

3. Diminished blood supply: In late adult life, the brain may undergoe progressive atrophy, mainly
because of reduced blood supply (ischemia) as a result of atherosclerosis in cerebral vessels. This is
called senile atrophy which is seen in the brain and heart (tissues containing permanent cells).

4. Inadequate nutrition e.g. marked muscle wasting in profound protein-calorie malnutrition
(marasmus), chronic inflammation, cancer (cachexia).

5. Loss of endocrine stimulation e.g. loss of estrogen stimulation after menopause results in physiologic
atrophy of the endometrial, vaginal epithelium & breast.

6. Pressure atrophy: Atrophy occurs due to tissue compression for a long time e.g. an enlarging benign

tumor can cause atrophy in the surrounding compressed tissues.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/59)

Mechanism of atrophy: Atrophy results from decreased protein synthesis and increased protein degradation
in cells.

1. Decreased protein synthesis: Protein synthesis decreases because of reduced trophic signals (c.y.
those produced by growth receptors), which enhance uptake of nutrients and increase mRNA
translation.

2. Increased protein degradation:
Example:

* Lysosomes contain acid hydrolases (e.g. cathepsins) and other enzymes that degrade endocytosed
proteins from the extracellular environment and the cell surface as well as some cellular
components.

* Ubiquitin-proteasome pathway is responsible for the degradation of many cytosolic and nuclear
proteins e.g. in cancer cachexia.

* Increased number of autophagic vacuoles: These are membrane-bound vacuoles within the cell
that contain fragments of cell components (e.g. mitochondria, ER) that destined for destruction and
into which the lysosomes discharge their hydrolytic contents. The cellular components are then digested.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/60)

tissue that is termed as brown atrophy. Heart shows it in chronic starvation.

Cellular Aging

Q. What do you mean by cellular aging?

Q. Short note: Cellular aging.

Ans.

Cellular aging: Cellular aging is the result of a progressive decline in cellular function and viability caused
by genetic abnormalities and the accumulation of cellular and molecular damage due to the effects of exposure
to exogenous influences.

(Ref: Robbins/10'"/67)

Individuals age because their cells age. Aging has important health consequences, because age is one of the
strongest independent risk factors for many chronic diseases, such as cancer, Alzheimer disease, and ischemic
heart disease.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/67)

Causes / mechanisms of cellular aging: Cellular aging results from a combination of following factors-

1. Accumulation of DNA damage (e.g. by free radicals)

2. Reduced capacity of the cell to divide (replicative senescence)
3. Defective protein homeostasis

4. Dysregulated nutrient sensing
