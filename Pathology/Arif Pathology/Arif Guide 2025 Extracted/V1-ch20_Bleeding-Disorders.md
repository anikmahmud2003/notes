Primary Hemostasis

Primary hemostasis - make a platelet plug.

Secondary hemostasis - make fibrin mesh by coagulation cascade to stabilize platelet plug.

Q. What are steps of primary hemostasis?
Ans.

1. Vasoconstriction

2. Platelet adhesion

3. Platelet activation

4. Platelet aggregation.

Q. What causes vasoconstriction?

Ans.

Vasoconstriction - mediated neurally and chemically. When endothelium is disrupted, endothelial cells secrete
endothelin for vasoconstriction. NO and prostacyclin production goes down (they vasodialate).

* Endothelin - powerful vasoconstrictor
* NOand prostacyclin (prostaglandin I) - vasodialator.

Q. What happens in platelet adhesion?

Ans.

Platelet adhesion - VWF (von willieband factor) is a glue that binds to exposed collagen in damaged tissue. It
then binds to glycoprotein VIII, and then glycoprotein IB (integrin IB) on platelet. VWF mainly comes from
endothelial cells, but are also found in alpha granules platelets.

Q. Short note: Haemorrhagic disorder / bleeding disorder.
Ans.
Hemorrhagic disorder: The haemorrhagic disorders are a group of disorders of widely differing etiology,
which have in common an abnormal tendency to bleed due to a defect in the mechanism of haemostasis.
(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/338)

Clinical features of bleeding disorders:

(a) Spontaneous bleeding into the skin, mucous membranes and internal tissues.

(b) Excessive or prolonged bleeding following trauma or surgery.

(c) Bleeding from more than one site.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6""/338)

Pathogenesis of bleeding disorder: Three major components of the normal haemostatic mechanism- the
vascular, platelet, and coagulation components act together in a co-ordinated fashion to arrest bleeding. A
breakdown in the normal haemostatic mechanism i.e. a defect in any one of these three components causes an
abnormal tendency to bleed.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/338)

Haemophilia is called X-linked recessive disorder: Hemophilia is the classic example of an X-linked

Defective gene is located on X chromosome.

Males are affected; females are carrier.
Affected male will not transmit the disorder to his son (no male to male transmission), because his Y

chromosome cannot carry the hemophilic gene. However, all (100%) of his daughters will be carrier
of hemophilia because they inherit his X chromosome containing hemophilic gene. Most of these
carrier women will be unaffected clinically because of presence of a normal allele from mother.

Female carrier will transmit the disorder to half of her sons and cartier state to half of her daughters
