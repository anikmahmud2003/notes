Environmental Diseases

Q. Define environmental diseases.

Ans.
Environmental diseases: Environmental diseases are conditions caused by exposure to chemical or physical

agents in the ambient, workplace, and personal environments.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/410)

Q. Short note: Environmental pollution. (DU-13Ju)

Ans.
Environmental pollution: Environmental pollution is the introduction of contaminants into the natural
environment that causes adverse change.

1. Air pollution:
* Outdoor air pollution: The commonest pollutants are sulfur dioxide, carbon monoxide, ozone,
nitrogen dioxide, lead, and particulate matter.

* Indoor air pollution: The commonest pollutant is tobacco smoke, but additional offenders are CO,
nitrogen dioxide, asbestos, volatile substances containing polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons
generated by cooking oils and coal burning, wood smoke, bioaerosols, radon, formaldehyde etc.

Water pollution: By different wastes and metals.

Soil contamination: Occurs when chemicals are released by spill or underground leakage.

Noise pollution: e.g. roadway noise, aircraft noise, industrial noise as well as high-intensity sonar.
Light pollution: Includes light trespass, over-illumination and astronomical interference.

Metals as environmental pollutants: Lead, mercury, arsenic, and cadmium are the heavy metals
most commonly associated with harmful effects in human.

Effects of Alcohol

Q. What are the diseases associated with ethanol abuse?
Q. What are the adverse effects of ethanol?

Ans

Diseases associated with ethanol abuse/ adverse effects of ethanol:

Acute alcoholism:
= Hepatic changes: Fatty change or hepatic steatosis.

= Gastric changes: Acute gastritis and ulceration.

= CNS changes: Alcohol acts as CNS depressant. Consequently there is stimulation and disordered
cortical, motor, and intellectual behaviour. Respiratory arrest may follow.

Chronic alcoholism: Chronic alcoholism virtually affects all organs and tissues, but principally

affects the following:

* Liver: Fatty change, alcoholic hepatitis, cirrhosis, portal hypertension, increased risk of
hepatocellular carcinoma.

= Gastrointestinal tract: Massive bleeding from gastritis, gastric ulcer, esophageal varices.

* Thiamine (Vitamin B,) deficiency: Peripheral neuropathies, Wemicke-Korsakoff syndrome,
cerebral atrophy, cerebellar degeneration, optic neuropathy.

= Cardiovascular: Alcoholic Cardiomyopathy (dilated congestive cardiomyopathy), hypertension,
decreased level of HDL, Increased risk of coronary heart disease.

= Pancreas: Acute and chronic pancreatitis.

= During pregnancy: Fetal alcohol syndrome (Microcephaly, growth retardation, facial
abnormalities in the new born, reduction in mental functions of the child as the child grows older)

Q. Short note: Kwashiorkor. (DU-10Ja)
Ans.

Kwashiorkor: Kwashiorkor occurs when protein deprivation is relatively greater than the reduction in total
calories.

Kwashiorkor occurs in children who have been weaned (often too early) and are subsequently fed an
exclusively CHO diet.

Symptoms:
1. Generalized or dependent edema

2. Weight loss (60-80% of normal)
3. Characteristic skin lesions “flaky paint” appearance

Q Shert note: Beriberi. (DU-13Ja,10Ju)
ARs.

Beriberi: Beriberi is a severe thiamine deficiency syndrome seen in population consuming refined grain.
This syndrome found in areas where polished rice is the major component of the diet.

Types: Adult beriberi is divided into two types-
a. Polyneuropathy (dry beriberi)
b. Beriberi heart disease (wet beriberi)
Wet beriberi:
1. Beriberi heart disease (wet beriberi) is associated with peripheral vasodilation, leading to-
= More rapid arterio-venous shunting of blood
= High-output cardiac failure, and
= Eventually peripheral edema.

2. The heart may be normal, have subtle changes, or be markedly enlarged and globular (owing to four
chamber dilation), with pale flabby myocardium.

3. The dilation thins the ventricular walls.

4. Mural thrombi are often present, particularly in the dilated atria.

Dry beriberi:
1. It takes the form of a non-specific peripheral neuropathy with myelin degeneration and disruption of
axons involving motor, sensory and reflex arcs.

2. It usually first appears in the legs, but it may also extends to the arms, so classically these patients
present with toe drop, foot drop and wrist drop.
3. This progressive sensory loss is accompanied by muscle weakness and hyporeflexia or areflexia.

(Ref: Robbins/10/443 + 8'"/1328)
