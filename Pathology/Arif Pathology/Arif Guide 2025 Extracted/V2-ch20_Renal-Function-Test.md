Q. Short note: Casts in urine with interpretation. (RU-13Ju)

Ans.

Casts: These are solidified or coagulated proteins formed in the renal tubules. These are microscopic narrow
cylindrical structures with parallel sides and rounded ends, varying in size and shape. Common casts are
cellular casts (epithelial, RBC, and WBC), granular cast and hyaline cast.

1. Epithelial casts- Found in renal tubular necrosis (RTN)

2. RBC casts- Found in acute glomerulonephritis (it is the hallmark of AGN)
3. WBC casts- Found in pyelonephritis

4. Granular casts- Found in glomerulonephritis

5. Hyaline casts- Normal findings.
(Ref: Prof. Khaleque/203; Long Cases in Clinical Medicine/1*/376; Lecture SSMC)

Q. Why serum creatinine level is more specific than serum urea level as a renal function test?

Q. Why serum creatinine estimation is more valuable test than estimation of blood urea? (DU-07Ju)
Q. Give the clinical importance of serum creatinine. (DU-11Ju,10Ja)

Ans. (Very Important for VIVA)

Serum creatinine level is more specific than serum urea level as a renal function test: Blood urea is a poor
guide to renal excretory function as it varies with protein intake, liver metabolic capacity and renal perfusion.

Serum creatinine is more specific as it is produced from muscle at a constant rate and almost completely filtered
at the glomerulus. If muscle mass remains constant, changes in creatinine concentration reflect changes in GFR.
(Ref: Davidson’s/20"/461)

Detection of Bence-Jones protein in urine:

1. Heat test: Bence-Jones proteins have unique behavior in heating. Urine containing Bence-Jones
