Q. Short note: Osteosarcoma. (DU-09Ja,07Ju, SMU-23/20M,16Ja, BUP-22M, CMU-
22M, 1 4Ja,12Ju, RU-19M,17/ 16Ju)

Ans. (Very Important for VIVA)

Qsteosarcoma: Osteosarcoma is the most common primary malignant tumor of bone and accounts for
approximately 20% of bone cancers.

Age group: The age distribution of osteosarcoma is bimodal, with 75% occurring before 20 years of age. A
smaller peak occurs in older adults (after 60 years of age), in whom it is frequently associated with
predisposing conditions such as Paget disease of bone, infarcts, and prior radiation (sometimes called
secondary osteosarcomas). Men are affected slightly more often than women (1.6: 1).

Sites of origin: Although any bone can be involved, tumors usually arise in the metaphyseal region of the
long bones; almost 50% are near the knee in the distal femur or proximal tibia.

Fig: Major sites of origin of osteosarcomas.

Morphology:

Gross:
= Osteosarcomas are bulky, gritty, gray-white tumors that often contain hemorrhage and cystic

degeneration.
« The tumours frequently destroy the surrounding cortices and produce soft tissue masses.
= The tumors spread extensively in the medullary canal, and replace hematopoietic marrow.
= Infrequently, they penetrate the epiphyseal plate, or enter the joint.

Microscopic:
= The tomour cells vary in size and shape (pleomorphism) and frequently have large hyperchromatic
nuclei.
* Bizarre tumor giant cells, and abundant mitoses including abnormal (e.g. tripolar) forms are
common.

= Extensive necrosis and intravascular invasion are also common.
* Neoplastic cells can also produce cartilage; if abundant, such tumors are classified as
chondroblastic osteosarcoma.

Chondrosarcoma: Chondrosarcomas are malignant cartilage-producing tumors. After osteosarcoma,
chondrosarcoma is the second most common malignant matrix-producing tumor of bone.

Sites of origin: Chondrosarcomas commonly arise in the axial skeleton, especially the pelvis, shoulder, and

Morphology:
Gross:

translucent cartilage, but the matrix is often gelatinous or myxoid and can ooze from the cut surface.

Microscopic:
* The malignant cartilage infiltrates the morrow space & surrounds pre-existing bony trabeculae.

Q. Write down in brief about Ewing sarcoma. (SMU-23M)

Q. Short note: Ewing tumor. (DU-05Ja)

Ans.

Ewing sarcoma: Ewing sarcoma is a malignant bone tumor characterized by primitive round cells without
obvious differentiation.

It accounts for approximately 6% to 10% of primary malignant bone tumors and the second most common
group of bone sarcomas in children.

Age group: Most affected individuals are 10 to 15 years old, and approximately 80% are younger than 20
years. Boys are affected slightly more frequently than girls.

Sites of origin: Ewing sarcoma usually arises in the diaphysis of long tubular bones, especially the femur and
the flat bones of the pelvis and presents as a painful, enlarging mass.

Morphology:

Gross:
* Ewing sarcoma usually arises in the medullary cavity and invades the cortex, periosteum, and soft tissue.
* The tumor is soft, tan-white, and frequently contains areas of hemorrhage and necrosis.

Histology:
* It is composed of sheets of uniform small, round cells with scant cytoplasm that may be clear due to
abundant glycogen.
« The presence of Homer-Wright rosettes (which are rounded cell clusters with a central fibrillary
core) indicates neuroectodermal differentiation.
" Fibrous septae may also be present, but there is little stroma.
* Geographic necrosis may be prominent.

Q. Give in short the pathogenesis (CU-19M) & pathology of rheumatoid arthritis.

Ans.

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA): Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a chronic autoimmune disorder that principally
attacks the joints, producing a non-suppurative proliferative and inflammatory synovitis.

RA often progresses to articular cartilage destruction and, in some cases, joint fusion (ankylosis).

Extraarticular lesions may involve skin, heart, blood vessels, and lungs, leading to clinical manifestations that
overlap with other autoimmune disorders including systemic lupus erythematosus and scleroderma.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/1199)

against self-antigens and inflammation caused by cytokines, predominantly secreted by CD4+ T cells. CD4+
T helper (T,;) cells initiate the autoimmune response by reacting with an arthritogen, perhaps microbial or a
