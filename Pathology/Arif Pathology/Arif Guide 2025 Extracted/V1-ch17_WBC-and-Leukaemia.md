Q. Write down the functions of WBC.
Ans.
Functions of WBC:
1. Neutrophils: Neutrophils are migratory phagocytic cells. They locate, ingest and kill microbes. They
are the most important cells involved in the body’s defences against microorganisms, and lack of
adequate numbers of neutrophils predisposes to infection, particularly by bacteria.

2. Eosinophils: Allergic reaction, killing of parasites.
3. Monocytes and macrophages: Phagocytosis and catabolism of necrotic material.

4. Lymphocytes: Humoral and cell-mediated immunity.
(Ref: de Gruchy/6""/187)

Q. Short note: Agranulocytosis.

Ans.

Agranulocytosis: Literally, it means absence of granulocytes from the peripheral blood. It is a clinical
syndrome characterized by complete or ‘almost complete’ absence of neutrophils in the peripheral blood, with
severe constitutional symptoms and marked necrotic ulceration, especially of the mouth.

Causes of agranulocytosis: Drug idiosyncrasy-
= Antipyretic and NSAIDs e.g. aminopyrine, amidopyrine and noramidopyrine etc.
* Tranquillizers e.g. chlorpromazine, promazine, and other phenothiazines.
= Antithyroid drugs e.g. propylthiouracil, carbimazole and methimazole.
= Antibiotics and antimalarials e.g. sulphonamides, dapsone etc.

Clinical features:

History of exposure of offending agents e.g. drugs.

Sudden onset of high grade fever.

Severe sore throat and prostration.

Ulcerating necrotizing lesions on the gingiva, floor of the mouth, buccal mucosa, pharynx, or
anywhere within the oral cavity are characteristic features of agranulocytosis.

23M,22Nv/M,21Nv,20/19M, BUP-22Nv)
Ans.
Leukaemia: Leukaemia is a neoplastic disorder characterized by abnormal proliferation of haemopoietic cells
causes progressively increasing infiltration of the bone marrow, and in most cases liver, spleen, lymph node
and other lymphatic tissues.
(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/206)
Or,
Leukaemia is the malignant neoplasm of haemopoietic stem cells characterized by diffuse replacement of bone
marrow by neoplastic cells which in most cases appear in the blood and my infiltrate the liver, spleen, lymph
nodes and other tissues including brain.
(Ref: Lecture DMC)
Or,
Leukemias are a heterogeneous group of malignant neoplastic disorders of uncommitted or partially committed
haemopoietic stem cell characterized by diffuse infiltration and replacement of normal hemopoietic tissues by
neoplastic cells in the bone marrow and usually the presence of leukemic cells in the peripheral blood.
(Ref: Basic Hematology, Dr. Mainuddin/77)

Neoplastic cells are immature haemopoietic cells. Neoplastic cells (leukaemic cells) are most commonly of
myeloid or lymphoid series, but rarely of erythroid or megakaryocyte series.

Acute Leukaemia

Q. What is acute leukaemia? (SU-03M)
Ans.
Acute leukaemia: Acute leukaemias are malignant disorders characterized by uncontrolled proliferation of
immature haemopoietic cells i.e. blast cells, either myeloblast or lymphoblast. It is rapidly fatal if untreated,
but potentially curable with appropriate treatment.
Or,

This is a malignancy in which the marrow precursor cells are blocked at any early step of differentiation.
Primitive blasts accumulate in the bone marrow, spill over into the peripheral blood and may infiltrate tissue. It
is defined as presence of over 30% blast cell in the bone marrow at clinical presentation.

(Ref: Lecture DMC)

Nice to Know: (Very Important to Know)
= Acute leukaemia may occur at any age. In children, especially young children, it is usually |
lymphoblastic in type, while in adults it is usually myeloid.

= In acute leukaemia more than 30% of the bone marrow consists of blast cells. Blast cells less |
than 30% but more than 5% (usually 20%) indicates myelodysplastic suyndrome. ;

= Inchronic leukaemias, b/ast cells are present in smaller proportions, and the course of the disease |
is less aggressive than acute leukaemias but are Jess responsive to therapeutic intervention. These '
conditions are more commonly seen in middle aged and elderly patients.

= The chronic leukaemias are distinguished from acute leukaemias by their slower progression. '
Paradoxically, they are also more difficult to cure. So, acute leukaemia is worst than chronic |
leukaemia.

= Without therapeutic intervention, acute leukemia follows a short and precipitous course, marked |
by anemia, infection, and hemorrhage, and death occurs within 6 to 12 months. '
