Q. What is immunoproliferative disorder? (RU-11Ju)

Ans.

Immunoproliferative disorders: Immunoproliferative disorders (also known as “immunoproliferative
diseases” or “immunoproliferative neoplasms”) are disorders of the immune system that are characterized by
the abnormal proliferation of the primary cells of the immune system, which includes B cells, T cells and
Natural killer (NK) cells, or by the excessive production of immunoglobulins (also known as antibodies).

These disorders are subdivided into three main classes, which are-

1. Lymphoproliferative disorders
2. Hypergammaglobulinemia, and
3. Paraproteinemia.

(Ref: http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoproliferative_disorder)

Q. Short note: Paraprotein. (CU-13Ja)

Ans.

Paraprotein: Paraprotein is one type of immunoglobulin molecule or one type of heavy chain or one type of
light chain (monoclonal protein). It is produced under neoplastic proliferations of monoclonal cells of the
lymphoid series.

Paraproteinaemia: These are the disorders characterized by abnormal proliferation of immunoglobulin-
producing cells with an increase in serum level of a single homogenous immunoglobulin or its fragments.

Or,
Paraproteinemia is the presence of excessive amounts of a single monoclonal gammaglobulin (called a
paraprotein) in the blood.

Or,
The term paraproteinaemia refers to the presence of a monoclonal immunoglobulin band in serum and reflects
the synthesis of immunoglobulin from a single clone of plasma cells.

(Ref: Hoffbrand/6"/273,286)

Synonym: Monoclonal gammopathy, plasma cell dyscrasia.

Causes / Disorders associated with paraproteinaemia: (V.V./ for VIVA)
Multiple myeloma

Waldenstrom’s macroglobulinaemia

Chronic lymphatic leukaemia

Non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma

Benign monoclonal gammopathy

Heavy chain disease

Primary amyloidosis.

Q. What is solitary myeloma?
Ans.

Solitary myeloma (Plasmacytoma): It is a single neoplastic lesion of plasma cell origin. About 3 - 5% of
plasma cell neoplasms present as a solitary lesion of either bone or soft tissue. The bony lesions tend to occur

in the same locations as in multiple myeloma. Extraosseous lesions are often located in the lungs, oropharynx,

or nasal sinuses.
(Ref: Robbin’s/10"/607 + 7'/681)

Q. Short note on: Bence-Jones protein. (DU-05S, RU-15Ja, CU-10Ja)

Ans.

Bence-Jones protein: It is an immunoglobulin light chain either « (kappa) or A (lambda).

Bence-Zones proteinuria: When urine contains Bence-Zones proteins which is an free monoclonal k or A-
light chain of immunoglobulin is called Bence-Zones proteinuria.

It is found in various pathological conditions like-

1. Multiple myeloma

2. Macroglobulinaemia
3. Amyloidosis

4. Lymphoma

5. Leukaemia.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6""/287)
Detection of Bence-Jones protein in urine:

1. Heat test: Bence-Jones proteins have unique behavior in heating. Urine containing Bence-Jones
proteins flocculates when heated slowly to 50-60°C. The flocculated proteins on boiling, and reappears
on cooling below 60°C.

Q. What are the causes of hyperviscosity syndrome?
Ans.
Causes of hyperviscosity syndrome:
1. Polycythemia (most common)
2. Myeloma
3. Acute or chronic leukaemias
4. Rarely haemophilia, treated with massive dose of cryoprecipitate.

(Ref: Hoffbrand/6'"/284)

Q. What are the effects of hyperviscosity syndrome?

Ans.
Effects of hyperviscosity syndrome: Hyperviscosity syndrome is characterized by the following:

« Visual impairment associated with venous congestion, which is reflected by striking tortuosity and
distention of retinal veins; retinal hemorrhages and exudates can also contribute to the visual problems.

« Neurologic problems such as headaches, dizziness, deafness, and stupor, all stemming from sluggish
blood flow and sludging.

« Bleeding related to the formation of complexes between macroglobulins and clotting factors as well as
interference with platelet functions.

* Cryoglobulinemia resulting from the precipitation of macroglobulins at low temperatures, which
produces symptoms such as Raynaud phenomenon and cold urticaria.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/611)
