(Important for VIVA)

Q. What do you mean by pelvic inflammatory disease (PID)? Write down the etiology, pathogenesis
and complication of PID. (CU-10Ja)
Ans.
Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID): Pelvic inflammatory disease (PID) is an infection that begins in the vulva
or vagina and spreads upward to involve most of the structures in the female genital system, resulting in pelvic
pain, adnexal tenderness, fever, and vaginal discharge.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/988)

Etiology:
1. Gonococcus infection (most common cause)
2. Chlamydia infection
3. Staphylococci
4. Streptococci
5. Coliform bacteria
6. Clostridium perfringens.

(Ref: Robbins/10'"/988)

Pathogenesis:
= Gonococcal PID: Inflammatory changes start to appear approximately 2 to 7 days after inoculation.
Endocervical mucosa is the most common site of initial involvement. Gonococcal infection may also
begin in the Bartholin gland and other vestibular, or periurethral glands. From these sites, the
organisms may spread upward to involve the fallopian tubes and tubo-ovarian region.

= Non-gonococcal PID: The non-gonococcal bacterial infections that follow induced abortion, dilation
and curettage of the uterus, and other surgical procedures are thought to spread upward from the uterus
through the lymphatics and venous channels rather than on the mucosal surfaces. Therefore, these
infections tend to produce more inflammation within the deeper layers of the organs than gonococcal
infections.

Complications:

1. Acute complications:
=  Peritonitis
* Bacteremia (which in tum may result in endocarditis, meningitis, and suppurative arthritis).

2. Remote complications / chronic sequelae:
* Infertility, and tubular obstruction
= Ectopic pregnancy
* Pelvic pain
* Intestinal obstruction (due to adhesions between the small bowel and the pelvic organs).

(Ref: Robbins/10"/989)

Q. Short note: Carcinoma in situ, cervix. (RU-13Ja) (Important for VIVA)

Ans.
Cervical intra-epithelial neoplasia (CIN): CIN is a spectrum of precancerous changes in cervical
epithelium, and is graded on progressively severe degree of changes. It includes dysplasia and CIS (carcinoma

in situ).
Characteristics:
1. They represent a continuum of morphologic change with indistinct boundaries.
2. They do not invariably progress to cancer and may spontaneously regress, with the risk of persistence
or progression to cancer increasing with the severity of the precancerous change.
3. They are associated with human papillomavirus (HPV), and high-risk HPV (types 16, 18 & above) are
found in increasing frequency in high-grade CIN.
4. Progression to invasive carcinoma (when it occurs) may take place in a few months to more than a decade.

Classification: CIN have been classified in a variety of ways-
A. Cervical intra-epithelial neoplasia (CIN) system (depending on the extent of epithelial
involvement):
1. CIN-I: Mild dysplasia
2. CIN-II: Moderate dysplasia
3. CIN-III: Severe dysplasia (carcinoma in situ).

B. Dysplasia / carcinoma in situ system:
1. Mild dysplasia
2. Severe dysplasia (carcinoma in situ)

C. Squamous intra-epithelial lesion (SIL), current classification: The three-tier classification system
(CIN) has been recently simplified to a two-tiered system-
1. Low grade squamous intra-epithelial lesion (LSIL): CIN-I (mild dysplasia).
2. High grade squamous intra-epithelial lesion (HSIL): CIN-II & CIN-III (moderate & severe
dysplasia, carcinoma in situ).

Q. Short note: Transformation zone. (RU-19Nv)

Ans.
Transformation zone: Anatomically, cervix is divided into the exocervix (visible on vaginal exam) and

endocervix.

=  Exocervix is lined by squamous epithelium.
= Endocervix is lined by columnar (mucus-secreting) epithelium.

The point where the squamous and columnar epithelium meets is referred to as the sguamocolumnar junction.

With the onset of puberty, the squamocolumnar junction undergoes eversion, causing the columnar epithelium
to become visible on the exocervix. The exposed columnar cells, however, eventually undergo squamous
metaplasia, forming a region called the transformation zone, where tumors most commonly arise.

So, the area of the cervix where the columnar epithelium abuts the squamous epithelium is termed the
“transformation zone.”

= It is one of the most common neoplasms in women.

Morphology of cervical carcinoma: Invasive cervical carcinoma may manifest as fungating (exophytic) or
infiltrative masses. On histologic examination-

= Squamous cell carcinomas: These are composed of nests and tongues of malignant squamous
epithelium, either keratinizing or non-keratinizing, invading the underlying cervical stroma.

= Adenocarcinomas: They are characterized by proliferation of glandular epithelium composed of
malignant endocervical cells with large, hyperchromatic nuclei and relatively mucin-depleted
cytoplasm, resulting in dark appearance of the glands, as compared with the normal endocervical
epithelium.

« Adenosquamous carcinomas: They are composed of inter-mixed malignant glandular and squamous
epithelium.

» Neuroendocrine carcinomas: They typically have an appearance similar to small-cell carcinoma of
the lung, but differ in being positive for high-risk HPVs.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/998,999)

bw

Q. What is Meigs syndrome? (RU-18Nv) (/mportant for VIVA)

Q. Short note: Meigs syndrome. (RU-18Ja, 1 7Nv, 1 6Ja,14/12/1 1Ju, 10Ja)

Ans.

Meigs syndrome: The triad of ovarian fibroma, ascites and hydrothorax (usually only of right side) is
designated as Meigs syndrome. Its genesis is unknown.

The ascites and pleural effusion resolve spontaneously when the ovarian tumour is removed.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/1027; BRS Pathology/S'"/306; D.C. Datta/5"/616)
