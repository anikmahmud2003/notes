Structure of lipoprotein: A typical lipoprotein consists of-

1. A lipid core-containing non polar triglyceride and cholestery! ester.

2. A single surface layer of phospholipid, free cholesterol and apoproteins. Phospholipid and

cholesterol are so oriented that their polar groups face outward to the aqueous medium, making the
particle water soluble.

Fig: Structure of a lipoprotein.

Functions of lipoproteins: Since lipids are insoluble in water, they cannot alone travel in the aqueous blood
plasma. Lipoproteins are water soluble. So lipids can easily travel in blood as lipoprotein. So lipoproteins act
as transport vehicle for lipid in plasma.

1. Chylomicrons: Transport exogenous TAG from intestine to liver & peripheral tissue.

2. VLDL:

= Transport endogenously synthesized TAG from liver to peripheral tissue.
= Acts as precursor of LDL

3. LDL: Transport cholesterol from liver to peripheral tissue.

HDL: Transport cholesterol from peripheral tissues to liver for its degradation and excretion
(Reverse cholesterol transporter).

Clinical importance:
1. High LDL level causes increased cholesterol deposition in arterial wall resulting in atherosclerosis.

2. HDL removes cholesterol from peripheral tissues and thus prevents formation of atherosclerosis.
3. Imbalance in the rate of TG formation in liver and in VLDL secretion leads to fatty liver.

Q. Name the anti-atherogenic lipoproteins. Describe the role of HDL-cholesterol.
Q. What is the clinical importance of HDL? (CU-09Ja)

Q. Why HDL is beneficial for our health, explain? (DU-22Nv)

Ans.

Anti-atherogenic lipoprotein: HDL.

Role of HDL-cholesterol: HDL transports cholesterol form the peripheral tissue to the liver. In the liver,
this cholesterol can be either repackaged into lipoproteins, converted into bile acids or secreted into the bile in
unchanged form for removal from the body. So, the more the HDL-cholesterol, the less the cholesterol

deposition in peripheral tissue and the Jess chance of atherosclerosis. That is why HDL is known as anti-
atherogenic or, sometimes called “good cholesterol”.

A high level of HDL-C seems to protect against cardiovascular diseases, and low HDL cholesterol levels (less
than 40 mg/dL) increase the risk for heart disease. So, HDL particle is considered as protection to the body’s
cardiovascular health. So it is good / beneficial for health.

(Ref: Harper’s/29"/242; Lippincott’s/5"/234-237)

Q. What is good and bad cholesterol? Why they are called so? (BUP-21 Nv/M)

Q. Why HDL is called good cholesterol (BUP-22M) and LDL is bad cholesterol? (DU-
23Nv,08Ja)

Ans.

HDL is called good cholesterol and LDL is bad cholesterol: HDL is called good cholesterol, because HDL
removes cholesterol from the peripheral tissues to the liver where it is metabolized and excreted in bile, thus it

reduces tissue atherogenecity.
On the other hand, LDL is called bad cholesterol, because LDL carries cholesterol from the liver to the

peripheral tissues, thus it increases tissue atherogenecity.
(Ref: Davidson’s/21°'/451)
