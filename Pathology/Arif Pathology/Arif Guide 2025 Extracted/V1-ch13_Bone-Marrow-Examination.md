Q. What is bone marrow? Mention the normal composition of bone marrow.
Ans.

Bone marrow: Bone marrow is a spongy organ that fills the centre of various bones, where stem cells produce
RBC, WBC, and platelets.

Bone marrow is usually the primary site of hemopoiesis in adult. It replaces 1% of the body’s RBC daily,
creating more than 220 million RBC/day.

Q. Classify bone marrow. (RU-18Ja)
Ans.
Classification of bone marrow:

1) Red bone marrow: In newbom, the entire skeleton is occupied by the red bone marrow. By the age of
20, red bone marrow is found only in the diploe of skull bones, in the ribs, sternum, vertebrae, in the
cancellous part of some of the short bones and, at the ends of long bones. It consists of an interlacing
network of reticular fibers into which reticular cells are attached.

2) Yellow bone marrow: At the age of 20, most of the bone marrow changes into the yellow variety
which consists mainly of adipose cells with an admixture of macrophages, undifferentiated
mesenchymal cells & reticular cells.

(Ref: A.K. Datta General Anatomy/8"/79)

Q. Define bone marrow aspiration.
Ans.

Bone marrow aspiration: Bone marrow aspiration means aspiration of particles of bone marrow by suction
via a wide-bore needle inserted into the bone marrow cavity.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/11)

Q. What are the various stages of bone marrow examination?
Ans.
Various stages of bone marrow examination:

Methods:
1. Aspiration or needle biopsy
2. Trephine biopsy

Site of bone marrow puncture: Please see above.
Stages of bone marrow examination (Aspiration biopsy):

(a) The patient lies on his back.
(b) Clean the skin over sternum or other site with 70% alcohol (e.g. ethanol) or 0.5% chlorhexidine.
(c) Anaesthesize the area upto periosteum with local anaesthetic.
(d) A strong, wide-bore, short beveled needle fitted with a stilette and an adjustable guard should be used.
(e) After piercing the skin and subcutaneous tissues, when the needle point reaches the periosteum, adjust
the guard on the needle to allow it to penetrate about 5 mm further.
(f) Push the needle with a boring motion into the cavity of the bone.
(g) Remove the stilette.
(h) Draw about 0.2 ml of liquid marrow with a syringe.
(i) Make films on glass slides from the liquid marrow sample for microscopy.
(j) Fix the film with methyl alcohol for 2 minutes.
(k) Stain by the usual Romanowsky technique.
(1) Then assess / examine-
* The detail of the developing cells (e.g. normoblastic or megaloblastic)
The proportion of the different cell lines (myeloid : erythroid ratio)
The presence of cell foreign to the marrow (e.g. secondary carcinoma)
The cellularity of the marrow
The amount of iron stores present in macrophages or in developing erythroblasts.
(Ref: Dacie & Lewis/10"/116,117; de Gruchy/6"/11)

fe aine haps: Trephine is used to obtain a biopsy specimen in any case where aspiration gives a ‘dry’ tap
or ‘blood’ tap. Posterior iliac spine is the usual site. The trephine specimen is obtained by inserting the
biopsy needle into the bone and using a to-and-fro rotation to obtain a core of tissue and is examined as a

histological specimen after fixation in formalin, decalcification and sectioning.
(Ref: Hoffbrand/6"/28,31; Dacie & Lewis/10"/124)

b) Hairy cell leukaemia

c) Leukaemia

d) Other myeloproliferative disorders

e) Metastatic carcinoma

f) Lymphoma

g) Multiple myeloma

h) Hodgkin's disease

i) Tuberculosis (involving the bone marrow)

j) Densely hypercellular bone marrow.
(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/13; Lecture SSMC & DMC)

Q. What is trephine biopsy? (DU-11/09Ja)
Q. Short note: Trephine biopsy. (DU-19Nv,16Ju)
Ans.
Trephine biopsy: Trephine biopsy is a form of aspirating bone marrow which is performed by rotating a
specialized biopsy needle under pressure, usually into the iliac crest, to obtain a core extending from the
periosteum into the interstices of the bone marrow cavity.

(Ref: de Gruchy/6"/13; Dacie & Lewis/10"/124)

Procedure: Posterior iliac spine is the usual site for trephine biopsy. The trephine specimen is obtained by
inserting the biopsy needle into the bone and using a to-and-fro rotation to obtain a solid core of bone
including marrow and is examined as a histological specimen after fixation in formalin, decalcification and
sectioning.

(Ref: Hoffbrand/6"/28; Dace & Lewis/10"/124)

Indications of trephine biopsy:
» Suspicion of leukaemia

= Myeloproliferative disorders

* Myelodysplasia

« Aplastic anemia

* Malignant lymphoma

=# Myeloma

* Amyloidosis

= Secondary carcinoma

= Causes of splenomegaly

* Pyrexia of unknown origin (PUO)

» Any cause where aspiration gives a ‘dry’ or ‘blood’ tap.

Significance of trephine biopsy: It provides a panoramic view of the marrow from which overall marrow
architecture, cellularity & presence of fibrosis or abnormal infiltrates can be reliably determined.
(Ref: Hoffbrand/6"/28,31; de Gruchy/6"/13)
