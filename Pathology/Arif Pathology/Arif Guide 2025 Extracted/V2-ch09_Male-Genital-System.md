Teratoma: Teratoma refers to GCTs having various cellular or organoid components reminiscent of the
normal derivatives of more than one germ layer.

They may occur at any age from infancy to adulthood.
Malignant potential: It is most frequently malignant.

squamous cell carcinoma.

* Gross appearance is heterogenous with solid, sometimes cartilaginous, and cystic areas.
* Haemorrhage and _ necrosis usually indicate admixture with embryonal carcinoma,
choriocarcinoma or both.

It results from nodular hyperplasia of prostatic stromal and epithelial cells and often leads to urinary
obstruction. It is characterized by the formation of large, fairly discrete nodules in the periurethral region of the

prostate, which, when sufficiently large, compress and narrow the urethral canal to cause partial, or sometimes

It is not a premalignant lesion.

Age of incidence: Over 50 years, most often between 60 & 70.

Morphology:
Gross:
« Prostate is enlarged, weight become 3 to 5 times normal (60 to 100 gm).
Nodules in lateral lobe may compress urethra to a slit-like orifice.
Cross section shows hyperplastic nodules of various size, colour and consistency.
Nodules that contain mostly glands are yellow-pink and soft and exude a milky white prostatic fluid.
Nodules composed primarily of fibromuscular stroma are pale gray and firm.
There is no true capsule.

Microscopic:

® Individual nodules contain small to large to cystically dilated glands that are separated by bland
spindle-shaped stromal cells.
The glands are lined by two layers of cells, an inner columnar secretory cell layer and an outer layer of
cuboidal or flattened basal epithelium, and infolding of the glands may produce a papillary architecture.
In markedly enlarged glands, compromise of the vascular supply may produce prostatic infarcts, which

(Ref: Robbins/10"/976)

Q. Give the pathogenesis of benign prostatic hyperplasia.
Ans.
Pathogenesis of benign prostatic hyperplasia: The main androgen in the prostate is dihydrotestosterone

(DHT), which is the ultimate mediator of prostatic growth. It is synthesized in the prostate from
testosterone through the action of type 2 5a-reductase.

DHT binds to and activates androgen receptors (ARs) found in both stromal and epithelial prostate cells.
Binding of DHT stimulates ARs to translocate from the cytoplasm to the nucleus and activate the
transcription of androgen-dependent genes, which encode several growth factors and their receptors.

= Short note: Prostate-specific antigen (PSA). (CU-18M)

= Prostate-specific antigen (PSA) is a product of prostatic epithelium and is normally secreted in the
semen. It is an androgen-regulated serine protease whose function is to cleave and liquefy the seminal
coagulum formed after ejaculation.

In normal men, only minute amounts of PSA circulate in the serum.

Normal value of PSA: 4 ng/ ml.

Causes of increased PSA: PSA is organ specific but not cancer specific. Serum levels of PSA are elevated in-
* Carcinoma of prostate
* Benign prostatic hyperplasia (BPH)
=  Prostatis
* Prostate gland infarction
= Instrumentation of prostate
* Ejaculation.

Clinical importance of PSA:
1. Measurement of PSA is the most important test used in the diagnosis and management of prostate

cancer.
20-40% of patients with a serum PSA > 4.0 ng/ml will have prostate cancer on biopsy.
High levels of serum PSA (> 100 ng/ml) almost always indicate distant bone metastases.
PSA is most useful for monitoring response to treatment and disease progression.
It is also a tumour marker of some breast cancers.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/981; Davidson’s/23"/439,1322,1324)
