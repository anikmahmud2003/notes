Q. Enumerate the functions of liver.
Ans.
Functions of liver:

* Secretory function: Formation and secretion of bile.

* Metabolic function (Nutrient & vitamin metabolism): Liver is the main organ for almost all types
of metabolism of glucose and other sugars, amino acids, lipids (fatty acids, cholesterol. hpoproteins).
fat-soluble vitamins & water-soluble vitamins. It is the main site for alcohol metabolism.

= Synthetic function: Synthesis of plasma proteins (except gamma globulins), vitamin-K dependent
clotting factors- (II, VII, IX, X), steroid binding and other hormone-binding protems.

* Excretory function: It excretes drug, alcohol. bilirubin. hormone. cholesterol etc.

= Storage function: Liver stores glycogen, vitamin- A. D, B,:. iron, folic acid etc_

* Detoxification function: Liver detoxifies inactivates toxin, steroids, other hormones, NH: & drugs
such as penicillin, ampicillin, erythromycin etc.

= Haemopoietic function: In first trimester of fetus, liver is the mam site of production of red blood cells

(Ref: Guyton/11°/860-862: Davidson’s/21°/923-925)

Q Why prothrombin time is more specific than total protein estimation? (DU-09Ja) (Must be
asked in VIVA)

Ans.

Prothrombin time is more specific than total protein estimation: Prothrombin is produced only by liver.
Whereas, some plasma proteins such as immunoglobulins are produced outside the liver (i.e. by plasma cells).
So, total protein concentration in blood does not directly reflect the status of liver function. That’s why
prothrombin time is more specific than total protein estimation.

Q. Which one is more liver specific ALT/AST and why?

Ans.

ALT is more specific for liver damage than AST, as AST is also present in heart, brain, kidney and muscle.
High levels of AST are seen in hepatic necrosis as well as myocardial infarction, congestive cardiac failure and
muscle injury.

Q. What do you mean by direct and indirect bilirubin?

Q. What is indirect bilirubin? (CU-20/10Ju)

Ans.

Direct bilirubin: Conjugated bilirubin is called direct bilirubin. In aqueous solution, it is water soluble, and
reacts rapidly (within one minute) with the reagent diazotized sulfanilic acid in van den Bergh reaction. That's
why it is called “direct reacting” or direct bilirubin.

Indirect bilirubin: Unconjugated bilirubin is called indirect bilirubin. \t is not soluble in water. In plasma,
it is bound to the albumin. It is obtained by subtracting the ‘direct reacting’ bilirubin from the total bilirubin.
(Ref: Lippincott’s/3"’/283; Prof. Khaleque/194)

Mechanism / reasons: Normally, bilirubin passes through the liver and is excreted as bile through the
intestines. Jaundice occurs when bilirubin builds up faster than a newborn's liver can break it down and pase iI

1. Newborns make more bilirubin than adults do since they have more turnover of red blood cells

3. Too large an amount of bilirubin is reabsorbed from the intestines before the baby gets rid of it in the stool

* Cerebral palsy
* Deafness
* Kernicterus (brain damage from very high bilirubin levels).
