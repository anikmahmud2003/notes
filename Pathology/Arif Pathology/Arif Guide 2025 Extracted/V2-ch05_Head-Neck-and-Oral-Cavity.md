Q. What is leukoplakia? (CU-19/18Nv) State its common sites and its pathology.
Q. Short note: Leukoplakia. (DU-03M) (For VIVA)

Ans.
Leukoplakia: Leukoplakia is defined by the WHO as “a white patch or plaque that cannot be scraped off and

cannot be characterized clinically or pathologically as any other disease.”

Until proven otherwise by means of histologic evaluation, all leukoplakias must be considered precancerous.

Age at incidence: Adults at any age, but they are usually found in persons 40 to 70 years of age, with a2: 1
male preponderance.

Cause: Multifactorial origin, but most important cause is the use of tobacco (cigarettes, pipes, cigars and
certain forms of smokeless tobacco).

Sites: May occur anywhere in the oral cavity. But the favoured sites are-

* Buccal mucosa
* Floor of the mouth
* Ventral surface of tongue, palate and gingiva.

Fig: Leukoplakia.

Q. What is erythroplakia? (CU-18Nv)
Q. Short note: Erythroplakia. (For VIVA)
Ans.

Erythroplakia: Erythroplakia is a red, velvety, possibly eroded area within the oral cavity that usually remains
level with or may be slightly depressed in relation to the surrounding mucosa.

The epithelium in such lesions tends to be markedly atypical, incurring a much higher risk of malignant
transformation (pre-malignant).

Age at incidence: Adults at any age, but they are usually found in persons 40 to 70 years of age, with a2: |
male preponderance.

Cause: Multifactorial origin, but most important cause is the use of tobacco (cigarettes, pipes, cigars and
certain forms of smokeless tobacco).

Histology of erythroplakia:

* Severe dysplasia / carcinoma in situ / minimally invasive carcinoma (approx. 90% cases)
* An intense subepithelial inflammatory reaction with vascular dilatation accounts for the reddish
clinical appearance of the lesion.

Clinical importance: It is a precancerous state. Risk of malignant transformation (SCC) is much higher
than is leukoplakia.

(Ref: Robbins/10'"/734)

(N.B: Intermediate forms that have the characteristics of both leukoplakia and erythroplakia are termed
speckled leukoerythroplakia.)
(Ref: Robbins/10'"/735)

Q. What is Sjogren syndrome?

Ans.

Sjigren syndrome: Sjégren syndrome is a chronic disease characterized by dry eyes (keratoconjunctivitis
sicca) and dry mouth (xerostomia) resulting from immunologically mediated destruction of the lacrimal
and salivary glands.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/229)

(N.B: It occurs as an isolated disorder (primary form), also known as the sicca syndrome, or more often in
association with another autoimmune disease (secondary form). Among the associated disorders, rheumatoid
arthritis is the most common, but some patients have SLE, polymyositis, scleroderma, vasculitis, mixed
connective tissue disease, or thyroiditis.)

(Ref: Robbins/10"/229)

Pleomorphic adenoma (mixed tumour): Pleomorphic adenoma is a benign, slow-growing neoplasm

composed of a heterogenous mixture of epithelial and mesenchymal cells.

(Ref: Robbins/10'/751)

Pleomorphic adenomas are the most common salivary gland neoplasms. They represent about 60% of
tumors in the parotid, are less common in the submandibular glands, and are relatively rare in the minor
salivary glands.

Cause: Is little known, but radiation exposure increase the risk.

Characteristics:
* Pleomorphic adenomas are benign tumors that consist of a mixture of ductal (epithelial),
myoepithelial, and mesenchymal cells, which explains why they are also termed mixed tumors.
* Malignant transformation is rare. When they appear, these cancers are among the most aggressive
of all salivary gland neoplasms, with mortality rates of 30% to 50% at 5 years
* Cancers are usually adenocarcinomas or undifferentiated carcinomas.

Clinical features:
1. Painless, slow growing, mobile, discrete masses within the parotid or submendibular areas or in
the buccal cavity.

2. Often, the tumour is difficult to remove completely because of its proximity to the facial nerve,
and it is likely to recur after resection.

3. The rate of recurrence following parotidectomy is about 4%.

Fig: Pleomorphic adenoma.

Morphology:
Macroscopic:
* Rounded, well demarcated masses that rarely exceed 6 cm in greatest dimension.
* They are encapsulated with some exceptions e.g. in palate, where the capsule is not fully developed.
* Cut surface is gray-white with myxoid and blue translucent areas of chondroid (cartilage-like)
stroma.

Microscopic / Histologic:
= Great heterogeneity.
* The epithelial elements resembling ductal cells or myoepithelial cells are arranged as ducts, acini,
irregular tubules, strands, or sheets of cells.
= These elements are typically dispersed within a background of loose myxoid and hyaline tissue
containing island of cartilage and, rarely, foci of bone.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/748; BRS Pathology/5"/226)

Q. Why it is called pleomorphic adenoma? (RU-18M)

Ans.

Naming of pleomorphic adenoma: Pleomorphism is a term used in histology and cytopathology to describe
variability in the size, shape and staining of cells and/or their nuclei. An adenoma is a benign tumor
originating in glandular tissue.

Q. Give the morphologic features of adenoid cystic carcinoma. (RU-03Ja)
Ans.
Adenoid cystic carcinoma: It is a relatively uncommon tumor, which in approximately 50% of cases is
found in the minor salivary glands (in particular the palatine glands). Among the major salivary glands, the
parotid and submandibular glands are the most common locations.

(Ref: Robbins/10"/751)

Morphologic features of adenoid cystic carcinoma:

Gross appearance:
1. They are generally small, poorly encapsulated, infiltrative, gray-pink lesions.

Microscopic:
1. They are composed of small cells having dark, compact nuclei and scant cytoplasm.
2. The tumor cells are organized in a cribriform growth pattern that resembles swiss cheese.
3. The spaces between the tumor cells are often filled with hyaline material thought to represent
excess basement membrane.
4. Less common histologic patterns are tubular and solid.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/751)
