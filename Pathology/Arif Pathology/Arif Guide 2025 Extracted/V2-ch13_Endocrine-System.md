Histology: The thyroid gland is divided by thin fibrous septa into lobules composed of follicles (about 20-40)
which are the main functional units of the thyroid. The follicles are lined by cuboidal to low columner

thyroglobulin (which contains the thyroid hormones within its molecule). The thyroid gland follicles also
contain a population of parafollicular cells, or C cells, which synthesize and secrete the hormone calcitonin.

Q. What are the inflammatory conditions of the thyroid gland?
Ans.

Inflammatory conditions of the thyroid gland (thyroiditis): Thyroiditis encompasses a diverse group of
disorders characterized by some form of thyroid inflammation. This discussion focuses on the three most
common and clinically significant subtypes:
= Hashimoto thyroiditis
= Granulomatous (de Quervain’s) thyroiditis (painful) - probably secondary to a viral infection
* Subacute lymphocytic (painless) thyroiditis (postpartum thyroiditis).
(Ref: Robbins/10"/1178)

Q. Describe briefly the pathogenesis of Hashimoto thyroiditis. (DU-06Ja)
Q. Short note: Hashimoto thyroiditis. (DU-11Ja) (Important for VIVA)
Ans.

Hashimoto thyroiditis: Hashimoto thyroiditis is an autoimmune disease that results in destruction of the
thyroid gland and gradual and progressive thyroid failure.

Epidemiology: It is the most common cause of hypothyroidism in areas of the world where iodine levels are
sufficient. This disorder is most prevalent between 45 and 65 years of age and is more common in women
than in men, with a female predominance of 10:1 to 20:1.

Pathogenesis: Hashimoto thyroiditis is caused by a breakdown in self-tolerance to thyroid auto-antigens >
This results in progressive autoimmune destruction of thyroid cells by infiltrating cytotoxic T cells, locally
released cytokines, or by antibody-dependent cytotoxicity.

Induction of thyroid autoimmunity is accompanied by a progressive depletion of thyrocytes by apoptosis
and replacement of the thyroid parenchyma by mononuclear cell infiltration and fibrosis. Multiple
immunologic mechanisms may contribute to thyroid cell death, including-

= CD8+ cytotoxic T cell-mediated cell death: CD8+ cytotoxic T cells may cause thyrocyte destruction.

= Cytokine mediated cell death: Excessive T-cell activation — Production of Ty! inflammatory
cytokines e.g. y-IFN — Recruitment and activation of macrophages — Damage to follicles.

* Binding of anti-thyroid antibodies (anti-thyroglobulin, and anti-thyroid peroxidase antibodies)
followed by antibody dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity.

Grave’s Disease

Q. Mention the expected laboratory findings in Grave’s disease.

Q. Short note: Grave’s disease. (BUP-22Nv,16Ja, DU-19M,15/14/10/08Ju) (Important for
VIVA)

Ans.

Grave’s disease: Grave’s disease is an autoimmune thyroid disorder which the most common cause of
endogenous hyperthyroidism and is characterized by a triad of clinical findings-

1. Hyperthyroidism associated with diffuse enlargement of the thyroid gland
2. Infiltrative ophthalmopathy with resultant exophthalmos
3. Localized, infiltrative dermopathy (sometimes called pretibial myxedema).

Age: It has a peak incidence between 20 & 40 years of age. Women are affected as much as 10 times more
frequently than men.

Pathogenesis: Grave’s disease is an auto-immune disease, caused by the production of multiple
autoantibodies against the TSH receptor. These are as. under:

= Thyroid-stimulating immunoglobulin (TSI) - stimulates increased release of thyroid hormone.
= Thyroid growth stimulating immunoglobulins (TGI) - stimulates proliferation of follicular epithelium.
= TSH-binding inhibitor immunoglobulins (TBII) - inhibitory to binding of TSH to its own receptor.

Depending upon its action as inhibitory or stimulatory to follicular epithelium, it may result in alternate
episodes of hypo- and hyperthyroidism.

Morphology:
Macroscopically:
= Symmetrical enlargement of thyroid gland due to diffuse hypertrophy and hyperplasia of thyroid
follicular epithelial cells.
= Increase in weight to over 80 gm.
* On cut section, the parenchyma has a soft, meaty appearance resembling normal muscle.

Microscopically:
= The follicles are small and are lined by tall columnar epithelium and more crowded than usual.
= There is considerable epithelial hyperplasia and hypertrophy and formation of papillary infoldings
of piled up epithelium into the lumina of follicles.
* The colloid is markedly diminished.
* The stroma shows increased vascularity and accumulation of lymphoid cells; germinal centers are
common.

Goiter: The term ‘goitre’ means any enlargement of the thyroid gland, which is caused by compensatory
hyperplasia and hypertrophy of the follicular epithelium in response to thyroid hormone deficiency.

Pathogenesis: Impairment of thyroid hormone synthesis leads to a compensatory rise in the serum TSH level,
which, in turn, causes hypertrophy and hyperplasia of thyroid follicular cells and, ultimately, gross
enlargement of the thyroid gland (goiter).

Types of goiter:

* Endemic goiter
= Non-endemic / Sporadic goiter

2. Multinodular goiter
= Toxic multinodular goiter

Q. What are the causes of hyperfunctional and hypofunctional goiter? (CMU-24M)

Ans.
Causes_of hyperfunctional goiter (Overactive thyroid): A hyperfunctional goiter is associated with

excessive production of thyroid hormones (hyperthyroidism). Common causes include:

1. Graves’ disease — An autoimmune disorder where antibodies (TSI -  thyroid-stimulating
immunoglobulins) overstimulate the thyroid, causing diffuse toxic goiter and hyperthyroidism.

2. Toxic multinodular goiter (Plummer’s disease) — Multiple nodules in the thyroid become
hyperactive and produce excessive thyroid hormones.

3. Toxic adenoma — A single benign tumor in the thyroid autonomously produces excess thyroid
hormone.

4. Thyroiditis (Subacute or silent thyroiditis) — Inflammation of the thyroid gland, often viral or
autoimmune, can cause transient hyperthyroidism.

5. Excess iodine intake (Jod-Basedow phenomenon) — Occurs when individuals with underlying
thyroid disorders are exposed to excess iodine, triggering excessive thyroid hormone production.

Causes_of hypofunctional goiter _(underactive thyroid): A hypofunctional goiter is associated with
insufficient production of thyroid hormones (hypothyroidism), usually due to:
1. Iodine deficiency — The most common cause worldwide; insufficient iodine leads to decreased thyroid
hormone synthesis, stimulating TSH release and thyroid enlargement.
2. Hashimoto’s thyroiditis — An autoimmune disorder where the immune system attacks the thyroid,
leading to gradual destruction and reduced hormone production.
3. Congenital hypothyroidism — Thyroid hormone deficiency present from birth, often due to thyroid
dysgenesis or enzyme defects.
4. Goitrogenic foods or drugs — Certain foods (e.g., cruciferous vegetables, soy) and drugs (e.g.,
lithium, amiodarone) can inhibit thyroid hormone synthesis.
5. Post-treatment hypothyroidism — Following radioactive iodine therapy, thyroid surgery, or anti-
thyroid drug treatment for hyperthyroidism, the gland may become underactive.

Both hyperfunctional and hypofunctional goiters can present with an enlarged thyroid, but their underlying
causes and systemic effects differ significantly.

Q. Enumerate in short about the prognosis of thyroid cancer. (CU-08Ja)

Ans.

Prognosis of thyroid cancer: The prognosis largely depends on several factors including age, extent of
invasion and stage at presentation (presence or absence of distant metastasis).

* Papillary carcinoma: Excellent prognosis, with a 10-year overall survival rate in excess of 95%. 5-
20% of patients have local or regional recurrence, and 10-15% have distant metastases.

= Follicular carcinoma: 10-year overall survival rate 50-70%.

* Medullary carcinoma: Pognosis is excellent if no metastases. It has a late metastases and spread by
lymphatics and blood. 10-year overall survival rate 60-70%.

* Anaplastic carcinoma: There is no effective therapy for anaplastic thyroid carcinoma, and the disease
is almost uniformly fatal. Although metastases to distant sites are common, in most cases death occurs
in less than one year as a result of aggressive growth and compromise of vital structures in the neck.
10-year overall survival rate 5-10% and median survival about 2 months.

The Endocrine Pancreas

The endocrine pancreas consists of about | million clusters of cells, the islets of Langerhans, which contain
four major and two minor cell types. The four main types are B, a, 5, and PP (pancreatic polypeptide) cells.
They can be differentiated by the ultrastructural characteristics of their granules, and by their hormone content.

= The £ cells produce insulin, which regulates glucose utilization in tissues and reduces blood glucose
levels, as will be detailed in the discussion of diabetes.

= The a cells secrete glucagon, which stimulates glycogenolysis in the liver and thus increases blood
sugar.

= The 6 cells secrete somatostatin, which suppresses both insulin and glucagon release.

= The PP cells secrete pancreatic polypeptide, which exerts several gastrointestinal effects, such as
stimulation of secretion of gastric and intestinal enzymes and inhibition of intestinal motility. These
cells not only are present in islets but also are scattered throughout the exocrine pancreas.

There are also two rare cell types, D/ cells and enterochromaffin cells.
* DI cells elaborate vasoactive intestinal polypeptide (VIP), a hormone that induces glycogenolysis and
hyperglycemia; it also stimulates gastrointestinal fluid secretion and causes secretory diarrhea.
* Enterochromaffin cells synthesize serotonin and are the source of pancreatic tumors that cause the
carcinoid syndrome.
(Ref: Robbins/10"/1097)

Pathogenesis of type 1 diabetes mellitus: Type 1 diabetes is generally considered a T-cell-mediated
autoimmune disease involving destruction of the insulin-secreting B cells in the pancreatic islets.

As in all autoimmune diseases, genetic susceptibility and environmental factors play important roles in the
pathogenesis.

mechanisms of f-cell destruction are-

cells, CD8+ cytotoxic T cells, NK cells & B lymphocytes. This lesion is called insulitis.

b. Locally produced cytokines such as IFN-y, produced by T cells, and TNF and IL-1, produced by
macrophages damage f-cells.

c. Autoantibodies against islets cells and insulin are produced in 70-80% of patients.
= Genetic susceptibility: HLA-DR3 and DR4 are associated with increased susceptibility to type 1 diabetes.

« Environmental factors: Viruses such as_ coxsackieviruses group B, mumps, measles,
cytomegalovirus, rubella, and infectious mononucleosis.
