Q. What is haemopoiesis? Mention the sites of haemopoiesis. (BUP-22M)

Q. Define haemopoiesis / haematopoiesis. (RU-16/14Ju)
Ans.

Haemopoiesis: Haemopoiesis means formation of cellular elements of blood. It encompasses proliferation,
differentiation and functional maturation of stem cells to terminally differentiated blood cells. It consists of-

1. Erythropoiesis: Formation of RBC.
2. Granulopoiesis: Formation of granulocytes (neutrophil, eosinophil & basophil).
3. Thrombopoiesis/ Megakaryopoiesis: Formation of platelets.
4. Lymphopoiesis: Formation of lymphocytes.
a. Thymic lymphopoiesis - formation of T (thymic) lymphocytes.

Q. What do you mean by extramedullary hemopoiesis? Mention sites and causes of
extramedullary hemopoiesis. (Very Important for VIVA)

Ans.

Extramedullary hemopoiesis (EMH): It is defined as haemopoiesis occurring in organs outside of the bone
marrow. It may be physiological or pathological.

1. Physiological: For example, during fetal development, before formation of mature marrow, EMH
occurs in the yolk sac, fetal liver and spleen.

2. Pathological: It is more frequently associated with pathologic processes in a response to
erythropoiesis failure in bone marrow. Examples are-

* Haemoglobinopathies (e.g. sickle cell disease)
* Thalassaemia

Q. What is ‘megakaryocytes’?

Megakaryocytes: Giant precursors of platelets (1 megakaryocyte = upto 3000 platelets).

Q. How platelets are formed from megakaryocytes?

Ans.

Formation of platelets from megakaryocytes: Platelets appear to be formed by protrusion into the bone
marrow sinusoids of pseudopods of megakaryocyte cytoplasm, which detach into the bloodstream and
fragment to yield small discoid platelets.
