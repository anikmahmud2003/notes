## ✍️ Answer Script — Group A

### Q1. What are the risk factors of atherosclerosis? How an atherosclerotic plaque can lead to MI? How you can diagnose MI in pathology laboratory? 2+3+2

#### Risk factors of atherosclerosis (2 marks)
| Modifiable | Non-modifiable |
|---|---|
| 🔴 Hypertension | 🔴 Age |
| 🔴 Diabetes mellitus | 🔴 Male sex |
| 🔴 Smoking | 🔴 Family history / genetics |
| 🔴 Dyslipidemia (↑LDL, ↓HDL, ↑TG) | 🔴 Genetic predisposition |
| 🔴 Obesity / physical inactivity | |

+ Chronic inflammation (CRP, cytokines).

#### Plaque → MI (3 marks)
```
Atherosclerotic plaque (fatty core + fibrous cap + inflammation)
   ↓
🔴 Plaque rupture / erosion → thrombogenic surface
   ↓
Thrombus formation → coronary artery occlusion
   ↓
Ischemia → myocardial cell injury → necrosis → myocardial infarction (MI)
```

#### Laboratory diagnosis of MI (2 marks)
- 🔴 **Cardiac biomarkers:** Troponin T / Troponin I (↑ most specific), CK-MB (↑ early), Myoglobin (↑ early).
- ECG: ST-segment elevation / ST depression, T wave inversion, Q waves → acute MI / old MI.
- Clinical: chest pain (crushing, radiating), ECG changes.

### Q2. A 56 years old smoker male presented with firm, fixed lymph nodes at right supraclavicular region. FNAC of the lymph nodes reveal anaplastic squamous cell. Chest X-Ray revealed circumscribed coin like lesion near hilar region of right lung. What is your diagnosis? How will you confirm the diagnosis? 1+2

#### Diagnosis
🔴 **Squamous cell carcinoma of the lung (bronchogenic carcinoma, central/nodular type)** — fixed supraclavicular (Virchow's node) lymphadenopathy = metastatic spread from the lung primary; coin-like lesion near hilum = central squamous carcinoma compressing bronchus.

#### Confirmation
- **Bronchoscopy + biopsy** → histopathology (sheets of pleomorphic malignant squamous cells) + **IHC** (CK5/6, p63/p40 positive) → confirm squamous cell carcinoma.
- **Staging:** contrast CT chest, brain MRI, PET/CT, bone scan; TNM staging.

## ✍️ Answer Script — Group B

### Q1. Classify hepatic neoplasms. How a hepatic mass can be diagnosed? Describe the pathogenesis of HCC from HBV infection. 2+2+3

#### Classification of hepatic neoplasms (2 marks)
| Category | Tumors |
|---|---|
| **Benign** | Hepatocellular adenoma, focal nodular hyperplasia, cavernous hemangioma |
| **Primary malignant** | 🔴 Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), cholangiocarcinoma, combined HCC-cholangiocarcinoma, hepatoblastoma |
| **Metastatic** | Colon, breast, lung, pancreas (most common overall) |

#### Diagnosis of hepatic mass (2 marks)
- **USG / CT / MRI** (triple-phase CT: arterial enhancement + rapid **washout** = HCC), **Serum AFP**, **biopsy** (USG-guided) if needed.

#### Pathogenesis of HCC from HBV (3 marks)
```
Chronic HBV infection
   ↓
Hepatocyte injury + inflammation (immune-mediated, HBx)
   ↓
Fibrosis / cirrhosis
   ↓
Dysplasia → HCC
```

### Q2. A testicular mass. What may be the causes? How will you investigate the case? 1+2

#### Causes of testicular mass (1 mark)
Hydrocele, spermatocele, varicocele, epididymitis/torsion, **seminoma**, non-seminomatous germ cell tumour, metastasis.

#### Investigation (2 marks)
- Scrotal **USG**, **FNAC/biopsy**, **tumor markers** (AFP, hCG, LDH, inhibin), hormonal profile (testosterone, FSH/LH).

## ✍️ Answer Script — Group A (cont.) — Optional Questions from Q3, Q4, Q5 (since Q1 & Q2 are compulsory)

### Q3. Enumerate the causes of generalized lymphadenopathy. What are the differences between Hodgkin and Non-Hodgkin lymphomas? Mention the variants of R-S cells with the tumors they found in. 1.5+2+1.5

- **Causes:** infections (EBV, TB, HIV, bacterial), malignancies (lymphoma), autoimmune.
- **Hodgkin vs NHL:** Hodgkin has **Reed–Sternberg cells** (CD15+, CD30+, MUM1+, EMA+); NHL — B-cell (most), T-cell, NK-cell lymphomas; different cell markers/clinical behavior.
- **R-S cell variants:** lacunar (classical Hodgkin), binucleated (Burkitt), multinucleated (etc.).

### Q4. What are the obstructive lung diseases with their etiology? What is COPD? 4+1

- **Obstructive lung diseases:** COPD (emphysema, chronic bronchitis), asthma, bronchiectasis, cystic fibrosis, hypersensitivity pneumonitis.
- **COPD:** chronic **obstructive** airflow limitation from emphysema (α1-antitrypsin, smoking) / chronic bronchitis (smoking).

### Q5. Classify glomerular diseases. How you can differentiate between nephrotic and nephritic syndromes form urine examination? 4+1

- **Glomerular diseases:** primary (glomerulonephritis: IgA nephropathy, post-streptococcal, lupus, diabetic) vs secondary (hypertension, tumors).
- **Nephrotic vs nephritic (urine):** nephrotic → **proteinuria >3.5 g/day** (+ hypoalbuminemia, edema, lipiduria, low/normal urine A/B ratio); nephritic → **hematuria + RBC casts + oliguria**, urine protein moderate (<3.5 g/day), urine A/B ratio >1.

### Q5. Short note (any two): a. Gall bladder stone, b. Cirrhosis, c. Renal stone 2.5+2.5

- **Gall bladder stone:** cholesterol/pigment stones; risk factors: age, female, obesity; pathogenesis: bile stasis → cholesterol precipitation.
- **Cirrhosis:** end-stage liver fibrosis (bridging septa, regenerative nodules, vascular shunting).
- **Renal stone:** Ca oxalate/pH, infection, etc.
