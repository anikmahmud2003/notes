There are three basic types of symbiosis:

a. Mutualism

b. Commensalism

c. Parasitism

Mutualism: Mutualism is a relationship in which both species are benefited. (FOS)

[Old book: Mutualism = Symbiosis (both organism benefited)]

e.g., relation between some normal.flora and human.

Humans, and the bacteria in our gut. Lactobacilli are a common type of bacteria found in yogurt,
cheese, and some plants. So, when you eat such food, lactobacilli will make a home out of your
intestines by feeding off of the sugars you eat while simultaneously helping you digest that sugar.
Both parties benefit, so this is also a mutualistic relationship.

Commensalism: Commensalism is a relationship in which one species is benefited while the

other species is not affected. (APO)

e.g., association between most of the Normal flora and human.

An example is the bacterial flora found on human skin. But, there is evidence the bacteria confer
some protection on the host (which would be mutualism). Another example: Candida albicans and

human.

Parasitism: Parasitism is a symbiotic relationship in which one species (the parasite) is benefited
_ while the other species (the host) is harmed. (f©#)
e.g., all pathogenic organism (Bacteria, all virus, all parasites) and human.

Amensalism -A relationship in which one organism is harmed while the other is not affected.
arasitism - A relationship in which one organism benefits and the other is harmed.

The gut normal flora plays an important role in mental health (Gut brain axis

Maintaining the pH.

Coliform bacteria:

Coliform bacteria are universally present in large numbers in the gastrointestinal tract (colon)

of warm-blooded animals. Coliforms may also found in the aquatic environment, in soil and on
vegetation.

Coliform bacteria are gram-negative bacilli that ferment lactose (as they possess B-
galactosidase) to produce acids and gases under their optimal growth temperature of 35-37°C.

Coliform bacteria will not likely cause illness. However, their presence (especially E. coli)
in drinking water indicates the possibility of contamination with disease-causing organism
(pathogens) in the water system.

They are-
I. Escherichia coli
2. Klebsiella pneumoniae

3. Enterobacter species (E. cloacae, E. aerogenes)
4. Citrobacter freundii
5. Serratia

How Lactobacillus prevent colonization of pathogenic bacteria?
Due to the effect of estrogen, the vaginal flora of adult women consists primarily of Lactobacillus

species. Lactobacilli are responsible for producing the acid that keeps the pH of the adult woman’s
vagina low. Low pH suppress the growth of potential pathogens.

However, suppression of Lactobacilli by antibiotics can lead to overgrowth of C. albicans result in
vaginal candidiasis (moniliasis). %

Probiotics: .
Probiotics are live, nonpathogenic. bacteria (or yeast) that may be effective in the treatment or
prevention of certain human diseases.
They prevent colonization of pathogenic bacteria (colonization resistance) by blocking the binding
receptors.
a. Oral administration of live Lactobacillus rhamnosus or Lactobacillus acidophilus
strain, preventing diarrhoea in young children.
b. Administration of Saccharomyces boulardii reduces the Cl. difficile infection.
c. Ifmental illness occurs due to dysbiosis (imbalance) of normal gut flora, treatment with
probiotics exert protective effects.

Prebiotics:
Prebiotics are the dietary nondigestible fibers which when administered, stimulate the growth

and activity of commensal microorganisms and thereby exert beneficiary effect to the host
indirectly.
