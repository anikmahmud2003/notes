Q4
d) Mention the important properties of Staphylococcus aureus. **
Ans:
  • Gram stain: Gram-positive cocci, 1μm, arranged in grape-like clusters
  • Catalase: Positive
  • Coagulase: Positive
  • Hemolysis: β-hemolytic on blood agar
  • Mannitol: Ferments mannitol
  • Growth: Facultative anaerobe
  • Motility & Spore: Non-motile, non-spore forming
  • Capsule: Occasionally capsulated (microcapsule)
  • Pigment: Golden yellow due to staphyloxanthin
  • Normal habitat: Skin, anterior nares, nasopharynx
Q5
e) Mention the important virulence factors/toxins & enzymes of S. aureus with functions. *
Ans:

A. Cell wall & associated components

  • Protein A: Binds Fc of IgG → prevents complement activation & opsonization → anti-phagocytic
  • Teichoic acid & Lipoteichoic acid: Endotoxin-like activity, role in septic shock
  • Peptidoglycan: Endotoxin-like activity
  • Microcapsule: Inhibits phagocytosis
  • Clumping factor (Bound coagulase): Binds fibrinogen → forms clumps

B. Toxins

  • Enterotoxins: Superantigen → staphylococcal food poisoning (prominent vomiting)
  • TSST-1 (Toxic Shock Syndrome Toxin): Superantigen → Toxic Shock Syndrome
  • Exfoliatin (Epidermolytic toxin): Protease → Scalded Skin Syndrome
  • Hemolysins (α,β,γ,δ): Lyse RBCs, γ also acts as leukocidin
  • P-V Leukocidin (Panton-Valentine): Pore-forming toxin, damages leukocytes

C. Enzymes

  • Coagulase (free): Forms fibrin clot → walls off infection
  • Staphylokinase: Fibrinolysin
  • Hyaluronidase: Spreading factor
  • DNase/Nuclease: Liquefies pus
  • β-lactamase: Drug resistance (penicillinase)
  • Protease, Lipase, Catalase: Tissue invasion & survival
Q6
f) Different diseases produced by S. aureus. ****
Ans:

1. Pyogenic (Suppurative) Diseases

  • Skin: Boil, furuncle, carbuncle, folliculitis, impetigo
  • Wound infections, abscesses
  • Pneumonia (post-viral)
  • Osteomyelitis
  • Septicemia, Endocarditis
  • Meningitis
  • Conjunctivitis

2. Toxigenic Diseases

  • Staphylococcal food poisoning: Preformed enterotoxin
  • Toxic Shock Syndrome (TSS): TSST-1 mediated
  • Scalded Skin Syndrome (Ritter's disease): Exfoliatin mediated
Q7
g) Importance of protein A.
Ans: Protein A is a cell wall component of S. aureus. It binds to the Fc portion of IgG at the complement binding site, preventing complement activation. As a result, C3b is not formed, opsonization is reduced and phagocytosis is prevented. This is an important anti-phagocytic virulence factor. Coagulase-negative staphylococci do not produce Protein A.
Q8
h) Give the mechanism of staphylococcal food poisoning. ***
Ans:
S. aureus grows in protein & carbohydrate-rich food (cream pastries, bakery items)
↓
Preformed heat-stable Enterotoxin produced
↓
Ingestion of contaminated food
↓
Enterotoxin acts as a SUPERANTIGEN → directly activates CD4+ T cells
↓
Release of IL-1 (from macrophages) & IL-2 (from CD4+ T cells)
↓
GI damage & Mast cell activation → Serotonin release
↓
Serotonin stimulates enteric nervous system via Vagus nerve
↓
Acts on Vomiting Centre of CNS → Prominent vomiting followed by watery diarrhoea

Key: Prominent vomiting is characteristic of staphylococcal food poisoning. If loose motion comes first followed by vomiting → think gastroenteritis from other causes.

Q9
i) What is MRSA, VRSA, VISA? **
Ans:
  • MRSA (Methicillin Resistant Staphylococcus aureus): Resistant to methicillin and all β-lactam antibiotics due to mecA gene coding for altered Penicillin Binding Protein (PBP2a). Multi-drug resistant.
  • VISA (Vancomycin Intermediate Staphylococcus aureus): Shows intermediate resistance to vancomycin.
  • VRSA (Vancomycin Resistant Staphylococcus aureus): Fully resistant to vancomycin due to acquisition of vanA gene (usually from enterococci) substituting D-lactate for D-alanine in peptidoglycan.
Drug of choice for MRSA: Vancomycin. For VRSA/VISA: Daptomycin or Quinupristin-Dalfopristin.
Q10
j) Which antibiotic is suitable for treatment of MRSA? *
Ans: Vancomycin is the drug of choice for treatment of MRSA infections.

Ans: Based on M-protein (type-specific antigen of Group A S. pyogenes), Griffith classified into more than 100 serotypes:

  • Nephritogenic strains: Cause Acute Post-streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (APSGN) e.g. M types 2,12,49,55,57,59-61

Q15
5) What are the diseases/immunological diseases caused by S. pyogenes? ***
Ans:

1. Pyogenic (Suppurative)

  • Pharyngitis (sore throat)
  • Impetigo/Pyoderma
  • Erysipelas, Cellulitis
  • Otitis media, Sinusitis
  • Pneumonia
  • Necrotizing fasciitis
  • Puerperal sepsis
  • Osteomyelitis, Meningitis

2. Toxigenic

  • Scarlet fever - due to Erythrogenic toxin
  • Streptococcal Toxic Shock Syndrome - due to Pyrogenic exotoxin A

3. Immunological (Non-suppurative)

  • Acute Rheumatic Fever (ARF) - follows pharyngitis (2 weeks later), Type II hypersensitivity (molecular mimicry)
  • Acute Post-streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (APSGN) - follows pharyngitis or pyoderma (1-3 weeks later), mainly Type III hypersensitivity
Q16
6) State the pathogenesis of AGN/Post streptococcal AGN **
Ans:
Nephritogenic strain of S. pyogenes (skin infection/pyoderma or rarely pharyngitis)
↓
Antibody formation against M-protein
↓
Formation of Antigen-Antibody (Immune) Complex in circulation
↓
Deposition of immune complex in Glomerular Basement Membrane
↓
Activation of Complement (C3)
↓
C3a, C5a act as chemotactic factors → Neutrophil aggregation
↓
Release of lysosomal enzymes & toxins from neutrophils
↓
Glomerular injury → Acute Glomerulonephritis
Type: Mainly Type III hypersensitivity (Immune complex mediated)
Q17
7) State the pathogenesis of rheumatic fever. ***
Ans:
Rheumatogenic strain of S. pyogenes causing pharyngitis (M types 1,3,5,6,14,18,19,24)
↓
Antibody formation against streptococcal M-protein
↓
Molecular mimicry: These antibodies cross-react with host tissue antigens
(Heart valve glycoproteins, Cardiac myosin, Joint tissues, sometimes Brain)
↓
Autoimmune reaction
↓
Type II Hypersensitivity Reaction
↓
Inflammation of heart (carditis), joints (arthritis), CNS (chorea), skin (erythema marginatum, subcutaneous nodules)
↓
Acute Rheumatic Fever
Q18
8) What is ASO titer?
Ans: ASO (Anti-Streptolysin O) titer is the estimation of antibodies against Streptolysin O produced by S. pyogenes.
  • Normal value: < 200 Todd units/ml (varies by lab)
  • Significance: Elevated in most streptococcal infections except pyoderma and post-streptococcal glomerulonephritis
  • Diagnostic use: Important in diagnosis of Acute Rheumatic Fever
Q19
9) Mention the important properties of S. pneumoniae.
Ans:
  • Gram stain: Gram-positive diplococci, lancet-shaped
  • Capsule: Prominent polysaccharide capsule (antiphagocytic) - basis for Quellung reaction
  • Hemolysis: α-hemolysis on blood agar
  • Optochin: Sensitive
  • Bile solubility: Positive
  • Inulin fermentation: Positive
  • Catalase: Negative
  • Growth: Enhanced by 5-10% CO₂
  • Habitat: Upper respiratory tract commensal
Q20
10) Mention the predisposing factors of S. pneumoniae. *
Ans:
  • Depressed cough reflex: Alcoholism, drug intoxication, cerebral impairment
  • Abnormal mucociliary clearance: Viral infections, smoking, bronchial obstruction
  • Circulatory disorders: Pulmonary congestion, congestive cardiac failure
  • Splenectomy: Loss of splenic clearance of capsulated bacteria
  • Chronic diseases: Sickle cell anemia, nephrotic syndrome (spleen auto-infarcts in sickle cell)
  • Head trauma: CSF leakage through nose
  • Immunocompromised states

🔸 C. Neisseria Questions

Q22
2) What are the virulence factors of N. gonorrhoeae? ***
Ans:
  • Pili: Attachment to genital mucosa, antiphagocytic
  • Opa proteins (Protein II): Adhesion & invasion
  • Por protein (Protein I): Prevents phagolysosome fusion, binds C3b
  • Rmp (Reduction modifiable protein): Forms pores with Por protein
  • Lipooligosaccharide (LOS): Endotoxic activity
  • IgA protease: Cleaves secretory IgA
  • β-lactamase (Penicillinase): In PPNG strains (plasmid mediated)
  • Iron binding protein (Fbp)
Q23
3) Discuss the pathogenesis of gonorrhea. *
Ans:
Sexual contact / birth
↓
Attachment to columnar non-ciliated epithelium of urethra/cervix by Pili & Opa proteins
↓
IgA protease hydrolyzes secretory IgA
↓
Local cellular invasion
↓
Marked PMN response & phagocytosis
↓
Por protein prevents phagolysosome fusion → survival inside neutrophils
↓
Inflammation extends to submucosa
↓
Gonorrhea (urethritis/cervicitis)
Q24
4) State the lab dx of gonorrhea/gonococcal urethritis or prostatitis. *****
Ans:

1. Specimen Collection

  • Male (acute): Urethral discharge
  • Male (chronic): Prostatic smear after prostatic massage
  • Female: Endocervical swab (High vaginal swab not ideal)
  • Newborn: Conjunctival swab

2. Microscopy

  • Gram stain: Gram-negative diplococci, kidney-shaped, intracellular within polymorphs (highly diagnostic)

3. Culture

  • Media: Thayer-Martin media or New York City media (modified chocolate agar with antibiotics)
  • Conditions: 5-10% CO₂ (Candle jar), 37°C
  • Transport: Stuart's or Amies transport medium

4. Biochemical Tests

  • Oxidase test: Positive
  • Sugar fermentation: Glucose fermented, Maltose NOT fermented

5. Confirmatory

  • PCR: Rapid, sensitive & specific (confirmatory)
Q25
6) Discuss the complication of gonorrhea ***
Ans:

In Males

  • Epididymitis
  • Prostatitis
  • Urethral stricture (from fibrosis)
  • Proctitis (anal intercourse)

In Females

  • Salpingitis
  • Pelvic Inflammatory Disease (PID)
  • Infertility (20% of salpingitis cases)
  • Ectopic pregnancy
  • Bartholinitis

Others

  • Ophthalmia neonatorum: In newborns
  • Disseminated Gonococcal Infection (DGI): Arthritis, tenosynovitis, dermatitis, pustular skin lesions, septicemia (rare)
  • Pharyngitis, Proctitis depending on route
DGI more common in individuals with deficiency of late complement components (C6-C9)
Q26
7) "Gonorrhoea is a recurrent disease": Is it true or false? - Explain. *
Ans: TRUE
  • N. gonorrhoeae is a facultative intracellular organism → survives inside neutrophils
  • Treatment of intracellular organisms is difficult
  • Females are often asymptomatic carriers → reservoir of infection
  • Antigenic variation of pili & Opa proteins → reinfection possible
  • No effective vaccine available
Q27
10) What are the virulence factors of N. meningitidis? **
Ans:
  • Polysaccharide capsule: Antiphagocytic (serogroups A,B,C,Y,W135)
  • Pili: Attachment to nasopharyngeal mucosa
  • IgA protease: Cleaves secretory IgA
  • Lipooligosaccharide (LOS): Endotoxic → causes septic shock, DIC, hemorrhage
  • Factor H Binding Protein (FHBP): Binds factor H → inhibits complement → reduces opsonization & MAC formation
  • Outer membrane proteins (Por A, Por B)
Q28
11) State the pathogenesis of N. meningitidis. ***
Ans:
Airborne droplet transmission
↓
Colonization of nasopharynx (often asymptomatic)
↓
Pili mediate attachment, IgA protease breaks down mucosal IgA, Capsule prevents phagocytosis
↓
In susceptible individuals → invasion of mucosa
↓
Entry into bloodstream → Meningococcemia
↓
Crosses blood-brain barrier → reaches meninges
↓
Meningitis (with or without meningococcemia)
High risk in C6-C9 complement deficiency
Q29
12) State the lab diagnosis of N. meningitidis. **
Ans:

Specimen:

CSF (most important), blood, nasopharyngeal swab

1. Microscopy

  • Gram stain of CSF: Gram-negative diplococci, lens/plano-convex shaped, intracellular & extracellular

2. Culture

  • Media: Chocolate agar (preferred for CSF as sterile) or Blood agar
  • Conditions: 5-10% CO₂, 37°C
  • Note: Thayer-Martin not necessary for CSF (no normal flora)

3. Biochemical

  • Oxidase: Positive
  • Sugar fermentation: Glucose & Maltose fermented

4. Rapid tests

  • Latex agglutination: Detects capsular antigen in CSF
  • Capsular swelling (Quellung) test

5. Confirmatory

  • PCR

📚 2. Introduction & Classification of Cocci

Cocci are spherical bacteria. Classification is best done by Gram stain followed by catalase test, then hemolysis and further tests.

General Classification of Cocci

Gram Stain → Gram Positive Cocci (Violet) vs Gram Negative Cocci (Red)

Gram +ve Cocci → Catalase Test
• Catalase +ve → Staphylococcus (clusters)
• Catalase -ve → Streptococcus/Enterococcus (chains/pairs)

Gram -ve Cocci → Neisseria, Moraxella, Veillonella (diplococci)

Differences Between Staphylococcus & Streptococcus

PointsStaphylococcusStreptococcus
Shape & ArrangementRound cocci, grape-like clustersRound cocci, chains or pairs
CatalasePositiveNegative
CoagulasePresent in S. aureusAbsent
HemolysisMostly β-hemolyticα, β or γ
ColonyLarge, opaqueSmall, translucent (pin-point)
PusThick, creamyThin, serous

🧫 3. Staphylococcus

General: Greek - Staphyle (bunch of grapes), Kokkos (berry). Multiply in different planes → cluster formation.

Classification

A) Based on Coagulase Production

  • Coagulase positive: S. aureus
  • Coagulase negative: S. epidermidis, S. saprophyticus & others

B) Based on Pigment Production

  • S. aureus: Golden yellow (staphyloxanthin)
  • S. albus: White
  • S. citrus: Lemon yellow/greenish

Disease Spectrum by Species

SpeciesDiseases
S. aureusBoil, carbuncle, impetigo, folliculitis, wound infection, food poisoning, TSS, SSS, pneumonia, osteomyelitis, endocarditis, septicemia
S. epidermidisProsthetic device infections (heart valves, joints), IV catheter infections, CSF shunt infections, endocarditis
S. saprophyticusUTI in sexually active young women (honeymoon cystitis) - 2nd common after E. coli

Laboratory Diagnosis of S. aureus

  1. Specimen: Pus/swab from lesion (collect from base/margin, not center)
  2. Microscopy: Gram-positive cocci in clusters
  3. Culture:
    • Nutrient agar: Golden yellow colonies
    • Blood agar: β-hemolysis
    • Mannitol Salt Agar: Selective & differential
  4. Biochemical: Catalase +, Coagulase +, Mannitol fermenter
  5. Molecular: PCR for MRSA/VISA/VRSA detection

🔗 4. Streptococcus

General: Streptos = twisted/chain. Multiply in single plane → chain formation.

Important Streptococcal Species

  • S. pyogenes - Group A β-hemolytic
  • S. agalactiae - Group B β-hemolytic
  • S. pneumoniae - α-hemolytic, diplococci
  • Viridans group - α-hemolytic
  • Enterococcus (E. faecalis, E. faecium) - Group D
  • S. bovis/gallolyticus - Group D

Virulence Factors of S. pyogenes

A. Cell Wall

  • M-protein: Anti-phagocytic (main)
  • C-carbohydrate: Group specificity
  • Hyaluronic acid capsule: Anti-phagocytic
  • Lipoteichoic acid: Adhesion

B. Toxins

  • Streptolysin O: Oxygen-labile, antigenic (ASO)
  • Streptolysin S: Oxygen-stable, hemolytic
  • Erythrogenic toxin: Scarlet fever
  • Exotoxin B: Necrotizing fasciitis

C. Enzymes

  • Streptokinase: Fibrinolytic (used therapeutically)
  • DNase (Streptodornase): Liquefies pus (stronger than staph)
  • Hyaluronidase: Spreading factor
  • C5a peptidase: Inhibits chemotaxis

Laboratory Diagnosis of S. pyogenes

  1. Specimen: Throat swab (pharyngitis) or wound swab (skin infection)
  2. Microscopy: Gram-positive cocci in chains
  3. Culture:
    • Blood agar: Pin-point colonies with wide clear zone of β-hemolysis
    • Crystal violet blood agar: Selective
  4. Biochemical: Catalase (-), Bacitracin sensitive
  5. Serology: ASO titer (↑ in ARF), Anti-DNAse B (↑ in pyoderma & AGN)

Differences: ARF vs Post-Streptococcal GN

FeatureAcute Rheumatic FeverPost-Streptococcal GN
Preceding infectionPharyngitis onlyPharyngitis or Pyoderma
Latent period2 weeks1-3 weeks
StrainsRheumatogenicNephritogenic
HypersensitivityType II (molecular mimicry)Type III (immune complex)
ASOMarkedly raisedMay be normal or less raised

🧬 5. Neisseria (Gram-Negative Cocci)

Gram-negative diplococci. Aerobic, oxidase positive, fastidious.

Comparison: N. gonorrhoeae vs N. meningitidis

FeatureN. gonorrhoeae (Gonococcus)N. meningitidis (Meningococcus)
HabitatGenital tractNasopharynx (normal flora)
CapsuleAbsentPresent
ShapeKidney-shaped diplococciLens/plano-convex diplococci
Maltose fermentationNegativePositive
Glucose fermentationPositivePositive
PlasmidUsually presentRare
β-lactamaseCommon (PPNG)Rare
LocationPredominantly intracellularIntra & extracellular
VaccineNot availableAvailable (Conjugate/Polysaccharide)
DiseaseGonorrhea, Ophthalmia neonatorum, PIDMeningitis, Meningococcemia

Culture Media for Neisseria

  • Chocolate agar: Basic enriched media. Heat-lysed blood inactivates inhibitors.
  • Thayer-Martin media: Modified chocolate agar + Vancomycin, Colistin, Nystatin, Trimethoprim. Selective for Neisseria
  • New York City media: Another selective medium
For CSF in meningitis: Use Chocolate agar (not Thayer-Martin) as CSF is sterile.

📊 Quick Revision Summary

Must Know Catalase/Coagulase

  • Staph: Catalase +, Coagulase + (S. aureus)
  • Strep/Entero: Catalase -
  • Neisseria: Oxidase +, Catalase +

Key Drug Choices

  • MRSA: Vancomycin
  • VRSA/VISA: Daptomycin/Quinupristin-Dalfopristin
  • S. pneumoniae meningitis: Ceftriaxone (+ Vancomycin)
For Meningitis Age-wise: "ELHS" - E.coli, Listeria (neonates), H.influenzae, S.pneumoniae + N.meningitidis in older
Neisseria sugar fermentation: "Gonorrhea ferments Glucose only. Meningitis ferments Glucose + Maltose"
