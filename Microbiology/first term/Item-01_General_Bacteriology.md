Limitations of Koch’s postulates:

1. Some diseases are not associated with microorganism. E.g., Genetic, autoimmune, non-
communicable disease as like DM, HTN. Some pathogens isolated from patients who are
asymptomatic carner (such as Sa/monella typhi)

2. Some organism cannot be cultured in artificial media. E.g., 7reponema pallidum,
Mycobacterium leprae; Prions.

3. Some organism cannot produce disease in all experimental animal (all host). E.g., Neisseria
gonorrhoeae, M. tuberculosis.

4. Some diseases have mixed organism (more than one).

In a patient with a sore throat, the presence of a few B-hemolytic streptococci is
insufficient for a microbiological diagnosis, whereas the presence of many would be

sufficient.

In a patient with fever, a-hemolytic streptococci in the throat are considered part of the
normal flora, whereas the same organisms in the blood are likely to be the cause of
bacterial endocarditis.

Overcome of limitation of Koch’s postulates:
1. Serological and molecular methods of diagnosis of disease.

2. Molecular Koch’s postulate.

Molecular Koch’s Postulate:
a. The manifestation of diseased (Phenotypic expression) should be associated with
pathogenic strain of a species (Gene)
b. Inactivation of the suspected gene(s) result in a measurable loss of pathogenicity
(manifestations) yy
Reversion of the inactive gene should restore the disease phenotype.

Koch’s phenomenon: | 4
Koch observed that a guinea pig already infected with the tubercle bacillus responded with an

exaggerated expression (hypersensitivity reaction) when injected with the tubercle bacillus or its
protein. This hypersensitivity reaction is known as Koch’s phenomenon.
