P1/A/01

Give four (04) character of prokaryotic cells. Mention six (06) differences between exotoxin and endotoxin. 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Four characters of prokaryotic cells (1.5 marks)

  • Primitive nucleus — prokaryotic cells possess a nucleoid (primitive nucleus), lacking a true nucleus, nuclear membrane, nucleoli and mitotic apparatus.
  • No membrane-bound organelles — mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus and lysosomes are absent.
  • Ribosome of 70S type (50S + 30S) — differs from eukaryotic 80S ribosome.
  • Cell wall contains peptidoglycan; cytoplasmic membrane lacks sterol (except Mycoplasma); chromosome is a single circular DNA (no introns).

Part 2 — Six differences between exotoxin and endotoxin (2 marks)

#FeatureExotoxinEndotoxin
1NatureExotoxin: toxic proteinEndotoxin: Lipid A of LPS (lipopolysaccharide)
2Source organismMainly Gram-positive bacteriaGram-negative bacteria
3ReleaseSecreted during growth into surrounding mediumReleased on bacterial cell lysis
4ToxicityHighly toxic — small amounts effectiveLess toxic — large amounts required
5AntigenicityHighly antigenic; toxoid vaccine can be preparedWeakly antigenic; no toxoid vaccine
6Heat stabilityLabile — destroyed at 60–80°CStable — resists boiling

Additional differentiating points: Exotoxin is heat-labile, highly antigenic and produces no fever; endotoxin is heat-stable, weakly antigenic and is pyrogenic (causes fever via IL-1/TNF-α stimulation of hypothalamus).

P1/A/02

Define culture and culture media. Why is bacterial spore a highly resistant structure? 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Culture and culture media (1.5 marks)

  • Culture: Process of isolation and multiplication of a specific microorganism on a suitable culture medium under controlled conditions.
  • Culture media: Nutrient media (blood agar, nutrient broth), selective media (MacConkey, SS agar, E. coli media), differential media (blood agar), anaerobic media (anaerobic agar).

Part 2 — Why is bacterial spore a highly resistant structure? (2 marks)

  • Spore is a multi-layered structure: spore core (rich in calcium dipicolinate) → spore wall → spore cortex → spore coat (with keratin-like protein).
  • Low water content and very low metabolic/enzymatic activity make it resistant.
  • Resistant to heat, chemicals, disinfectants and drying; spores can survive many years in soil.
P1/A/03

How will you differentiate Staphylococcus from Streptococcus from a skin lesion? Give the importance of ASO titer. What is MRSA? 2 + 1 + 0.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Differentiation of Staphylococcus from Streptococcus (skin lesion) (2 marks)

  • Staphylococcus: Gram-positive cocci arranged in grape-like clusters; catalase positive; skin lesions (boil, impetigo, wound infection) — pus is golden yellow, thick and creamy, lesion is localized (coagulase forms clot).
  • Streptococcus: Gram-positive cocci arranged in chain or pair; catalase negative; skin lesions (cellulitis, erysipelas, impetigo, necrotizing fasciitis) — pus is whitish, thin and watery, lesion is spreading (hyaluronidase/spreading factors).
  • Confirmation by culture: blood agar; crystal violet blood agar selective for S. pyogenes.

Part 2 — Importance of ASO titer (1 mark)

ASO (Anti-streptolysin O) titer — antibody against streptolysin O (pyrogenic exotoxin of S. pyogenes). Elevated (>200 IU/mL) in most streptococcal infections. Important in diagnosis of rheumatic fever (1–4 weeks after streptococcal pharyngitis/skin infection; type II hypersensitivity cross-reaction with heart/joint antigens).

Part 3 — What is MRSA? (0.5 marks)

MRSA = Methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus; resistant to all β-lactam drugs (penicillin, cephalosporins) via mecA gene encoding altered penicillin-binding protein (PBP2a); multidrug resistant; vancomycin is drug of choice.

P1/A/04

A man of 25 years has complaints of urethritis accompanied by dysuria and purulent discharge. What will be the probable diagnosis? Mention the methods of diagnosis. 1 + 2.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Probable diagnosis (1 mark)

Probable diagnosis: Gonococcal urethritis (Neisseria gonorrhoeae) — sexually transmitted infection presenting with urethritis, dysuria and purulent discharge in a 25-year-old.

Part 2 — Methods of diagnosis (2.5 marks)

  • Urethral smear — Gram stain: gram-negative diplococci (gonococcus, kidney-shaped).
  • Culture — Thayer-Martin or GC agar; N. meningitidis requires chocolate agar, N. gonorrhoeae requires Thayer-Martin medium.
  • Molecular — NAAT/PCR from urine or swab.
P1/A/05

Describe bacterial growth with respect to- growth requirements, growth curve with its importance, importance of generation time & whether O2 is essential for all bacteria or not. 2+2.5+1+1.5

Marks: 7
✅ Answer

1. Bacterial growth requirements (2 marks)

  • Nutritional: essential elements (C, N, S, P), vitamins, accessory factors (V factor NAD, X factor heme), trace elements.
  • Environmental: optimum temperature (35–37°C), pH (7.2–7.4), oxygen, CO₂, moisture, osmotic pressure.

2. Growth curve & its importance (2.5 marks)

  • Phases: Lag (adaptation, no multiplication) → Log/exponential (rapid multiplication) → Stationary (multiplication = death) → Decline (death > multiplication).
  • Acute phase of disease occurs in log phase; cell-wall-acting antibiotics are most effective here; release of exotoxin/endotoxin and spore formation occur in later phases.

3. Importance of generation time (1 mark)

  • Doubling time: E. coli 18–20 min; M. tuberculosis 18–24 h; M. leprae 18–30 days. Slow generation time → prolonged treatment (TB, leprosy).

4. Is O₂ essential for all bacteria? (1.5 marks)

  • No — O₂ is not essential for all bacteria.
  • Obligate aerobes require O₂; obligate anaerobes are killed by O₂ (lack SOD/catalase → toxic H₂O₂/O₂⁻).
  • Facultative and aerotolerant anaerobes grow without O₂ (e.g., Lactobacillus, streptococci).

P1/B/01

Enumerate gram positive rods. "Bacteria are sometimes beneficial to us" - Explain with Lactobacillus. 2 + 1.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Gram positive rods (2 marks)

  • Bacillus species (aerobic, spore-forming): B. anthracis, B. cereus, B. subtilis, B. stearothermophilus.
  • Clostridium species (anaerobic, spore-forming): C. tetani, C. botulinum, C. perfringens, C. difficile.
  • Corynebacterium (C. diphtheriae), Listeria monocytogenes, Actinomyces, Nocardia, Lactobacillus spp. (anaerobic).

Part 2 — Beneficial bacteria: Lactobacillus (1.5 marks)

Lactobacillus (normal flora of gut/vagina) produces lactic acid → maintains acidic pH → prevents overgrowth of pathogenic bacteria; aids digestion; gut flora synthesizes vitamins (B, K) and provides colonisation resistance — hence "Bacteria are sometimes beneficial to us".

P1/B/02

Define sterilization and disinfection. Write down the origin of drug resistance. 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Sterilization vs Disinfection (1.5 marks)

  • Sterilization: killing/removing of all microorganisms including spores.
  • Disinfection: killing/removing many microorganisms to a level not likely to cause infection; some pathogens/spores may survive (sporostatic).

Part 2 — Origin of drug resistance (2 marks)

  • Spontaneous mutations in bacterial genes.
  • Acquisition of resistance genes via plasmids (e.g., β-lactamase), transposons (jumping genes, e.g., mecA cassette), phage transduction, integrons.
  • Source: gut normal flora / environmental bacteria.
P1/B/03

Give five (05) peculiar properties of Proteus. How will you diagnose enteric fever? 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Five peculiar properties of Proteus (1.5 marks)

  • Urease positive.
  • Produces phenylalanine deaminase.
  • Highly motile (peritrichous flagella).
  • Swarming growth on blood agar (expanding rings) due to high motility.
  • Responsible for fishy odour (ammonia) and UTI with renal stones (struvite).

Part 2 — Laboratory diagnosis of enteric fever (2 marks)

  • Blood culture — gold standard (1st week).
  • Stool culture — (3rd–4th week); MacConkey/SS agar.
  • Widal test — 2nd week; 4-fold rising titer significant.
  • PCR — confirmatory.
P1/B/04

Give the pathogenesis of gas gangrene. "Can all species of Corynebacterium can cause diphtheria?" - Justify your answer. 2 + 1.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Pathogenesis of gas gangrene (2 marks)

Clostridium spp. (e.g., C. perfringens, C. septicum, C. histolyticum) spores enter via contaminated wound → anaerobic germination → exotoxin (lecithinase/α-toxin) → myonecrosis with gas production (C₂H₂) in tissues.

Part 2 — Can all species of Corynebacterium cause diphtheria? (1.5 marks)

No. Only Corynebacterium diphtheriae (toxigenic strains; toxin via lysogenic conversion) causes diphtheria. Other Corynebacterium species cause different infections (e.g., C. pseudotuberculosis → lymphadenitis).

P1/B/05

Discuss the following topics on Mycobacterium: Classification of Mycobacterium; virulence factors of M. tuberculosis; laboratory diagnosis of pulmonary TB and abbreviate MDR-TB. 2+2+2.5+0.5

Marks: 7
✅ Answer

1. Classification of Mycobacterium (2 marks)

  • M. tuberculosis complex: M. tuberculosis, M. bovis, M. leprae, M. africanum.
  • Atypical (NTM/MOTT): M. avium, M. ulcerans, M. kansasii, etc.

2. Virulence factors of M. tuberculosis (2 marks)

  • High mycolic acid content (60–71%) in cell wall → acid-fast, resistant to lysozyme.
  • Cord factor (trehalose-6-phosphate), arabinogalactan, PE/PPE proteins, ESAT-6, CFP-10, MPB70/71.
  • Anti-phagocytic α-crystalline protein; granulomatous inflammation.

3. Laboratory diagnosis of pulmonary TB (2.5 marks)

  • Sputum smear (Ziehl–Neelsen / fluorescent auramine-rhodamine), culture (Lowenstein-Jensen, BACTEC), GeneXpert PCR (rpoB), Mantoux test.

4. Abbreviate MDR-TB (0.5 marks)

MDR-TB = multidrug-resistant tuberculosis — resistance to isoniazid (INH) + rifampicin (RIF) (most effective first-line drugs).

P2/A/01

What are the symbiotic relationship between two organisms? Define intermediate host and mention three (03) examples when man is intermediate host. 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Symbiotic relationship (1.5 marks)

  • Symbiosis: close relationship between two species where at least one is benefited.
  • Types: Mutualism (both benefit), Commensalism (one benefits, other unaffected), Parasitism (parasite benefits, host harmed).

Part 2 — Intermediate host & man as intermediate host (2 marks)

Intermediate host: host in whose body the parasite undergoes larval/a sexual development before reaching the definitive host.

Examples where man is the intermediate host: Plasmodium, Toxoplasma gondii, Echinococcus granulosus; also Taenia solium (cysticercosis in man).

P2/A/02

Enlist extra-intestinal lesion of Entamoeba histolytica. State the specimens and microscopic findings of amoebic liver abscess. 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Extra-intestinal lesions of E. histolytica (1.5 marks)

  • Liver — amoebic liver abscess (commonest).
  • Lung — amoebic lung abscess.
  • Brain — amoebic brain abscess.
  • Spleen — amoebic splenic abscess.
  • Skin — granulomatous ulceration (amoebiasis cutis).

Part 2 — Amoebic liver abscess: specimens & microscopy (2 marks)

  • Specimen: USG-guided pus aspiration (also stool & blood).
  • Microscopy: anchovy-sauce (chocolate) pus; three zones — central necrotic zone (no trophozoite), intermediate zone (degenerated hepatocytes + trophozoites), peripheral zone (healthy hepatocytes + trophozoites).
  • Stool: cysts in ~15% cases.
P2/A/03

Mention four (04) cestodes with mode of transmission and their host. Give four causes of autoinfection. 2 + 1.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Four cestodes (2 marks)

  • Taenia saginata — beef tapeworm; definitive host man, intermediate cattle; undercooked beef → taeniasis.
  • Taenia solium — pork tapeworm; definitive host man/pigs; undercooked pork → intestinal taeniasis; eggs → cysticercosis.
  • Echinococcus granulosus — dog tapeworm; definitive dog, intermediate sheep (man = dead-end); dog faeces → hydatid cyst.
  • Diphyllobothrium latum — fish tapeworm; definitive man, intermediate copepod & fish; undercooked fish → diphyllobothriasis.

Part 2 — Four causes of autoinfection (1.5 marks)

  • Internal autoinfection by Taenia solium (retrograde peristalsis → eggs hatch → oncosphere).
  • External autoinfection by Enterobius vermicularis (scratching perianal area → hand-to-mouth).
  • Autoinfection by Strongyloides stercoralis (larvae reinfect intestine).
  • Autoinfection via fecal–oral route / blood transfusion routes as applicable.
P2/A/04

A student takes food with contaminated hands after examining a fresh stool specimen. The hands are contaminated with eggs of either Ascaris lumbricoides or Enterobius vermicularis. Will he develop ascariasis or enterobiasis? Explain. 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Answer (3.5 marks)

Enterobiasis would develop if the hands carried Enterobius vermicularis eggs (they are laid in the perianal region, not in stool).

Ascarisis would develop if the hands carried Ascaris lumbricoides eggs (laid in stool).

P2/A/05

Describe Leishmania with respect to its (new world and old world species, Vector of Kala-azar, Complications of Kala-azar, laboratory diagnosis of PKDL). 2+1+2+2

Marks: 7
✅ Answer

1. Old World vs New World Leishmania (2 marks)

  • Old World: L. donovani, L. infantum, L. tropica, L. major, L. aethiopica; transmitted by Phlebotomus sandfly.
  • New World: L. chagasi, L. mexicana, L. braziliensis; transmitted by Lutzomyia sandfly.

2. Vector of Kala-azar (1 mark)

Female sandfly Phlebotomus argentipes (biological vector; promastigote → amastigote).

3. Complications of Kala-azar (2 marks)

  • Pancytopenia (anaemia, leucopenia, thrombocytopenia) → secondary infections, bleeding.
  • Post-Kala-azar Dermal Leishmaniasis (PKDL).

4. Laboratory diagnosis of PKDL (2 marks)

  • History of Kala-azar + clinical features (macule/patch/nodule).
  • Samples: slit skin smear (SSS), skin biopsy, blood; screening rK39 ICT; microscopy (LD bodies); PCR confirmatory.

P2/B/01

Mention six (06) features of trematodes. Mention the intestinal, lungs, liver & blood flukes. 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Six features of trematodes (1.5 marks)

  • Leaf-like, flat, unsegmented; size 1 mm – several cm.
  • Head with two suckers (oral + ventral), no hooks.
  • Monoecious/hermaphrodite (except Schistosoma diecious).
  • Incomplete alimentary canal (no anus).
  • No body cavity.
  • Body covered by tegument.

Part 2 — Flukes by site (2 marks)

  • Blood flukes: S. haematobium, S. japonicum, S. mansoni.
  • Lung fluke: Paragonimus westermani.
  • Intestinal flukes: Fasciolopsis buski, Gastrodiscoides hominis.
  • Liver flukes: Fasciola hepatica, Clonorchis sinensis.
P2/B/02

Give four (04) characteristics of T. vaginalis. Why helminthic diseases are now uncommon in Bangladesh? 2 + 1.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Four characteristics of T. vaginalis (2 marks)

  • Flagellate protozoan (luminal flagellate) of genital tract.
  • Causes trichomoniasis (frothy discharge, strawberry cervix).
  • Only trophozoite form (no cyst stage).
  • Highly motile — jerky/twitchy motility in wet mount.

Part 2 — Helminthic diseases now uncommon in Bangladesh (1.5 marks)

  • Mass deworming programmes (school children, albendazole).
  • Improved sanitation / WASH — safe drinking water, reduced open defecation.
P2/B/03

How D. latum causes anaemia? Mention the peculiarities in life cycle of S. stercoralis. 2 + 1.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — How D. latum causes anaemia (2 marks)

Diphyllobothrium latum releases unsaturated fatty acids interfering with intrinsic factor (Castle) → B12 absorption blocked; also competes for B12 → macrocytic (megaloblastic) anaemia.

Part 2 — Peculiarities in life cycle of Strongyloides stercoralis (1.5 marks)

  • Two distinct life cycles — parasitic in human, free-living in soil.
  • Ovoviviparous + parthenogenesis.
  • Hyperinfection (reinfection/autoinfection in immunocompromised).
P2/B/04

A 25-year-old man from the Chittagong Hill Tracts presents with fever and chills for 3 days. What is your diagnosis? How will you differentiate the two common species in the laboratory? 1 + 2.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Diagnosis (1 mark)

Most likely malaria (endemic Chittagong Hill Tracts area; fever + chills).

Part 2 — Differentiate two common species (2.5 marks)

  • Plasmodium vivax vs Plasmodium falciparum in peripheral blood.
  • P. falciparum: band forms/gamatest; P. vivax: typical trophozoites in thin smear.
  • Diagnosis: peripheral blood smear (Giemsa), PCR, antigen (NS1/IgM/IgG).
P2/B/05

Write down from filaria (Causative organism & infective form; Classical Vs. Occult filariasis; Lab dx; Provocation test; conditions when microfilariae not found in blood). 1+2+2+1+1

Marks: 7
✅ Answer

1. Causative organism & infective form (1 mark)

Wuchereria bancrofti (lymphatic filaria); infective form = 3rd stage larva (L3) via Culex quinquefasciatus bite.

2. Classical vs Occult filariasis (2 marks)

  • Classical: adult worms in lymphatics causing obstruction.
  • Occult: hypersensitivity to microfilarial antigens (microfilariae destroyed in tissues).

3. Laboratory diagnosis (2 marks)

  • Thick/thin blood films with Giemsa stain; urine (chyluria); provocation test.

4. Provocation test (1 mark)

DEC provocation test: DEC induces microfilariae to appear in peripheral blood even during daytime.

5. Conditions when microfilariae are not found in blood (1 mark)

  • Occult filariasis; elephantiasis (chronic); nocturnal periodicity (blood collection time).

P2/C/02

What are the cytopathic effect of virus. Give three conditions of viral persistence. 2 + 1.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Cytopathic effects (2 marks)

Changes in virus-infected host cell: rounding & darkening of cell, cell death/lysis, malignant transformation, inclusion bodies, multinucleated giant cells, syncytia.

Part 2 — Three conditions of viral persistence (1.5 marks)

  • Latent infection (e.g., herpesviruses).
  • Chronic infection (e.g., HIV, HBV).
  • Slow viral infection (HIV, prions, HCV).
P2/C/03

What do you mean by early and late protein? Mention the site of replication of DNA and RNA viruses including the exceptions. 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Early vs Late protein (1.5 marks)

  • Early protein: enzymes needed for replication (before replication) — polymerase, protease.
  • Late protein: structural proteins after replication — capsid, envelope.

Replication sites of DNA & RNA viruses (2 marks)

  • DNA viruses: replicate in nucleus (except Poxvirus → cytoplasm).
  • RNA viruses: replicate in cytoplasm (except Orthomyxo/Retro/Delta → nucleus).

P2/C/05

Describe the Candidia albicans: four (04) important properties; lesions produced by it; predisposing factors; laboratory diagnosis. 1.5+1.5+2+2

Marks: 7
✅ Answer

1. Four important properties of Candida albicans (1.5 marks)

  • Also called "white fungus".
  • Yeast-like fungus — oval yeast with single bud; in tissues yeast/pseudohyphae.
  • Only fungus found as normal flora in human body.
  • Only fungus that takes Gram stain — Gram positive; germ tube positive.

2. Lesions produced (1.5 marks)

Oral thrush, vulvovaginal candidiasis (moniliasis), esophageal candidiasis, onychomycosis, diaper rash, systemic candidiasis (candidemia, meningitis).

Antibiotics, steroids, immunosuppression (AIDS), diabetes, pregnancy, old age, trauma, catheterization, neutropenia.

4. Laboratory diagnosis (2 marks)

Wet mount, Gram stain, culture on SDA + blood agar, germ tube test (C. albicans specific), carbohydrate fermentation.

Mention the mode of transmission of hepatitis virus. Give the post exposure prophylaxis of Rabies. 1.5 + 2

P2/D/02

Is long term protection from influenza vaccine possible?- Please explain. What do you mean by HIV infection and AIDS including abbreviation? 1.5 + 2

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Influenza vaccine & long-term protection (1.5 marks)

  • Influenza virus undergoes antigenic drift (point mutations) → annual vaccine update required.
  • Antigenic shift (reassortment) → pandemics.
  • Long-term protection from a single vaccine dose is not possible; annual vaccination is needed.

Part 2 — HIV infection vs AIDS (2 marks)

  • HIV = Human Immunodeficiency Virus (Retroviridae, lentivirus, ssRNA+).
  • AIDS = Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome — advanced HIV stage (CD4 <200/µL or AIDS-defining illness).
P2/D/03

Mention four (04) vector & four (04) host of DNA cloning. Which one is better between PCR & qPCR?- explain. 2+1.5

Marks: 3.5
✅ Answer

Part 1 — Four vectors & four hosts of DNA cloning (2 marks)

  • Vectors: plasmid, bacteriophage/cosmid, BAC (Bacterial Artificial Chromosome), YAC (Yeast Artificial Chromosome).
  • Hosts: E. coli, yeast (S. cerevisiae), mammalian cells (CHO/HEK), insect cells (Sf9).

Part 2 — PCR vs qPCR (1.5 marks)

qPCR (real-time PCR) is better than conventional PCR — it quantifies DNA in real time (fluorescence), needs no post-PCR gel analysis, has high sensitivity/specificity and detects low copy numbers.

P2/D/05

A 20 yrs. person presented with high fever, rashes and severe myalgia for 3 days. He has a travelling history of Dhaka 1 weeks before. What is your diagnosis? Give the pathogenesis, laboratory diagnosis, preventive measures of this conditions. 1+2.5+2+1.5

Diagnosis (1 mark)

Most likely Dengue fever — high fever, rash, severe myalgia, travel history to Dhaka.

Preventive measures (1.5 marks)
