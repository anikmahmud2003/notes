Hyphae:
If mold form long branching filamentous then it 19 called hyphae. Two types: septate and non-
septate. Non-septate hyphae are multinucleated (coenocytic). The growth of hyphae occurs by
extension of the tip of the hyphae, not by cell division all along the filament.
     *       Septate hyphae: usually pathogenic hyphae (Dermatophytes, systemic fungus )
     -       Non septate hyphae: Most saprophytic hyphae, Penicillium, Mucor.

Mycelium:
If mold or hyphae continuously grow and form a mat (tangled mass) then it is called mycelium.
(পেঁচানো সুতোর মত).

Dimorphic fungi:
Dimorphic fungi are the fungi which exist as molds (hyphae) in the environment at room
temperature (25°C) and yeast in human tissues at body temperature (37°C).

   1. Specimen/ Sample collection:
          a. According to site of infection

                After 30 minutes of treating with KOH (Potassium hydroxide), the fungi can be

        Useful in diagnosis of systemic mycosis but less so in other fungal infection.

                        b. Isolation and identification of organisms: by culture.

Why does fungus grow well in SDA media?
  1. High dextrose (glucose) content: excellent source of carbon and energy.
  2. Presence of peptone: source of nitrogen, vitamins and minerals.
  3. Acidic or low pH (pH 5.6): support the growth of fungus.
  4. Addition of antibiotic frequently (Penicillin, Streptomycin and Cycloheximide)

Why don’t most of the bacteria grow in SDA media?
  5. Acidic or Low pH (pH 5.6): most bacteria prefer pH 7.
  6. Addition of antibiotic frequently (Penicillin, Streptomycin and Cycloheximide)
  7. High dextrose content: raised osmotic pressure by high dextrose inhibit bacterial
               cells.
NB:
    8.         Penicillin: inhibit gram-positive bacteria.
    9.         Streptomycin: inhibit gram-negative bacteria
      10. Cycloheximide: inhibit saprophytic (non-pathogenic) fungus & some yeast.

                                 PCR: principle and procedure.
Principle of PCR:
PCR is the technique used to amplify a particular DNA fragments or segments, generating
millions of copies.
The process relies on thermal cycling (repeated heating and cooling) and the enzymatic action
of DNA polymerase.


Components of PCR:
   1.       Template DNA: the DNA sequence or fragment to be amplified.
   2.       Primers: short DNA sequences (single stranded) that bind to the target DNA.
   3.       DNA polymerase (Taq polymerase): a heat-stable enzyme that synthesizes new DNA
            strands.
   4.       Deoxynucleotide Triphosphate (dNTPs): building blocks (A, T, C, G) for DNA
            synthesis.
   5.    Buffer Solution: maintains optimum pH and ionic strength.
   6. Magnesium ions (৬162): Cofactor for DNA polymerase.

Steps of PCR (Cycle: approximately 45 cycle)
   1.    Denaturation: at 94-96°C.
         Double stranded DNA is heated to separate into two single strands.
   2.    Annealing: at 50-70°C.
            Primers (short DNA fragment) bind to complementary sequence on the target single
            stranded DNA.
   3.       Extension or elongation: at 72°C.
            DNA polymerase (Taq polymerase) extends the primers to form new DNA strands by
            adding nucleotides / dNTPs (A, T, C, G).
