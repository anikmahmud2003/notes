## P5: Mention one parasite where man acts as both intermediate and definitive host. **

> 📌 Hint:T. solium

Taenia solium (pork tapeworm)— man acts as both definitive and intermediate host. In man adult worm lives in small intestine (intestinal taeniasis, man = definitive host), but if man ingests theeggs(faecal contamination or autoinfection), the larva (cysticercus cellulosae) develops in tissues like brain, muscle and eye (cysticercosis) — here man behaves as the (accidental) intermediate host.

## P9: What do you mean by dead end intermediate host with example? *

> 📌 Hint:in hydatid cyst and cysticercosis caused by T. solium: man is dead end intermediate host: because rare access to eat human flesh

Dead-end intermediate host:a host in which the larva develops, but the parasite's life cyclecannot be completedbecause the definitive host hasno (or very rare) accessto eat that host's flesh (or the infected tissue never reaches the definitive host). The cycle simply stops there — it becomes a "dead end."
Examples:
- Man in Echinococcus granulosus infection (hydatid cyst):the cyst develops in man, but dogs cannot eat human flesh, so man is a dead-end intermediate host.
- Man in Taenia solium cysticercosis:cysticercus cellulosae develops in human tissues, but pigs/man-eating definitive sources rarely access human flesh, so the cycle ends.

## P10: What do you mean by accidental host with example? **

> 📌 Hint:E. granulosus: man accidental host

Accidental host:a host which isnot the natural hostof a parasite, but becomes infected occasionally (by chance). The parasite usually infects an unusual host and often cannot complete its normal life cycle there.
Example:man is the accidental host forEchinococcus granulosus(dog tapeworm). The natural cycle is dog → sheep; but man incidentally ingests the eggs (from dog feces) and develops hydatid cyst.
Other examples: free-living amoeba (Acanthamoeba/Naegleria) are facultative parasites that accidentally infect man and cause CNS disease.

## P12: What do you mean by symbiosis, commensalism, parasitism, mutualism? ****

> 📌 Hint:close relationship of two species where at least one is benefited

- Symbiosis:a close relationship between two species in whichat least one species is benefited. It has 3 basic types — mutualism, commensalism and parasitism.
- Mutualism:relationship in whichboth species are benefited(e.g., Lactobacilli in the human gut feeding on sugars and helping digestion).
- Commensalism:one species is benefited while theother is not affected(e.g., some normal flora of skin with human).
- Parasitism:one species (the parasite) is benefited while theother species (host) is harmed(e.g., all pathogenic organisms, E. histolytica with man).

## P20: What do you mean by autoinfection? ***

> 📌 Hint:transmission by ownself

Autoinfection:when the patient himself carries the organism (parasite) and isself-reinfected without requiring any external source.
Two types:
- External autoinfection:the parasite is transmitted to the same person bycontaminated hand(e.g., hand-to-mouth after scratching the perianal region in enterobiasis).
- Internal autoinfection:the parasite reaches the same person byreverse peristalsis(e.g., retrograde migration of larva in enterobiasis, hyperinfection in strongyloidiasis).

## P28: Define premunition. ***

> 📌 Hint:Plasmodium and Schistosoma

Premunition(concomitant immunity): a type of immunity in which there isrelative resistance to re-infection while the host still carries the existing parasites. It does not eliminate the present infection, but partially protects the patient against a fresh (re-)infection.
- If the immune system is suppressed, the infection flares up and symptoms reappear.
- With complete cure (elimination of the parasite), premunition disappears.
- Seen inmalaria(mostly falciparum) andschistosomiasis.

## P29: Define protozoa. Mention important criteria of protozoa. *****

> 📌 Hint:unicellular eukaryotic, morphologically & functionally complete single cell unit

Protozoa(Greek: protos = first, zoon = animal):unicellular eukaryoticorganisms,morphologically and functionally complete, which act as a single "cell-like unit". A single cell of protozoa performs all life functions.
Important criteria of protozoa:
- Unicellular eukaryote (kingdomProtista)
- Single cell performsall functions— reproduction, digestion, respiration, excretion, etc.
- Two morphological forms:trophozoite(motile, feeding, pathogenic) andcyst(non-motile, resistant, infective)
- Movement by pseudopodia / flagella / cilia / gliding
- Can reproduce both sexually and asexually

---

## P30: "Protozoa act as a single cell like unit"- explain. ****

> 📌 Hint:single cell performs all functions — reproduction, digestion, respiration, excretion

Protozoa is morphologically and functionally complete:a single cell of protozoa isnot just a part of an organism — it is the whole organism. Unlike metazoa where different cells perform different functions, in protozoa:
- Digestion— food vacuoles within the same cell
- Respiration— gaseous exchange across the same cell membrane
- Excretion— contractile vacuoles remove waste from the same cell
- Reproduction— the cell divides and reproduces itself
- Movement & sensation— pseudopodia/flagella/cilia of the same cell
So the single protozoan cell is "morphologically and functionally complete" — it acts as a single cell-like unit.

## P33: Can sporozoa move? (Yes. They have no organ of locomotion but can move. Their movement is called "gliding". Highest gliding motility: Toxoplasma gondii > Plasmodium > other sporozoa.)

> 📌 Hint:they have no organ of locomotion but can move by gliding motility; Toxoplasma gondii highest

Yes, sporozoa can move.Although they haveno organ of locomotion(no pseudopodia, flagella or cilia), they move bygliding motility— a smooth, gliding movement over the substrate (using actin-myosin motor of the apical complex).
Order of gliding motility:Toxoplasma gondii > Plasmodium spp. > other sporozoa.
The tachyzoite stage of Toxoplasma shows the highest gliding motility among the sporozoa.

## P40: What do you mean by encystation and excystation? ****

> 📌 Hint:encystation: trophozoite to cyst; excystation: cyst to trophozoite

Encystation:the process of transformation oftrophozoite → cyst. It occurs inside the lumen of the infected individual's intestine. In E. histolytica it occurs in thelarge intestine.
Excystation:the process of transformation ofcyst → trophozoite, occurring when the cyst enters a new host's alimentary canal. In E. histolytica, excystation occurs in theterminal ileum and caecum— a mature quadrinucleated cyst gives rise to8 amoebulae(by division).
Note: encystation and excystation may occur inone and the same host; a new host is needed only for continuation of the species.

## P46: Mention the pathogenesis of amoebiasis? * (Only for written)

> 📌 Hint:host + infective form + mode of transmission + life cycle sequence

Pathogenesis of amoebiasis (E. histolytica):
- Infective form:mature quadrinucleated cyst;Pathogenic form:trophozoite.
- Host:man (definitive host); no intermediate host.Transmission:feco-oral — ingestion of contaminated food/water with the cyst; housefly may act as mechanical vector.
- Site of lesion:large intestine (mucosa & submucosa).
- Cyst passes through the stomach (wall resistant to gastric juice) →excystationin terminal ileum and caecum → quadrinucleated amoeba forms 8 amoebulae → adhere to colonic mucus → multiply by binary fission.
- In90%, trophozoites produce no lesion — transform to cyst and pass in feces.
- In~10%, trophozoites release proteolytic enzymes (histolycin) — lyse tissue and produceflask-shaped ulcers→ blood + mucus in stool (amoebic dysentery).
- Some trophozoites enter deeper layers → pass throughportal veininto the liver →amoebic liver abscess(anchovy-sauce pus) and rarely other extra-intestinal sites.
- With healing of the lesion, encystation occurs and cysts pass in feces — cycle repeats.

## P68: Why there is anaemia in kala-azar? *********

## P70: How will you diagnose a case of visceral leishmaniasis or Kala-azar? ********

## P88: Why there is anaemia in malaria? *****

> 📌 Hint:mechanical lysis, autoimmune lysis, splenic removal of infected and uninfected RBCs

## P89: State the pathogenesis of cerebral malaria. ****

> 📌 Hint:PfEMP-1 of P. falciparum enhances knob formation, cytoadherence, resetting, clumping in microvasculature such as brain capillaries

Pathogenesis of cerebral malaria (P. falciparum):
1. P. falciparum expresses a specialized antigenPfEMP-1(P. falciparum erythrocyte membrane protein 1).
2. Knob formation:infected RBCs with high surface PfEMP-1 develop knob protuberances on the cell membrane.
3. Cytoadherence:parasitized RBCs with sticky knobs adhere to the endothelial surface; PfEMP-1 binds endothelial receptors (CD36, ICAM-1).
4. Rosetting:infected RBCs bind to uninfected RBCs (via CR1 receptors).
5. Clumping:parasitized RBCs aggregate in the microvasculature (e.g., brain capillaries).
6. Plugging & occlusion:microvessels are plugged →occlusion of cerebral microcirculation → cerebral anoxia → coma, paralysis.
Clinical features: hyperpyrexia, headache, coma, paralysis; death occurs in 15–20% of cerebral malaria cases (md).

## P100: Write down the characteristics of cestodes, trematodes and nematodes. *********

## P110: Write in short note on hydatid cyst. ********

> 📌 Hint:larval stage of E. granulosus; structure: pericyst, ectocyst, endocyst; hydatid fluid & sand

Thehydatid cystis the larval stage of E. granulosus. When the oncosphere (liberated from the ingested egg) settles in an organ and gets encysted by host tissue, it develops into a large fluid-filled bladder-like cyst. Greek 'hydatis' means 'a drop of water'.
Structures of hydatid cyst:
- Pericyst:outermost layer, host-derived; consists of fibrous tissue and blood vessels.
- Ectocyst:outer/middle cuticular layer, parasite-derived; tough elastic, glycan-rich acellular hyaline layer (~1 mm), resembles the white of a hard-boiled egg.
- Endocyst:inner germinal layer, parasite-derived; thin (22–25 µm) cellular layer that forms the ectocyst on the outer side and the brood capsules with scoleces (protoscolices) on the inner side, and secretes hydatid fluid.
Hydatid fluid & sand:
- Clear colourless fluid, low specific gravity (1.005–1.010), slightly acidic pH (~6.7). Contains salts, proteins; antigenic and toxic.
- Hydatid sand:granular deposit of brood capsules and protoscolices which break off and settle at the bottom.
Site:Liver (commonest, ~70%), then lungs (20–30%), spleen, kidney, brain, eyes, bones.

## P134: State the pathogenesis of anaemia caused by Hook worm. ******* (release anticoagulant, continuous oozing main)

> 📌 Hint:release anticoagulant, continuous oozing is main; chronic blood loss

Hookworm anaemia is amicrocytic hypochromic (iron-deficiency) anaemia(rarely dimorphic) caused bychronic blood lossfrom the small intestine. Mechanism:
1. Sucking of blood:the adult hookworm attaches to the intestinal mucosa and sucks blood as its food.
2. Release of anticoagulant:the worm's oesophageal glands release an anticoagulant (and anti-allergic inhibitor) which prevents clotting of blood at the attachment site, producingcontinuous oozing of blood(the main mechanism).
3. Migration:the worm changes its attachment site, leaving multiple bleeding points.
4. Nutritional loss:the worm also consumes iron and vitamin B12/folic acid from the host's diet.
Loss of iron → microcytic hypochromic anaemia (IDA); loss of vitamin B12/folic acid → macrocytic anaemia; loss of both → dimorphic anaemia.

## P149: State the pathogenesis of filariasis. **

> 📌 Hint:(none)

3. Wrong time of blood collection— due tonocturnal periodicity, microfilariae are only found at night (10 pm–4 am); collecting blood during daytime gives a negative result.
4. After treatment / in early prepatent period before microfilariae appear.

## P153: What is periodicity or nocturnal periodicity? *** (microfilariae found at 10pm to 4 am)

> 📌 Hint:microfilariae found at 10pm to 4 am

Periodicityis the time when most of the microfilariae are found in theperipheral blood.
Nocturnal periodicity= microfilariae appear in the peripheral blood in large numbersonly at night, between 10 pm and 4 am, and disappear during the day (they hide in deep capillaries of lungs, kidneys, heart, great vessels).
- Nocturnal periodicity — seen inWuchereria bancrofti,Brugia malayi(vector Culex bites at night).
- Diurnal periodicity (day time) —Loa loa.
- Sub-periodic — present throughout with slight afternoon increase.
- Non-periodic — any time (Mansonella, Onchocerca).

## P154: What do you mean by provocation test? ****** (DEC is given, that induces microfilariae to appear in peripheral blood even during day time)

> 📌 Hint:DEC is given, that induces microfilariae to appear in peripheral blood even during day time

Provocation test (DEC provocation test):a dose ofdiethylcarbamazine (DEC)is given to the patient, whichinduces the microfilariae to appear in the peripheral blood even during the daytime. Blood is then collected and examined for microfilariae.
This test is helpful in detecting filariasis when blood is collected at the wrong time (during the day) or when microfilariae are scanty.

## P158: Why freshly passed stool of worm infected patient cannot infect another host? ** (for molting of worm, soil is needed).

> 📌 Hint:for molting of worm, soil is needed

Freshly passed stool of a worm-infected patientcannot infect another hostbecause the eggs/larvae passed arenot infective at the time of passage.
- Most soil-transmitted helminths need a period ofdevelopment (embryonation/maturation & molting) in the soilbefore they become infective.
- The immature rhabditiform larva / unembryonated eggmust moult in the soil(warm, moist) to form theinfective filariform larva (L3)before it can penetrate the skin.
- Hence, direct person-to-person spread from fresh stool does not occur — soil is essential for maturation/molting.
