## M1: What is fungus? Mention the characteristics of fungus. Fungus vs. Bacteria. ********

> 📌 Hint:Eukaryotic, chitin cell wall, 80S ribosomes, exospore, etc.

Fungus:Fungi are chlorophyll-free eukaryotic organisms that may be unicellular or multicellular and reproduce by sexual and asexual spores. The term comes from Greekmykosormykesmeaning fungus/mushroom.Medical mycologyis the branch dealing with medically important fungi, andmycosisis any fungal infection.
Characteristics of Fungus:
- Cell type:Eukaryote with a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles (mitochondria, ER).
- Cell number:May be unicellular (yeast) or multicellular (mold).
- Cell wall:90% polysaccharide, 10% protein. Containschitin(polymer of N-acetyl glucosamine),chitosan(soluble derivative of chitin), andβ-glucan(polymer of D-glucose).
- Cytoplasmic membrane:Contains sterols — ergosterol and zymosterol.
- Ribosomes:80S (40S + 60S).
- Chlorophyll:Absent — they require an organic carbon source.
- Reproduction:By budding (asexual) or by sexual and asexual spores.
- Oxygen requirement:Mostly strict aerobes; some facultative anaerobes. No obligate anaerobes.
- Natural habitat:Mostly in the environment (soil, decaying organic matter).

## M2: Mention the composition of cell wall and cell membrane of fungi. ****

> 📌 Hint:Cell wall: chitin, D glucan; Cell membrane: ergosterol

Cell Wall of Fungi:
- Composed of90% polysaccharideand10% protein.
- Chitin:A polysaccharide composed of long chains ofN-acetyl glucosamine (NAG). Chitin is insoluble in most solvents and has no free amino groups.
- Chitosan:A soluble derivative of chitin that has free amino groups.
- β-D-Glucan:A polymer of D-glucose. Target of echinocandin antifungals.
Cell Membrane (Cytoplasmic Membrane) of Fungi:
- Containsergosterolandzymosterol(sterol components).
- Ergosterol is the key target of many antifungal drugs (amphotericin B, nystatin bind directly; azoles and terbinafine inhibit its synthesis).

## M6: What do you mean by hyphae, pseudohyphae and mycelium? ***

> 📌 Hint:Hyphae = filaments; pseudohyphae = chained buds; mycelium = tangled mass of hyphae

Hyphae:Long, branching filamentous structures formed by mold. Growth occurs byextension of the tip(not by cell division along the filament). Two types:septate(with cross walls) andnon-septate / coenocytic(multinucleated, no cross walls).
- Septate hyphae:Usually pathogenic — dermatophytes, systemic fungi.
- Non-septate hyphae:Mostly saprophytic —Mucor,Rhizopus.
Pseudohyphae:Formed in some yeasts (especiallyCandida) when buds remain attached to the mother cell, forming a chain of elongated cells. They are NOT true hyphae because they lack the continuous cytoplasmic flow seen in true hyphae.
Mycelium:When hyphae grow continuously and form atangled mass or matof growth, it is called mycelium. This is visible to the naked eye and gives mold colonies their characteristic cottony/powdery appearance.

## M10: Write down the methods of laboratory diagnosis of fungal infections/dermatophytoses/ring worm infections. ***********

> 📌 Hint:KOH prep, culture on SDA, staining, PCR, Wood's lamp, hair perforation test

1. Specimen Collection:
- Skin scraping (from margin of lesion and redness area)
- Nail clipping
- Hair plucking
- Swabs (oral, throat, vaginal) for Candida
- Blood (for serology in systemic mycosis)
2. Microscopical Examination:
- KOH wet mount preparation(most common): KOH 10–40% dissolves keratin → fungi visualized after 30 min. Findings: skin/nail — branching septate hyphae (± arthrospore); hair — ectothrix or endothrix.
- Staining:Calcofluor white (most common), Lactophenol cotton blue (for culture mount), Indian ink (forCryptococcuscapsule), Gram stain (only forCandida— shows gram-positive yeast), Methenamine silver, PAS, Giemsa, H&E.
3. Culture / Isolation and Identification:
- SDA (Sabouraud's Dextrose Agar)— most common media.
- Mold: incubate at25°C for 2–4 weeks. Yeast: at37°C for 2–4 weeks.
- Naked eye: colony color (white, brown, greenish-yellow), texture (powdery, cottony, velvety, granular).
- Microscopy of culture: stain with lactophenol cotton blue → macroconidia & microconidia morphology.
- Other media: SDA + chloramphenicol & cycloheximide, dermatophyte test agar, potato dextrose agar, corn meal agar, mycobiotic agar.
4. Immunological Tests:Antigen detection & antibody detection (mainly for systemic mycosis; not usually for dermatophytosis).
5. Molecular Detection:PCR.
6. Other Tests:
- Wood's lamp:Microsporumspecies show fluorescence under UV — used for tinea capitis.
- Hair perforation test:ForMicrosporum&Trichophyton.
- Skin test:For systemic fungi, dermatophytes,Sporothrix, andCandida.
- Germ tube test:DifferentiatesC. albicansfrom otherCandidaspecies.

## M13: Name three common fungal staining methods. ***** (calcoflour white ***, lactophenol cotton blue ***, indian ink, methenamine silver stain, gram staining, giemsa stain)

> 📌 Hint:Calcofluor white, lactophenol cotton blue, Indian ink, methenamine silver, Gram stain, Giemsa

Three most common fungal stains:
1. Calcofluor White— a fluorescent stain that binds to chitin and cellulose in fungal cell walls. Used for direct visualization of fungi in clinical specimens.
2. Lactophenol Cotton Blue (LPCB)— the most common stain for examining fungal cultures. Cotton blue stains the chitin in fungal walls; lactic acid preserves fungal structures; phenol acts as a disinfectant.
3. Indian Ink— a negative capsular stain used to visualize the wide unstained capsule ofCryptococcus neoformans. The capsule appears as a clear halo against a dark background.
Other important stains:
- Gram stain— only forCandida(shows gram-positive yeast).
- Giemsa stain— histopathological stain; also used for blood films.
- Methenamine Silver (GMS)— histopathological stain for fungi in tissue sections.
- PAS (Periodic Acid Schiff)— stains fungal polysaccharides magenta.
- H&E (Hematoxylin & Eosin)— general tissue stain.
- Fluorescent antibody staining

## M14: Why KOH is used in fungal wet mount preparation? ********* (to dissolve the keratin layer, easy visualization of fungus)

> 📌 Hint:To dissolve the keratin layer, easy visualization of fungus

KOH (Potassium Hydroxide)is used in fungal wet mount preparation because:
- Dissolves keratin:KOH (10–40%) dissolves the keratin present in skin, hair, and nail specimens. This removes the obscuring host tissue debris that would otherwise hide the fungal elements.
- Easy visualization of fungi:After keratin dissolution, the refractile fungal hyphae, spores, and other structures become clearly visible under the microscope.
- KOH does NOT dissolve chitin— the fungal cell wall component — so fungi remain intact while the surrounding host tissue is cleared.
Procedure:Place specimen on slide → add KOH (10–40%) → wait 30 minutes → examine under microscope with or without staining.
Note:For specimens from non-skin sites (e.g. blood, CSF), normal saline wet mount is used instead of KOH.

## M15: Why SDA media is used to diagnosed fungus? *** (acidic pH optimum for fungus not for bacteria, high sugar conc.: fungus is a sugar loving organism, added antibiotics: prevent bacterial growth)

> 📌 Hint:Acidic pH optimum for fungus not for bacteria, high sugar conc., added antibiotics

Sabouraud's Dextrose Agar (SDA)is the most commonly used media for fungal diagnosis because of three key properties:
1. Acidic pH (5.6):Optimal for fungal growth. Most bacteria prefer neutral pH (~7.0) and cannot grow well at this acidic pH — so fungi are selectively encouraged while bacteria are inhibited.
2. High dextrose (glucose) concentration:Provides an excellent source of carbon and energy. Fungi are sugar-loving organisms (saccharophilic). The high sugar also raises osmotic pressure, which inhibits many bacteria.
3. Added antibiotics:Penicillin (inhibits gram-positive bacteria), streptomycin (inhibits gram-negative bacteria), and cycloheximide (inhibits saprophytic/non-pathogenic fungi) are frequently added to further ensure only pathogenic fungi grow.
Additional components:Peptone provides nitrogen, vitamins, and minerals.

## M24: S/N: Germ tube test. ** (used to differentiate Candida albicans to other candida spp)

> 📌 Hint:Used to differentiate Candida albicans from other Candida species

Germ Tube Testis a specific test forCandida albicans. It distinguishesC. albicans(which produces germ tubes) from otherCandidaspecies (which do not).
Procedure:
1. Take a small amount of specimen from a colony of culture.
2. Mix with0.5 ml of human or sheep serum.
3. Incubate at37°C for 2 hours.
4. Examine under microscope after wet mount preparation.
Result:Tube-like projections or outgrowths of yeasts (germ tubes) are found →positive→ confirmsC. albicans.
Significance:This is a rapid, simple, and inexpensive test widely used in clinical microbiology labs.C. albicansis the onlyCandidaspecies that reliably produces germ tubes in serum.

## M26: Write down the peculiar properties of Pneumocystis jiroveci/ Explain- "what type of organism is Pneumocystis jiroveci"? *** (at morphology: like parasites and at molecular level: like fungus)

> 📌 Hint:At morphology: like parasites; at molecular level: like fungus

Pneumocystis jiroveciis a unique organism that has features of both fungi and parasites. It is now classified as afungusbased on molecular evidence.
Peculiar Properties:
- Human species:Pneumocystis jiroveci;Rat species:Pneumocystis carinii.
- Transmission:Inhalation of airborne organism.
- Disease:Atypical pneumonia (plasma cell pneumonia) — ground glass opacity on imaging.
- Mortality:Approximately 100% if untreated.

- Cell membrane containscholesterol(not ergosterol — unlike typical fungi).
- In tissue, appears ascysts(resembles protozoal cysts).
- Does not grow on standard fungal culture media.
- Antifungal drugs are largely ineffective.
- Major surface glycoprotein shows antigenic variation similar toTrypanosoma brucei.
Why it is classified as a fungus:
- Molecular analysis: rRNA sequencing, mitochondrial DNA analysis, and major enzyme studies support fungal classification (related to yeasts likeSaccharomyces cerevisiae).
- Cell wall containsβ-glucans.
Laboratory Diagnosis:
- Specimen: lung biopsy, bronchoalveolar lavage (sputum not suitable).
- Staining: methenamine silver, Giemsa, or fluorescent antibody stain → typical cysts.
- Culture: difficult.
- ELISA for antigen & antibody — only for epidemiological purpose.
- PCR.

- Microscopy:Non-septate (coenocytic) hyphae, irregular, right-angle branches.
White Fungus:
- Disease:Candidiasis (especially invasive candidiasis).
- Causative agents:Candida albicans(sometimesC. tropicalis,C. auris).
- Why called "white":In candidiasis (like oral thrush),white patchesare seen on the tongue, mouth, or throat.

## M29: What is fungal ball? *** (lungs lesion caused by Aspergillus fumigatus)

> 📌 Hint:Lung lesion caused by Aspergillus fumigatus

Fungal ball (Aspergilloma)is a lung lesion caused byAspergillus fumigatus. It is a mass of fungal hyphae, mucus, and cellular debris that colonizes a pre-existing lung cavity (e.g., from tuberculosis, sarcoidosis, or bronchiectasis).
Key features:
- Appears as aradiopaque structureon chest X-ray.
- Changes positionwhen the patient is moved from erect to supine posture — this mobility is diagnostic.
- Usually non-invasive but can cause hemoptysis (coughing up blood).
Classification ofAspergilluslung diseases:
1. Allergic aspergillosis (asthma, ABPA)
2. Fungal ball / Aspergilloma(colonization of cavity)
3. Invasive aspergillosis (in immunocompromised)

## M31: What is "id reaction"? ** (hypersensitivity reaction by circulating fungal antigen but not by fungus)

> 📌 Hint:Hypersensitivity reaction by circulating fungal antigen but not by fungus

"Id reaction" (Dermatophytid reaction)is ahypersensitivity reactionthat occurs in response tocirculating fungal antigens— without the actual presence of fungus (hyphae) at the site of the reaction.
- It is a systemic immune response — the fungal antigens from the primary infection site travel via the blood and trigger an allergic reaction elsewhere on the skin.
- The "id" reaction sites do NOT contain fungal organisms on microscopy or culture.
- It is aType IV hypersensitivityreaction.
- Commonly seen in dermatophytosis (ringworm) — e.g., vesicular eruptions on hands when feet are infected.

## M32: What do you mean by dematiaceous fungi? * (having melanin or melanin like pigment. Exophiala, Cladosporium or Hortaea etc. but Mucor is not dematiaceous fungi)

> 📌 Hint:Having melanin or melanin-like pigment. Exophiala, Cladosporium or Hortaea etc. but Mucor is not dematiaceous

Dematiaceous fungiare fungi that havemelanin or melanin-like pigmentin the wall of their hyphae and/or spores. Their conidia or hyphae appeardark-colored (gray or black), which is why they are called "dematiaceous" (from Latindematiaceous= dark-pigmented).
Important dematiaceous fungi:
1. Fonsecaea—F. pedrosoi,F. compacta
2. Phialophora—P. verrucosa
3. Cladophialophora—C. carrionii,C. bantiana
4. Exophiala—E. dermatitidis,E. jeanselmei,E. spinifera,E. moniliae
5. Cladosporium/Hortaea—C. werneckii(=Hortaea werneckii)
Important:Mucoris NOT a dematiaceous fungus— it does not contain melanin pigment.
Diseases caused by dematiaceous fungi:
- Chromoblastomycosis:Chronic granulomatous infection of cutaneous and subcutaneous tissues.
- Pheohyphomycosis:Primary or opportunistic infection ranging from superficial tissue to deep organs.

## M33: Mention the name of sexual and asexual spore.* (sexual spore: zygospore, ascospore, basidiospore, asexual spore: arthrospore, blastospore, chlamydospore, sporangiospore, microconidia and macroconidia)

> 📌 Hint:Sexual: zygospore, ascospore, basidiospore; Asexual: arthrospore, blastospore, chlamydospore, sporangiospore, microconidia, macroconidia

A. Sexual Spores:
1. Zygospore:Single large spore with thick walls.
2. Ascospore:Formed inside a sac called anascus.
3. Basidiospore:Formed externally on the tip of a pedestal called abasidium.
B. Asexual Spores (Conidia):
1. Arthrospore:Arises by fragmentation of the ends of hyphae. Example:Coccidioides immitis.
2. Blastospore (Blastoconidia):Formed by budding. Multiple buds that do not detach form pseudohyphae. Example:Candida albicans.
3. Chlamydospore:Rounded, thick-walled, quite resistant. Example:Candida albicans.
4. Sporangiospore:Formed within a sac (sporangium) on a stalk by molds. Example:RhizopusandMucor.
5. Microconidia:Small conidia. Example:Aspergillus,Microsporum.
6. Macroconidia:Large conidia. Example:Microsporum.
Note:Fungi that do NOT form sexual spores are termedfungi imperfecti(Deuteromycotina).
