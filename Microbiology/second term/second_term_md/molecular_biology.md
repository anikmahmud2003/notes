# Molecular Biology

## MB1: What is molecular biology? **

> 📌 Hint:the branch of biology that studies biomolecules — DNA, RNA, proteins

Molecular biology is the branch of biology that studies thestructure, function, and interactions of biomolecules(cellular molecules such asDNA, RNA, and proteins) that are essential for life.
It combines principles from multiple disciplines:
- Biochemistry— chemical basis of biological reactions
- Genetics— heredity and gene function
- Microbiology— study of microorganisms at molecular level
- Biophysics— physical principles underlying biological structures
Molecular biology focuses on:
- Gene expression (transcription and translation)
- DNA replication
- Protein synthesis
- Molecular mechanisms of diseases

## MB3: What do you mean by central dogma of genetics? ** (Transcription and Translation)

> 📌 Hint:Transcription and Translation

Thecentral dogma of geneticsdescribes the flow of genetic information within a cell. It states that information flows from:
DNA → RNA → Protein
The three main processes are:
Process
Direction
Description
Replication
DNA → DNA
DNA makes a copy of itself during cell division. Enzyme: DNA polymerase.
Transcription
DNA → RNA
A segment of DNA is copied into mRNA by RNA polymerase in the nucleus.
Translation
RNA → Protein
mRNA is decoded by ribosomes in the cytoplasm to synthesize a specific protein (polypeptide chain).
Exception — Reverse Transcription:In retroviruses (e.g., HIV), the enzymereverse transcriptaseconverts RNA back into DNA (RNA → DNA), which reverses the usual flow. This was later added to the central dogma by Francis Crick.

## MB7: What is DNA cloning & recombinant DNA (rDNA) technology? **

> 📌 Hint:DNA cloning = making identical copies; rDNA = combining DNA from two sources

DNA Cloningis a molecular biology technique of creatingidentical copies of a specific DNA fragment (gene)by inserting it into a vector (plasmid or virus), which is then introduced into a host cell (bacteria or yeast) to replicate and produce multiple copies of the gene of interest.
Recombinant DNA (rDNA) Technologyis a set of molecular techniques used tocombine DNA from two different sourcesand insert them into a host organism to produce new genetic combinations. The resulting DNA molecule is called recombinant DNA.
Types of DNA Cloning:
- Recombinant DNA cloning (traditional cloning)
- PCR cloning (TA/Blunt-End cloning)
- Gateway cloning (site-specific recombination)
- Gibson assembly (seamless cloning)
- TOPO cloning (topoisomerase-based)
- BAC cloning (Bacterial artificial chromosome)
- YAC cloning (Yeast artificial chromosome)

## MB11: What is Cosmids, Fosmid, CRISPR-Cas9 & gene therapy?

> 📌 Hint:cosmids = plasmid + cos site; CRISPR-Cas9 = gene editing; gene therapy = fixing faulty genes

Cosmids:
A cosmid is ahybrid plasmid vectorthat contains thecos site of bacteriophage λ (lambda). The plasmid portion allows replication in bacteria, while the cos site allows packaging of large DNA fragments into bacteriophage particles. Used for cloning larger DNA fragments (~30–45 kb) than standard plasmids.
Fosmids:
Fosmids are similar to cosmids but aremore stable. They are derived from bacterialF-plasmids (fertility plasmids). They also carry the cos site and can hold ~40 kb inserts.
CRISPR-Cas9 Gene Editing:
CRISPR-Cas9 is a revolutionarygene-editing toolthat allows precisely cutting and modifying DNA in living cells.
- CRISPR=ClusteredRegularlyInterspacedShortPalindromicRepeats
- Cas9= CRISPR-Associated protein 9 — an enzyme that acts like "molecular scissors" to cut DNA at a specific target site.
Applications of CRISPR-Cas9:
- Medicine: Correct genetic disorders (sickle cell anemia, β-thalassemia), gene therapy, cancer immunotherapy (modifying T cells)
- Agriculture: Disease-resistant crops, improved nutritional content (Golden Rice)
- Industry: Create microbes for biofuel production
- Research: Knockout/modify specific genes, study gene function, design custom organisms
Gene Therapy:
Gene therapy is a technique that involvesfixing faulty genes or adding functional new geneswithin a person to treat or prevent disease. It can involve:
- Replacing (Editing)a faulty mutated gene by introducing a functional gene
- Inactivating (Silencing)a faulty mutated gene
- Introducing (Adding)a new gene
Types of Gene Therapy:
Basis
Type
Description
Based on gene type
Somatic gene therapy
Targets non-reproductive cells; not inherited
Germ-line gene therapy
Targets ovum/sperm cells; inherited. Currently restricted in humans due to ethical concerns.
Based on delivery site
Ex vivo
Cells taken from patient, modified in lab, reintroduced (e.g., CAR-T cell therapy)
In vivo
Genetic material delivered directly into the body using viral vectors (e.g., Luxturna for retinal dystrophy)

## MB15: Give the principle of PCR. ** (amplification of fragment of DNA)

> 📌 Hint:amplification of fragment of DNA

Principle of PCR:
PCR is a technique used toamplify a particular DNA fragment or segment, generating millions of copies. The process relies onthermal cycling(repeated heating and cooling) and the enzymatic action ofDNA polymerase(specificallyTaq polymerase, a heat-stable enzyme fromThermus aquaticus).
Components of PCR:
Component
Role
Template DNA
The DNA sequence/fragment to be amplified
Primers (forward & reverse)
Short ssDNA that bind to complementary sequences on the target DNA and initiate synthesis
Taq DNA polymerase
Heat-stable enzyme that synthesizes new DNA strands
dNTPs (A, T, C, G)
Building blocks (deoxynucleotide triphosphates) for DNA synthesis
Buffer solution
Maintains optimum pH and ionic strength
Mg²⁺ ions
Cofactor for DNA polymerase activity
One cycle of PCR roughly doubles the amount of target DNA. After ~30–40 cycles, a single DNA molecule can be amplified to over a billion copies (2namplification).

## MB16: Mention the basic steps of PCR. ** (denaturation, annealing and elongation)

> 📌 Hint:denaturation, annealing and elongation

Each cycle of PCR consists ofthree basic steps. Approximately30–45 cyclesare performed:
Step
Temperature
Duration
Description
1. Denaturation
94–96°C
15–30 sec
Double-stranded DNA is heated to separate (denature) into two
single strands

2. Annealing
50–70°C
15–30 sec
Primers (short DNA fragments)
bind (anneal)

3. Extension / Elongation
72°C
Depends on length of target
Taq DNA polymerase
extends the primers by adding nucleotides (dNTPs: A, T, C, G) in the 5'→3' direction, forming new complementary DNA strands.
In real-time PCR (qPCR), an additional step — fluorescence detection — occurs during annealing or extension.
Post-PCR analysis (gel electrophoresis):For conventional PCR, the amplified product is run on an agarose gel to check:
- No band → PCR negative or failed
- Single sharp band → PCR positive (successful)
- Multiple bands → Non-specific amplification or contamination
- Faint bands → Low DNA concentration (weakly positive)
