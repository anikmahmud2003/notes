## Paper 1 — General Bacteriology, Systemic Bacteriology & Immunology (October 2026)

### Group A — General Bacteriology & Immunology

#### P1/A/01 — Give four (04) character of prokaryotic cells. Mention six (06) differences between exotoxin and endotoxin. (1.5 + 2)

- **Four characters of prokaryotic cells:** Primitive nucleus/nucleoid; no membrane-bound organelles; 70S ribosome; peptidoglycan in cell wall / single circular DNA.
- **Exotoxin vs Endotoxin — six differences:**
  1. Nature: toxic protein vs Lipid A of LPS.
  2. Source: mainly Gram-positive vs Gram-negative.
  3. Release: secreted during growth vs released on cell lysis.
  4. Toxicity: highly toxic vs less toxic.
  5. Antigenicity: highly antigenic vs weakly antigenic.
  6. Heat stability: labile vs stable (boils).

#### P1/A/02 — Define culture and culture media. Why is bacterial spore a highly resistant structure? (1.5 + 2)

- **Culture/media:** Culture = isolation & multiplication of a specific microorganism on suitable media under controlled conditions; media = nutrient, selective, differential, anaerobic media.
- **Spore:** multi-layered structure (core with calcium dipicolinate, wall/cortex/coat, keratin-like protein, low water, low metabolic activity) → resistant to heat, chemicals, disinfectants; survives years in soil.

#### P1/A/03 — How will you differentiate Staphylococcus from Streptococcus from a skin lesion? Give the importance of ASO titer. What is MRSA? (2 + 1 + 0.5)

- **Staph vs Strep:** Staph = clusters, catalase +, golden/yellow thick creamy pus, localized; Strep = chains, catalase −, whitish thin watery pus, spreading.
- **ASO titer:** anti-streptolysin O; >200 IU/mL in most strep infections; important in rheumatic fever.
- **MRSA:** methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus* (mecA → PBP2a); vancomycin drug of choice.

#### P1/A/04 — A man of 25 years has complaints of urethritis accompanied by dysuria and purulent discharge. What will be the probable diagnosis? Mention the methods of diagnosis? (1 + 2.5)

- **Diagnosis:** Gonococcal urethritis (*Neisseria gonorrhoeae*).
- **Methods:** Gram stain (gram-negative diplococci), culture (Thayer-Martin/GC agar), NAAT/PCR.

#### P1/A/05 — Describe bacterial growth with respect to- growth requirements, growth curve with its importance, importance of generation time & whether O2 is essential for all bacteria or not. (2+2.5+1+1.5)

- **Growth requirements:** nutritional (C/N/S/P, vitamins, V & X factors) + environmental (pH, temp, O₂, CO₂, moisture).
- **Growth curve:** lag → log → stationary → decline; acute phase of disease in log phase; cell-wall-acting antibiotics most effective in log phase.
- **Generation time:** *E. coli* 18–20 min; *M. tuberculosis* 18–24 h; *M. leprae* 18–30 days; slow generation → long treatment.
- **O₂ essential?** No — obligate aerobes need O₂; obligate anaerobes die (no SOD/catalase); facultative & aerotolerant anaerobes exist.

### Group B — Systemic Bacteriology & Pharmacology (AMR)

#### P1/B/01 — Enumerate gram positive rods. "Bacteria are sometimes beneficial to us" - Explain with Lactobacillus. (2 + 1.5)

- **Gram positive rods:** Bacillus (B. anthracis, B. cereus, B. subtilis, B. stearothermophilus); Clostridium (C. tetani, C. botulinum, C. perfringens, C. difficile); Corynebacterium (C. diphtheriae), Listeria, Actinomyces, Nocardia, Lactobacillus.
- **Lactobacillus:** beneficial — lactic acid → acidic pH → colonisation resistance; gut flora → digestion, vitamins B/K.

#### P1/B/02 — Define sterilization and disinfection. Write down the origin of drug resistance. (1.5 + 2)

- **Sterilization:** kill/remove all microorganisms including spores.
- **Disinfection:** kill/remove many microorganisms to a safe level (spores may survive).
- **Origin of drug resistance:** chromosomal mutations; plasmid (β-lactamase), transposon (mecA), phage transduction, integrons.

#### P1/B/03 — Give five (05) peculiar properties of Proteus. How will you diagnose enteric fever? (1.5 + 2)

- **Proteus peculiarities:** Urease positive; phenylalanine deaminase; highly motile; swarming growth on blood agar; fishy odour (ammonia); UTI with renal stones (struvite).
- **Enteric fever diagnosis:** Blood culture (gold standard, wk 1); stool culture (wk 3–4); Widal test (wk 2, 4-fold rise significant); PCR.

#### P1/B/04 — Give the pathogenesis of gas gangrene. "Can all species of Corynebacterium can cause diphtheria?" - Justify. (2 + 1.5)

- **Gas gangrene:** Clostridium spores → contaminated wound → anaerobic germination → α-toxin/lecithinase → myonecrosis + gas production.
- **Corynebacterium diphtheria:** No — only *C. diphtheriae* (toxigenic strains) causes diphtheria.

#### P1/B/05 — Discuss Mycobacterium: Classification; virulence factors of M. tuberculosis; lab diagnosis of pulmonary TB and abbreviate MDR-TB. (2+2+2.5+0.5)

- **Mycobacterium:** M. tuberculosis complex; atypical (NTM/MOTT); virulence factors (mycolic acid, cord factor, ESAT-6, etc.); lab dx (sputum smear, culture, GeneXpert); **MDR-TB** = resistant to isoniazid + rifampicin.

## Paper 2 — Parasitology, Mycology, Virology & Molecular Biology (September 2026)

### Group A — Parasitology

#### P2/A/01 — Symbiotic relationship? Intermediate host? Man as intermediate host? (1.5 + 2)

- Symbiosis: mutualism, commensalism, parasitism. Intermediate host: host with larval stage before definitive host.
- Man as intermediate host: *Plasmodium*, *Toxoplasma gondii*, *Echinococcus granulosus*; also *Taenia solium*.

#### P2/A/02 — Extra-intestinal lesion of *E. histolytica*? Specimens & microscopy of amoebic liver abscess? (1.5 + 2)

- Extra-intestinal: liver, lung, brain, spleen, skin. Liver abscess: USG aspirated pus; anchovy-sauce pus; 3 zones (central necrotic, intermediate, peripheral).

#### P2/A/03 — Four cestodes with transmission/host; four causes of autoinfection? (2 + 1.5)

- Cestodes: T. saginata, T. solium, E. granulosus, D. latum. Autoinfection causes: T. solium (internal), E. vermicularis (external), *Strongyloides* (SS), etc.

#### P2/A/04 — Stool with contaminated hands (Ascaris vs Enterobius)? Ascariasis or enterobiasis? (1.5 + 2)

- *A. lumbricoides* → ascariasis; *E. vermicularis* → enterobiasis (eggs in perianal region).

#### P2/A/05 — Describe Leishmania: old/new world species, vector of Kala-azar, complications, lab diagnosis of PKDL? (2+1+2+2)

- Old World: *L. donovani*, *L. infantum*, *L. tropica*, *L. major*, *L. aethiopica* (Phlebotomus). New World: *L. chagasi*, *L. mexicana*, *L. braziliensis* (Lutzomyia). Vector: female sandfly *Phlebotomus argentipes*. Complications: pancytopenia, PKDL. PKDL lab dx: history + slit skin smear/biopsy, rK39 ICT, LD bodies, PCR.

### Group B — Parasitology

#### P2/B/01 — Six features of trematodes; intestinal, lungs, liver & blood flukes? (1.5 + 2)

- Trematode features: leaf-like flat unsegmented; two suckers; monoecious/hermaphrodite; incomplete gut; no body cavity; tegument.
- Flukes: blood — *S. haematobium*, *S. japonicum*, *S. mansoni*; lung — *Paragonimus westermani*; intestine — *F. buski*, *G. hominis*; liver — *F. hepatica*, *C. sinensis*.

#### P2/B/02 — Four characteristics of T. vaginalis. Why helminthic diseases are now uncommon in Bangladesh? (2 + 1.5)

- T. vaginalis: flagellate protozoan; causes trichomoniasis; trophozoite only; motile. Helminthic diseases uncommon: mass deworming + improved sanitation/WASH.

#### P2/B/03 — How D. latum causes anaemia? Peculiarities in life cycle of S. stercoralis? (2 + 1.5)

- D. latum: unsaturated fatty acids → intrinsic factor block → B12 deficiency → macrocytic anaemia. S. stercoralis: two life cycles, ovoviviparous, parthenogenesis, hyperinfection.

#### P2/B/04 — 25-year-old from Chittagong Hill Tracts, fever & chills 3 days → diagnosis? Differentiate two common species? (1 + 2.5)

- Diagnosis: Malaria. Differentiate *P. vivax* vs *P. falciparum* by peripheral smear (band forms vs typical trophozoites), PCR.

#### P2/B/05 — Filaria: causative organism & infective form; classical vs occult; lab dx; provocation test; conditions when microfilariae not found? (1+2+2+1+1)

- *Wuchereria bancrofti* L3 via Culex; classical (lymphatic obstruction) vs occult (hypersensitivity); lab dx: Giemsa films, DEC provocation; MF not found in occult/elderly-stage.

### Group C — Virology & Mycology

#### P2/C/01 — Draw & label enveloped virus; five DNA viruses? (2.5 + 1)

- Enveloped: nucleic acid + capsid + lipid envelope. DNA viruses: HSV, VZV, HBV, HPV, CMV (among five).

#### P2/C/02 — Cytopathic effects? Three conditions of viral persistence? (2 + 1.5)

- CPE: rounding/darkening, lysis, inclusion bodies, giant cells. Persistence: latency, chronic infection, slow viral infection.

#### P2/C/03 — Early/late protein? Site of replication of DNA/RNA viruses incl. exceptions? (1.5 + 2)

- Early = enzymes before replication; late = structural proteins after replication. DNA → nucleus (Pox → cytoplasm); RNA → cytoplasm (Orthomyxo/Retro/Delta → nucleus).

#### P2/C/04 — 40-yr-old woman, round well-circumscribed skin lesions with itching in groin → disease, causative agents, method of diagnosis? (1 + 2.5)

- Tinea cruris; dermatophytes (T. rubrum, T. mentagrophytes, E. floccosum, M. canis); KOH wet mount, culture, Wood's lamp.

#### P2/C/05 — Describe *Candida albicans*: four important properties; lesions; predisposing factors; laboratory diagnosis? (1.5+1.5+2+2)

- Properties: white fungus, yeast, only normal-flora fungus, germ tube positive, Gram positive. Lesions: oral thrush, vulvovaginal, esophageal, onychomycosis, systemic. Predisposing: antibiotics, steroids, immunosuppression, diabetes. Lab dx: wet mount, Gram, culture SDA, germ tube, carbohydrate fermentation.

### Group D — Virology

#### P2/D/01 — Mode of transmission of hepatitis virus? Post-exposure prophylaxis of Rabies? (1.5 + 2)

- HAV/HEV → fecal-oral; HBV/HCV/HDV → parenteral, sexual, vertical. Rabies PEP: wound care, HRIG + vaccine (days 0,3,7,14).

#### P2/D/02 — Influenza long-term protection? HIV infection vs AIDS (incl. abbreviation)? (1.5 + 2)

- Influenza: antigenic drift → annual vaccine; shift → pandemic. HIV = Human Immunodeficiency Virus; AIDS = Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome (CD4 <200/µL or AIDS-defining illness).

#### P2/D/03 — Four vector & four host of DNA cloning; PCR vs qPCR? (2+1.5)

- Vectors: plasmid, cosmid, BAC, YAC. Hosts: *E. coli*, yeast, mammalian, insect. **qPCR is better** than PCR (quantitative, real-time, fluorescence, no post-PCR gel).

#### P2/D/04 — Complications of measles? Enlist oncogenic virus? (1.5 + 2)

- Complications: otitis media, pneumonia, encephalitis, SSPE, immune suppression. Oncogenic viruses: HPV, HBV, HCV, EBV, HTLV-1, HHV-8, HIV, MCPyV.

#### P2/D/05 — Fever, rash, severe myalgia × 3 days, travel to Dhaka 1 week → diagnosis? Pathogenesis, lab dx, prevention? (1+2.5+2+1.5)

- Diagnosis: Dengue fever. Pathogenesis: Aedes bite; ADE in secondary infection → DHF/DSS. Lab dx: NS1, IgM/IgG, PCR. Prevention: vector control, vaccine, personal protection.
