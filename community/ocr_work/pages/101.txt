Classification of carriers:

* According to type:

1) Incubatory carriers: Incubatory carriers are those who shed the infectious agent during the
incubation period of disease, These usually occur during the last few days of the incubation
period, e.g. measles, mumps, polio, pertussis.

2) Convalescent carriers: Convalescent carriers are those who continue to shed the disease
during the period of convalescence; e.g. typhoid fever, dysentery, cholera, diphtheria &
whooping cough.

3) Healthy carriers: They are the victims of subclinical infection who have developed carrier

State without suffering from overt disease but are nevertheless shedding the disease agent, eg.
Poliomyelitis, cholera, salmonellosis and diphtheria.

% According to duration: A
1) Temporary carriers: Temporary carriers are those who shed the infectious agent for short
Periods of time. These include the incubatory, convalescent and healthy carrier.

Criteria / Elements of carrier state:
1) The presence in the body of the disease agent.

2) The absence of recognizable symptoms and signs of disease.

3) The shedding of the disease agent in the discharges or excretions, thus acting as a source of
infection for other persons.
