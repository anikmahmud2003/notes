Control and Prevention of Air Pollution

Q. How air pollution can be prevented? [SUST-16N]

Q. How outdoor [RU-11J,10Ju] / indoor [CU-11Ju] air pollution can be prevented?

Answer

Prevention and control of air pollution; The WHO has recommended the following procedures for the

prevention and control of air pollution:

1) Containment: It means prevention of escape of toxic substance into the ambient air which can be
achieved by enclosure, ventilation and air cleaning .A major contribution in this field is the
development of arrestors for the removal of containment.

2) Replacement: It means replacement of a technological process causing air pollution by a process
that does not pollute the air.

3) Dilution: Dilution is valid so long as it is within the self-cleaning capacity of the environment. For
example, some air pollutants are readily removed by vegetation.

4) Legislation: Air pollution can be controlled by legislation and its appropriate application.

5) International action: To deal with air pollution on a world scale, the WHO has established an
international network of laboratories for the monitoring and study of air pollution. They issue
warnings of air pollution when and where necessary.

Q. How air borne diseases can be controlled?
Answer
Control of air borne diseases:
1) By giving vaccines we can prevent and control air borne diseases such as- diphtheria, tuberculosis,
pertussis, measles, mumps, chicken pox etc.
2) Inhalation of air pollutants (e.g. silicosis, siderosis, anthracosis, lead poisoning can be prevented by
legislation of law.
3) To reduce the impact of air pollution, location of the industries should be outside from the human
habitation.
4) Industrial air pollution can be controlled by prevention of escape of toxic substances into the
ambient air by engineering methods and by adequate disposal of waste products.
5) Health education is also necessary to prevent the air borne diseases.

Q. How air in a room is disinfected?
Q. Enumerate the methods of disinfection of indoor air. [RU-18N,15J]
Answer
Disinfection of air:
1) Mechanical ventilation: This reduces vitiated air and bacterial density.
2) UV radiation: Effective for disinfection in the operation theaters and infectious disease wards.
3) Chemical mists: e.g. triethylene glucol vapours are effective air bactericides, particularly against
droplets nuclei and dusts.
4) Dust control: application of oil to floors of hospital wards reduces the bacterial contents of the air.
