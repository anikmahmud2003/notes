Q. State the life cycle of itch mite. Mention the diseases spread by it.

Answer ;
Life cycle of itch mite: There are four stages in the history of life of an itch mite —
1) Egg.
2) Larva.
3) Nymph.
4) Adult.

The life cycle from egg to adult may take 10 to 15 days. The adult mites live for 1 to 2 months,

+ Disease spread by itch mite: Scabies or itch.

Itch mite & Scabies

Q. Describe itch mite.

Q. Define scabies.

Answer

Itch mite / Sarcoptes scabiei | Acarus scabiei:

The itch mite (Sarcoptes scabiei or Acarus scabiei) is an extremely small, globular arthropod just
visible to the naked eye. The female parasite burrows into the epidermis where it breeds and causes the
condition known as scabies or itch.

[Ref- Park / 24" / 818]

Q. What are the main diagnostic features of scabies?
Answer
Main diagnostic features of scabies are:
1) Intense itching at night.
2) More than one member in the family has affected in adults,
3) Face and scalp are not usually affected in adults.
4) Itching of nipples in female and itching papules in males of penis or scrotum.
5) Examination reveals follicular lesion at the affected site.
6) Secondary infection leads to crusted papules and pustules.
7) Confirmatory diagnosis — Searching of parasite in skin debris under microscope.

[Ref- Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 5 / 289 + Park / 24" / 819]

Q. How scabies can be controlled?
Q. Discuss control measures of scabies. [DU-10Ju, CU-17M,13J]
Q. Write short note on: Control of scabies. [SUST-18N]
Answer
Controls of scabies:
1) Sanitation control:
a) Improvement of personal hygiene.
b) Allaffected persons of a family should be treated simultaneously.
c) Affected children should be excluded from the school until the day after treatment.
d) Before treatment all patients should have a good soap and warm water bath.

2) Chemical control (treatment of scabies patient):
a)

Benzyl benzoate (25%) emulsion- smeared all over the body from neck to feet except face
and genital areas for successive days. Application should be repeated after 12 hours and
after a further 12 hours a bath should be taken.

b) Permethrin cream (5%).

c) Monosulfiram (Tetmosol) (5%) - thrice daily applications.

d) Sulphur ointment (2.5 to 10%): Daily for 4 days.
