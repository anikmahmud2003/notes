Central tendency: In case of large number of observation there is generally a tendency of the observation to
cluster around a central point. This is known as central tendency. There are mainly 3 main measure of

central tendency:
> Mean

> Median
> Mode

‘> Mean: There are three types of mean:
¥ Arithmetic mean
¥ Geometric mean
Y Harmonic mean
Arithmetic mean: It is widely used in statistical calculation. It is sometimes called simply the
mean. It is the sum of all the observations divided by the number of observations. Symbolically
an arithmetic mean is ‘X’
X = YX/N; Y-X= Summation of all the observations
N= Number of observation
X = Mean value

For example, pulse rate of some 10 individual were 68, 74, 56, 64, 75, 66, 59, 73, 55 & 60.
Here, X= 68+ 74+ 56+ 64+ 75+ 66+ 59+ 73+55 +60 = 650
N= 10
X = DX/N= 650/10 = 65.
Advantage: It is easy to calculate and understand.
Disadvantage:
> Sometimes it may be unduly influenced by abnormal values in distribution.
> Sometimes it may even look ridiculous.

b. Geometric mean: It is the ‘n’th root of product of ‘n’ observations.

Median: It is the middle most value of a series when observations are arranged in ascending or
descending order. If n is odd, the value in the (n+1)/2th position is the median. For example, in the
following series the value of (9+1)/2th e.g. 5" position that is so 10 is the median.

4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14

If there is an even number of observations (‘n’ is even), then there is no single middle value
i.e. there are two middle positions, these are n/2 & (n/2 +1). The median is then usually defined to the
arithmetic mean of the two middle values in the n/2 & (n/2 +1)th position.

the series is the mode. For example in the series: 7, 9, 8, 6, 9, 11, 14 9 is the mode.
