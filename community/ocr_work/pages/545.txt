532. — ENDEAVOUR COMMUNITY MEDICINE FOR WRITTEN (SAQ, MCQ), VIVA & OSPE

Lead poisoning / Plumbism

Q. Define plumbism.
Q. List at risk workers for plumbism. [DU-14Ju]
Q. Write short note on: Plumbism. [RU-17N,16M]

Answer ,
Plumbism /saturnism: Chronic exposure of lead (Pb) is called plumbism.

1) Facial pallor.
2) Anaemia. .
3) Lead line / ‘Burtonian line’: A strippled blue line is seen at the gums in 50-70% cases. It appears at

the junction with teeth, within a week of exposure especially in the upper jaw.
4) Colicky pain. |
5) Constipation.
6) Lead palsy. |
7) Lead encephalopathy (present in almost every case & frequently encountered in children). |
8) Effect on CVS: Hypertension & permanent arteriolar degeneration.
9) Effect on kidneys: Chronic intestinal nephritis.
10) Sterility & menstrual abnormality.
11) Lead osteopathy (in case of children and young individuals).
12) Effect on liver: Acute or chronic degeneration may occur.
13) Optic atrophy.
14) Other effects: Dyspepsia, anorexia, emaciation, general weakness, exhaustion, alopecia etc.

Management of chronic lead poisoning:
A) Curative:
1) Removal of the patient from the source of poison.
2) Chelating agent: Disodium calcium edetate is the best chelating agent for Pb.
Dose: 1gm twice daily is given by slow I/V drip over a period of 1 hour, mixed with 5%
glucose saline for 5 days.

3) Potassium or sodium iodide 1-2 gm, thrice daily to eliminate circulatory Pb through urine.

4) Sodium bicarbonate 20-30 gm/day in 4-5 divided doses.

5) To prevent constipation magnesium sulphate purgative is used.

6) For severe colic 0.5-1 mg of atropine sulphate and /or hot compression on the abdomen is
useful.

7) Leg encephalopathy in children may need surgical decompression of the skull.

8) Symptomatic treatment should be given.

B) Prophylaxis:
1) To prevent chronic lead poisoning in the factory: Maintenance of proper ventilation,

maintenance of personal hygiene of the workers and their periodic medical checkup is
necessary.

2) Advice should be given to wash fruits before eating and avoidance of canned foods that are
preserved in containers lined by lead.

3) Lead vermilion, cosmetics and dyes may be used keeping the danger in mind.

Complications of lead poisoning:

1) Gastroenteritis.

2) Malnutrition.

3) Intercurrent infection.
4) Hepatic failure.

5) Respiratory failure.
6) Renal failure.
