Hepatitis-B

Hepatitis B:

Hepatitis B (formerly known as “serum hepatitis” is a systemic infection with major pathology in the
liver, caused by hepatitis B virus (HBV) and transmitted usually by the parenteral route.

Persistent hepatitis B virus infection may cause progressive liver disease including chronic active

hepatitis and hepatocellular carcinoma.
[Ref- Park / 24" / 227]

Epidemiology of hepatitis B / serum hepatitis:
“* Occurrence: it is endemic in all over the world, especially in tropical and developing countries.
“ Epidemiological triad:

1) Agents factors:
> Agent: causative agent is hepatitis B virus double stranded DNA virus (originally

known as the ‘‘Dave patient’’)

Reservoir of infection: man is the only reservoir of infection.

Infective material: contaminated blood is the main source although it is found in
saliva, vaginal secretion and semen of infected person.

Resistance: it is quite stable and capable of surviving for days on environment surface.
Period of communicability: usually several months or until disappearance of HBsAg
& appearance of surface Ab.

VV VV

2) Host factors:
> Age: the highest prevalence of the surface antigen is found in the 20-40 years.
> High-risk group: high-risk groups comprise doctors, nurses, dentists, hospital staff,
laboratory workers, homosexual, percutaneous drug addicts, infants of HBV carrier
mothers & patients who are immuno-compromised, recipient of blood transfusion.
> Humoral and cellular response: Ab form in a week or low after onset of jaundice first
core Ab then and ‘e’ antibody.

3) Environmental factors: Hepatitis B may occur around the year & it has no seasonal pattern.

“+ Natural history:
1) Reservoir: Patient, carrier.

2) Source of infection: infected blood products
