Q. What is nosocomial infection? [DU-14J]
Q. Write short note on: Nosocomial infection. [SUST-14Ju]
Answer

Nosocomial infection / Hospital acquired infection;

Nosocomial (hospital acquired) infection is an infection originating in a patient while in a hospital or
other health care facility. It denotes a new disorder (unrelated to the patient’s primary condition) associated with
being in a hospital. That is it was not present or incubating at the time of admission or the residual of an
infection acquired during a previous admission. It includes infections acquired in the hospital but appearing
after discharge and also such infections among the staff of the facility.

Examples: Infection of surgical wounds, hepatitis B and UTI.

Q. Define opportunistic infection. [DU-14Ju]
Answer

Opportunistic infection: ,
Infection by an organism that is not normally pathogenic but can cause disease if resistance is lowered

is called opportunistic infection. This is infection by an organism that takes the opportunity provided by a defect
in host defense to infect the host and hence cause disease.

Common organism: Including Herpes simplex (HSV), Cytomegalovirus (CMV), Toxoplasma etc.

Example: Opportunistic infections are very common in AIDS.
[Ref- Park /24"/99]

Q. Write short note on: Iatrogenic disease.
Answer

Iatrogenic (physician induced) disease:

Any untoward or adverse consequence of a preventive, diagnostic or therapeutic regimen or procedure
that causes impairment, handicap, disability or death resulting from a physician’s professional activity or from
the professional activity of other health professionals is called iatrogenic or physician induced disease. ,

Or,
latrogenic disease is any ill health induced by a medical practitioner’s actions.

Example:
> Allergic reaction caused by penicillin (of a previously known allergic history with penicillins).

> Aplastic anaemia caused by chloramphenicol.
> Hepatitis- B following blood transfusion.
