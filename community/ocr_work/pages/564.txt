Man and machine:

The unguarded machines, protruding and moving parts, poor installation of the plant, lack of safety
measures are the causes of accidents which is a major problem in industries. Working for long hours in
unphysiological postures is the cause of fatigue, backache, diseases of joints and muscles and impairment of the
worker’s health and efficiency.

Man and man:

These are the human relationships amongst workers themselves, and those in authority over them. In
modem occupational heath, the emphasis is upon the people, the conditions in which they live and work, their
hopes and fears and their attitudes towards their jobs, their fellow workers and employers. Stress at work may
disturb his sleep. Severe prolonged stress, no matter where it has been aroused, may produce serious physical or

mental symptoms which do not allow man to work efficiently. -

Occupational diseases:
Occupational diseases can be defined as diseases arising out of or in the course of employment.

Occupational health hazards: An industrial worker may expose to five types hazards:
1) Physical hazards, / occupational disease due to physical a ent:
Heat: Hyperpyrexia, heat exhaustion, heat syncope, heat cramps, burns & prickly heat.
Cold: Trench foot, frost bite, chilblains.
Light: Occupational cataract, miner’s nystagmus.
Pressure: Caisson disease, air embolism, blast.
Noise: Occupational deafness.
Radiation: Cancer, leukemia, aplastic anemia, pancytopenia.
Mechanical factors: Injuries, accidents.
Electricity: Burns.

2) Chemical hazards / occupational disease due to chemical agent:

> Local action: Dermatitis, eczema, ulcers, cancer
> Chemicals: Acids, alkalies, pesticides.
> Solvents: Carbon bisulphide, benzene, trichloroethylene, chloroform, etc.
> Inhalation:
Y Gases: Exposure to gases is a common hazard in industries.CO,, CO, HCN, CS),
NH, H2S, HCL, SO — these cause gas poisoning.
Y Metals & their compounds: Toxic hazards from lead, mercury, cadmium,
manganese, beryllium, arsenic, chromium etc.
