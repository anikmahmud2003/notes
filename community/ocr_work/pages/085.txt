Case control study: . a . .
A case control study involves two populations - Case (with disease) & Control (without disease).
In case-control studies, the unit is the individual rather than a group. The focus is on.a disease or some

other health problem that has already developed. 2
[Ref- Park /24 176)

The case control study has three distinct features:
1) Both exposure & outcome (disease) have occurred before the start of the study.
2) The study proceeds backwards from effect to cause.
3) It uses a control or comparison group to support or refute an inference.
4) Other features:
> Itis usually first & easier to test a causal hypothesis.
> Ituses a fewer number of subject.
> Study yields relative quick results.
> Relative inexpensive.
> Suitable to study rare diseases.

Basic steps of case control study: There are four basic steps in conducting a case control study-

1) Selection of cases and controls:
a) Selection of case:
Y Definition of case: It involves two specification
> Diagnostic criteria: The diagnostic criteria of the disease and the stage of
disease e.g. breast cancer stage I.
> Eligibility criteria: Only newly diagnosed cases.

Y Sources of case: Hospitals, General population.

b) Selection of controls: The control must be free from the disease under study. They must be
as similar to the cases as possible except for the absence of the diseased under study. Asa
rule comparison group is identified before a study is done.

Source of controls:
> Hospital controls,
> Relatives
> Neighborhood controls
> General population.
