Methods of data presentation:

A) Tabulation: Three types of tables are in use:
1) Master table: In this table all initial readings as per designed proforma are serially recorded.

When the number of observation is large and several attributes have to be studied, the master
table is must.

2) Simple table: In this the characteristic under observation is fixed and the number of frequency
of events is small.

3) Frequency distribution table: this is the most important table in statistical work. In a
frequency table large, unsorted data is presented in a small manageable number of groups. This

can be prepared using either type of data, qualitative and quantitative data.

B) Drawings (charts, diagrams & maps):
1) Bar chart: bar charts are way of presenting a set of numbers by the length of a bar-the length
of which is proportional to the magnitude to be represented. The following are some examples

of bar charts:
Y Simple bar chart: Bars may be vertical or horizontal. The bars are usually separated

by appropriate spaces with an eye to neatness and clear presentation.
Y Multiple / compound bar charts: In this type, two or more bars can be grouped

together.
¥ Component bar chart: In this type, the bars are divided into two or more parts, each

part representing a certain item and proportional to the magnitude of that particular
item.

Histogram: It consists of a series of blocks. The class intervals are given along the horizontal

axis and the frequency along the vertical axis.

3) Frequency polygon: It is simply a derivation from the histogram obtained by joining the
midpoints of various histogram blocks.

4) Frequency curve: When the number of observations is large, the polygon loses angulations
and it becomes a frequency curve.

5) Line chart or graph: It shows the trend of an event over a period of time.

6) Scatter or Dot diagram: A graphic presentation is made here to show the extent and nature of
correlation between two variables such as height and weight.

7) Pie or Sector diagram: In this type, data are presented in a circle. Degrees of angles and the
area of the sectors denote the relative frequency.

8) Map diagram or Spot map: These are prepared to show geographical distribution of the
frequencies of the characteristics. The spot map also helps in locating the index case in an
epidemic.

9) Pictogram: In this type, small picture:
