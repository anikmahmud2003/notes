Vector: Vector may be defined as an arthropod or any living carrier (e.g. snail) that transport an infectious
agent to a susceptible individua [Ref- Park /24" / 193

Classification of medically important vector:
A) Invertebrate type: Arthropod vectors fall into seven orders largely.

1) Diptera: Flies and mosquitoes.

2) Siphonaptera: Fleas. 2

3) Orthoptera: Cockroaches.

4) Anoplura: Sucking lice.

5) Hemiptera: Bugs, including kissing bugs.
6) Acarina: Ticks and mites.

7) Copepoda: Cyclops.

B) Vertebrate type: Mice, rodents, bats.
[Ref- Park /24"/ 103]

Q. Enumerate the factors which influence the ability of vectors to transmit disease.
Answer
The factors which influence the ability of vectors to transmit disease are:

1) Host feeding preferences.

2) Infectivity.

3) Susceptibility.

4) Survival rate of vectors in the environment.

5) Domesticity that is degree of association with the man.

6) Suitable environmental factors.
[Ref- Park /24"/ 103]

Successful parasitism

Q. What do you mean by successful parasitism?
Answer
Successful parasitism: There are four stages in successful parasitism —
» First, the infectious agent must find a Portal of Entry by which it may enter into the host. There are
many portal of entry, e.g. respiratory tract, alimentary tract, genitourinary tract, skin etc.
> On gaining entry into the host, the organism must reach the appropriate tissue or site of election in
the body where it may find optimum conditions for its multiplication and survival.
> Thirdly, the disease agent must find a way out of the body in order that it may reach a new host and
propagate its species otherwise it may become a dead-end infection. Example — rabies, tetanus,
bubonic plague.
» After leaving the human body, the organism must survive in the external environment for sufficient
period till a new host is found.
[Ref- Park /24" / 104]
