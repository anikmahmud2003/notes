Autoclave:

Sterilizers which operate at high temperatures (in excess of 100°C) and pressure are called autoclaves.

Types of autoclave: They fall into two categories — i
1) Double chamber autoclave &

|
2) Single chamber autoclave |

Time, temperature & pressure used in autoclave:

|
121°C temperature under 15 Ibs/sq inch pressure for 15 minutes. Ht

Principle of autoclave: {i}

1. Atatmospheric pressure, water boils at 100°C i}

2. With the rise of pressure, the boiling point of water also rises e.g.

3. Steam under pressure unmixed with air has more temperature than that mixed with air.

4. Steam under pressure has more penetrating power. This is due to fact that, steam condenses to water on
contact with materials.

5. During condensation of steam to water, a large amount of latent heat is liberated, thus increasing the i|}
efficacy of sterilization.

Uses of autoclaving:
Autoclaving is widely used in hospital and laboratory practice. It destroys all forms of organisms Hi

including spores. It is the most effective method for sterilization of linen, dressings, gloves, syringes, blunt
surgical instruments and culture media. It is not suitable for sterilization of plastics and sharp instruments by
autoclave.

Q. Give the mechanism of action of dry heat and moist heat. l
Answer i|
Mechanism of action of dry heat: 1

1. Denaturation of cell protein

2. Oxidative damage

3. Toxic effect of elevated electrolyte. |

Mechanism of action of moist heat: i
1. Loss of functional integrity of cytoplasmic membrane. |
2. Denaturation and coagulation of structural proteins and enzymes. |
3. Breakage of DNA strands.
