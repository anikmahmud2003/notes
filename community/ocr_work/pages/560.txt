1) Reconstruction and rehabilitation are time consuming. Nevertheless planning for reconstruction
start immediately after the disaster and it is important to include measures for the
reconstruction of the damaged area in the national disaster plan.

2) Planners should play due attention not only to the erection of new households and public
buildings but also to industry, agriculture and all other socioeconomic aspects of
reconstruction.

3) Statistical information on the following points is required before the planning for
reconstruction-

> Number of affected person, there geographical distribution, age group, occupation etc.
> Damage to water supply and health centre.

> Number and standard of house destroyed.

> Available manpower, materials, equipment and financial sources.

4) The authorities responsible for rehabilitation should establish priorities: reconstruct roads and
public buildings, execute programmes for replacing destroyed housing: exercise economic and

technical control of labour and material.

Q. How can you mitigate the health effects of disaster? [SUST-13Ju]

Answer
Mitigation / reduction of the health effects of disaster: Emergency prevention and mitigation involves
measures designed either to prevent hazards from causing emergency or to lessen the likely effects of

emergencies. These measures include —
> Food mitigation works.
> Appropriate land-use planning.
> Improved building codes.

> Reduction or protection of vulnerable population and structures.
