Q. Write short note on: Types of epidemic. [SUST-13Ju]
Answer
Epidemic: (Epi = Upon, demos = people)
Epidemic means the unusual occurrence in a community or region of disease, specific health-related

behaviour (e.g. smoking) or the health related events (e.g. traffic accidents) clearly in excess of ‘expected
occurrence.

Example of epidemic disease:
Y Communicable diseases: Measles, chicken pox & cholera.

Y Noncommunicable disease: CHD, lung cancer.
Y Health-related behaviour: Smoking.
Y Health-related events: Traffic accidents.

Types / classification of epidemics: Three major types of epidemic are distinguished —

1) Common-source epidemics:
> Single exposure or point source epidemics (e.g. food poisoning, Bhopal gas tragedy).

> Continuous or multiple exposure epidemics (e.g. the Legionnaire's disease in the summer of
1976 in Philadelphia (USA), Gonorrhoea).

2) Propagated epidemics:
> Person to person (epidemics of hepatitis-A and polio).

> Arthropod vector.
> Animal reservoir.

3) Slow (modern) epidemics: e.g. cancer.

Q. Draw and explain different epidemic curves.
Q. Draw and explain the diagram of a course of typical propagated epidemic.
Answer
Epidemic curve:
A graph of the time distribution of epidemic cases is called the “epidemic curve”.
In other word, epidemic curve is a histogram that describes an outbreak of disease by plotting the
number of cases of a disease by date of onset.

The epidemic curve may suggest:
> A time relationship with exposure to a suspected source,

> Acyclical or seasonal pattern suggestive of a particular infection and common source or
propagated spread of the disease.
