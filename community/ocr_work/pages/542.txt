Soil Pollution

Q. Classify soil borne disease with causative agents.
Answer

Soil borne diseases:
1) Bacterial:
> Anthrax — B. anthracis
> Tetanus — Cl. tetani
> Gas gangrene > Cl. welchi

2) Parasitic:
> Ankylostomiasis + A. Duodenale
> Ascariasis + A. Lumbricoides
> Taeniasis — Taenia solium

3) Indirectly: (due to dampness of soil)
> Rheumatism
> Respiratory diseases
[Ref- Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 4" / 492]

Q. How will you prevent soil borne diseases?
Answer
Prevention of soil borne diseases:
1) Prevention of contamination with soil
2) By wearing shoes
3) Maintenance of personal hygiene
4) Health education
5) Maintenance of hygiene of wounded skin
6) Early diagnosis and prompt treatment of soil borne disease.

Q. Write short note on: Arsenicosis. [SUST-15J,13Ju,1 Ju]

Answer

Arsenicosis:
‘Arsenicosis means the clinical conditions that develop due to excess level of arsenic in the blood.

Arsenic problem in Bangladesh:

Background information:
In the late 1970s, United Nations agencies such as UNICEF provided the rural people with | million

shallow tube wells. The villagers dug 3 million more such wells at their own cost. Not only shallow tube wells,
thousands of deep tube wells were also installed throughout the country in the last two decades and have been in
use to irrigate the cultivated lands and support the ongoing development in the agricultural sector. 1993 the tube
well water was found to be contaminated with arsenic in certain areas of the country which was first sounded by
a Kolkata chemical analyst in 1988. Continued investigation since 1993 detected water from shallow tube wells
in many places of 4 districts out of 64 districts of Bangladesh to have been contaminated with arsenic- throwing
about 15 million people at risk of slow arsenic poisoning. As per WHO standard the recommended limit of
arsenic concentration in potable water is 0.01mg/liter, in Bangladesh that limit has been set at 0.05mg/liter.
