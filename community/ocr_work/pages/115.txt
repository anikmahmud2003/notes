Communicable Diseases

Q. What is communicable disease?
Answer
Communicable disease:

An illness due to specific infectious agent or it’s toxic products capable of being directly or indirectly
transmitted from man to man, animal to animal or from the environment (through air, dust, soil, water, food
etc.) to man or animal.

[Ref- Park / 24" / 98}

Component:
> Agent: The seed.

> Host: The soil.
> Route of transmission: The climate.
Q. State the cardinal characteristics of infectious diseases.
Answer
Cardinal characteristics of infectious diseases: For an infectious disease to occur, the following conditions or
events must be take place —
1) There must be an aetiologic agent, such as parasite, bacteria, virus, fungus, rickettsia, etc. that
produces disease,
2) There must be a reservoir (human, animal or others) where the biologic agent can propagate (ie.
live, multiply and die in the natural state).
3) There must be a portal of exit from a reservoir and a portal of entry into susceptible host in
order for a disease to be caused elsewhere.
4) An organism (etiologic agent) must be transmitted, either directly or indirectly, from one place
to another.
5) There must be a susceptible host.
[Ref- Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 5" /89]
