Ozone Layer Depletion

Q. Define ozone layer.
Q. State the impact of ozone layer depletion on community health. [DU-14Ju]
Answer

Ozone layer / Ozone shield / Ozonosphere:

The ozone layer refers to a region of earth’s stratosphere that absorbs most of the Sun’s ultraviolet (UV)
radiation. It contains high concentrations of ozone (Os) relative to other parts of the atmosphere, although still
very small relative to other gases in the stratosphere. It is situated roughly between 15 and 35 km (9 and 22

miles) above earth’s surface.

Source of ozone layer:
UV light strikes ordinary oxygen molecules containing two oxygen atoms (O,), splitting them into

individual oxygen atoms (atomic oxygen). The atomic oxygen then combines with unbroken O, to create ozone
(Os).

Functions of ozone layer:
The ozone layer absorbs 97-99% of the sun’s medium-frequency UV light (from about 200 nm to
315 nm wavelength), which otherwise would potentially damage exposed life forms near the surface.
The United Nations General Assembly has designated September 16 as the International Day for the
Preservation of the Ozone Layer.

Effects of ozone layer depletion on community health: Ozone layer depletion increases in surface UV ray
which lead to:

1) Basal & squamous cell carcinoma of skin.

2) Malignant melanoma of skin.

3) Cortical cataract.

4) Immunesuppression.

5) Increased oxidation process in human body & aging process.
