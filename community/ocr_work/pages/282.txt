Q. “At present arthropod-borne disease contribute a major public health”- explain it with the
mechanism of transmission of disease by arthropod. [RU-14Ju]
Answer
Transmission of arthropod-borne disease: 3 types of transmission —
1) Direct contact: the arthropods are directly transferred from man to man through close contact e.g.
scabies and pediculosis.
2) Mechanical transmission: the disease agent is transmitted mechanically by the arthropod, e.g.
diarrhoea, dysentery, typhoid fever, food poisoning & trachoma by the house fly.
3) Biological transmission: When the disease agent multiplies or undergoes some developmental
change with or without multiplication in the arthropod host, then it is called biological transmission.
This may be of 3 types -
> Propagative: When the disease agent undergoes no cyclical changes, but multiplies in the
body of the vector; e.g. plague bacilli in rat fleas.
> Cyclo-propagative: the disease agent undergoes cyclical changes and multiplies in the
body of the arthropod; e.g. malaria parasite in anopheles mosquito.
> Cyclo-developmental: When the disease agent undergoes cyclical change but does not
multiply in the body of the arthropod; e.g. filarial parasite in culex mosquito and guinea

worm embryo in cyclops.
