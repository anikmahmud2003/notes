Ancylostomiasis (Hook worm disease)

Q. Write down the epidemiology of hook worm infestation.
Answer
Epidemiology of ancylostomiasis / hook worm disease:
Occurrence: Ancylostoma duodenale and the Necator americanus are found in every country
where the freezing temperature does not last for long and the sanitation is poor.
> Agent: Ancylostoma duodenale and the Necator americanus.
> Reservoir: Man is the only reservoir.
> Host: Negroes are less susceptible than others. Males are affected more than females because of
greater exposure. ‘
> Environment: Agricultural land and mines are ideal for the development of hookworm larva.
Moist, loose, sandy soil rich in organic matter favours larval development.
> Mode of transmission: The larva enters the body by penetrating the skin when and where it comes
in contact with it. The most common sites include the skin between the toes, dorsum of the feet, and
inner aspect of the sole.
[Ref- Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 5" / 269]

Q. Discuss approaches of hookworm prevention and control. [DU-07J]

Q. Suggest some method to prevent A. duodenale infestation in agriculture workers.
Answer

Prevention & control measures of hookworm infestation:

1) Sanitary disposal of faeces:
> The only long term solution is the sanitary disposal of human excreta.
> Promote the use of low cost “sanitary latrines” in rural areas.
> Achange of in farming practices may also be needed, that is not to use raw faeces or
untreated sewage as fertilizer.

2) Chemotherapy: Periodic case findings and treatment of all infected persons in the community will
reduce the worm burden and frequency of transmission. Several effective drugs are available for the
treatment of hookworm infection -
