Experimental Epidemiology

Q. Discuss experimental epidemiology.

Answer

Experimental epidemiology / experimental study:
> Meant the study of epidemics among colonies of experimental animals such as rat & mice.
> In modern usage, experimental epidemiology is often equated with randomized controlled trials

Aims of experimental studies:
Y To provide scientific proof of etiological (or risk) factors.

Y To provide a method of measuring the effectiveness and efficiency of health services.

Types of experimental studies:
1) Randomized controlled trials (RCT).

2) Non-randomized trial or non-experimental trials.

Advantages of experimental studies:
1) The experimental animal can be bred in laboratories & manipulated easily according to the wishes

of the investigators.
2) They multiply and enable the investigators to carry out certain experiments (i.e. genetic
experiments which in human population would take several years and involve many generations.

Disadvantages / limitations of experimental studies:
1) Not all human diseases can be reproduced in animals.
2) a the conclusions derived from animal experiments may not be strictly applicable to human
eings.
3) Costly.
4) Ethical problems.
