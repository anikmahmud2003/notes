Gonorrhoea

Epidemiology of gonorrhea:
¢ Agent: Neisseria gonorrhoeae.
¢ Reservoir: Reservoir of infection is man who may be symptomatic or asymptomatic.
¢ Portals of exit and entry: Mucous membrane surfaces of the body lined by non-squamous epithelium
(as the organism can only infect columnar and transitional epithelium).

Host: Everyone is susceptible to gonococcal infection. It affects both sexes, males more than females
(the ratio being 7:4).

e Mode of transmission:
- Sexual activity.
- From infected mother to her new born.
* Incubation period: Usually 2-7 days, may be longer.

[Ref- Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 5" / 283]
Diagnosis of gonorrhea:
1) History of sexual exposure.
2) Laboratory investigations:
> Gram negative intracellular diplococci may be seen in stained smear of pus from infected tracts.
> Culture of exudate on selective medium. Women should have endocervical and rectal cultures
taken, and homosexual men require urethral and rectal cultures.
> Pharyngeal cultures should be done in all individuals who have oral sex.
