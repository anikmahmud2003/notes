Q. For the street beggars with disability which type of health care is suitable? [CU-17N,16N]
Q. Write short note on: Rehabilitation. [SUST-18N,17N,1 Ju, 10J]

Answer
Rehabilitation: Rehabilitation is the combined and coordinated use of medical, social, educational and

vocational measures for training and retraining the individual to the highest possible level of functional ability.

Types of rehabilitation:

1) Medical rehabilitation: Restoration of function.

2) Vocational rehabilitation: Restoration of the capacity to earn a livelihood,
3) Social rehabilitation: Restoration of family and social relationships.

4) Psychological rehabilitation: Restoration of personal dignity and confidence.

Examples of rehabilitation:

> Establishing schools for the blind.
> Provision of aids for the crippled.
> Re-constructive surgery in leprosy etc.

Community diagnosis

Q. Define community diagnosis. [CU-11J,10J]

Q. Explain community diagnosis. [DU-18M,16M]

Q. Discuss community diagnosis with example. [SUST-10Ju/J, RU-16J,11Ju]

Q. Write the importance of community diagnosis in the prevention of diseases. [CU-12J]

Answer

Community diagnosis:

It may be defined as the pattern of disease in a community described in terms of the important factors
which influence this pattern.
[Ref- Park / 24" /53]

Or,
Community diagnosis generally refers to the identification and quantification of health problems ina

community in terms of mortality and morbidity rates and ratios and identification of their correlates for the

purpose of defining those individuals or groups at risk or those in need of health care.
[Ref- Park / 24" /96]

Data for community diagnosis:
1) Age and sex distribution of the population; the distribution of population by social groups.

2) Vital statistical rates such as the birth rates, death rates.

3) Incidence and prevalence of important diseases in the community.
[Ref- Park / 24/54]

Importance of community diagnosis in the prevention of diseases:
1) By quantification of health problems, we lay down priorities in disease control and prevention.
2) Quantification of morbidity and mortality can serve as a benchmark for the evaluation of health services

at a later date.
3) Quantification of health problems can be a source of new knowledge about disease distribution,

causation and prevention.
[Ref- Park / 24" / 96]
