Q. Define ecology. [RU-16N, CU-12J]
Answer
Ecology:

Ecology is defined as “‘the science of mutual relationship between living organisms and their
environments.”

; Human ecology is a subject of more general science of ecology.

A full understanding of health requires that humanity be seen as part of an ecosystem. The human
ecosystem includes in addition to the natural environment all the dimensions of the man-made environment -
physical, chemical, biological, psychological: in short, our culture and all its products. Disease is embedded in
the ecosystem of man. Health according to ecological concepts is visualized as a state of dynamic equilibrium

between man and his environment.
[Ref- Park / 24/21]

Q. What is the main distinction between epidemiology and ecology?
Answer
The main distinction between epidemiology and ecology:

Epidemiology is the study of the relationship between variations in man’s environment and his state of
health (or disease), while ecology embraces the interrelationship of all living things. In this regards,
epidemiology constitutes a special application of human ecology or that part of ecology relating to the state of
human health.

Community diagnosis:

It may be defined as the pattern of disease in a community described in terms of the important factors

Community diagnosis generally refers to the identification and quantification of health problems ina

purpose of defining those individuals or groups at risk or those in need of health care.
[Ref- Park / 24" /96]

Data for community diagnosis:
1) Age and sex distribution of the population; the distribution of population by social groups.

2) Vital statistical rates such as the birth rates, death rates.

Importance of community diagnosis in the prevention of diseases:
1) By quantification of health problems, we lay down priorities in disease control and prevention.
2) Quantification of morbidity and mortality can serve as a benchmark for the evaluation of health services

at a later date.
3) Quantification of health problems can be a source of new knowledge about disease distribution,

causation and prevention.
