**Screening:** ;
Disease screening refers to the “identification of hidden or unknown diseases or defects in a large group
of apparently healthy individuals in the community by means of rapidly applied tests, examinations or other
procedures”.
[Ref- Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 5® / 23]
Example:
1) Pap smear of cervical cancer.
2) Serological blood test for syphilis.
3) Chest X-ray for pulmonary tuberculosis.
4) Test for BP blood sugar, blood cholesterol.
Aims and objective of screening:
1) To sort out from large group of apparently healthy person those likely to have a disease or at
increased risk of the disease under study.
2) To bring those who are apparently abnormal under medical supervision & treatment.
3) To alter the natural history of disease in a significant proportion of those who are identified as
positive by earlier diagnosis & subsequent treatment.

  - Abnormal: treatment

Uses of screening:
1) Case detection
2) Control of disease
3) Research purposes
4) Education opportunities
1) Mass screening: It implies screening of whole population or a subgroup (e.g. serological test to
know malaria infection amongst the population of an area). ; ; ;
2) High risk or selective screening: Screening in high risk groups e.g. cervix cancer in lower social
group and diabetes, hypertension, breast cancer in higher social group.
3) Multiphase screening: Application of two or more screening tests.
