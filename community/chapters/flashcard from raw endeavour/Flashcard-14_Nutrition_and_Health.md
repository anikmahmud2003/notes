Malnutrition predisposes to infection & infection to malnutrition:
Infectious diseases are an important conditioning factor responsible for malnutrition, particularly in
small children. Diarrhoea, intestinal parasites, measles, whooping cough, malaria, tuberculosis all contribute to
malnutrition. In fact it is a vicious circle- infection contributing to malnutrition, and malnutrition causing an
otherwise minor childhood ailments to become killers. It has been shown that where environmental conditions
are poor, small children may suffer from some infection or the other for almost half of their first three years of
life. The interrelationship between malnutrition and infection has been well documented.

<summary><b>Q. What are the advantages and disadvantages of Gomez’s classification of PEM? [DU-09J]</b></summary>

**Advantages of Gomez’s classification:**
1) It is based on weight retardation.
2) It locates the child on the basis of his or her weight in comparison with a normal child of the same
age.
3) Weight is widely recorded and the classification is easy to compute.
4) This classification has a prognostic value for hospitalized children.
Disadvantages of Gomez’s classification:
1) A cut-off point of 90% of reference is high (80% being approximately equivalent to -2SD or the 3"
percentile). Thus some normal children may be classified as 1* degree malnourished.
2) By measuring only weight for age, it is difficult to know if the low weight is due to a sudden acute
episode of malnutrition or to long-standing chronic undernutrition.
