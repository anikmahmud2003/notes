<summary><b>Q. What is communicable disease?</b></summary>

**Communicable disease:**
An illness due to specific infectious agent or it’s toxic products capable of being directly or indirectly
transmitted from man to man, animal to animal or from the environment (through air, dust, soil, water, food
etc.) to man or animal.
[Ref- Park / 24" / 98}
Component:
  - Agent: The seed.
  - Host: The soil.
  - Route of transmission: The climate.

<summary><b>Q. State the cardinal characteristics of infectious diseases.</b></summary>

**Cardinal characteristics of infectious diseases:** For an infectious disease to occur, the following conditions or
events must be take place —
1) There must be an aetiologic agent, such as parasite, bacteria, virus, fungus, rickettsia, etc. that
produces disease,
2) There must be a reservoir (human, animal or others) where the biologic agent can propagate (ie.
live, multiply and die in the natural state).
3) There must be a portal of exit from a reservoir and a portal of entry into susceptible host in
order for a disease to be caused elsewhere.
4) An organism (etiologic agent) must be transmitted, either directly or indirectly, from one place
to another.
5) There must be a susceptible host.
[Ref- Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 5" /89]

- Direct transmission:
1) Direct contact: Infection may be transmitted by direct contract from skin to skin, mucosa
to mucosa or mucosa to skin of the same or another person.
Example: STD and AIDS, leprosy, skin & eye infection.
2) Droplet infection: This is the direct projection of a spray of droplets of saliva and naso-
pharyngeal secretions during coughing, scenting or speaking and spitting. The sizes of the
es are less than S mm and spread is limited to a distance of 30-60 cm between source and
ost.
Example: Common cold, diphtheria, whooping cough.
3) Contact With soil: The disease agent may be acquired by direct exposure to the disease agent
in soil, compost or clearing vegetable matter.
Example: Hookworm, larval, tetanus, mycosis etc.
4) Inoculation into skin or mucosa: The disease agent may be included directly into the skin or
mucosa.

A major aspect of communicable disease control relates to “breaking the chain of transmission” or
interruption of transmission. This may mean changing some components of man’s environment to prevent the

1) Water treatment / water purification: To prevent water borne diseases e.g. typhoid, dysentery,
hepatitis A & E, cholera, gastroenteritis etc.
2) Control of source of infection: Clean practices such as hand washing, adequate cooking, prompt
refrigeration of prepared foods and withdrawal of contaminated foods.
3) Control of the vector: Vector borne diseases can be prevented by control of vectors and their
breeding places.

<summary><b>Q. Define quarantine. [SUST-16N]<br>Q. Write short note on: Quarantine. [SUST-18M]</b></summary>

**Quarantine:**
It has been defined as “the limitation of freedom of movement of such well persons or domestic animals
exposed to communicable disease for a period of time not longer than the longest usual incubation period of the
disease, in such manner as to Prevent effective contact with those not so exposed.”
[Ref- Park / 24" / 129]
Types of quarantine:
1) Absolute quarantine.
2) Modified quarantine: e.g. a selective partial limitation of the freedom of movement, such as
exclusion of children from school.
3) Segregation: e.g. removal of susceptible children to homes of immune persons.
[Ref- Park / 24" / 129]

<summary><b>Q. Discuss ‘emerging & re-emerging diseases’ with example. [SUST-16M]<br>Q. Write short note on: Emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases.</b></summary>

**Emerging diseases:**
Emerging infectious diseases are those whose incidence in humans has increased during the last two
decades or which threaten to increase in the near future. The term also refers to newly appearing infectious
diseases or diseases that are spreading to new geographical areas such as cholera in South America and yellow
fever in Kenya. The most dramatic example of new disease is AIDS.
Re-emerging diseases:
The term re-emerging diseases refers to the diseases which were previously easily controlled by
chemotherapy and antibiotics, but now they have developed antimicrobial resistance and are often appearing in
epidemic form.
The factors responsible for emergence and re-emergence of infectious diseases:
1) Unplanned and under-planned urbanization.
2) Overcrowding and rapid population growth.
3) Poor sanitation.
4) Inadequate public health infrastructure.
5) Resistance to antibiotics. ' ‘ ere
6) Increased exposure of humans to disease vectors and reservoirs of infection in nature.
7) Rapid and intense international travel. .

(and occasionally C. ulcerans) and characterized in its typical form by the presence of membrane
(pseudomembrane) in the throat and generalized toxaemia.

the other sites such as eyes (conjunctiva), nose or vagina.

often chronic.

<summary><b>Q. Write short note on: Shick test.</b></summary>

Shick test; Intradermal test which test two things —
- To know the immune status of the person against diphtheria,
- To sce the state of hypersensitivity to diphtheria toxin.
Procedure:
___ 0.2 ml of Shick test toxin is injected into the skin of forearm intradermally. Same amount of toxin
inactivated by heat (at 60°C for 15 minutes) is injected as a control into opposite arm.
Reaction: The following reaction may be observed —
1) Positive reaction: A circumscribed area of redness upto 3 cm or more in diameter appears at the
injection area of the test arm within 48 hours. Some swelling and tenderness may be noted. The
injection area of the control arm shows no changes. The positive test indicates that the individual is
susceptible to diphtheria,
2) Negative reaction: Both arms shows no reaction at all. The negative test indicates that the person
is immune to diphtheria.
3) Pseudo-positive Reaction: Here a red flush appears (much less circumscribed) on both the arms
within 6 to 18 hours which fades away completely within 3 to 4 days. The reaction is due to
hypersensitivity to protein and should be interpreted as Schick negative.
4) Combined Reaction: In this case red areas appear on both arms, but reaction at the injection site of
the test arm may persist for 5 to 7 days or more whereas reaction at the control arm disappears on
days 3 or 4 as this is due to allergy. It is a positive reaction which indicates that the person is
susceptible.

**Drug resistance:**
_Drug resistance means that certain strains of tuberculosis bacilli are not killed by the antituberculosis
drugs given during the treatment.
Causes of drug resistance:
Drug resistance is caused by inadequate treatment'and poor tuberculosis control programmes. The most
common reasons for the development of resistance are:
  - Incorrect prescription.
  - Irregular supply of drugs.
  - Non-compliance of treatment.
  - Lack of supervision and follow-up.
Types of drug resistance: The resistance may be of two types —
1) Primary or pre-treatment resistance: It is the resistance shown by the bacteria in a patient, who
has not received the drug in question before.
2) Secondary or acquired resistance: Here the bacteria were sensitive to the drug at the start of the
treatment but became resistant to the particular drug during the course of treatment with it.
[Ref- Park / 24" / 189, 211]
Prevention of drug Resistance:
1) Treatment with two or more drugs in combination.
2) Using drugs to which the bacteria are sensitive
3) Ensuring that the treatment is complete, adequate and regular.

<summary><b>Q. Describe the mechanism of OPV.<br>Q. Write short note on: OPV.</b></summary>

**Mechanism of OPV:** J
On administration, the live vaccine strains infect intestinal epithelial cells. After replication, the virus is
transported to the Payer’s patches where a secondary multiplication with subsequent viraemia occurs. The virus
spreads to other areas of the body resulting in the production of circulating antibodies which prevent
dissemination of the virus to the nervous system and prevent paralytic polio.
Intestinal infection stimulates the production of IgA secretory antibodies which prevent subsequent
infection of the alimentary tract with wild strains of polio virus, and thus is effective in limiting virus
transmission in the community. Thus OPV induces both local and systemic immunity.
The vaccine progeny is excreted in the faeces and secondary spread occurs to household contacts and
susceptible contacts in the community. Non-immunized persons may therefore, be immunized. Thus widespread
herd immunity” results, even if only approximately 66% of the community is immunized.
Advantages of OPV:
1) Since given orally, it is easy ‘o administer and does not require the use of highly trained personnel
2) Induces both humeral and intestinal immunity (PV induces only humoral immunity but not local or
intestinal immunity).
3) Antibody is quickly produced, even a single dose elicits substantial immunity.
4) The vaccine excretes the virus and so infects others who are also immunized thereby (herd
immunity).
5) Useful in controlling epidemics.
6) Relatively inexpensive.
Complication of OPV: OPV is remarkably free from complications. However, being living viruses, the
vaccine viruses, particularly type-3 do mutate in the course of their multiplication in vaccinated children, and
Tare cases of vaccine-associated paralytic polio have occurred in recipients of the vaccine, & their contacts,
Contraindications of OPV:
1) Acute infectious diseases.
2) Fever.

4) Patients suffering from leukaemias and malignancy.

**Role of ICDDR,B in the control of diarrhocal disease:**
Bangladesh is the host country for the international center for diarrhoea disease research, Bangladesh
(ICDDR,B) it was formerly known as cholera research laboratory and was established in 1960.The cholera
research laboratory was transformed into ICDDR,B in 1978 she current mandate of the ICDDR,B is to
undertake and promote research on diarrhoea disease and the related subjects of nutrition and fertility with the
aim to prevent and control diarrhoea diseases and improving health care. The major achievements of the center
are briefly described below.
1. Development of ‘Dhaka solution’: Dhaka solution containing physiologically balanced
electrolytes to replace the loss of water and salts caused by cholera. This single solution is now
used to rehydrate patients with severe dehydration due to any diarrhoea and is made by a
Bangladesh government plant as cholera saline.
2. Development of oral rehydration therapy (ORT): Since intravenous solution is expensive,
attention was tumed to find less expensive ways to treat dehydration due to diarrhoea. Researchers
in Bangladesh and in India found that if glucose is added to a fluid of similar composition to the
Dhaka solution, absorption of water and salts is resumed during diarrhoea and rehydration by oral
route becomes possible. Since then glucose based oral rehydration salts (ORS) was tried world over
for ORT and was shown to be effective for prevention and correction of dehydration due to
diarrhoea caused by any enteropathogen. It is now the mainstay of the global programme of WHO
for control of diarrhoeal disease.
3. Development and evaluation of cholera vaccine: A series of vaccine trials had been conducted in
the Matlab field station of the center and the classical bacterial cholera vaccine was found to be
useless for all practical purposes as it conferred limited and short -lived immunity. Largely due to
the results of these trials. It was decided to withdraw the requirement of the cholera vaccine
certificate for international travel. However, search for effective cholera vaccine is still on.
4. Development of improved ORS: Though glucose based ORS is effective for rehydration, cheaper
and better substitutes for glucose were searched for. Sucrose (table sugar) was found to be equally
effective. '
5. Rice -ORS: Molla and his colleagues at ICDDR B. found that when cereal starch was substituted
for glucose in ORS, the ORS could reduce the stool volume and the duration of diarrhoea
significantly. Now cereal based ORS (rice - ORS) is routinely used in the ICDDR,B treatment
centers for diarrhoea patients.
6. Dissemination of knowledge on diarrhoea research: This is done through ICDDR,B’s
publication and library services.
