Health education:

The definition adapted by the national conference on preventive medicine in USA, is ‘health education

Changing concept of health education: Historically health education has been committed to disseminating
information and changing human behaviour. Following Alma -At declaration the changing concepts are —

- Prevention of disease and promotion of healthy lifestyles.

- The modification of individual behaviour to modification of "social environment" in which the
individual lives.

- Community participation and community involvement.

- Promotion of individual and community "self-reliance".

Erinciples of health education: It is possible to abstract certain principles of learning & use them in health

education.

1) Credibility: It is the degree to which the massage to be communicated is perceived as trustworthy

by the receiver.

2) Interest: It is a psychological principle that people are unlikely to listen to those things, which are
not to their interest. It is salutary to remind ourselves that health teaching should relate to the
interests of the people.

Participation: It is a key word in health education. Participation is based on the psychological

principle of active learning, which is better than passive learning.

Motivation: In every person, there is a fundamental desire to learn. Awakening this desire is called

- motivation. There are two types of motives - primary & secondary.

5) Comprehension: In health education, we must know the level of understanding education and

6) Reinforcement: Few people can learn all that is new in a single contact. Repetition at intervals is

campaign needs reinforcement.

7) Learning by doing: Learning is an action process; not a memorizing one in the narrow sense.

8) Known to unknown: In health education work, we proceed from the known to the unknown i.e.

9) Setting an example: The health educator should set a good example in the things he is teaching.

10) Good human relations: Good human relations are of utmost importance in learning. Studies have

his technical qualifications.

the elements of the system (e.g. massage, channels) in the light of feedback from his audience.

respect and regard. In the work of health education, we try to penetrate the community through the
local leaders - the village headman, the school- teacher or the political worker.

Motivation:
Motivation is an intra-psychic instinct, desire or force of attitude or behavioural change. In every
person, there is a fundamental desire to learn. Awakening this desire is called motivation.

According to William Scot, Motivation means ‘a process of stimulating people to action to accomplish
desired goals’.

It involves a chain reaction as illustrated below:
Felt needs +> Wants or goals > Tension — Satisfaction of wants — Actions for achieving goals.

Motivation of people results in:
a. Improved commitment

b. Improved participation &
c. Improved performance.

There are two types of motives:
1. Primary motives: (e.g. sex, hunger, survival) are driving forces initiating people into action; these
motives are inborn desires.
2. Secondary motives: (e.g. praise, love rivalry, rewards and punishment, recognition) are based on
desires created by outside forces or incentives.

; In health education, motivation is an important factor i.e. the need for incentives is a first step in
learning to change. The incentives may be positive or negative.

Example:

; To tell a lady, faced with the problem of overweight, to reduce her weight because she might develop
Cvs disease or it might reduce her life span, may have little effect, but to tell her that by reducing her weight
she might look more charming and beautiful, she might accept health advice.

Q. What are compliance behaviour and avoidance behaviour?

Behaviour:
Human behaviour is the result of physical and mental factors (body & mind) interacting in complicated
ways. Behaviour is “the total reactions (of an individual) accessible to external observation.

[Ref- Park / 24" / 710]
Or,
The various voluntary movements undertaken by the body are in response to motives and decisions.
They are described as a whole as behaviour, Behavior can refer to every activity of the body as an individual.
Similar activities are thus classified as patterns of behaviour.
[Ref- Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 5" / 470]

Classification of behaviour:
1. Health behaviour:
i) Compliance behaviour: It is the result of motives that move towards a desired action. For
examples, accepting immunization, taking nutritious food, exercising, brushing teeth, bath etc.
ii) Avoidance behaviour: This is the result of motives that move away from disliked or feared
ee For examples, stopping the use of cigarette smoking, avoiding fatty food an alcoholic
rinks.

2. Ilness behavior: It refers to how people react with symptoms. In illness behaviour, people will wait to
see whether the symptoms persist or worsen after detecting a symptom and if the symptom continue the
affected person will seek help before seeking medical help.

3. Treatment behavior: It refers to those activities used to cure diseases and restore health.

[Ref- Park /24"/711 + Rashid, Khabir, Hyder / 5"/ 471]

Behavior as cause of disease:

Personal habits related to life style, which are strongly connected with certain diseases such as smoking with
