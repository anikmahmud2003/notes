<summary><b>Q1. What do you mean by psychosocial environment? (S10,Q1)</b></summary>

> 🔴 **Psychosocial environment** (social environment) — one of the three components of the external environment (with physical & biological). It includes the man-made surroundings & **way of living** of people in the community.

**Components:**
- **Customs** (e.g. purdah, social taboos, food habits) and **culture**.
- **Habits** — smoking, alcohol, drug abuse, life-style.
- **Income / socio-economic status** (poverty).
- **Occupation** & working conditions.
- **Religion, beliefs & practices.**
- Education, social organization; factors like **overcrowding, illiteracy, ignorance** and poverty — all influence health (e.g. disease-cantered customs, food taboos predispose to malnutrition, poverty → disease).

> 🎯 Psychosocial environment = **customs, culture, habits, income, occupation, religion — "man's way of living".**

<summary><b>Q2. Define environment with its classification. (S12,Q1)</b></summary>

### Definition
> 🔴 **Environment** = all the **external factors — living & non-living, material & non-material — which surround man.**

### Classification
**1) Internal environment** — relates to the tissues, organs & organ systems and their harmonious functioning.

**2) External / macro environment** — all that is external to the individual human host (air, water, food, housing), with which he is in constant interaction.

For descriptive purpose, the external environment is divided into **three closely-related components:**
| Component | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | Water, air, soil, housing, wastes, radiation |
| **Biological** | Plant & animal life — bacteria, viruses, rodents, insects, animals |
| **Social / psychosocial** | Customs, culture, habits, income, occupation, religion |

> 🎯 Environment = **all external factors around man** · 3 components = **Physical + Biological + Social (psychosocial).**

<summary><b>Q3. Mention the characteristics of safe and wholesome water. What are the common waterborne diseases in Bangladesh? (S1,Q3)</b></summary>

### Characteristics of safe and wholesome water
> 🔴 **Safe & wholesome water** is water that is:
1. **Free from pathogenic agents.**
2. **Free from harmful chemical substances.**
3. **Pleasant to taste** — free from colour and odour.
4. **Usable for domestic purposes.**

### Common waterborne diseases in Bangladesh
**Biological (infective agent in water):**
1. **Viral:** viral hepatitis **A & E**, **poliomyelitis**, rotavirus diarrhoea.
2. **Bacterial:** **cholera**, typhoid & paratyphoid, bacillary dysentery, *E. coli* diarrhoea.
3. **Protozoal:** **amoebiasis**, giardiasis.
4. **Helminthic:** roundworm, threadworm, hydatid disease.
5. **Leptospiral:** Weil's disease.

**Aquatic intermediate host:** guinea worm (cyclops), schistosomiasis (snail).

**Chemical hazards:** arsenic pollution, excess fluoride (fluorosis), nitrates, lead (plumbism).

> 🎯 Safe water = **no pathogens, no harmful chemicals, good taste** · WB diseases = **cholera + typhoid + Hep-A/E + polio + amoebiasis + rotavirus.**

<summary><b>Q4. Mention the uses and daily requirements of water. What are the sources of water? Tell the major differences between the deep and shallow well. (S2,Q3)</b></summary>

### Uses of water
1. Essential for body functions (physiological).
2. Domestic use — drinking, cooking, washing, bathing, flushing, gardening.
3. Public purposes — cleaning streets, swimming pools, fire fighting.
4. Industrial purposes — processing & cooling.
5. Agricultural purposes — irrigation.
6. Power production — hydro/steam power.
7. Waste removal.

### Daily requirement of water
- **Basic physiological (drinking) requirement: ~2 litres/head/day.**
- **150–200 litres/head/day** is considered adequate (more in hospitals); depends on climate, standard of living & habits.

### Sources of water
| Source | Examples |
|---|---|
| **1. Rain** | — |
| **2. Surface water** | Impounding reservoirs, rivers & streams, tanks/ponds/lakes |
| **3. Ground water** | **Shallow wells, deep wells**, springs |

### Difference between shallow and deep well
| Trait | Shallow well | Deep well |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Taps subsoil water **above the first impervious layer** | Taps water from the water-bearing stratum **below the first impervious layer** |
| Hardness | Moderately hard | Much harder |
| **Bacteriological quality** | Often **grossly contaminated** | **Pure/safe** water |
| Yield | Usually goes dry in summer | Constant supply, machine-dug (several hundred feet) |

> 🎯 Water need = **2 L (drink) / 150–200 L (adequate)** · Sources = **rain, surface, ground** · Shallow well = above 1st impervious layer · Deep well = below it (safest).

<summary><b>Q5. When it may be shallow and deep well? Explain contamination and pollution of water. (S3,Q3)</b></summary>

### When a well is shallow / deep
- **Shallow well** — taps **subsoil water** (water above the **first impervious layer** in the ground); yields limited water, notoriously liable to pollution unless well-constructed.
- **Deep well** — taps water from the stratum **below the first impervious layer**; machine-dug, often several hundred metres deep; furnishes the **safest water**.

### Contamination vs Pollution of water
| Pollution | Contamination |
|---|---|
| Impurity (foreign material) in water — **turbidity** present | Presence of **pathogenic organisms** in water |
| **Turbidity +**; NO special concern about pathogens | **Turbidity ±** (may be clear) |
| Pathogens **doubtful** (but danger of disease) | **Pathogens definite** (disease certain) |
| Assessed by **suspended solids & BOD** | Assessed by **microscopy/culture** |

> 🎯 Shallow = subsoil (above 1st layer, polluted) · Deep = below 1st layer (safe) · **Pollution = turbidity/BOD · Contamination = definite pathogens.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 491, 494

<summary><b>Q6. Mention the sources of water pollution. List the common water borne diseases in Bangladesh. (S4,Q3)</b></summary>

### Sources (causes) of water pollution
**A. Natural (impurities):**
1. **Dissolved impurities** — gases (carbonic acid, H₂S), salts (chlorides, Ca/Mg sulphates), **metals (arsenic, iron, lead)**, organic matter.
2. **Suspended impurities** — inorganic (sand, silt, mud, clay), organic (vegetable & animal matter), bacteria, ova of worms.

**B. Man-made:**
1. **Sewage** — decomposable organic matter & pathogenic agents.
2. **Industrial & trade wastes** — toxic agents (salts to complex organic chemicals).
3. **Agricultural pollutants** — fertilizers & pesticides.
4. **Physical pollutants** — heat (thermal pollution), radioactive substances.

### Common waterborne diseases in Bangladesh
**Cholera, typhoid/paratyphoid, bacillary dysentery, acute watery diarrhoea (rotavirus, E. coli), viral hepatitis A & E, polio, amoebiasis, giardiasis** + **arsenicosis** (chemical hazard of groundwater).

> 🎯 Pollution sources = **sewage + industrial wastes + agricultural pollutants + natural impurities** · Diseases = **cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A/E, polio, amoebiasis.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 493–494

<summary><b>Q7. List the water related diseases in Bangladesh. What are the methods of supplying safe drinking water in a community? (S5,Q3)</b></summary>

### Water-related diseases in Bangladesh
- **Water-borne (infective):** cholera, typhoid/paratyphoid, infectious hepatitis (A & E), polio, bacillary dysentery, rotavirus diarrhoea, amoebiasis, giardiasis, leptospirosis.
- **Water-washed:** trachoma, scabies, skin & eye infections (by scarcity of water for hygiene).
- **Water-based:** helminthic infections (ascariasis, threadworm); schistosomiasis.
- **Aquatic insect–related:** **malaria, filariasis, dengue (vector-borne)**.
- **Chemical/geogenic:** **arsenicosis**, fluorosis, goitre, plumbism, methaemoglobinaemia.

### Methods of supplying safe drinking water in a community
1. **Protection of source** — deep/sanitary tube wells (best for rural areas unless flooded), protected springs, sanitary wells.
2. **Large-scale water purification:** storage (sedimentation) → **filtration (slow sand or rapid sand filter)** → **disinfection (chlorination, ozonation, UV)**.
3. **Chlorination** — bleaching powder/chlorine solution/HTH; free residual chlorine 0.5 mg/L for 1 hour.
4. **Distribution system** — piped water supply to households with surveillance of quality.
5. For small communities — household purification (boiling, chemicals, filters) & **hygiene education**.

> 🎯 Safe supply = **protected sources + storage/filtration + chlorination + piped distribution + surveillance.**

<summary><b>Q8. What are the small scale water purification processes? Explain breakpoint chlorination. (S6,Q3)</b></summary>

### Small-scale (household) water purification
1. **Boiling** — "rolling boil" for **5–10 minutes** kills bacteria, spores, cysts & ova.
2. **Chemical disinfection:**
   - **Bleaching powder (CaOCl₂)** — 30 gm/100 gallons; stock solution 1 tsp/pint → 1 ml sterilizes 10 gallons in 30 min.
   - **KMnO₄** — 0.5 part/100,000 parts, sterilizes in 4–6 hours.
   - **Alum** — 2–5 gm/gallon (clears muddy water); **copper sulphate** — 0.1–0.25 ppm (algae).
   - **Calcium oxide**, **chlorine (halozone) tablets** — 1 tablet (0.5 gm)/20 litres, **iodine** 2 ppm, **bromine**, **Nesfield tablets**, HTH.
3. **Filtration (domestic filters):** Pasteur–Chamberland, **Berkefeld**, Katadyn filters.
4. **Well disinfection** (small community) — bleaching powder (see Q13).

### Breakpoint chlorination
> 🔴 When chlorine is added to water, free residual chlorine first increases, then **falls to zero** as chlorine combines with ammonia/ammoniacal nitrogen (forming chloramines). The point at which **all combined chlorine has been destroyed and free residual chlorine begins to appear proportionately** to further doses is the **breakpoint**; the corresponding dose = **breakpoint dosage**.

- **Significance:** dosing beyond the breakpoint ensures **free residual chlorine** (0.5 mg/L) — proof of adequate disinfection.

> 🎯 Small-scale = **boiling + chlorine compounds/tablets + domestic filters** · Break point = **residual chlorine restarts rising after destroying chloramines.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 501–502

<summary><b>Q9. Enumerate the difference between slow and rapid sand filtration methods of water purification. What is super chlorination? (S7,Q3)</b></summary>

### Slow vs Rapid sand filtration
| Trait | Rapid sand filter | Slow sand filter |
|---|---|---|
| Space | Very little | Large area |
| Rate of filtration | 2–3 m³/hour (15× higher) | Slow |
| Effective size of sand | Larger | 0.2–0.3 mm |
| Preliminary treatment | **Chemical coagulation + sedimentation** | Not required |
| Cleaning | **By back-washing** | By scraping the top layer |
| Operation skill | Highly skilled | Less skilled |
| Removal of bacteria | 98–99% | **99.9–99.9%** |
| Removal of colour/turbidity | Good | Good |
| Cost | Higher | Cheaper |

### Super chlorination
> 🔴 **Super chlorination** = addition of **large doses of chlorine** (followed by **dechlorination**) after disinfection. Applicable to **heavily-polluted water whose quality fluctuates greatly** (e.g. flood water, emergency supplies).

> 🎯 Slow sand = **large area, cheaper, vital layer, 99.9% germ removal** · Rapid sand = **coagulation-first, back-washing, less space, highly skilled.**

<summary><b>Q10. Mention the characteristics of ideal disinfectants of water. What do you mean by Chlorine demand? (S8,Q3)</b></summary>

### Characteristics of an ideal water disinfectant
1. **Cheap and easily available.**
2. **Soluble/miscible in water.**
3. **Rapidly lethal** to bacteria, cysts, ova and viruses even in **small concentrations**.
4. **Not corrosive** (to pipes & storage) and **not irritating** to humans.
5. **Does not impart taste, colour or odour** to water.
6. **Stable** — retains disinfecting power for long.
7. **Leaves a residual concentration** (measurable, e.g. free residual chlorine) to guard against re-contamination.
8. **Safe** — no cumulative harmful effect on health.

### Chlorine demand
> 🔴 **Chlorine demand** = difference between the **amount of chlorine added** to water and the **residual chlorine** remaining after the contact period.
**Chlorine demand = Total chlorine added − Chlorine residual.**
- The dose actually applied = **chlorine demand + free residual chlorine (0.5 mg/L for 1 hour)**.
- Purity of water monitored by chlorine demand → low demand means clean water; **zero residual throughout = all chlorine consumed by organic matter** (inadequate disinfection).

> 🎯 Ideal disinfectant = **cheap, soluble, rapid germicidal, no taste/odour, stable, leaves residual** · Cl-demand = **added − residual chlorine.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 501–503

<summary><b>Q11. How can you disinfect drinking water in small scale? Tell the formation and roles of vital layer in water purification. (S9,Q3)</b></summary>

### Disinfection of drinking water in small scale
1. **Boiling** for 5–10 minutes.
2. **Bleaching powder** — 30 gm/100 gallons; stock solution (1 tsp in 1 pint) — 1 ml sterilizes 10 gallons in half-an-hour.
3. **Chlorine tablets** (Halozone) — 0.5 gm tablet for 20 L water.
4. **KMnO₄** — 0.5 part/100,000 parts (sterilizes in 4–6 hrs); **alum** for muddy water; **CuSO₄**; **calcium oxide**; **iodine** 2 ppm; **Nesfield tablets**; **HTH**.
5. **Domestic filters:** Pasteur–Chamberland, Berkefeld, Katadyn.
6. **Well disinfection with bleaching powder** (see Q13).

### Vital layer (biological layer) — formation & roles
> 🔴 The **vital layer** (schmutzdecke/biological/zoogloeal layer) is the **slimy gelatinous film** that forms on the sand bed of a **slow sand filter** soon after it is newly laid.

- **Formation:** newly-laid filter acts as a mechanical strainer; the sand surface soon becomes covered with a slimy growth — this **"ripening of the filter"** takes several days, extending **2–3 cm** into the top sand.
- **Composition:** thread-like **algae, plankton, diatoms and bacteria**.
- **Roles (it is "the heart" of the slow sand filter):**
  1. Removes **organic matter**.
  2. **Holds back bacteria**.
  3. **Oxidizes ammoniacal nitrogen into nitrates**.
  4. Helps yield **bacteria-free water**.

> 🎯 Vital layer = **slimy biological film on slow-sand filter** · Roles = **traps bacteria, oxidises NH₃→nitrates, removes organic matter.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 498–499, 502

<summary><b>Q12. What is superchlorination? Mention its effect on health. (S11,Q3)</b></summary>

### Super chlorination
> 🔴 **Super chlorination** = the addition of **large doses of chlorine** to water (followed by **dechlorination**) after the disinfection has been accomplished. It is applicable to **heavily polluted water whose quality fluctuates greatly** (flood water, emergency/field supplies).

### Effects on health
- The **excess chlorine** (beyond demand) makes water **unpalatable** — objectionable **taste & odour** (chlorinous smell); possible **irritation of the throat/gastro-intestinal upset** in sensitive persons.
- Hence super-chlorinated water is **dechlorinated** (since the free residual chlorine is far above the recommended 0.5 mg/L) **before** it is distributed for drinking — so treated water becomes safe & acceptable.
- Used effectively to control **waterborne epidemics** in heavily-contaminated sources.

> 🎯 Superchlorination = **heavy dose of chlorine + dechlorination**, for heavily-polluted water · health concern = **taste/odour/irritation → must dechlorinate.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 501–502

<summary><b>Q13. What are the recommended criteria of drinking water set by WHO? (S12,Q3)</b></summary>

### WHO criteria/guidelines for drinking water quality
The guidelines relate to **four variables:**
1. **Acceptability aspects** — physical: **free from turbidity, colour, taste & odour**, pleasant temperature.
2. **Microbiological aspects** — primary indicator: **coliform organism**; supplementary: faecal streptococci, sulphite-reducing clostridia; guideline: **must not be detectable in any 100 ml sample**.
3. **Chemical aspects**:
   - **Inorganic:** chloride 200 mg/L; **hardness** Ca 100–200 mg/L; H₂S 0.05–0.1 mg/L; iron 0.1 mg/L; sodium 200 mg/L; sulphate 270 mg/L; zinc 4 mg/L; manganese <0.3 mg/L.
   - **Organic constituents** — negligible (no chlorinated hydrocarbons/aromatic hydrocarbons).
   - **Arsenic: 0.01 mg/L (WHO); 0.05 mg/L (Bangladesh).**
4. **Radiological aspects** — gross **α-activity 0.1 Bq/L**, gross **β-activity 1.0 Bq/L**.

> 🎯 WHO guidelines = **acceptability + microbiological (no coliform/100 ml) + chemical (incl. As 0.01 mg/L) + radiological limits.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 503–504

<summary><b>Q14. Mention the microbiological standard of drinking water. (S13,Q3)</b></summary>

### Bacteriological (microbiological) standard of drinking water
> 🔴 **Coliform organism** = the **primary bacteriological indicator** of faecal pollution (supplementary: faecal streptococci, *Cl. perfringens*).

**Standards for pure/acceptable water:**
1. **No sample** should contain **E. coli in 100 ml**.
2. No sample should contain **more than 3 coliform organisms per 100 ml**.
3. **Not more than 5%** of samples throughout the year should contain coliform in 100 ml.
4. **No two consecutive samples** should contain coliform organisms in 100 ml.
5. In distribution systems: **E. coli/thermo-tolerant coliform must not be detectable in any 100 ml** sample.

**Why coliform is chosen as indicator:** constantly present in abundance in human intestine; foreign to potable water; easily detected by culture even 1 bacterium/100 ml; survive longer than pathogens & more resistant to natural purification → their presence suggests probable intestinal pathogens.

> 🎯 Microbiological standard = **no E. coli/100 ml · ≤3 coliforms/100 ml · ≤5% samples ± coliform · no 2 consecutive +ve samples.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 504–505

<summary><b>Q15. What are the steps of well disinfection? (S14,Q3)</b></summary>

### Steps of well disinfection (with bleaching powder)
> The most effective & cheapest method of disinfecting wells is by **bleaching powder**:

1. **Find the volume of water in the well:**
   - Measure depth of water column (h) & diameter (d) in metres (average of several readings).
   - Volume in litres = (3.14 × d² × h / 4) × 1000.
2. **Find the amount of bleaching powder required:**
   - Estimate **chlorine demand** (Horrock's apparatus); roughly **2.5 gm of good quality bleaching powder per 100 litres** (≈0.7 mg applied chlorine/L).
3. **Dissolve bleaching powder in water:** put in a bucket (≤100 gm/bucket), make a thin paste, add water to ¾ fill, stir, allow lime to settle 5–10 min, decant the **supernatant chlorine solution** (discard the chalk/lime).
4. **Deliver the chlorine solution into the well:** lower the bucket below the water surface and **agitate** (move the bucket violently vertically & laterally several times) so the chlorine mixes intimately.
5. **Contact period: allow 1 hour** before water is drawn for use.
6. Check residual chlorine (e.g. **orthotolidine/DPD test**) after 1 hour.

> 🎯 Well disinfection = **measure volume → estimate bleaching powder → make chlorine solution → mix in well → wait 1 hour.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 492, 502

<summary><b>Q18. Define and classify the hardness of water. List the water borne diseases in Bangladesh. (S26,Q3)</b></summary>

### Hardness of water — definition & classification
> 🔴 **Hardness of water** = the **soap-destroying power** of water (due to Ca & Mg salts).

**Two types:**
1. **Temporary hardness** — due to **bicarbonates of calcium & magnesium** (held in solution by CO₂); removed by **boiling**.
2. **Permanent hardness** — due to **sulphates & chlorides of calcium & magnesium**; removed by **adding sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃)**.

**Degrees:** soft water = hardness producing ions < 1 mEq/L (< 50 mg CaCO₃/L); **very hard water > 6 mEq/L (> 300 mg/L)**. One mEq/L = 50 mg CaCO₃ in 1 L.

### Water-borne diseases in Bangladesh
Cholera, typhoid/paratyphoid, bacillary dysentery, rotavirus diarrhoea, infectious hepatitis A & E, poliomyelitis, amoebiasis, giardiasis (plus arsenic-related illness).

> 🎯 Hardness = **soap-destroying power** · Temporary = **bicarbonates (boiled off)** · Permanent = **sulphates/chlorides (Na₂CO₃).**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 505–506, 493

<summary><b>Q20. What are the advantages and disadvantages of hardness of water? Mention the process of removal of hardness. (S28,Q3)</b></summary>

### Advantages of hard water
1. **Palatable** & pleasant to taste (better than distilled).
2. **Nutritious** — Ca & Mg salts are essential for health (low CHD risk zones often have hard water).
3. **Less corrosive** to pipes (hard water dissolves less lead from lead pipes).
4. Beneficial for crops/agriculture.

### Disadvantages of hard water
1. Consumes **more soap & detergent**; stains clothes & shortens their life.
2. Adversely affects **cooking** (vegetables/tea).
3. Shortens life of industrial machinery (pipes & fixtures) — **scaling**.
4. **Furring/scaling of boilers** on heating → greater fuel consumption, sometimes **boiler explosion**.

### Removal of hardness
| Temporary hardness | Permanent hardness |
|---|---|
| **Boiling** | Addition of **sodium carbonate (Na₂CO₃)** |
| Addition of **lime** | **Base-exchange process** |
| Addition of sodium carbonate | **Permutit (zeolite) process** |

- Ca(HCO₃)₂ →(heat) CaCO₃ + H₂O + CO₂; CaSO₄ + Na₂CO₃ → CaCO₃ + Na₂SO₄.

> 🎯 Hard water pros = **taste + Ca/Mg nutrition + less corrosive** · cons = **more soap, boiler scaling** · removal = **boiling/lime (temp.) + Na₂CO₃/base exchange (perm.)**.

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 505–506
