<summary><b>Q1. How can you prevent and control STDs in a community? (S26,Q2)</b></summary>

> 🔴 **STDs** = a group of communicable diseases transmitted predominantly by **sexual contact**, caused by bacterial (N. gonorrhoeae, T. pallidum, C. trachomatis, H. ducreyi), viral (HIV, HPV, HBV, HSV), protozoal, fungal & ectoparasitic agents.

### Control of STDs in a community
**A) Initial planning:**
1. **Problem definition** — prevalence rate, geographical distribution, population groups at risk.
2. **Establishment of priorities** — size of the problem, consequences, resources, political commitment, victims' age/sex/occupation/residence.
3. **Setting objectives.**
4. **Considering intervention strategies.**

**B) Intervention strategies:**
1. **Case detection:**
   - **Screening** of high-risk groups (prostitutes, truck & bus drivers, army/police/marine, refugees, blood donors, pregnant women, industrial workers).
   - **Contact tracing** — identify, locate, investigate & treat the sexual partners.
   - **Cluster tracing** — test other persons attending the same source.
2. **Case holding & adequate treatment** of patients and contacts.
3. **Epidemiological (presumptive) treatment.**
4. **Personal prophylaxis** — **use of condoms**, washing exposed parts with soap & water, washing after intercourse.
5. **Health education** — motivation of sexual behaviour (avoid multiple partners), aimed at the young & vulnerable groups, couples, community leaders.

**C) Support components:** STD clinics, laboratory services, PHC, information system, **legislation** (mandatory early treatment, contact naming, screening of high-risk groups), social welfare (rehabilitation of prostitutes, recreation, marriage counselling, restriction of pornographic literature).

**D) Prevention of blood-borne STD transmission:** screening of donor blood, exclusion of high-risk donors, strict sterilization, **disposable syringes & needles**.

**E) Monitoring & evaluation** of the programme.

> 🎯 STD control = **health/sex education + condoms + screening & contact tracing + adequate treatment + blood screening + monitoring.**

> 📖 Ch-05, Endeavour PDF pp. 217–219

<summary><b>Q2. Enumerate the risk factors of coronary heart disease. (S17,Q2)</b></summary>

### Risk factors of CHD (Modifiable)
1. **Cigarette smoking.**
2. **High blood pressure (hypertension).**
3. **Elevated serum cholesterol (hyperlipidaemia / raised LDL).**
4. **Diabetes mellitus.**
5. **Obesity** (especially abdominal). *
6. **Sedentary habits / physical inactivity.**
7. **Stress** (personality — type-A behaviour); alcohol dependency.

### Risk factors of CHD (Non-modifiable)
1. **Age** — increases with age.
2. **Sex** — more in males (females after menopause).
3. **Family history** of CHD.
4. **Genetic factors.**

> 🎯 CHD risk factors = **smoking + HTN + hypercholesterolaemia + DM + obesity + inactivity + stress** (modifiable) · **age/sex/family history** (non-modifiable).

> 📖 Ch-06, Endeavour PDF pp. 255

<summary><b>Q3. State the preventive approaches for the rheumatic fever. (S25,Q2)</b></summary>

### Prevention of rheumatic fever / rheumatic heart disease
**1. Primary prevention (prevent first attack — treat streptococcal sore throat):**
- **Improvement of environment, housing & nutritional status** (break the poverty–disease–poverty cycle).
- **Prophylactic antibiotic for sore throat:** **Phenoxymethyl penicillin 250 mg 6-hourly for 10 days** (adequate treatment of every beta-haemolytic streptococcal throat infection).
- Early diagnosis & treatment of streptococcal pharyngitis.

**2. Secondary prevention (prevent recurrences — long-term prophylaxis):**
- **Benzathine penicillin 1.2 million units IM once monthly** (or **6 lakh units if < 30 kg**); alternatively **oral phenoxymethyl penicillin 250 mg 12-hourly**.
- **Duration:**
  - At least **5 years after the last attack or up to 18 years of age** (whichever is longer).
  - If **carditis** present — at least **10 years after the last attack or up to 25 years**, whichever is longer.
  - Severe valvular disease / post-valve surgery → **lifelong** prophylaxis.

**3. Non-medical measures:** improved living conditions & socio-economic status, reduction of overcrowding.

> 🎯 RF prevention = **primary (treat sore throat 10 days) + secondary (monthly benzathine penicillin, 5 yrs/18 yrs, or 10 yrs/25 yrs if carditis).**

> 📖 Ch-06, Endeavour PDF pp. 262–263

<summary><b>Q4. Define obesity. List the health problems with obesity. (S28,Q7)</b></summary>

### Definition of obesity
> 🔴 **Obesity** = abnormal growth of the **adipose tissue** due to enlargement of fat cell size (**hypertrophic obesity**) or increase in fat cell number (**hyperplastic obesity**) or both.
- Operationally: **BMI ≥ 30** (30–39.9) = obese.
- **Types:** generalized obesity, abdominal (central) obesity.

### Health problems (risk) associated with obesity
1. **Type-2 diabetes mellitus.**
2. **Hypertension & coronary heart disease.**
3. **Gall stones** (cholelithiasis) & fatty liver.
4. **Osteoarthritis** (weight-bearing joints).
5. **Respiratory disorders — obstructive sleep apnoea.**
6. **Cancer** (Ca colon, post-menopausal Ca breast).
7. **Menstrual irregularity** (e.g. PCOS → infertility).
8. **Stress incontinence.**
9. Social & psychological problems (stigma, low self-esteem).

> 🎯 Obesity = **BMI ≥ 30 · adipose-tissue growth** · Health problems = **DM, HTN/CHD, gallstones, OA, sleep apnoea, PCOS, cancer.**

> 📖 Ch-06, Endeavour PDF pp. 274
