## Q1. What are the mode of transmission of disease by arthropods? (S7,Q5)

### Transmission of arthropod-borne disease — 3 types
1. **Direct contact** — arthropods directly transferred from man to man through close contact; e.g., **scabies and pediculosis.**
2. **Mechanical transmission** — the disease agent is transmitted **mechanically** by the arthropod (on feet, legs, mouth parts); e.g., **diarrhoea, dysentery, typhoid fever, food poisoning & trachoma by the house fly.**
3. **Biological transmission** — the disease agent **multiplies or undergoes a developmental change (with or without multiplication)** in the arthropod host; e.g., **malaria, plague, filariasis.**

> 🎯 Arthropod transmission = **Direct contact · Mechanical · Biological.**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF pp. 282–283

## Q2. What are the types of biological transmission. Cite an example. (S8,Q7)

### Types of biological transmission
> 🔴 **Biological transmission** = when the disease agent multiplies or undergoes some developmental change, with or without multiplication, in the arthropod host. Three types:

1. **Propagative** — the agent **undergoes no cyclical change but multiplies** in the body of the vector; e.g., **plague bacilli in rat fleas.**
2. **Cyclo-propagative** — agent undergoes **cyclical changes and multiplies** in the arthropod; e.g., **malaria parasite in anopheles mosquito.**
3. **Cyclo-developmental** — agent undergoes **cyclical change but does not multiply**; e.g., **filarial parasite in culex mosquito** and **guinea worm embryo in cyclops.**

> 🎯 Biological = **Propagative (plague) · Cyclo-propagative (malaria) · Cyclo-developmental (filariasis).**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF pp. 282–283

## Q3. Classify flies. What are the diseases transmitted by house fly. (S9,Q7)

### Classification of flies (public health importance)
> 📌 Flies are insects (Class Insecta) of the order **Diptera, family Muscidae.** The following classification (Park) is commonly asked:

**A) Non-biting flies (Cyclorrhapha):**

**B) Biting flies:**
- Stable fly — *Stomoxys calcitrans*.
- (Other biting flies — tsetse fly, black fly.)

### Diseases transmitted by house fly (*Musca domestica*)
| Disease | Causative agent |

| Typhoid & paratyphoid fever | *Salmonella typhi, S. paratyphi* |
| Diarrhoea | *E. coli, Shigella, Rotavirus, Norwalk virus, Enterovirus* |
| Dysentery | *Shigella* |
| Cholera | *V. cholerae* |
| Gastro-enteritis | *E. coli* |
| Amoebiasis | *E. histolytica* |
| Helminthic infestation | Different helminthes |
| Conjunctivitis | *Haemophilus influenzae,* Staphylococci |
| Poliomyelitis | Polio virus type 1, 2, 3 |
| Trachoma | *Chlamydia trachomatis* |
| Anthrax | *B. anthracis* |
| Yaws | *T. pertenue* |

> 🎯 House fly = **"porter of infection" (mechanical) — typhoid, dysentery, cholera, diarrhoea, trachoma, polio.**

## Q5. Mention the life cycle and control measures of sand fly. (S11,Q6)

### Life cycle of sand fly (complete metamorphosis — 4 stages)
1. **Egg** — laid in damp dark places in the vicinity of cattle sheds and poultry.
2. **Larva** — hairy maggots with a distinct head; become pupa in about **2 weeks.**
3. **Pupa** — lasts about **1 week.**
4. **Adult** — average life about **2 weeks.**

> 📌 **Habits:** infest dwellings at night, shelter by day in holes/crevices of walls, dark rooms, stables; **only females bite** (irritating, painful); confined to within **50 yards of breeding places.**

### Control measures of sand fly
1. **Insecticides:** DDT **1–2 g/m²** or lindane **0.25 g/m²** (single application effective; DDT residue lasts 1–2 years, lindane ~3 months). Spray human dwellings, cattle sheds.
2. **Sanitation:** removal of shrubs & vegetation within **50 yards** of dwellings; filling cracks & crevices in walls and floors; keeping cattle sheds & poultry houses **away from human habitations.**

> 🎯 Sand fly control = **insecticides (DDT/lindane) + sanitation (remove vegetation, seal crevices).**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF pp. 291–292

## Q6. Difference between sand fly and mosquito. (S12,Q7)

### Difference between mosquito and sand fly (morphology)
| Trait | Mosquito | Sand fly |
|---|---|---|
| **Size** | Larger | Small (about 1/4 of mosquito, hairy) |
| **Wings** | Dorsally placed; upright & lanceolate compared with body | Small, broad, oval |
| **Head** | Semi-globular; large eyes, proboscis, a pair of palpi & antennae | Large eyes, proboscis, pair of palpi & antennae |
| **Thorax** | Rounded; one pair of wings & 3 pairs of legs | Not rounded; one pair of wings & 3 pairs of legs |
| **Legs** | Long compared to size of body | Short |
| **Body hair** | Not a hairy insect | **Hairy insect** |

> 🎯 Sand fly = **small, hairy, short-legged, mostly wingless resting posture** vs mosquito = **long legs, lanceolate wings, not hairy.**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF p. 291

## Q8. Name the louse borne diseases and their control measures. (S16,Q7)

### Louse — types
1. **Head louse** — *Pediculus capitis*.
2. **Body louse** — *Pediculus corporis*.
3. **Pubic or crab louse** — *Phthirus pubis*.

### Diseases caused by lice (louse-borne diseases)
1. **Epidemic typhus** (Rickettsia prowazekii — body louse).
2. **Relapsing fever** (borrelia — body louse).
3. **Trench fever** (body louse).
4. **Vagabond's disease** / skin pigmentation.
5. **Dermatitis** — due to scratching and secondary infection.

### Control measures of louse infestation
**A) Insecticidal control:**
- Head & crab louse — lotion containing **0.5% malathion**, left on for 12–24 hrs then washed.
- Body louse — powder containing **1% malathion** (treatment of choice).

**B) Personal hygiene:**
- Regular bath with soap & water.
- Regular washing & cleaning of hair.
- Clothing, towels & sheets washed and pressed with **hot iron**.
- **Autoclaving** of clothes and bedding.
- **Health education.**

> 🎯 Louse = 3 types (head/body/crab); diseases = **epidemic typhus, relapsing fever, trench fever**; control = **0.5–1% malathion + personal hygiene.**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF pp. 295–296

## Q9. What is blocked flea. Name the flea indices. Mention flea control measures. (S19,Q6)

### Blocked flea
> 🔴 A flea may ingest up to **0.5 cu mm of blood** containing as many as **5,000 plague bacilli**. The bacilli multiply enormously in the gut of the rat flea and may **block the proventriculus** so that no food can pass through. Such a flea is called a **"blocked flea."**

- A blocked flea faces **starvation & death** (unable to obtain a blood meal) and makes frantic attempts to bite and suck blood over and over again; **each bite regurgitates (inoculates) plague bacilli into the wound** → transmission of plague.
- A **partially blocked flea is more dangerous** than a completely blocked one (lives longer); infected fleas may live up to a year, some species survive up to 4 years in burrow microclimate.

### Flea indices
1. **Specific flea index** — number of a particular flea species (e.g., *X. cheopis*) per rat examined.
2. **Total flea index** — total number of fleas per rat (all species).
3. **Flea rate** — percentage of rats infested with fleas.
4. **Infection (or plague) rate** — percentage of fleas infected with plague bacilli.

### Flea control measures
1. **Insecticides** — cheapest and most widely used: **10% DDT dust.**
2. **Repellents** — diethyltoluamide, benzyl benzoate.
3. **Rodent control** — flea control should be **followed by rodent control.**

> 🎯 Blocked flea = **proventriculus blocked by P. pestis → regurgitates bacilli on biting → plague.** Flea indices = **specific & total flea index + infection rate.** Control = **DDT dust + rodent control.**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF pp. 294–295

## Q10. Name the diseases transmitted by itch mite and treatment of that disease. (S20,Q6)

### Itch mite (*Sarcoptes scabiei* / *Acarus scabiei*)
> The itch mite is an extremely **small, globular arthropod** just visible to the naked eye. The **female parasite burrows into the epidermis** where it breeds and causes **scabies (itch).**

- **Life cycle (4 stages):** Egg → Larva → Nymph → Adult. Egg to adult takes **10–15 days**; adults live 1–2 months.
- **Disease spread:** **Scabies** — a highly contagious infectious skin disease, characterized by papulo-vesicular eruption along burrows containing mites & eggs, with **intense itching more at night.**

### Treatment of scabies (chemical control)
1. **Benzyl benzoate (25%) emulsion** — smeared over the whole body (neck to feet, except face & genitals) for successive days; repeat after 12 hrs, then bath after further 12 hrs.
2. **Permethrin cream (5%).**
3. **Monosulfiram / Tetmosol (5%)** — thrice daily.
4. **Sulphur ointment (2.5–10%)** — daily for 4 days.
5. **Gamma benzene hexachloride (GBH/γ-HCH) 1%.**
6. **Crotamiton (10%)** cream or lotion.

> 🎯 Itch mite → **scabies**; treat = **benzyl benzoate 25% / permethrin 5% / GBH 1%.**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF pp. 297–298

## Q11. Define and classify insecticides. What are the insecticides used in residual and space spray? (S21,Q7)

### Insecticide — definition
> 🔴 **Insecticides** are **substances which are used to kill insects.**

### Classification
**A) According to mode of action:**
1. **Contact poisons** — e.g., Pyrethrum, DDT, HCH, Dieldrin.
2. **Stomach poisons** — e.g., Paris green.
3. **Fumigants** — e.g., Sulphur dioxide.

**B) According to origin and composition:**
1. **Plant derivatives** — e.g., pyrethrin, rotenone.
2. **Pure chemicals** — e.g., oils & oil products, Paris green.
3. **Synthetic chemicals:**
   - Chlorinated hydrocarbons — DDT, dieldrin, HCH.
   - Organophosphates — diazinon, malathion, chlorthion.
   - Synthetic pyrethroids — deltamethrin, permethrin.
4. **Biological compounds** — *Bacillus thuringiensis* serotype H-14, *Bacillus sphaericus*.

### Insecticides used in residual & space spray
- **Residual sprays:** **DDT, Lindane, Malathion, OMAS-33** (adult mosquitoes best controlled by spraying houses).
- **Space sprays (mist/fog):** **Pyrethrum extract, Malathion, Fenitrothion.**

> 🎯 Insecticide = **kill insects; residual: DDT/lindane/malathion · space: pyrethrum/malathion/fenitrothion.**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF pp. 286 (residual/space), 299 (define & classify)

## Q13. Name the control measures of itch mite. (S23,Q7)

### Control measures of scabies (itch mite)
**1) Sanitation control:**
- Improvement of **personal hygiene.**
- **All affected persons of a family treated simultaneously.**
- Affected children **excluded from school** until the day after treatment.
- Before treatment, all patients have a good **soap & warm-water bath.**

**2) Chemical control (treatment):**
- **Benzyl benzoate (25%) emulsion** — whole body neck to feet, repeated after 12 hrs, then bath.
- **Permethrin cream (5%).**
- **Monosulfiram (Tetmosol) 5%** — thrice daily.
- **Sulphur ointment (2.5–10%)** — daily for 4 days.
- **Gamma benzene hexachloride (GBH) 1%.**
- **Crotamiton (10%)** cream or lotion.

> 🎯 Itch mite control = **treat whole family simultaneously + benzyl benzoate/permethrin/GBH + personal hygiene.**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF pp. 297–298

## Q14. What are the diseases transmitted by soft and hard ticks. (S24,Q6)

### Ticks
> Ticks are important arthropods of **class Arachnida.** Two types — **Hard ticks (ixodidae)** & **Soft ticks (argasidae).** Life cycle: Egg → Larva → Nymph → Adult. Transmission: by biting.

### Diseases transmitted by **hard ticks**
1. **Tick typhus** (Rocky Mountain spotted fever).
2. **Viral encephalitis** (e.g., Russian spring-summer encephalitis).
3. **Viral fevers** (e.g., Colorado tick fever).
4. **Viral haemorrhagic fevers** (e.g., Kyasanur Forest Disease — KFD in India).
5. **Tularaemia.**
6. **Tick paralysis.**
7. **Human babesiosis.**

### Diseases transmitted by **soft ticks**
1. **Q fever.**
2. **Relapsing fever.**
3. **KFD (Kyasanur Forest Disease).**

> 🎯 Hard ticks → **tick typhus · viral encephalitis/fevers · tularaemia · babesiosis** · Soft ticks → **Q fever · relapsing fever.**

> 📖 Ch-07, Endeavour PDF p. 296
