## Q1. What do you mean by food fortification and food enrichment. Give example of each of them. (S1,Q7)

### Food fortification
> 🔴 **Food fortification** (WHO) = the process whereby **nutrients are added to foods (in relatively small quantities)** to maintain or improve the quality of the diet of a group, a community or a population.

**Indications:**
1. When the natural value is lost in food processing — vitamin-B is added in wheat flour (western countries) and in rice (eastern countries).
2. To improve the quality of food — vitamin A, D and iron are added to many baby foods.
3. To prevent a specific disease — iodine is added to table salt in areas where goitre is endemic.

**Examples of fortification:**
1. **Milk** — fortified by the addition of vitamin A & D.
2. **Common salt** — fortified with potassium iodide (KI) in goitre-endemic areas.
3. **Wheat flour (atta)** — fortified with vitamins & minerals, protein content increased by admixture with edible groundnut.
4. **Edible oils** — fortification of **vanaspati** with vitamin A.

### Food enrichment
> 📌 **Food enrichment** = addition of dietary essentials to a food to **restore the total content** of the former.

- Example: adding back **vitamin-B and iron to polished/refined cereals and flour** in which they were lost during milling/processing.

> 🎯 Fortification = **add nutrients to improve quality** · Enrichment = **restore nutrients lost during processing (put back what was removed).**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 483–484

## Q2. Mention the function of food, why egg is considered as reference protein? (S2,Q7)

### Functions of food
1. **Yielding energy** — carbohydrates, proteins & fats provide energy (kcal).
2. **Building and maintaining the body** — proteins & minerals for growth and repair.
3. **Protecting the body & regulating the tissue function** — vitamins, minerals, water.

> 🎯 Food = **energy + body building + protection/regulation.**

### Why egg is considered as reference protein
- A **reference protein** is one which produces **1 gm of tissue for each gm consumed** (biological value = 100); other proteins are compared with it.
- In 1965, the **FAO/WHO Committee recommended egg as the reference protein** because:
  1. **High biological value & digestibility.**
  2. It contains **all the essential amino acids in adequate amount.**
  3. **100% absorbable.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 439 and 450

## Q3. Mention the food borne disease. (S3,Q7)

### Food borne diseases
> 🔴 **Food borne disease** = "a disease, usually either infectious or toxin in nature, **caused by agents that enter the body through the ingestion of food.**"

**Classification:**
**A) Food borne intoxication:**
1. Naturally occurring toxins in some foods — **Lathyrism** (β-oxalyl amino alanine, khesari dhal), **Endemic ascites** (pyrrolizidine alkaloids — jhunjhunia seeds).
2. Toxins produced by bacteria — **Botulism**, **Staphylococcal poisoning.**
3. Toxins produced by fungi (mycotoxins) — **Aflatoxin, Ergot, Fusarium toxins.**
4. Chemical poisoning — heavy metals (mercury in fish, cadmium in shellfish, lead in canned food), oils/petroleum solvents (TCP), migrant chemicals from package materials, asbestos, pesticide residues (DDT, BHC).

**B) Food borne infections — with causative agents:**
| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| Bacterial | Typhoid fever, Paratyphoid fever, Salmonellosis, Staphylococcal intoxication, *Cl. perfringens* illness, Botulism, *B. cereus* food poisoning, *E. coli* diarrhoea, non-cholera vibrio, *V. parahaemolyticus*, Streptococcal infection, Shigellosis, Brucellosis |
| Viral | Viral hepatitis, Gastroenteritis |
| Parasitic | Taeniasis, Hydatidosis, Trichinosis, Ascariasis, Amoebiasis, Oxyuriasis |

> 🎯 Food borne disease = **ingestion of contaminated food → intoxication (toxins) OR infection (organisms).**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 481–482

## Q4. What are the dietary goals? (S4,Q7)

### Dietary goals (WHO recommended)
> 📌 All countries should develop a **national nutrition and food policy** setting out "dietary goals" (the "**prudent diet**") recommended by WHO Expert Committees.

1. **Dietary fat** should be limited to approximately **15–30% of total daily energy intake.**
2. **Saturated fats** should contribute **no more than 10%** of total energy; **unsaturated vegetable oils** should be substituted for the remaining fat requirement.
3. **Excessive consumption of refined carbohydrate** should be avoided; some carbohydrate **rich in natural fibre** should be taken.
4. **Energy-rich sources** such as fats and alcohol should be restricted.
5. **Salt intake** should be reduced to an average of **not more than 5 g/day.**
6. **Protein** should account for approximately **10–15%** of daily intake.
7. **Junk foods** such as colas, ketchups and other foods that supply **empty calories** should be reduced.

> 🎯 Dietary goals = **Fat 15–30% (sat <10%) · CHO with fibre · salt <5 g · protein 10–15% · avoid junk food.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF p. 447

## Q5. Name the source and functions of protein. (S5,Q5)

### Sources of protein
1. **Animal source (first class / reference / biologically complete / high biological value):** milk, meat, **eggs**, cheese, **fish** and fowl — contain **all essential amino acids in adequate amounts.**
2. **Vegetable source (second class / low biological value / biologically incomplete):** pulses, cereals, beans, nuts, oil seeds, cakes — **deficient (lacking) in one or more essential amino acids.**

### Functions of protein
1. **Body building.**
2. **Repair and maintenance of body tissue.**
3. **Maintenance of osmotic pressure.**
4. Synthesis of certain substances — **antibodies, plasma proteins, haemoglobin, enzymes, hormones and coagulation factors.**
5. Protein also **supplies energy** (4.1 kcal/g).

> 🎯 Protein = **body-building food; animal protein = first class; vegetable protein = second class.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 449–450

## Q6. Name the source and function of fat? (S6,Q5)

### Sources of fat
- **Animal fat:** ghee, butter, cheese, eggs, fat of meat & fish.
- **Vegetable fat:** stored in seeds — groundnuts, mustard, sesame, coconut.
- **Other sources (invisible fats):** cereals, pulses, nuts & vegetables (small quantities).

### Functions of fat
1. **High-energy food** — 9 kcal/gm.
2. **Vehicles for fat-soluble vitamins.**
3. In the body, fats **support viscera** (heart, kidney, intestine).
4. Fat beneath the skin provides **insulation against cold.**
5. Fats **give flavour** to food.
6. Vegetable fats are rich sources of **essential fatty acids (EFAs)** — needed for growth, structural integrity of cell membrane & decreased platelet adhesiveness.
7. **EFAs reduce serum cholesterol & LDL.**
8. **Cholesterol** is a component of membranes & nervous tissue and a **precursor of steroid hormones and bile acids.**
9. **Polyunsaturated fatty acids** are precursors of **prostaglandins** (local hormones).

> 🎯 Fat = **energy (9 kcal/g) + vitamin carrier + support + insulation + EFA source.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 451–452

## Q7. Name the source and functions of carbohydrate? (S7,Q1)

### Sources of carbohydrate (3 main sources)
1. **Starches** — cereals, roots and tubers, plant stems.
2. **Sugars** — monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose) and disaccharides (sucrose, lactose, maltose).
3. **Cellulose** — contributes to dietary fibre.

### Functions of carbohydrate
1. **Major/main source of energy** — supplies 60–80% of caloric requirements.
2. **Protein-sparing action** — spares protein for body-building & growth.
3. **Absolute requirement by brain** — brain & CNS are dependent on glucose; prolonged hypoglycaemia may cause irreversible brain damage.
4. **Energy supply for muscle work** — muscle glycogen → lactic acid (glycolysis).
5. **Required for oxidation of fat** — "fat burns in a fuel of carbohydrate"; excess fat + CHO deficiency → **ketosis.**
6. **Synthesis of pentose sugars** — ribose & deoxyribose (DNA/RNA, NAD, FAD).
7. **Synthesis of non-essential amino acids** — via keto-acids (e.g. pyruvic acid).
8. **Synthesis of fat** — excess CHO is converted into fat, stored in adipose tissue.

> 🎯 CHO = **cheapest main energy source + protein sparer + brain fuel + fat oxidation.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF p. 440

## Q8. List the essential amino acids. What are the limiting amino acids. Say protein deficiency diseases. (S8,Q6)

### Essential amino acids
> 🔴 **Essential amino acids** = amino acids which **cannot be synthesized in the body** but are essential for growth and maintenance, so they **must be supplied in the diet.** There are **eight:**
1. Isoleucine
2. Tryptophan
3. Methionine
4. Valine
5. Leucine
6. Lysine
7. Threonine
8. Phenylalanine

### Limiting amino acids
> 📌 Amino acids **present in inadequate amounts** in a protein, which **limit the utilization of other amino acids** for protein synthesis.
- **Cereal proteins are deficient in lysine and threonine.**
- **Pulse proteins are deficient in methionine.**
- These are called **"limiting" amino acids** — they limit the biological value of the food protein.

> 🎯 When two vegetarian foods are eaten together (**rice + dhal**), proteins **supplement one another (supplementary action of protein)** → comparable to animal protein.

### Protein deficiency diseases
1. **Marasmus** (lack of protein **and** calorie).
2. **Kwashiorkor** (lack of **protein only**).
3. **Nutritional anaemia.**
4. **Low birth weight infants.**
5. **Infection** (increased susceptibility).

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 450–451 (essential AA), 451 (limiting/supplementary), 476 (protein deficiency diseases)

## Q9. What are the criteria for a good quality protein. (S9,Q6)

### Criteria / assessment of quality of protein
1. **Amino acid content of protein** — presence of all essential amino acids in adequate amounts.
2. **Digestibility and suitability** to meet the protein needs of the body — evaluated by:
   - **Biological value (BV)** — a good protein produces ~1 gm tissue per gm consumed (BV ≈ 100, e.g., egg).
   - **Digestibility coefficient.**
   - **Protein efficiency ratio (PER).**
   - **Net protein utilization (NPU).**
3. **Growth** — capacity to support normal growth in infants/children.

> 🎯 Good protein = **eggs (BV 100, all EAA, 100% absorbable) — hence the reference protein.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF p. 451

1. Its **high biological value & digestibility.**
2. It contains **all the essential amino acids in adequate amount.**
3. It is **100% absorbable.**

> 🎯 Reference protein = **egg → BV 100, all EAA, fully absorbed.**

## Q14. What is fat and oil. Classify fat. What are the measures to be taken to rear a healthy skin. (S14,Q6)

### Fat and oil
> 🔴 Fats (lipids) are made of **fatty acids (saturated, monounsaturated, polyunsaturated), triglycerides, phospholipids, glycolipids** etc. Fats are **solid at 20°C**; they are called **"oil" if liquid at that temperature.**

### Classification of lipid
1. **Simple lipids** — e.g. **triglycerides.**
2. **Compound lipids** — e.g. **phospholipids.**
3. **Derived lipids** — e.g. **cholesterol.**

### Measures to rear / keep a healthy skin
1. **Adequate intake of essential fatty acids (EFAs)** — their deficiency causes **rough, dry skin (phrenoderma / toad skin).**
2. **Adequate intake of vitamin A** — maintains integrity of skin & epithelial tissue (prevents follicular hyperkeratosis).
3. **Adequate intake of water & dietary fibre** (hydration of skin).
4. **Personal cleanliness / proper hygiene** and care of the skin.
5. **Nutritionally balanced diet** (vitamins B-complex, C — collagen formation & skin integrity).

> 🎯 Healthy skin = **EFA + vit-A + water + cleanliness (EFA deficiency → phrenoderma / toad skin).**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 451–452 (fat/oil, classification), 452 (phrenoderma), 456–457 (vit-A & skin)

## Q19. Enumerate the deficiency disorders due to Vit-A. Mention the daily requirement of Vit-A. (S19,Q5)

### Vitamin-A deficiency disorders
**Ocular (Xerophthalmia) — stages of ocular changes (X series):**
1. **Night blindness** — earliest (inability to see dim light, especially late evening).
2. **Conjunctival xerosis** — conjunctiva becomes dry, muddy and wrinkled, non-wettable.
3. **Bitot's spot** — triangular, white/yellowish **foamy spots** on the bulbar conjunctiva.
4. **Corneal xerosis** — cornea dull, dry and non-wettable, eventually opaque.
5. **Corneal ulceration / keratomalacia** — corneal ulceration; grave medical emergency; if the eye collapses, **vision is lost.**

**Extra-ocular effects:**
- **Growth retardation** (impaired skeletal formation).
- **Skin** — rough & dry, atrophy of hair follicles and of sweat & sebaceous glands (follicular hyperkeratosis).
- **GIT** — keratinization of epithelial cells → increased bacterial infection.
- **Respiratory tract** — metaplasia of epithelial lining (columnar → stratified squamous).
- **Urinary tract** — UTI, stone formation.
- **Reproduction** — degeneration of germinal epithelium → **sterility in males.**

> 🎯 VAD eye sequence = **Night blindness → Conjunctival xerosis → Bitot's spot → Corneal xerosis → Keratomalacia (blindness).**

### Daily requirement of vitamin-A (RDA)
| Group | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Adult (man & woman) | **600 µg RE/day** (≈1 g = 3,000,000 IU; 1 retinol eq = 1 µg retinol = 0.167 µg-re equivalent β-carotene ×) |
| Pregnant / Lactating | higher (~950 µg RE/day) |
| Infants (0–12 m) | ~350 µg RE/day |
| Children (1–6 y) | ~400 µg RE/day |

Prevention schedule (Bangladesh): **vitamin A capsule — 6 months–1 yr: 100,000 IU every 6 months; 1–6 yr: 200,000 IU every 6 months.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 455–458

## Q20. Mention the role of sunlight in the synthesis of Vit-D in human body? (S21,Q5)

### Role of sunlight in vitamin-D synthesis
> 🔴 Vitamin D is **synthesized in the body by the action of UV rays of sunlight on 7-dehydrocholesterol in the skin.**

- Because of this capacity, **vitamin D₃ is not a dietary requirement at all in conditions of adequate sunlight** — it can be synthesized in adequate amounts by simple exposure to sunlight, **even for five minutes per day.**
- Forms: **Calciferol (vitamin D₂)** from plant sterol; **Cholecalciferol (vitamin D₃)** from animal fats & fish liver oils.
- **Active form: 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol.**
- Hence vitamin D is regarded as a **"kidney hormone"** (does not meet the classic definition of a vitamin, since it is synthesized by the body).

> 🎯 **UV rays of sunlight + 7-dehydrocholesterol in skin → vitamin D₃** · "sunshine vitamin".

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF p. 460

## Q22. Tell the signs and symptoms of Rickets. What is the daily requirement of Vit-D. (S23,Q5)

### Rickets — signs & symptoms
> 🔴 **Rickets** occurs usually in **young children (6 months to 2 years)** due to **reduced calcification of bone**, characterized by **growth failure, bone deformity, muscular hypotonia, tetany and convulsions.**

Main clinical features:
- **Growth failure** / delayed milestones.
- **Bone deformities** — bowing of legs, knock-knee, **craniotabes** (soft skull), **frontal bossing.**
- **Rachitic rosary** (beading at costo-chondral junctions), Harrison's sulcus.
- **Muscular hypotonia** (flabby muscles).
- **Tetany and convulsions** (due to low serum calcium).
- In adults (mainly women, esp. during pregnancy & lactation): **osteomalacia.**

### Daily requirement of vitamin-D
| Group | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Adult | **2.5 µg (100 IU)** |
| Infant & children | **5.0 µg (200 IU)** |
| Pregnancy & lactation | **10.0 µg (400 IU)** |

> 🎯 Rickets = **children 6 m–2 y: growth failure + bone deformity + hypotonia + tetany.** VIT-D: **adult 100 IU · child 200 IU · pregnancy 400 IU.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 455, 460

## Q23. Enumerate the functions and daily requirement of Vit-E. (S24,Q6)

### Functions of vitamin E (tocopherol)
1. **Potent biological antioxidant** — prevents oxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids (PUFA) & vitamin A; protects cell membranes (structural integrity).
2. **Prevents RBC haemolysis** — protects the integrity of red cells.
3. Deficiency → **sterility** (degenerative changes in germinal epithelium — testicular atrophy / abortion).

### Daily requirement of vitamin-E
- Adults: **0.8 mg/g of essential fatty acids (EFA)** in the diet.

> 📌 Vitamin E sources: vegetable oils (wheat germ oil, safflower, sunflower, corn oil), leafy vegetables, milk, butter, egg.
> 🎯 Vit-E = **anti-oxidant "tocopherol"; deficiency → sterility; 0.8 mg/g of EFA.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 455–456

## Q24. Name some water soluble vitamins. Mention the functions, daily requirements and deficiency disorders of Vit- B1, B2, B6 and B12? (S25,Q5)

### Water-soluble vitamins
**Vitamin-B complex (thiamine B1, riboflavin B2, niacin B3, pantothenic acid B5, pyridoxine B6, biotin B7, folic acid, cobalamine B12) + vitamin C.**

### B1 (Thiamine)
- **Functions:** Coenzyme in **carbohydrate metabolism** (thiamine pyrophosphate); nerve & cardiac function.
- **Daily requirement:** ~**1.0–1.2 mg** (0.5 mg/1000 kcal).
- **Deficiency disorders:** **Beriberi** (dry — peripheral neuritis; wet — cardiac; infantile — 2–4 months) · **Wernicke's encephalopathy.**

### B2 (Riboflavin)
- **Functions:** Constituent of **flavoprotein coenzymes (FAD, FMN)** in tissue oxidation.
- **Daily requirement:** ~**1.1–1.3 mg** (0.6 mg/1000 kcal).
- **Deficiency disorders:** **Angular stomatitis, glossitis, cheilosis, nasolabial dyssebacia.**

### B6 (Pyridoxine)
- **Functions:** Coenzyme (**pyridoxal phosphate**) in amino acid & protein metabolism, neuro-transmitter synthesis.
- **Daily requirement:** Adult **2 mg**.
- **Deficiency disorders:** **Peripheral neuritis, cheilosis, glossitis, stomatitis** (rare anaemia).

### B12 (Cobalamine)
- **Functions:** Maturation of **RBCs**, DNA synthesis, **myelin (nerve) function**; coenzyme methylcobalamin / 5'-deoxyadenosyl-cobalamin.
- **Daily requirement:** ~**1 µg/day** (adult).
- **Deficiency disorders:**
  1. **Megaloblastic (pernicious) anaemia.**
  2. **Peripheral neuropathy.**
  3. **Subacute combined degeneration of the spinal cord** (demyelinating neurological lesions).

> 🎯 B1 → **Beriberi/Wernicke** · B2 → **Angular stomatitis** · B6 → **2 mg (neuritis)** · B12 → **Megaloblastic anaemia + cord degeneration.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 455–458

## Q25. Mention the sources of dietary iodine and its daily requirement. List the iodine deficiency disorder. (S26,Q7)

### Sources of dietary iodine
1. **Sea foods** — sea fish, sea salt.
2. **Other foods** — milk, meat, vegetables, cereals.
3. **Drinking water** — small content (1–50 µg/L).

### Daily requirement
> 🔴 **150 µg/day** (adult) — higher in pregnancy & lactation.

### Iodine deficiency disorders (IDD)
> 🔴 **IDD** = "all the effects of iodine deficiency on human growth and development that can be prevented by correction of iodine deficiency." (Factors: lack of iodine in food · goitrogens in cabbage/cauliflower.)

**By severity — spectrum:**
1. **Goitre** (Grade-I, II, III; multinodular).
2. **Hypothyroidism.**
3. **Subnormal intelligence, mental deficiency** (varying severity).
4. **Brain damage** — delayed motor milestones, hearing & speech defects, strabismus, nystagmus.
5. **Neuromuscular weakness** — spastic diplegia/quadriplegia.
6. **Endemic cretinism** — hypothyroid & neurological cretinism.
7. **Intrauterine death** (spontaneous abortion, miscarriage).

**By stage of life:**
- **Foetus:** abortion, stillbirth, brain damage.
- **Neonates:** neonatal goitre, brain damage.
- **Child:** goitre, impaired school performance, retarded physical development.
- **Adult:** goitre & complications, thyroid deficiency, impaired mental function.

> 🎯 Iodine → **thyroid hormone synthesis (T3 & T4); 150 µg/day; IDD = goitre → cretinism → brain damage → abortion.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 466–467

## Q26. List the milk borne disease. Mention the pasteurization process of milk. (S27,Q7)

### Milk borne diseases
- **Animal infections transmitted to man (primary):** TB, Brucellosis, Streptococcal infections, Salmonellosis, Q fever; (lesser) cowpox, FMD, anthrax, leptospirosis, tick-borne encephalitis.
- **Human infections via milk:** Typhoid & paratyphoid, Shigellosis, Cholera, EPEC; non-diarrhoeal (streptococcal infection, staphylococcal food poisoning, diphtheria, TB, enteroviruses, viral hepatitis).

### Pasteurization of milk
> 🔴 **Pasteurization** = heating of milk to such temperatures and for such periods of time as are required to **destroy any pathogens that may be present**, while causing **minimal changes** in composition, flavour & nutritive value.

**Types:**
1. **Holder (Vat) method:** milk kept at **63–66°C for at least 30 minutes**, then quickly cooled to **5°C**. Recommended for small & rural communities.
2. **HTST method (High Temperature Short Time):** milk rapidly heated to **~72°C, held for not less than 15 sec**, then rapidly cooled to **4°C**. Most widely used; very large quantities can be pasteurized per hour.
3. **UHT method (Ultra-High Temperature):** milk rapidly heated (usually in 2 stages) to **125°C for a few seconds**, then rapidly cooled & bottled.

> 🎯 Pasteurization = **Holder 63–66°C/30 min · HTST 72°C/15 sec · UHT 125°C/few sec.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 480–481

## Q27. Mention the criteria for fresh meat and fish. List the meat borne diseases. (S28,Q7)

### Criteria for fresh meat and fish
> 📌 A food is "fresh" when it has not lost its natural colour, odour, taste and texture.
- **Fresh meat:** bright red/dark red colour, firm & elastic on pressure, no foul odour, no slime/pus/parasites, not contaminated, veterinary-inspectional stamp present.
- **Fresh fish:** bright red gills, firm flesh, clear & bright eyes, natural odour, no slime on surface, present in a refrigerated/fresh state.

### Meat borne diseases
**1) Tapeworm / helminth infestations:**
- *Taenia solium* · *T. saginata* · *Trichinella spiralis* · *Fasciola hepatica*.

**2) Bacterial infections:**
- **Anthrax · Actinomycosis · Tuberculosis · Food poisoning.**

## Q28. Define balanced diet. Mention the daily requirements of food in balanced diet. (S29,Q7)

### Balanced diet — definition
> 🔴 **Balanced diet** = one which **contains a variety of foods in such quantities and proportions** that the need for **energy, amino acids, vitamins, minerals, fats, carbohydrate and other nutrients** is adequately met for maintaining health, vitality and general well-being, and also makes a small provision for extra nutrients to withstand short duration of leanness.
>
> Or, one which contains all the **proximate principles (protein, fat & carbohydrate), minerals and vitamins in due proportions**, at the same time meeting the total energy requirements for health, vitality & well-being.

### Percentage of food items in balanced diet
| Food item | Percentage of total energy |
|---|---|
| **Carbohydrate** | **55–75%** (about 65%) |
| **Fat** | **15–30%** |
| **Protein** | **10–15%** |

**Criteria / composition:**
1. Easily available, palatable, sufficient to satisfy taste & appetite.
2. Easily digestible, absorbable & assimilable.
3. Protein & fat from both animal & vegetable sources — at least one protein of **animal origin.**
4. Sufficient vegetables & fruits; vitamins & minerals in adequate quantities.
5. Contains cellulose/roughage to promote peristalsis.

**Importance:** maintenance of health, vitality & general well-being; safeguards population from nutritional deficiencies.

> 🎯 Balanced diet = **CHO 55–75% · Fat 15–30% · Protein 10–15%.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 442–443

## Q29. Define malnutrition. Give its classification. How can you assess the nutrition status of a community. (S30,Q6)

### Malnutrition — definition
> 🔴 **Malnutrition** = a **pathological state resulting from a relative or absolute deficiency or excess of one or more essential nutrients.**

### Classification (4 forms)
1. **Under-nutrition** — insufficient food taken over an extended period.
2. **Over-nutrition** — excessive quantity of food over an extended period.
3. **Imbalance** — disproportion among essential nutrients, with or without absolute deficiency of any nutrient.
4. **Specific deficiency** — relative or absolute lack of an individual nutrient.

### Assessment of nutritional status of a community
1. **Clinical examination** — WHO Expert Committee sign categories (not related to nutrition; needs further investigation; known to be of value — angular stomatitis, Bitot's spots).
2. **Anthropometry** — height & weight; **Gomez classification** (weight-for-age), **Welcome classification** (weight-for-age), **Waterlow's classification** (weight-for-height), **arm circumference (MUAC), skin-fold thickness.**
3. **Biochemical evaluation** — Hb%, stool & urine examination; serum iron, serum retinol, urinary iodine, serum folate, prothrombin time (vit-K), serum albumin (protein).
4. **Functional assessment** — structural integrity, host defence, haemostasis, reproduction, nerve function, work capacity.
5. **Dietary intake assessment (dietary survey)** — weighment of raw foods, weighment of cooked foods, oral questionnaire method.
6. **Vital & health statistics** — infant mortality rate, neonatal mortality rate, still-birth rate.
7. **Ecological studies / assessment of aetiological factors** — food balance sheet (per capita supply), socio-economic factors (family size), health & educational services, conditioning influences.

> 🎯 Assess nutrition = **Clinical + Anthropometry (Gomez/Welcome/Waterlow) + Biochemical + Dietary survey + Vital statistics + Ecological studies.**

> 📖 Ch-14, Endeavour PDF pp. 470–471 (malnutrition), 444–445 (assessment)
