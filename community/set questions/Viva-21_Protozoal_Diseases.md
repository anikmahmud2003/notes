## Q1. Classify Leishmaniasis. Mention the major clinical features. How can diagnosis this case? (S5,Q2)

### Classification of leishmaniasis
> 🔴 Depending on the clinical picture, leishmaniasis is classified into:
1. **Visceral leishmaniasis (Kala-azar)** — caused by *Leishmania donovani* (affects reticulo-endothelial system).
2. **Cutaneous leishmaniasis (Oriental sore)** — skin ulcers.
3. **Muco-cutaneous leishmaniasis (Espundia)** — destructive lesions of oro-nasal mucosa.

### Major clinical features (of visceral leishmaniasis / kala-azar)
1. **Insidious onset** with malaise, **irregular fever** (double rise of temperature).
2. **Weight loss** with normal/increased appetite.
3. **Non-tender progressive splenomegaly** (without hepatomegaly, later hepatic enlargement).
4. **Lymphadenopathy**.
5. **Darkening of the skin** (pigmentation) of the face, hands, feet and abdomen ("**kala-azar**" = black disease).
6. **Brittle hairs.**
7. **Anaemia.**
8. **Mucosal haemorrhage.**
9. Fever is usually **double rise in a day**, spiking and prolonged.

### Diagnosis of kala-azar
**1. Clinical diagnosis** — as above (fever + splenomegaly + pigmentation + cachexia in endemic area).

**2. Serological diagnosis:**
- **Direct Agglutination Test (DAT)**
- **ICT for rK-39 antigen** (strip test — field test)
- **ELISA**
- **Indirect Fluorescent Antibody Test (IFAT)**
- **Aldehyde (formol-gel) test**

**3. Parasitological diagnosis (gold standard):**
- **Microscopic examination** (Giemsa or Leishman stain) — demonstration of **amastigote forms (LD bodies)** in **splenic, bone-marrow, lymph-node aspirates**.

**4. Culture:** on **NNN medium** → **promastigotes** are found.

> 🎯 Leishmaniasis = **Visceral (kala-azar) · Cutaneous · Mucocutaneous** · Kala-azar = **fever + splenomegaly + dark skin** · Diagnosis = **LD bodies in aspirate / rK-39 ICT / DAT.**

## Q2. What is black water fever? (S11,Q2)

### Black water fever
> 🔴 **Black water fever** is a **serious complication of *Plasmodium falciparum* malaria**, seen in semi-immune (chronically exposed) persons, characterized by **massive intravascular haemolysis → haemoglobinuria → passage of dark black (port-wine coloured) urine**.

**Features:**
- Occurs in *P. falciparum* infection (especially after irregular quinine treatment).
- Sudden onset of **rigor, high fever**, backache; **rapid haemolysis → severe anaemia** & haemoglobinuria (dark urine).
- May be associated with **renal failure (acute tubular necrosis)** and jaundice (intravascular haemolysis).
- **Causes:** G6PD deficiency + oxidant drugs/quine; immune haemolysis of parasitized & non-parasitized RBCs.

> 🎯 Black water fever = **falciparum malaria + massive haemolysis + dark/black urine (+ acute renal failure).**

> 📖 Ch-05, Endeavour PDF pp. 204–206 (malaria complications)

## Q3. What is 'Black water fever'? Mention the complications of falciparum malaria. (S29,Q2)

### Black water fever
*As in Q2* — a complication of *P. falciparum* malaria with massive **intravascular haemolysis → haemoglobinuria (dark/black urine)**, severe anaemia & acute renal failure, seen in semi-immune patients (often after irregular quinine therapy).

### Complications of falciparum malaria (severe/complicated malaria)
1. **Cerebral malaria** (altered consciousness, coma — most dreaded).
2. **Severe anaemia**.
3. **Acute renal failure** (incl. **black water fever**).
4. **Black water fever** (haemoglobinuria).
5. **Pulmonary oedema / adult respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS)**.
6. **Hypoglycaemia**.
7. **Circulatory collapse / algid malaria** (shock).
8. **Haemostatic failure / DIC** (bleeding, petechiae).
9. **Hepatic dysfunction & jaundice**.
10. **Hyperparasitaemia**; convulsions; acidosis; multi-organ failure.

> 🎯 Falciparum complications = **cerebral malaria · severe anaemia · BWF/renal failure · ARDS · hypoglycaemia · algid shock · DIC.**

> 📖 Ch-05, Endeavour PDF pp. 204–206
