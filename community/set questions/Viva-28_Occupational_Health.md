<summary><b>Q1. Define occupational health and mention factors essential for safe occupational environment. (S1,Q4)</b></summary>

### Definition of occupational health
> 🔴 The **Joint ILO/WHO Committee on Occupational Health (1950):** "Occupational health should aim at the **promotion and maintenance of the highest degree of physical, mental and social well-being of workers in all occupations**; the **prevention** among workers of departures from health caused by their working conditions; the **protection** of workers in their employment from risks resulting from factors adverse to health; the placing & maintenance of the worker in an occupational environment adapted to this physiological & psychological equipment; and, to summarize, **the adaptation of work to man and each man to his job**."

### Occupational environment
**Occupational environment** = "the sum of external conditions and influences which prevail at the place of work and which have a bearing on the health of the working population."

**For a safe working environment, control the 3 types of interaction:**
1. **Man ↔ physical & chemical agents** — heat, cold, light, pressure, noise, radiation; chemicals, toxic dusts & gases.
2. **Man ↔ biological agents** — viruses, rickettsiae, bacteria, parasites (animals, contaminated water/soil/food).
3. **Man ↔ machine** — unguarded/protruding moving parts, poor installation → accidents; unphysiological posture → fatigue, backache.
4. **Man ↔ man** — human relationships, stress → physical/mental illness.

> 🎯 Occupational health = **promote + prevent + protect + adapt work to man & man to his job** · Safe environment controls **man–agent, man–machine, man–man** interactions.

<summary><b>Q2. What are the objects of occupational health? Tell about maternity benefit of female workers. (S2,Q4)</b></summary>

### Objects (objectives) of occupational health
1. **Promotion & maintenance** of the highest degree of physical, mental & social well-being of workers in all occupations.
2. **Prevention** among workers of departure from health by working conditions.
3. **Protection** of workers from risks resulting from factors adverse to health.
4. **Placing & maintenance** of workers in an occupational environment adapted to their physiological & psychological equipment.
5. **Adaptation of work to man & each man to his job.**

### Maternity benefit of female workers
- **Maternity benefit (ESIC):** paid for **12 weeks (now 24 weeks)**; for **miscarriage 6 weeks**; for sickness arising out of confinement **±30 days**.
- **Other maternal measures in industry:** maternity leave (12→24 weeks); **free antenatal, natal & post-natal services**; **prohibition of work in underground mines** & hazardous jobs during pregnancy; **nursery/crèche** arrangements for children of working mothers; children **under 14 years are not to be employed** (Factories Act).

> 🎯 Objectives = **promotion · prevention · protection · right placement · adapt work to man** · Maternity benefit = **12→24 weeks pay (+6 wks miscarriage).**

<summary><b>Q3. What do you mean by Ergonomics? Classify the common occupational hazards in exposed population. (S3,Q4)</b></summary>

### Ergonomics
> 🔴 **Ergonomics** (Greek: *ergon* = work, *nomos* = law) simply means **"fitting the job to the worker"**; its objective is to achieve the **best mutual adjustment of man and his work** for improvement of human efficiency & well-being.

### Classification of occupational hazards (5 types)
1. **Physical hazards:** heat (hyperpyrexia, heat exhaustion, heat syncope, heat cramps, burns, prickly heat), cold (trench foot, frostbite, chilblains), light (cataract, miner's nystagmus), pressure (caisson disease, air embolism, blast), **noise (occupational deafness)**, radiation (cancer, leukaemia, aplastic anaemia), mechanical factors (accidents/injuries), electricity (burns).
2. **Chemical hazards:** local (dermatitis, eczema, ulcers, cancer — acids, alkalies, pesticides), solvents (carbon bisulphide, benzene, chloroform), gases (CO₂, CO, HCN, CS₂, NH₃, H₂S, HCl, SO₂), **metals (lead, mercury, cadmium, manganese, beryllium, arsenic, chromium)**, dusts → pneumoconiosis, ingestion (Hg, Pb, As).
3. **Biological hazards:** brucellosis, leptospirosis, anthrax, actinomycosis, hydatidosis, psittacosis, tetanus, encephalitis, fungal infection.
4. **Mechanical hazards:** machinery, protruding/moving parts.
5. **Psychosocial hazards:** behavioural changes (hostility, anxiety, alcoholism, absenteeism), psychosomatic illness (fatigue, peptic ulcer, hypertension).

*(Also: occupational cancer & occupational dermatosis.)*

> 🎯 Ergonomics = **fit the job to the worker** · Hazards = **physical + chemical + biological + mechanical + psychosocial.**

> 📖 Ch-17, Endeavour PDF pp. 564–565

<summary><b>Q4. What are the common occupational hazards? Tell about the worker's benefits. (S4,Q4)</b></summary>

### Common occupational hazards
**Physical** (heat – hyperpyrexia/heat cramps, cold – trench foot/frostbite, light – cataract/nystagmus, pressure – caisson disease, noise – deafness, radiation – leukaemia/aplastic anaemia, mechanical – accidents, electricity – burns) · **Chemical** (dermatitis, gas poisoning – CO/CO₂/HCN/H₂S, metal poisoning – Pb/Hg/Cd/Mn/As, solvents, dusts → pneumoconiosis, ingestion) · **Biological** (brucellosis, anthrax, leptospirosis, tetanus, fungal infections) · **Mechanical** (accidents) · **Psychosocial** (stress, absenteeism) + occupational cancer & dermatosis.

### Worker's benefits (from industrial management — ESI)
1. **Medical benefit** — full medical care incl. hospitalization, free (OPD, drugs, specialists, investigations, domiciliary, MCH, immunization, FP, emergency, ambulance, health education, in-patient).
2. **Sickness benefit** — periodic cash payment (certified sick) for max **91 days in any 365-day period**.
3. **Maternity benefit** — **12 weeks (now 24)**, miscarriage 6 weeks.
4. **Disablement benefit** — temporary disablement ~**72% of wages**; permanent total disablement → full-rate life pension.
5. **Dependants' benefit** — on death from employment injury, widow/children get periodical payments (**40% above Standard Benefit Rate**).
6. **Funeral expenses** (≤ Rs.1000) & **rehabilitation allowance**.

> 🎯 Hazards = **physical/chemical/biological/mechanical/psychosocial** · Benefits = **medical + sickness (91 d) + maternity (24 w) + disablement (72%) + dependants.**

<summary><b>Q5. What are the heat related occupational hazards? List the general measures to protect the health of workers. (S5,Q4)</b></summary>

### Heat-related occupational hazards
1. **Heat hyperpyrexia (heat-stroke).**
2. **Heat exhaustion.**
3. **Heat syncope.**
4. **Heat cramps.**
5. **Burns** and **prickly heat (miliaria)**.
6. Dehydration & salt depletion; heat fatigue (reduced efficiency).

### General measures to protect the health of workers (ILO/WHO, 1953)
1. **Nutrition** — balanced diet & nutrition education.
2. **Communicable disease control** — early diagnosis, treatment, prevention & rehabilitation.
3. **Environmental sanitation** — safe water, food sanitation, sanitary latrines/urinals for both sexes, cleanliness, adequate floor space, **lighting, ventilation, temperature control**, protection against dusts/fumes, adequate housing near plant.
4. **Mental health** — promotion of health & happiness, relieve stress, treatment & rehabilitation of mental illness.
5. **Measures for women & children** — maternity leave, free antenatal/natal/postnatal care, prohibition of underground work, crèches, no child labour (<14 yrs).
6. **Health education.**
7. **Family planning & MCH services.**

> 🎯 Heat hazards = **hyperpyrexia/heat-stroke, exhaustion, syncope, cramps, prickly heat, burns** · Worker protection = **nutrition + infection control + sanitation + mental health + women/children + education.**

<summary><b>Q7. Define ergonomics. What are the chemicals that cause occupational hazards? (S7,Q4)</b></summary>

### Ergonomics
> 🔴 "**Fitting the job to the worker**" — best mutual adjustment of man & his work (improve human efficiency & well-being).

### Chemicals causing occupational hazards
**1. Local action (skin):** acids, alkalies, **pesticides** → dermatitis, eczema, ulcers, cancer.
**2. Solvents:** carbon bisulphide, benzene, trichloroethylene, chloroform.
**3. Inhalation — gases:** CO₂, **CO, HCN, CS₂, NH₃, H₂S, HCl, SO₂** (gas poisoning).

**6. Ingestion:** mercury, lead, arsenic, chromium, cadmium, phosphorus.

> 🎯 Chemicals = **gases (CO, HCN, H₂S, SO₂) + metals (Pb, Hg, Cd, As, Mn) + solvents + pesticides + dusts (pneumoconiosis).**

<summary><b>Q10. Define and classify pneumoconiosis? Give example of each of them. Enumerate the physical hazards among the factory workers. (S10,Q4)</b></summary>

### Pneumoconiosis — definition & classification
> 🔴 **Pneumoconiosis** = a **lung disease caused by inhalation of dust** particles (size **0.5–3 micron**) over a variable period of exposure, which may gradually cripple the worker by reducing working capacity via **lung fibrosis** & complications.

| Dust | Disease |
|---|---|
| **Inorganic:** Coal | **Anthracosis** |
| Silica (free SiO₂) | **Silicosis** |
| **Asbestos** | **Asbestosis** |
| Iron | Siderosis |
| **Organic:** Cotton | **Byssinosis** |
| Cane fibre (bagasse) | **Bagassosis** |
| Tobacco | Tobaccosis |
| **Hay/grain (mouldy)** | **Farmer's lung** |

- Preventive measures: **dust control, personal protection, periodic medical check-up** (no specific cure).

### Physical hazards among factory workers
**Heat** (hyperpyrexia, heat exhaustion, heat syncope, cramps, burns, prickly heat) · **Cold** (trench foot, frostbite, chilblains) · **Light** (occupational cataract, miner's nystagmus) · **Pressure** (caisson disease, air embolism, blast) · **Noise** (occupational deafness) · **Radiation** (cancer, leukaemia, aplastic anaemia, pancytopenia) · **Mechanical factors** (injuries, accidents) · **Electricity** (burns).

> 🎯 Pneumoconiosis = **dust (0.5–3 µ) lung fibrosis** · Coal→anthracosis, Silica→silicosis, Asbestos→asbestosis, Cotton→byssinosis, Hay→farmer's lung.

> 📖 Ch-17, Endeavour PDF pp. 566–567, 565

<summary><b>Q11. Name the asbestos fibres. What do you mean by Farmer's lung? (S11,Q4)</b></summary>

### Asbestos fibres
1. **Chrysotile (white asbestos)** — the most widely used.
2. **Crocidolite (blue asbestos)** — most dangerous (mesothelioma).
3. **Amosite (brown asbestos).**
*(-  exposure → **asbestosis** — lung fibrosis; mesothelioma of pleura/peritoneum; lung cancer.)*

### Farmer's lung
> 🔴 **Farmer's lung** = an **occupational (allergic) disease of the lungs due to inhalation of mouldy hay or grain dust.**
- **Pathogenesis:** in hay/grain with moisture >30%, bacteria & fungi grow rapidly → temperature rises to 40–50 °C → growth of **thermophilic actinomycetes** — **Micropolyspora faeni** is the main cause.
- **Pathology:** repeated attacks → pulmonary fibrosis, permanent lung damage, **cor pulmonale**.
- **Clinical (acute illness):** general — headache, **fever, malaise, muscle pain**; respiratory — **dry cough, breathlessness without wheeze**; signs — raised temperature.

> 🎯 Asbestos fibres = **chrysotile (white) · crocidolite (blue) · amosite (brown)** · Farmer's lung = **mouldy-hay dust + Micropolyspora faeni → fever, dry cough, fibrosis → cor pulmonale.**

> 📖 Ch-17, Endeavour PDF pp. 568

<summary><b>Q15. What do you mean by pre-placement examination? State its importance. (S15,Q4)</b></summary>

### Pre-placement examination
> 🔴 **Pre-placement examination** = a **medical examination carried out at the time of employment** — it includes the worker's **medical, family, occupational & social history**, a **thorough physical examination** and a battery of **laboratory & biological investigations**.

### Importance
1. To assess the **physical & mental abilities** of the applicant (employer-employee matching).
2. Serves as a **useful benchmark for future comparison** (baseline).
3. Helps to **place the right man in the right job.**
4. Forms basis for accident/disease prevention, disability & compensation evaluation.

> 🎯 Pre-placement exam = **medical at time of employment (history + exam + investigations)** · Value = **right-man-right-job + baseline + ability assessment.**

> 📖 Ch-17, Endeavour PDF pp. 576

<summary><b>Q16. Name the engineering measures for controlling the occupational hazards. (S16,Q4)</b></summary>

### Engineering measures for control of occupational hazards
1. **Design of building** — floor, walls, height, roof, windows, cubic space in original plan.
2. **Good housekeeping** — cleanliness, ventilation, lighting, washing, food arrangements, maintenance.
3. **General (good) ventilation** in factories.
4. **Mechanization** — to the fullest extent, to reduce contact with harmful substances.
5. **Substitution** — replace harmful material with harmless/less toxic one.
6. **Dust control** — water-sprays at the point of origin.
7. **Enclosure** — enclosing harmful materials/processes prevents escape of dust & fumes.
8. **Isolation** — offensive process isolated to save workers from exposure.
9. **Local exhaust ventilation.**
10. **Protective devices** — respirators, masks, earplugs, earmuffs, helmets, safety shoes, aprons, gloves, gum-boots, barrier creams, screens, goggles.
11. **Environmental & statistical monitoring; research.**

> 🎯 Engineering control = **building design + housekeeping + ventilation + mechanization + substitution + enclosure/isolation + local exhaust + PPE.**

> 📖 Ch-17, Endeavour PDF p. 576

### Pneumoconiosis — definition & examples
> 🔴 **Pneumoconiosis** = lung disease caused by inhalation of dust (0.5–3 micron) → lung fibrosis with reduced working capacity.

| Dust | Disease |
|---|---|
| Coal | Anthracosis |
| **Silica** | **Silicosis** |
| **Asbestos** | **Asbestosis** |
| Iron | Siderosis |
| Cotton | **Byssinosis** |
| Cane fibre (bagasse) | Bagassosis |
| Tobacco | Tobaccosis |
| Mouldy hay/grain | **Farmer's lung** |

### Benefits for employees
*As Q4/Q24* — medical (full care), sickness (91 days), maternity (24 weeks), disablement (72%/pension), dependants' benefit, funeral, rehabilitation.

> 🎯 Pneumoconiosis = **dust fibrosis: coal→anthracosis, silica→silicosis, asbestos→asbestosis, cotton→byssinosis, hay→farmer's lung** · Benefits = **see Q4.**
