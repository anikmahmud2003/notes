<summary><b>Q1. What do you mean by behavioural science? Mention the importance of behavioural science. (S1,Q7)</b></summary>

### Behavioural science
> 🔴 **Behavioural science** is the **scientific study of psychology, anthropology and sociology** which is concerned with and deals with **human behaviour**; it is a part of the **social science**.

**Social science** = the disciplines committed to the scientific examination of human behaviour — economics, political science, sociology, social psychology and social anthropology.
**Psychology** = *"the study of human behaviour — how people behave and why they behave in just the way they do."*

### Importance of behavioural science (in medicine & health)
1. **To know others better** — understand how people behave and why.
2. **To differentiate between the normal and the abnormal.**
3. **To help promote mental health in individuals and families.**
4. To understand the **social & cultural factors in the causation of disease** (medical sociology).
5. To improve **doctor-patient communication & relationship** and patient compliance.
6. To plan & implement **community health / behavioural-change programmes** (e.g. family planning, smoking cessation) based on people's beliefs, attitudes & motives.

> 🎯 Behavioural science = **psychology + anthropology + sociology → study of human behaviour** · Helpful for **normal/abnormal distinction, mental health, communication, health programming.**

> 📖 Ch-21 pp. 633–634; standard text

<summary><b>Q2. Define and classify family with example. Mention the major functions of family. (S2,Q7)</b></summary>

### Family (definition)
> 🔴 The **family is a primary unit in all societies** — a **group of biologically related individuals living together and eating from a common kitchen.** It differs from a *household* in that all members of a household may not be blood relations (e.g. servants).

### Types of family
1. **Nuclear / elemental family** — the married couple & their children (while still dependants), occupying the same dwelling space; the husband usually plays a dominant role.
2. **Joint family** — a number of married couples & their children living together in the same household; all men related by blood (their wives, unmarried girls & widows); **all property held in common**; authority vested in the **senior male member**; early & arranged marriage advocated; familial relations enjoy primacy over marital relations.
3. **Three-generation family** — common in the West; a household with representatives of 3 generations (young couples unable to afford separate housing continue to live with parents and have their own children). *Example: Mr. Karim living with 3 wives, 7 children & a nephew → **extended family**.*

### Major functions of the family
1. **Residence** — provides a clean & decent home.
2. **Division of labour** — male earns a living, female cares for children & runs the household (less distinct in industrialized/urbanized communities).
3. **Reproduction & bringing up of children.**
4. **Socialization** — teaching the young the values of society; transmitting information, culture, beliefs & codes of conduct.
5. **Economic function.**
6. **Social care** — giving *status in society*, protecting members from insult/defamation, regulating marital/political/religious/social activities, regulating **sex relations through incest-taboos**.
7. **Health functions** — child rearing (feeding, nutrition, hygiene, discipline), personality formation ("placenta" of physical/mental/social health), care of dependent adults (sick, pregnant, aged, handicapped), stabilization of adult personality.

> 🎯 Family = **biologically related group, common kitchen, primary unit** · Types = **nuclear · joint · three-generation (extended)** · Functions = **residence, labour division, reproduction, socialization, economy, social care.**

> 📖 Ch-22, Endeavour PDF pp. 642–643

<summary><b>Q3. Define motivation. Mention different motives with examples. (S3,Q7)</b></summary>

### Motivation (definition)
> 🔴 **Motivation** is an **intra-psychic instinct, desire or force of attitude or behavioural change.** In every person there is a fundamental desire to learn; **awakening this desire is called motivation.** According to *William Scot*: *"a process of stimulating people to action to accomplish desired goals."*

**Chain reaction:** Felt needs → Wants or goals → Tension → Actions for achieving goals → Satisfaction of wants.
**Motivation results in:** improved commitment · improved participation · improved performance.

### Different motives (types) with examples
1. **Primary motives** — driving forces initiating people into action; **inborn desires** — *examples: sex, hunger, survival.*
2. **Secondary motives** — desires created by **outside forces or incentives** — *examples: praise, love, rivalry, rewards & punishment, recognition.*

*Example in health education:* telling an overweight lady she will look more charming & beautiful by losing weight (rather than warning about heart disease) is more likely to make her accept health advice — positive incentive is often a first step in learning to change.

> 🎯 Motivation = **awakening the desire to learn → action → satisfaction** · Motives = **primary (sex, hunger, survival) + secondary (praise, love, reward, recognition).**

> 📖 Ch-21, Endeavour PDF pp. 625–626

<summary><b>Q4. Define personality and mention its types with their characteristic features. (S4,Q7)</b></summary>

### Personality (definition)
> 🔴 **Personality** is a key word in psychology — it implies **certain physical and mental traits which are characteristic of a given individual**; these traits determine to some extent the individual's **behaviour or adjustment to his surroundings.**

**Components of personality** — *physical* (height, weight, colour, facial expression, health), *emotional* (fear, anger, love, jealousy, guilt, worries), *intelligence* (intelligent or dull), *behaviour* (gentle, kind, affectionate, balanced, submissive, aggressive).

### Types of personality with characteristic features
1. **Extrovert (Type-A)** — **dashing, practical, active and showing off**; **easily mixes with people.**
2. **Introvert (Type-B)** — **reserved, shy and generally keeps to himself.**
3. **Mixture of both.**

> 🎯 Personality = **physical + mental traits determining behaviour/adjustment** · Types = **Extrovert (outgoing, mixes easily) · Introvert (reserved, shy) · mixture.**

> 📖 Ch-22, Endeavour PDF pp. 639–640

<summary><b>Q5. What are the principles of learning? (S5,Q7)</b></summary>

*(UH&FPO functions part of this Q → Viva-32)*

### Principles of learning
1. Learning is a **continuous, dynamic and ongoing process** and has **no required end**.
2. It is an **individual matter** — each learner learns his own way.
3. **Motivation is the key to learning.**
4. **Relevance** of the learning experience should be clear to the learner and must be related to his **needs of living**.
5. The learner should be able to **communicate back** to the teacher what he has learnt — **feedback is important**.
6. In the learning process there should be **no authority–dependency relationship** between teacher and student — it is **cooperative and collaborative**.

> 🎯 Principles = **continuous · individual · motivation-key · relevant · feedback · cooperative (no authority-dependency).**

> 📖 Ch-22, Endeavour PDF pp. 637–638

<summary><b>Q6. Define behavior. Enumerate different types of behavior with example. (S6,Q7)</b></summary>

### Behavior (definition)
> 🔴 **Behaviour** is *"the total reactions (of an individual) accessible to external observation."* It is the result of **physical and mental factors (body & mind) interacting in complicated ways** — the various voluntary movements undertaken by the body in response to motives and decisions.

### Types of behaviour with examples
1. **Health behaviour:**
   - **Compliance behaviour** — result of motives that move **towards** a desired action. *Examples: accepting immunization, taking nutritious food, exercising, brushing teeth, bathing.*
   - **Avoidance behaviour** — result of motives that move **away from** disliked or feared actions. *Examples: stopping cigarette smoking, avoiding fatty food & alcoholic drinks.*
2. **Illness behaviour** — how people react to symptoms; the affected person waits to see whether symptoms persist/worsen, and if they continue, **seeks help** before seeking medical help.
3. **Treatment behaviour** — the activities used to **cure disease and restore health** (following prescriptions & advice).

> 🎯 Behaviour = **total observable reactions of the individual** · Types = **health (compliance + avoidance) · illness · treatment.**

> 📖 Ch-21, Endeavour PDF pp. 633–634

### Culture (definition)
> 🔴 **Culture is defined as "learned behaviour which has been socially acquired"** — that complex whole which includes **knowledge, belief, art, law, morals, custom** and any other capabilities and habits acquired by man as a member of society.

**Culture includes:** knowledge · belief · attitude · prejudice · values · customs · morals · law.

### Types of personality with example
1. **Extrovert (Type-A)** — dashing, practical, active, showing off, easily mixes with people. *Example: a sales representative who mingles easily with customers.*
2. **Introvert (Type-B)** — reserved, shy, generally keeps to himself. *Example: a research worker who prefers solitude.*
3. **Mixture of both.**

> 🎯 Culture = **learned, socially acquired behaviour (knowledge, belief, art, law, morals, custom, values)** · Personality = **extrovert · introvert · mixture.**

<summary><b>Q8. What do you mean by social norms and values? (S8,Q7)</b></summary>

*(Health Assistant functions part of this Q → Viva-32)*

### Social norms
> 🔴 **Social norms** are the **expected patterns/rules of conduct** which govern the behaviour of individuals in a social situation — the "do's and don'ts" of a society. *Examples: greeting elders, rules of dress, table manners, modesty.* Norms are **learnt** (via family/socialization), vary between societies, and violations invite **sanctions** (disapproval, ridicule, ostracism).

### Values
> 🔴 **Values** are the **abstract ideals about what is good, right and desirable** shared by the members of a society — they guide behaviour and underlie norms. *Examples: honesty, truthfulness, respect for elders, family honour, religious faith.* Values are part of culture (culture = knowledge, belief, attitude, prejudice, values, customs, morals, law) and are transmitted through socialization.

> 🎯 Norms = **expected rules of conduct (what one should do)** · Values = **abstract ideals of the good/right (honesty, respect, faith)** — both are socially learnt.

> 📖 Ch-22, Endeavour PDF p. 644 (culture/values); standard text

<summary><b>Q9. What do you mean by leadership and kinship system in rural Bangladesh? Mention the qualities of a leader. (S9,Q7)</b></summary>

### Leadership
> 🔴 A **leader is any person who can influence a group of people and guide them to action.**

**Leadership functions** — director of members towards the set goal · creates & maintains a congenial working environment · sets goals for subordinates · motivates group members · maintains order & discipline · represents the group to superiors · gets regular feedback.
**Types of leadership (styles):** *autocratic* (leader decides & orders without consulting the group) · *democratic* (leader consults the group while implementing decisions) · *free-rein/laissez-faire* (leader leaves the group entirely to itself).

### Kinship system in rural Bangladesh
- **Kinship = relationship by blood (consanguinity) or by marriage (affinity).**
- In rural Bangladesh the **joint/extended family** forms the kinship unit — patrilineal descent, property & authority held by the senior male.
- Kinship ties decide **marriage, inheritance, occupation and social obligations** and strongly influence **health behaviour** (who decides on treatment, who cares for the sick/aged, home-delivery care by kin).

### Qualities of a good leader
A leader —
1. Should be **technically competent**.
2. Should be **capable of making quick decisions**.
3. Should be able to **give clear instructions and distribute the work-load appropriately**.
4. Should be **fair, objective and tolerant**.
5. **Gives credit where it is due.**
6. Is **approachable, available and accessible**.

> 🎯 Leader = **one who influences & guides the group** · Styles = **autocratic · democratic · free-rein** · Kinship = **blood/marriage ties (joint family, kinship decides care & health choices)**.

> 📖 Ch-22, Endeavour PDF pp. 643–644; kinship per standard text

<summary><b>Q10. What are the factors to be considered to improve the doctor-patient relationship? (S10,Q7)</b></summary>

### Doctor-patient relationship (background)
> 🔴 The doctor-patient relationship is **central to the practice of health care** and essential for the delivery of high-quality care in diagnosis & treatment; it forms one of the **foundations of contemporary medical ethics.**

### Role of the doctor in developing the relationship
1. Always keep in mind the **importance of preserving human life** from conception until death.
2. When examinations/treatments are **beyond his capacity, summon another doctor** who has the necessary ability.
3. **Maintain absolute professional secrecy** of the patient.
4. Must give necessary treatment in **emergency circumstances**, unless certain it can be given by others.

### Role of the patient
1. **Confidence** in the physician's competence; feel they can confide in him.
2. Furnish **complete information** about past illness, family history & circumstances of illness.
3. **Strictly follow the doctor's instructions** (diet, medicine, mode of life).
4. Pay a **reasonable fee**.

### Factors influencing / improving the relationship
1. **Communication on an emotional plane** — the doctor must give a **sympathetic ear** to the patient's & relatives' complaints → quick rapport.
2. **Communication on a cultural plane** — awareness of the culture & social organization of the community → flexibility in dealing with patients.
3. **Communication on an intellectual plane** — reduce the **"social distance"** between the well-to-do practitioner & the illiterate masses; a successful doctor is one who reduces this distance, communicates freely and wins confidence; **humour is the best icebreaker** for a patient frozen by fear & anxiety.

### Why the relationship is unsatisfactory in our country
Uncooperative attitude of the patient · differential treatment between rich & poor · lack of a sympathetic & friendly attitude of the doctor · the doctor's greed for money.

> 🎯 Improvement = **doctor (sympathy, secrecy, referral, emergency care) + patient (confidence, complete info, follow-up, reasonable fee) + emotional/cultural/intellectual communication (reduce social distance → better rapport).**

> 📖 Ch-22, Endeavour PDF pp. 644–646

<summary><b>Q15. What do you mean by mental retardation? Mention the common causes of mental retardation. (S16,Q7)</b></summary>

### Mental retardation (definition)
> 🔴 **Mental retardation** (mental subnormality) means **significantly sub-average general intellectual functioning (IQ < 70)** with onset **before the age of 18 years**, associated with **deficits in adaptive behaviour** (daily living, social & communication skills). *(Definition per standard text.)*

### Categories of mental retardation (IQ)
- **Mild** — IQ 70–50
- **Moderate** — IQ 49–35
- **Severe** — IQ 34–20
- **Profound** — IQ under 20 (syn. idiot; IQ 0–24)

### Common causes of mental retardation
1. **Genetic conditions** — Down's syndrome, Klinefelter syndrome, etc.
2. **Antenatal factors** — neural tube defects, Rh incompatibility, maternal infections.
3. **Perinatal factors** — birth injuries.
4. **Postnatal factors** — head injuries, accidents, encephalitis; physical & chemical agents (e.g. **lead and mercury poisoning**).
5. **Miscellaneous** — maternal malnutrition, **PEM**, iodine deficiency, etc.

*(Prevention: primary — genetic counselling, identification of those at risk, immunization (polio, rubella), nutrition, MCH care; secondary — early diagnosis, treatment, training & education.)*

> 🎯 **IQ < 70 + onset < 18 yrs + adaptive-behaviour deficit** · Causes = **genetic, antenatal, perinatal, postnatal (lead/mercury), PEM/iodine deficiency** · Categories = **mild 70–50 · moderate 49–35 · severe 34–20 · profound <20.**

> 📖 Ch-08, Endeavour PDF p. 313 (causes & categories); definition per standard text
