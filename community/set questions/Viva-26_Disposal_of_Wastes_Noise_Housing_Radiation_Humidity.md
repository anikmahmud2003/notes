## Q1. What do you mean by sewer, sullage, sludge and sewage? Where you can get sludge? (S10,Q3)

### Definitions
1. **Sewer** — an **underground pipe/conduit** of a sewage system that carries **sewage** (waste water).
2. **Sullage** — **waste water which does NOT contain human excreta** — e.g. waste water from **kitchens & bathrooms**.
3. **Sewage** — **waste water from a community** containing solid & liquid excreta, derived from houses, street & yard washings, factories and industries; a sewage system consists of sewers + pumps + all facilities for carrying sewage to the place of final disposal.
4. **Sludge** — the **solid settled portion of sewage**; includes the decomposable organic matter settled out in treatment (sedimentation) tanks.

### Where you get sludge
> Sludge is obtained from the **sedimentation tanks (primary/secondary)** of a **sewage treatment plant** — the sludge digester tanks where sewage solids are subjected to decomposition (finally used as manure/compost).

> 🎯 Sullage = **waste water without excreta (kitchen/bathroom)** · Sewage = **community waste water with excreta** · Sewer = **carrying pipe** · Sludge = **settled sewage solids (in treatment tanks).**

## Q2. Define and classify refuse. List the methods of refuse disposal. What are the components of water carriage system? (S19,Q3)

### Definition of refuse
> 🔴 **Refuse** = the **waste materials from homes, restaurants and other establishments** in the community — mainly **garbage, rubbish and ashes**.
- **Garbage** = putrefiable food wastes; **rubbish** = non-putrefiable (paper, polythene, glass, cans, metals, crockery, wood); **ashes** = residues of burnt fuel.

### Classification (by source) of refuse
1. **Street refuse** — leaves, straw, paper, animal droppings, litter.
2. **Market refuse** — large proportion of **putrid vegetable & animal matter**.
3. **Stable litter** — animal droppings & left-over animal feeds.
4. **Industrial refuse** — inert materials to highly toxic & explosive compounds.
5. **Domestic refuse** — ash, rubbish and garbage.

### Methods of refuse disposal (6 principal methods)
1. **Dumping** — in low-lying areas (WHO: most unsanitary method).
2. **Controlled tipping / sanitary land-filling** — most satisfactory where land is available (trench, ramp, area methods; temp >60 °C in 7 days kills pathogens).
3. **Incineration** — method of choice where land is scarce (**ideal for hospital refuse**).
4. **Composting** — "(**ideal/best method**)" — refuse with human excreta/sludge digested by bacteria → **compost** manure (hot fermentation destroys pathogens, flies' eggs).
5. **Manure pits** — rural method; refuse covered with earth daily → manure in 5–6 months.
6. **Burial** — trench 1.5 m wide, 2 m deep for small camps.

### Components of water carriage system
1. **Household sanitary fittings** (plumbing of buildings).
2. **House sewers.**
3. **Street/trunk sewers.**
4. **Sewer appurtenances** — manholes, traps, etc.

> 🎯 Refuse = **garbage + rubbish + ashes** (domestic, street, market, stable, industrial) · Disposal = **dumping, controlled tipping, incineration, composting, manure pits, burial (composting = best).**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 529–531

## Q3. Illustrate the water seal latrine. (S27,Q3)

### Water seal latrine (hand-flushed, for rural families)
> The **hand-flushed water seal latrine** is the **improved sanitary latrine for rural families**. The trap **holds water and forms the "water seal"**, which prevents flies & odours from the pit escaping into the latrine.

**Parts:**
1. **Location** — ≥ **50 feet (15 m) away** from the water-supply source; at a lower elevation (prevent contamination of water); not in flood-prone areas.
2. **Squatting plate** — impervious, washable, with raised footrests.
3. **Pan** — front width 12.5 cm, widest 20 cm; uniform slope front→back.
4. **Connecting pipe** — for indirect-type latrines.
5. **Trap** — a **bent pipe, ~7.5 cm diameter**, connected with the pipe; **holds water & provides the water seal**.
6. **Dug well (leach pit)** — ~7.5 cm diameter distributes, 3–3.5 m deep, covered.
7. **Superstructure** — provides shelter & privacy.
8. **Maintenance** — use only for its intended purpose (no refuse dumping).

> 🎯 Water seal latrine = **hand-flushed rural latrine · U-bend (trap) holding water = "water seal"** — blocks flies & smell.

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF p. 536

## Q4. Mention the physical changes in a crowded room. (S10,Q3)

### Physical changes in an ill-ventilated / crowded room
1. **Rise in indoor temperature** — from emanation of body heat.
2. **Increase in relative humidity** — due to moisture evaporated from skin & lungs.
3. **Decrease in air movement** — natural movement of air is impeded.
4. **Body odours** — from foul breath, perspiration, dirty clothes; unpleasant smell.
5. **Bacterial pollution** — bacteria (mainly saprophytes) discharged into the air during conversation, coughing, sneezing & loud speaking.

*(Plus chemical changes: CO₂ content increases & O₂ falls due to metabolism.)*

> 🎯 Crowded-room changes = **↑temp, ↑humidity, ↓air movement + body odours + bacterial discharge.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 514–515

## Q5. Define and classify humidity. Mention the health effects of humidity. Name the instrument used to measure humidity. (S23,Q3)

### Humidity — definition & classification
> 🔴 **Humidity (moisture)** = the **amount of water present in a unit volume of air**.

**Two classes:**
1. **Absolute humidity** — the **weight (mass) of water vapour in a unit volume of air.**
2. **Relative humidity** — the **percentage of moisture present in the air**, complete saturation being taken as 100%.

### Health effects of humidity
1. **Permanent exposure to low humidity (below ~30%)** causes **drying of the nasal mucosa**, which may **predispose to respiratory infection**.
2. **High humidity** causes a feeling of being **sticky and discomfort**; sweating is ineffective (impedes heat loss) → heat stress.

### Instruments to measure humidity
1. **Dry and wet bulb hygrometer.**
2. **Sling psychrometer.**
3. **Assmann (aspirated) psychrometer.**

> 🎯 Humidity = **water vapour/unit air** · Abs. = weight; Rel. = % of saturation · High → sticky/discomfort, Low <30% → dry mucosa → infection · Measure = **hygrometer/psychrometer.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF p. 516

## Q6. What are the audible and non-audible effects of noise? (S24,Q3)

### Auditory (audible) effects of noise
1. **Auditory fatigue — noise-induced temporary threshold shift (NITTS):** temporary loss, greatest at **90 dB region, 4000–6000 Hz**.
2. **Deafness:**
   - Daily exposure **up to ~85 dB** is about the limit tolerated without substantial hearing damage.
   - **Repeated/continuous exposure ~100 dB** → permanent deafness.
   - **Single exposure >160 dB** → rupture of **tympanic membrane**.

### Non-auditory effects of noise
1. **Interference with speech communication.**
2. **Annoyance.**
3. **Decreased efficiency** (of work/sleep).
4. **Physiological changes** — rise of **blood pressure, heart rate, breathing rate, intracranial pressure**; increased sweating; giddiness, nausea, fatigue.
5. **Economic loss.**

> 🎯 Auditory = **temporary threshold shift → deafness (85 dB limit, >160 dB ruptures TM)** · Non-auditory = **speech interference, annoyance, BP/HR rise, inefficiency.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF p. 519

## Q7. Mention the source of radiation. State the acute and long term (delayed) effects of radiation. (S25,Q3)

### Sources of radiation exposure
**A. Natural:**
1. **Cosmic rays.**
2. Environmental — **terrestrial & atmospheric.**
3. Internal — **Potassium-40, Carbon-14.**

**B. Man-made:**
1. **Medical & dental — X-rays, radio-isotopes.**
2. Occupational exposure.
3. **Nuclear — radioactive fallout.**
4. Miscellaneous — TV sets, radioactive-dial watches, isotope-tagged products, luminous markers.

### Effects of radiation
**A. Acute (immediate) general effects:**
1. **CNS syndrome** (≥10,000 rads) — headache, nausea-vomiting, papillotedema, raised intra-ocular pressure.
2. **Gastro-intestinal syndrome** — anorexia, nausea, vomiting, loose/mucoid stools, melaena.
3. **Bone-marrow syndrome** (≥ ~100–500 rads) — **pancytopenia**, ↓Hb%, WBC & platelets.

**B. Long-term / delayed effects:** aplastic anaemia, chronic radiodermatitis, **cataract, sterility, congenital malformation**, carcinoma (leukaemia, lymphoma, bone/skin tumours), **shortening of life**.

**C. Genetic effects:** chromosome & point mutations.

> 🎯 Radiation sources = **cosmic/terrestrial/internal (natural) + X-ray/nuclear/occupational (man-made)** · Acute = **CNS/GI/bone-marrow syndrome** · Delayed = **aplastic anaemia, cataract, leukaemia, sterility.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 520–522

## Q8. Enumerate the effects of noise on health. What do you mean by acute radiation sickness syndrome? (S29,Q3)

### Effects of noise on health
**Auditory:** auditory fatigue (NITTS) at 90 dB/4000–6000 Hz; **deafness** (>85 dB daily exposure, ~100 dB repeated, >160 dB single → TM rupture).
**Non-auditory:** interference with speech, annoyance, reduced efficiency; **physiological changes** — ↑ BP, heart rate, respiration, intracranial pressure, sweating; giddiness, nausea, fatigue; economic loss.

### Acute radiation sickness syndrome
> 🔴 **Acute radiation sickness / acute radiation syndrome** = the illness that develops after **whole-body exposure to a large, single dose of ionizing radiation**, occurring in three phases — **prodromal** (nausea, vomiting, malaise within hours) → **latent** (apparent recovery) → **manifest illness** — depending on the dose:
- **CNS syndrome (≥10,000 rads)** — headache, vomiting, papillotedema; death in hours–days.
- **Gastro-intestinal syndrome** — anorexia, nausea, vomiting, mucoid stools, melaena; water/electrolyte imbalance.
- **Bone-marrow (haemopoietic) syndrome** — **pancytopenia**, ↓Hb, WBC, platelets → infection & haemorrhage.

> 🎯 Acute radiation syndrome = **CNS / GI / bone-marrow syndromes after massive whole-body irradiation.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 519, 521–522

## Q9. List the major measures of controlling noise in an industry. (S30,Q3)

### Control of (industrial) noise
1. **City/industrial planning** — earmark **separate zones for industries** away from residential areas; **green belts** (thickly planted trees/bushes) between industry & dwellings.
2. **Reduction at source** — proper design, maintenance & mounting of machines (sound-proofing, dampening, substitution of noisy with quieter processes).
3. **Acoustic insulation of buildings** — sound-proofing of workshops/dwellings near noise.
4. **Protection of exposed workers** — **ear-muffs / ear-plugs (hearing protectors)** for all workers consistently exposed to loud noise; **periodic audiometric check-ups**.
5. **Rotation of workers / limiting duration of exposure** (daily exposure ≤85 dB).
6. **Legislation** — enforcement of permissible noise limits.
7. **Health education** — awareness of noise as a community & occupational hazard.

> 🎯 Industrial noise control = **zoning + green belt + sound-proofing + ear protectors + ≤85 dB + legislation + education.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 519–520
