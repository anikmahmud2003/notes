## Q1. Name the mechanism of disease transmission and biological transmission. (S5,Q6)

### Mechanism / mechanism of disease transmission
> 📌 The **infection chain** transmits communicable diseases from the reservoir (source) to the susceptible host through the **mode of transmission.** Transmission is of **two main mechanisms:**

**A) Direct transmission:**
1. **Direct contact** — skin to skin / mucosa to mucosa (e.g., STD & AIDS, leprosy, skin & eye infection).
2. **Droplet infection** — spray of droplets of saliva & nasopharyngeal secretions on coughing, sneezing, speaking (e.g., common cold, diphtheria, whooping cough).
3. **Contact with soil** (e.g., hookworm, tetanus, mycosis).
4. **Inoculation into skin or mucosa** (e.g., rabies by dog bite, hepatitis B by needles).
5. **Transplacental / vertical** (e.g., rubella, syphilis, CMV, HIV).

**B) Indirect transmission** — mainly by **5 'F's: flies, fingers, fomites, food & fluid:**
1. **Vehicle borne** — water & food borne (typhoid, cholera, hepatitis A), blood borne (hepatitis B, malaria).
2. **Vector borne** — mechanical (diarrhoea) & biological (malaria, plague).
3. **Air borne** — droplet nuclei (TB, influenza), dust (TB, Q fever), soil by wind (fungal spores).
4. **Fomite borne** (diphtheria, bacillary dysentery).
5. **Unclean hands & fingers** (typhoid, dysentery, hepatitis A).

### Biological transmission
> 🔴 **Biological transmission** = when the disease agent **multiplies or undergoes some developmental change (with or without multiplication)** in the arthropod (vector) host.

**Types:**
1. **Propagative** — agent **multiplies, no cyclical change** in the vector; e.g., **plague bacilli in rat fleas.**
2. **Cyclo-propagative** — agent undergoes **cyclical change and multiplies**; e.g., **malaria parasite in anopheles mosquito.**
3. **Cyclo-developmental** — agent undergoes cyclical change, **does not multiply**; e.g., **filarial parasite in culex mosquito, guinea worm embryo in cyclops.**

> 🎯 Biological transmission = **Propagative (plague) · Cyclo-propagative (malaria) · Cyclo-developmental (filariasis).**

> 📖 Ch-05, Endeavour PDF pp. 117–118 · Ch-07, Endeavour PDF p. 282

## Q2. Differentiate communicable and infectious disease. (S27,Q2)

### Definitions (Park)
> 🔴 **Infectious disease** = a **clinical manifest disease of man or animals resulting from an infection** (an agent multiplies in the host, signs & symptoms develop).
>
> 🔴 **Communicable disease** = an **illness due to a specific infectious agent or its toxic products**, capable of being **directly or indirectly transmitted** from man to man, animal to animal, or from the environment (air, dust, soil, water, food) to man or animal.

### Difference
| Point | Infectious disease | Communicable disease |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Disease resulting from an infection (agent present) | Infectious disease **capable of being transmitted** from person to person / animal / environment |
| **Transmissibility** | Necessary to occur (agent + host) but **not necessarily transmissible** to others | **Readily transmitted** (directly or indirectly) |
| **Examples** | Tetanus, mycosis (from soil — not spread man-to-man) | Measles, chickenpox, cholera, typhoid |
| **Relationship** | Broader term — includes all | A **sub-set** of infectious diseases (all communicable diseases are infectious, but **not all infectious diseases are communicable**) |

> 🎯 Communicable = **infectious + transmissible** (can spread from host to host).

> 📖 Ch-03, Endeavour PDF pp. 96–97

## Q3. Classify mode of transmission of disease with example. (S28,Q2)

### Modes of transmission of diseases
**A) Direct transmission:**
1. **Direct contact** — skin to skin / mucosa to mucosa; e.g., **STD & AIDS, leprosy, skin & eye infection.**
2. **Droplet infection** — spray of droplets (<5 mm, spread 30–60 cm); e.g., **common cold, diphtheria, whooping cough.**
3. **Contact with soil** — e.g., **hookworm, tetanus, mycosis.**
4. **Inoculation into skin or mucosa** — e.g., **rabies (dog bite), hepatitis B (needles & syringes).**
5. **Transplacental / vertical** — e.g., **rubella, toxoplasma, CMV, HSV, VZV, syphilis, HBV, HIV.**

**B) Indirect transmission** — mainly by **5 'F's: flies, fingers, fomites, food & fluid:**
1. **Vehicle borne:**
   - Water & food borne — **acute diarrhoea, typhoid fever, cholera, hepatitis A, food poisoning.**
   - Blood borne — **hepatitis B, malaria, syphilis.**
   - Organ transplantation — **CMV infection.**
2. **Vector borne:**
   - **Mechanical** — e.g., **diarrhoea** (house fly).
   - **Biological** — propagative (**plague**), cyclo-propagative (**malaria**), cyclo-developmental (**filariasis**).
3. **Air borne:**
   - **Droplet nuclei** (1–10 µm, dried residue of droplets) — **TB, influenza, chicken pox, measles.**
   - **Dust** — **TB, pneumonia, Q fever.**
   - From soil by wind — **fungal spores.**
4. **Fomite borne** — inanimate articles (soiled clothes, towels, cups, spoons); e.g., **diphtheria, typhoid fever, bacillary dysentery.**
5. **Unclean hands and fingers** — e.g., **typhoid fever, dysentery, hepatitis A, intestinal parasites.**

> 🎯 Transmission = **Direct (contact · droplet · soil · inoculation · vertical) + Indirect (5 F's: flies, fingers, fomites, food & fluid).**

> 📖 Ch-05, Endeavour PDF pp. 117–118
