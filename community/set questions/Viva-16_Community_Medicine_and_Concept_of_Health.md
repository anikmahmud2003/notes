<summary><b>Q1. Define social medicine &amp; preventive medicine. (S1,Q1)</b></summary>

### Social medicine
> 📌 By derivation, **social medicine** is the study of man as a **social being in his total environment** (physical, biological and socio-economic).

### Preventive medicine
> 📌 **Preventive medicine** is defined as *"the art and science of **health promotion, disease prevention, disability limitation and rehabilitation**"*.

> 🎯 Social medicine = **man + total environment** · Preventive medicine = **promotion + prevention + disability limitation + rehabilitation.**

> 📖 Ch-01, Endeavour PDF pp. 16–17

<summary><b>Q2. List the determinants of health. (S2,Q1)</b></summary>

### Determinants / factors of health
The factors which influence health, lying both **within the individual and externally in the society**, are collectively known as **determinants of health**:

1. **Biological determinants** — genetically determined traits (e.g. chromosomal anomalies, errors of metabolism, mental retardation, some diabetes).
2. **Behavioural & socio-cultural conditions** — way of life, cultural pattern, personal habits (harmful life style e.g. smoking; healthy life style e.g. adequate nutrition, sleep, physical activity).
3. **Environment** — internal (tissue, organ systems) & external:
   - **Physical:** air, water, soil pollution; heat, light, radiation; food & refuse.
   - **Biological:** human beings, plants, animals, micro-organisms, insects/birds.
   - **Psychosocial:** habits, moral values, occupation, religion, education, housing.
4. **Socio-economic conditions** — income/poor standard of living, education, occupation, political system.
5. **Health services** — comprehensive, equitable, reaching the periphery, accessible, acceptable & affordable.
6. **Aging of the population.**
7. **Gender.**
8. **Other factors** — food & agriculture, education, industry, social welfare.

> 🎯 Determinants = **Biological · Behavioural/socio-cultural · Environmental · Socio-economic · Health services · Aging · Gender + others.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 33–34

<summary><b>Q3. What is public health? Mention the areas of public health. (S3,Q1)</b></summary>

### Definition of public health
> The WHO expert committee, adapting **C.E.A. Winslow's (1920)** definition, has defined public health as:
> *"The science and art of **preventing disease, prolonging life, and promoting health and efficiency** through organized community efforts."*

### Areas / components of public health (from the definition)
1. **Sanitation of the environment.**
2. **Control of communicable infections.**
3. **Education of the individual in personal hygiene.**
4. **Organization of medical & nursing services** for early diagnosis and preventive treatment of disease.
5. **Development of social machinery** to ensure for every individual a standard of living adequate for the maintenance of health.

> 🎯 Areas of public health = **environmental sanitation · control of communicable infections · personal hygiene education · medical & nursing services · social machinery.**

> 📖 Ch-01, Endeavour PDF p. 20

<summary><b>Q4. What is positive health? List the function of a doctor &amp; mention the responsibility of a physician. (S7,Q1)</b></summary>

### Positive health
> 🔴 The state of **positive health** implies the notion of *"perfect functioning"* of the body & mind — it conceptualizes health **biologically, psychologically and socially**. A person who enjoys health at **three planes (good physical, mental and social well-being)** is said to be in a state of positive health.

### Functions of a doctor
1. Provision of **comprehensive health care** (preventive, promotive, curative & rehabilitative) to the individual, family & community.
2. **First contact / primary care** — diagnosis & treatment of common illnesses.
3. **Emergency life-saving services.**
4. **Health education** of patients & community.
5. **Referral** of complicated cases to higher centres & follow-up.
6. Participation in **national health programmes** (EPI, NID, family planning, MCH).
7. **Epidemiological surveillance** — reporting of notifiable diseases.
8. **Medico-legal duties & certification** (death, fitness, disability).

### Responsibilities of a physician
1. **To the patient** — care, confidentiality, informed consent.
2. **To the community** — promotion of public health & prevention of disease.
3. **To the profession** — professional ethics, collaboration with colleagues.
4. **To the state & self** — obeying law, maintaining professional standards & competence.

> 🎯 Doctor's watchwords = **comprehensive care · first contact · education · referral · national programmes · certification.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF p. 31 (positive health); functions/responsibilities — standard text (Park)

<summary><b>Q5. Define ecology &amp; ecological triad. (S8,Q1)</b></summary>

### Ecology
> 🔴 **Ecology** is *"the science of mutual relationship between **living organisms and their environments**"*. Health, in ecological terms, is a state of **dynamic equilibrium between man and his environment**.

### Ecological / epidemiological triad
> 🔴 The triad concept says: for a disease process to occur, there must be a unique combination of events — a **harmful agent** coming into contact with a **susceptible host** in a **suitable environment**.

- The three factors — **agent, host and environment** — form the corners of the epidemiological (ecological) triad.
- Occurrence of disease can be blocked by **intercepting the triangle at any of its three sides** (agent, host or environment) or at any linkage between them.

> 🎯 Triad = **Agent + Host + Environment** — break any corner to block disease.

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 34, 54

<summary><b>Q6. Define community medicine &amp; public health. State the spiritual dimension of health. (S16,Q1)</b></summary>

### Community medicine
> 📌 Community medicine (WHO, 1972) is *"a system of delivery of **comprehensive health care** to the people **by a health team** in order to **improve the health of the community**"*. Comprehensive health care = preventive + promotive + curative + rehabilitative care *from womb to tomb*.

### Public health
> 📌 The science and art of **preventing disease, prolonging life, and promoting health and efficiency** through organized community efforts (Winslow / WHO) — it is a **part** of community medicine.

### Spiritual dimension of health
> 📌 **Spiritual health** refers to that part of the individual **which reaches out and strives for meaning & purpose in life** — the intangible force that *transcends physiology and psychology*.

> 🎯 Spiritual health = **striving for meaning & purpose in life** (transcends body & mind).

> 📖 Ch-01, Endeavour PDF pp. 16, 20; Ch-02, Endeavour PDF p. 30

### Criteria of a mentally healthy person
One is labelled mentally healthy if he/she:
1. Is **free from internal conflicts.**
2. Is **well adjusted** (to self, others & environment).
3. **Searches for identity.**
4. Has a **strong sense of self-esteem.**
5. **Knows himself** — his needs, problems & goals.
6. Has **good self-control.**
7. **Faces problems and tries to solve them intelligently.**

> 🎯 Mentally healthy = **conflict-free · well-adjusted · self-esteem + self-control · problem-solving.**

<summary><b>Q8. What do you mean by community diagnosis &amp; treatment. (S18,Q1)</b></summary>

### Community diagnosis
> 🔴 It is *"the pattern of disease in a community described in terms of the important factors which influence this pattern"* — i.e. **identification & quantification of health problems** in a community in terms of mortality & morbidity rates/ratios, and identification of their correlates for defining groups **at risk or in need of health care**.

**Data for community diagnosis:**
1. Age & sex distribution; distribution by social groups.
2. Vital statistical rates — birth & death rates.
3. Incidence & prevalence of important diseases.

### Community treatment
> 🔴 Community treatment (community health action) = *"the **sum of steps decided upon to meet the health needs of the community**, taking into account the resources available and the wishes of the people, as revealed by community diagnosis"*.

- It must (1) effectively utilize all resources, (2) co-ordinate inter-sectorally, (3) encourage full community participation.
- **Example:** improvement of water supplies, immunization, health education, control of specific diseases, health legislation.
- **Levels of action:** individual → family → community.

> 🎯 Diagnosis = **identify & quantify health problems** → Treatment = **planned action using resources + community participation.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 65–66

<summary><b>Q10. Mention the important causes for mental illness. (S19,Q7)</b></summary>

### Important causes of mental illness
1. **Hereditary / genetic factors** — schizophrenia, manic-depressive psychosis, mental retardation.
2. **Organic / somatic brain factors** — head injury, intracranial infections & tumours, cerebral vascular disease, chronic physical illness.
3. **Psychological factors** — stress, anxiety, emotional deprivation in childhood, broken home, bereavement, maladjustment.
4. **Social & environmental factors** — poverty, unemployment, illiteracy, rapid urbanization, migration, breakdown of family & social support.
5. **Structural / constitutional factors** — personality make-up, aging of the population.
6. **Substance abuse** — alcohol and drug dependence.
7. **Societal changes** — excessive demands of modern life, competition, isolation.

> 🎯 Causes = **heredity · organic brain disease · psychological stress · socio-economic problems · aging · addiction.**

> 📖 Standard text (Park, Mental Health); Ch-18, Endeavour PDF pp. 582–583; Ch-08 (mental retardation causes, p. 305)

<summary><b>Q11. Mention the changing concept of public health. Define health team and mention its formation. (S19,Q1)</b></summary>

### Changing concept of public health (4 phases)
| Phase | Period | Goal / Aims |
|---|---|---|
| **Disease control phase** | 1880–1920 | Control of disease — control of man's physical environment (safe water, clean surroundings, wholesome housing, control of offensive trades) |
| **Health promotional phase** | 1920–1960 | Promotion of individual & community health — decrease mortality/morbidity, increase life expectancy (MCH, School & Industrial health services) |
| **Social engineering phase** | 1960–1980 | Prevention, treatment & rehabilitation of chronic diseases & behavioral disorders (health education, life-style modification) |
| **Health for All phase** | 1981–2000 | Attainment of a level of health that permits a socially & economically productive life |

### Health team
> 🔴 **Health team** = *"a group of persons who share a common health goal and common objectives, determined by community needs, toward which each member contributes according to his/her competence and skills"*.

**Formation / structure:**
1. **Health professionals** (full professional training) — physician (doctor), nurse.
2. **Auxiliary staff** (trained, less than full qualification, supervised by professionals) — nurses, pharmacist, medical assistant, lab technician, radiographer.
3. **Ancillary staff** — sweeper, ambulance driver, cook.

> 🎯 Health team = **Professionals + Auxiliaries + Ancillary staff.**

> 📖 Ch-01, Endeavour PDF pp. 20–23

<summary><b>Q12. Who are the responsible agencies for health. Describe spectrum of health. (S20,Q1)</b></summary>

### Responsible agencies for health
Health is both a personal responsibility and a major public concern — it needs the joint efforts of the whole social fabric:
1. **Individual responsibility** — observance of rules of diet, sleep, exercise; attention to personal hygiene.
2. **Community responsibility** — facilities, manpower, logistic support & funds; participation in planning, management & evaluation.
3. **State responsibility** — health is a constitutional right; government is legally bound to provide health care for every citizen.
4. **International responsibility** — co-ordination of different governments (e.g. smallpox eradication), WHO as central co-ordinating agency, UNICEF as another co-ordinating agency.

### Spectrum of health
> ```text
> Positive health → Better health → Freedom from sickness → Unrecognized sickness → Mild sickness → Severe sickness → Death
> ```
- The **health of an individual is not static** — it fluctuates within a range from optimum well-being to total dysfunction (**death**).
- The lowest point of the spectrum is **death**; the highest corresponds to the WHO definition of **positive health**.

> 🎯 Spectrum = **Positive health ⇄ Death** · Agencies = **Individual + Community + State + International.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 32, 35

<summary><b>Q15. Tell the major types if mental illness and mention important causes of mental illness. (S23,Q7)</b></summary>

### Major types of mental illness
1. **Psychoses** — schizophrenia, affective disorders (depression, mania) — severe, loss of insight & contact with reality.
2. **Neuroses** — anxiety neurosis, hysteria, phobia, obsessive-compulsive neurosis — insight preserved.
3. **Personality disorders.**
4. **Psychophysiological (psychosomatic) disorders** — e.g. peptic ulcer, hypertension, asthma.
5. **Mental subnormality / mental retardation** (organic brain damage, genetic).
6. **Addictions** — alcoholism, drug dependence.
7. **Mental disorders of old age** — senile dementia, Alzheimer's disease.

### Important causes of mental illness
1. Hereditary/genetic factors.
2. Organic brain disease (head injury, infections, tumours, CVA).
3. Psychological factors (stress, anxiety, childhood emotional deprivation, broken home).
4. Social & environmental factors (poverty, unemployment, urbanization, loss of social support).
5. Substance abuse (alcohol, drugs).
6. Aging of the population.

> 🎯 Types = **Psychosis · Neurosis · Personality & psychosomatic disorders · Mental retardation · Addiction.**

> 📖 Standard text (Park, Mental Health); Ch-18, Endeavour PDF pp. 582–583

<summary><b>Q16. List the characteristics of ideal health care. (S26,Q1)</b></summary>

### Characteristics of ideal health care
1. **Appropriateness (relevance)** — whether the service is needed in relation to essential human needs, priorities & policies.
2. **Comprehensiveness** — optimum mixture of preventive, curative & promotional services.
3. **Adequacy** — service proportionate to requirement.
4. **Availability** — ratio between population and health facility (population per centre, doctor-population ratio).
5. **Accessibility** — geographic, economic & cultural accessibility.
6. **Affordability** — cost within the means of the individual and the state.
7. **Feasibility** — operational efficiency, logistic support, manpower & material resources.

> 🎯 Ideal health care = **Appropriate · Comprehensive · Adequate · Available · Accessible · Affordable · Feasible.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF p. 45

<summary><b>Q18. Enumerate the common mental health problem in Bangladesh. Tell in short the preventive strategies for mental disorders. (S30,Q7)</b></summary>

### Common mental health problems in Bangladesh
1. **Neuroses** — anxiety neurosis (commonest).
2. **Depression** (affective psychoses).
3. **Schizophrenia** — chronic psychoses.
4. **Epilepsy.**
5. **Mental retardation.**
6. **Drug addiction** & substance abuse.
7. **Alcoholism** & tobacco dependence.
8. **Suicide** & deliberate self-harm.
9. Mental & behavioral disorders of old age (dementia).

### Preventive strategies for mental disorders
**1. Primary prevention (prevention of occurrence):**
- Proper orientation to **family life & marriage** (marital adjustment).
- **Healthy child-rearing practices** — emotional security in early childhood.
- **Education of parents & teachers**; diminishing stresses and demands of the school.
- **Health education** — fostering healthy family relationships & social support.
- **Socio-economic uplift** — reduction of poverty, unemployment & urban slums.
- Avoidance of alcohol & drugs.

**2. Secondary prevention (early diagnosis & treatment):**
- Early detection & prompt, adequate treatment of mental disorders.
- Screening of high-risk groups (e.g. families with mentally ill members, postpartum mothers).

**3. Tertiary prevention (rehabilitation):**
- **Social & vocational rehabilitation** of patients.
- **After-care & follow-up** — halfway homes, community mental health services.

> 🎯 Prevention = **Primary (family life, child-rearing, education) → Secondary (early Rx) → Tertiary (rehabilitation).**

> 📖 Standard text (Park, Mental Health); Ch-18, Endeavour PDF pp. 582–583 (complements Ch-02, p. 30)
