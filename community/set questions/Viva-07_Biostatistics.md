## Q1. Define Biostatistics & vital statistics. What are the common sources of data? (S1,Q5) · also (S30,Q5)

### Biostatistics
> 🔴 **Biostatistics** is the discipline concerned with **the collection, organization, analysis, presentation and interpretation of numerical facts/data** arising out of problems of **biology, medicine and public health** (health sciences).

### Vital statistics
> 🔴 **Vital statistics** is a branch of biostatistics that deals with the **statistical aspects / numerical records of "vital events" of human life** —
> 1. **Fertility** (births — natality)
> 2. **Mortality** (deaths)
> 3. **Morbidity** (sickness)
> 4. **Marriage**
> 5. **Divorce**

> 📌 Vital events are those concerning the **beginning and the end of life** (births, deaths, marriages, divorces, sickness) — obtained from **registered / routine records.**

### Common sources of data (health information)
1. **Population census.**
2. **Vital statistics / registration of vital events** (births, deaths, marriages).
3. **Mortality statistics** (death certificate, cause specific).
4. **Morbidity statistics.**
5. **Medical records linkage.**
6. **Epidemiological surveillance.**
7. **Other health-service records** — OPD, primary health centres, subcentres, polyclinics, private practitioners, MCH centres.
8. **Environmental health data.**
9. **Health manpower statistics.**
10. **Population surveys.**
11. **Other routine statistics related to health** — demographic, economic, social-security schemes.
12. **Non-quantifiable information.**
> 📌 International sources of vital statistics: WHO **"Epidemiological & Vital Statistics reports"** and UN **"Demographic Yearbook."**

> 🎯 Vital events = birth · death · marriage · divorce · sickness. Biostatistics = **its science, vital statistics = its life-events record.**

### Data
> 🔴 **Data** = sets of values recorded on one or more observational units; **discrete observations of attributes or events** (facts of observation stripped of opinion).

## Q3. What do you mean by population & sample. Mention the major techniques of sampling (S3,Q5)

### Population
> 🔴 **Population (universe)** = the **totality of all individuals (or objects/measurements) about which the data are to be collected** — a complete set of individuals having some common observable characteristic.

### Sample
> 🔴 **Sample** = a **part of the population** about which information is actually obtained, with a view to studying the population characteristics. It must be **representative of the population** (ensured by **random selection** of units).
- **Sample unit:** each member of the population from which data are collected.
- **Sampling frame:** the list of all sampling units in the population survey.
- **Sampling:** the procedure through which a sample is collected from the population (examining only a part of the universe).

### Major techniques of sampling

**A) Probability (random/chance) sampling:**
1. **Simple random sampling** — population small, homogenous, readily available; **each unit has an equal chance** of being drawn (lottery method / table of random numbers).
2. **Systematic sampling** — first unit selected at random, subsequent units at a **fixed interval** (e.g., filaria survey: every 10th house; 4, 14, 24...).
3. **Stratified sampling** — sample deliberately drawn so each portion represents a **corresponding stratum** of the universe (e.g., Muslims, Hindus, Christians).
4. **Multistage sampling** — sampling procedures carried out in **several stages** using random techniques.
5. **Cluster sampling** — clusters (villages, wards, slums, schools) are randomly selected groups; used for EPI/UCI vaccination-coverage evaluation.
6. **Multiphase sampling** — part of information from the whole sample, part from a **sub-sample.**

**B) Non-probability sampling:**
1. **Purposive/deliberate/judgment sampling** — items selected deliberately by the researcher.
2. **Quota sampling** — interviewers given a quota to be filled from different strata.
3. **Accidental sampling** — collected without the intention of the researcher at that occasion.
4. **Convenient sampling** — according to the convenience of the researcher.

> 🎯 Probability = **equal chance** (simple-random seed of all the rest). Non-probability = **researcher's will.**

> 📖 Ch-20, Endeavour PDF pp. 599–601

## Q5. What are the common methods of data collection? (S6,Q4) · also (S18,Q5) · (S22,Q4)

### Methods / types of data collection
**1. Observational method** — most commonly used; information obtained by the **investigator's own direct observation** without asking the respondent.
- Types — by planning: **structured / unstructured** observation · by participation: **participant / non-participant / disguised** observation · by control: **controlled / uncontrolled** observation.
- **Advantages:** subjective bias limited · gives what is **currently happening** · independent of respondent's willingness.
- **Disadvantages:** expensive · information very limited · unforeseen factors may interfere.

**2. Interview method** — presentation of **oral-verbal stimuli** with reply in oral-verbal response.
- Types: **personal interview** & **telephone interview.**

**3. Questionnaire method** — a questionnaire is **sent** to the persons concerned with a request to answer & return it (popular for big enquiries).

**4. Schedule method** — like questionnaire, but schedules are **filled in by specially appointed enumerators** (personal interview).

**5. Other methods:**
1. Warranty card · 2. Distributor or store audits · 3. Pantry audits · 4. Consumer panel · 5. Use of mechanical device · 6. Projective technique · 7. Depth interview · 8. Content analysis.

> 🎯 Four big methods = **Observe · Interview · Questionnaire (post) · Schedule (by enumerator).**

> 📖 Ch-20, Endeavour PDF pp. 590–591

## Q6. Name scales of measurements in biostatistics. (S7,Q5)

### Four basic scales of measurement
| Scale | Feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Nominal scale** | Qualitative variables **not arranged in any definite order** | Marital status (single, married, widowed); sex (male, female) |
| **2. Ordinal scale** | Qualitative variables that **can be ranked**; each rank bears same relationship to the next (position on a ladder) | Social class (I–IV); years of education (0, 1–5, 6–9...) |
| **3. Interval scale** | Equal intervals between classes; **zero does NOT mean absence** (e.g., a room at 20°C is not twice as hot as 10°C) | Temperature in °C |
| **4. Ratio scale** | Has **absolute zero (absence of attribute)**; ratio of numbers = ratio of amounts | Weight (kg), income (tk), mortality rate (deaths/1000) |

> 🎯 **Nominal = name only → Ordinal = order → Interval = equal gaps → Ratio = absolute zero.**

## Q7. What is variable. Classify variable with example. Mention the ways of presenting data. (S8,Q5)

### Variable
> 🔴 **Variable** = a characteristic of a person, object or phenomenon that **can take on different values** (e.g., age, height).

### Classification of variable
**A) Qualitative (categorical) variables** — values expressed as categories / non-numeric narrative values.
- **Example:** doctor, engineer, teacher; urban, rural.

**B) Quantitative (numerical) variables** — values expressed in numbers. Two types:
1. **Continuous variable** — infinite number of possible values in a given interval → **height (cm), weight (kg).**
2. **Discrete variable** — finite number of values in a given interval → **number of hospital beds, number of family members.**

**C) Other types:**
1. **Dependent variable** — depends on / is a consequence of another.
2. **Independent variable** — antecedent to the dependent variable (smoking → lung cancer; smoking = independent, lung cancer = dependent).
3. **Extraneous / confounding variable** — affects the dependent variable but not the purpose of study (alcohol = independent for oesophageal cancer; **smoking = confounding**).

### Ways of presenting data
1. **Tabulation** (master table, simple table, frequency distribution table).
2. **Drawings/diagrams** (bar charts, histograms, frequency polygon & curve, line chart) **&** pie, map, pictogram.
*(Details — Q16.)*

> 🎯 Confounding example for viva: **smoking + alcohol (CA-oesophagus) — smoking is the confounder.**

> 📖 Ch-20, Endeavour PDF pp. 591, 598

## Q8. How can you present your collected data. List the characteristics of a table? (S9,Q5) · also (S27,Q5-part)

### Methods of data presentation (overview)
**A) Tabulation** — three types: 1) **Master table** (all initial readings serially recorded) · 2) **Simple table** (one characteristic fixed, few events) · 3) **Frequency distribution table** (large unsorted data in small manageable groups).
**B) Drawings (charts, diagrams & maps)** — bar chart, histogram, frequency polygon, frequency curve, line chart, scatter diagram, pie/sector diagram, map/spot map, pictogram.
*(Details — Q16.)*

### Characteristics of a table
1. The table should be **numbered** (Table-1, Table-2...).
2. A **title** must be given — brief and self-explanatory.
3. **Headings** of columns or rows should be clear and concise.
4. The data must be presented according to **size or importance; chronologically, alphabetically or geographically.**
5. If percentages or averages are compared, they should be placed **as close as possible.**
6. **No table should be too large.**
7. A **vertical arrangement** is better than a horizontal one.
8. **Foot notes** may be given where necessary, providing explanatory notes or additional information.

> 🎯 Table rules: **numbered + titled + clear headings + ordered + vertical + not too large + footnote.**

## Q9. What is central tendency. What are the measures of central tendency? (S11,Q4)

### Central tendency
> 🔴 With a large number of observations there is generally a **tendency of the observations to cluster around a central point** — this is known as **central tendency.**

### Measures of central tendency (the 3 "statistical averages")
1. **Mean** (arithmetic, geometric, harmonic)
2. **Median**
3. **Mode**

> `Mean = ΣX/N` · `Median = (n+1)/2th` value of ordered series · `Mode = most frequent value`.

> 🎯 Central tendency = **where data cluster** → mean, median, mode.

## Q10. Tell us about mean, median and mode. (S12,Q4) · also (S15,Q4 partial) · (S17,Q4)

### Mean
> 🔴 **Mean** is the **sum of all observations divided by the number of observations** (`X = ΣX/N`).
- Three types: **arithmetic mean** (widely used), **geometric mean**, **harmonic mean.**
- **Example:** pulse of 10 persons — 68, 74, 56, 64, 75, 66, 59, 73, 55, 60 → ΣX = 650, N = 10 → **Mean = 65.**
- **Advantage:** easy to calculate & understand. **Disadvantage:** unduly influenced by abnormal values.

### Median
> 🔴 **Median** = the **middle-most value** of a series when observations are arranged in **ascending or descending order**.
- If **n is odd:** median = {(n + 1)/2}th value.
- If **n is even:** median = arithmetic mean of the two middle values (n/2th & n/2+1th).
- **Example:** series 4, 5, 7, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 (n=9) → 5th value = **10 = median.**

### Mode
> 🔴 **Mode** = the value which **occurs most frequently** (highest frequency in the series).
- **Example:** series 7, 9, 8, 6, 9, 11, 14 → **9 is the mode.**

> 🎯 Odd n → (n+1)/2th · Even n → mean of 2 middle values. Mode = most repeated number.

> 📖 Ch-20, Endeavour PDF p. 603

## Q11. What are the measures of dispersion? (S13,Q4) · also (S14,Q4-part)

### Dispersion
> 🔴 **Dispersion** means the **scattering (spread) of values of the variable** around the center.

### Measures of dispersion
1. **The Range.**
2. **The mean (average) deviation.**
3. **The standard deviation (SD).**

**1) Range** = difference between the **highest and lowest** figure in a given sample.
- Example: DBP 83, 75, 81, 79, 71, 90, 75, 95, 77, 94 — highest 95, lowest 71 → **Range = 95 − 71 = 24.**

**2) Mean deviation** = the **average of the deviations from the arithmetic mean** (ignoring the ± sign) — `MD = Σ(X − x̄)/N`. (Example: total deviation 56 over 10 → MD = 5.6.)

**3) Standard deviation (SD)** = the **"Root-Mean-Square-Deviation"** — denoted by Greek letter **σ**.
- Basic formula: `SD = √[Σ(x − x̄)²/n]`
- For sample size **≤ 30**: `SD = √[Σ(x − x̄)²/(n−1)]`

> 🎯 Dispersion = **Range (simplest) → Mean deviation → SD (best, most used).**

> 📖 Ch-20, Endeavour PDF pp. 606–607

## Q12. What do you mean by range and standard deviation (SD) (S14,Q4)

### Range
> 🔴 **Range** = difference between the **highest and lowest value** in a given sample (simplest measure of dispersion).
- **Example:** DBP of 10 individuals 83, 75, 81, 79, 71, 90, 75, 95, 77, 94 → highest 95, lowest 71 → **range = 95 − 71 = 24 mmHg.**

### Standard deviation (SD)
> 🔴 **SD** = the **Root-Mean-Square Deviation** — the most common & important measure of dispersion; denoted by **σ.**
- Steps of calculation:
  1. Take the deviation of each value from the mean, **(x − x̄)**.
  2. Square each deviation, **(x − x̄)²**.
  3. Add up the squared deviations, **Σ(x − x̄)²**.
  4. Divide by number of observations n [or (n−1) if n ≤ 30].
  5. Take the **square root** → SD.
- **Uses/importance:** summarizes the deviation of a large distribution from the mean in one figure as a unit of variation · indicates whether variation of an individual from the mean is **by chance or real** · helps find the **standard error** · helps find a **suitable sample size** for valid conclusions.

> 🎯 SD steps = **deviate → square → add → divide → square root.**

> 📖 Ch-20, Endeavour PDF pp. 606–608

## Q13. What is sampling. Mention the different types of sampling techniques. (S13,S15,Q5) · also (S16,Q4) · (S24/S26,Q5)

### Sampling
> 🔴 **Sampling** = the procedure through which a **part of the population (sample) is collected** so that the sample represents the population — a technique of securing information about the universe by **examining only a part of it.**
- **Advantages:** reduces cost · reduces time · reduces personnel needed · feasible when population is infinite · enables more accurate measurement by trained investigators · only way when testing **destroys the item** · allows estimation of sampling error.

### Different types of sampling techniques

**A) Probability (random) sampling:**
1. **Simple random sampling** — each unit has an **equal chance** of being drawn (population small, homogenous); lottery method or **table of random numbers**.
2. **Systematic sampling** — first unit random, then every **k-th** unit at regular intervals (e.g., every 10th house; 4, 14, 24, 34...).
3. **Stratified sampling** — universe divided into **strata**; each stratum proportionately represented (Muslims/Hindus/Christians).
4. **Multistage sampling** — sampling carried out in **several stages** using random techniques.
5. **Cluster sampling** — randomly selected **natural groups** (villages, wards, slums, schools); most often used to evaluate **EPI / UCI** vaccination coverage.
6. **Multiphase sampling** — part of information from the whole sample, part from a **sub-sample.**

**B) Non-probability sampling:**
1. **Purposive (deliberate/judgment)** · 2. **Quota** · 3. **Accidental** · 4. **Convenient.**
*(See Q3 for descriptions.)*

> 🎯 Simple-random needs **lots + number + random table/lottery.** Cluster sampling = **the EPI coverage tool.**

## Q16. Mention the ways of data presentation. (S18,Q5) · also (S26/Q28,Q5 part)

### Methods of data presentation

**A) Tabulation** — 3 types of statistical tables:
1. **Master table** — all initial readings (as per designed proforma) serially recorded; must for large data & many attributes.
2. **Simple table** — one characteristic fixed, number of events small.
3. **Frequency distribution table** — most important; large unsorted data in a small manageable number of groups (6–16 groups), can be made from qualitative or quantitative data.

**B) Drawings (charts, diagrams & maps):**
1. **Bar chart** — length of bar proportional to magnitude. Types: **simple** (vertical/horizontal bars separated by spaces) · **multiple/compound** (two or more bars grouped) · **component** (bar divided into parts, each part proportional to its magnitude).
2. **Histogram** — series of blocks; class intervals on horizontal axis, frequency on vertical axis (area ∝ frequency).
3. **Frequency polygon** — derived from histogram by joining midpoints of blocks.
4. **Frequency curve** — polygon loses angulations with large observations → smooth curve.
5. **Line chart / graph** — shows **trend of an event over time** (e.g., height, weight, temp).
6. **Scatter / dot diagram** — shows extent & nature of **correlation between two variables** (height & weight).
7. **Pie / sector diagram** — data in a **circle** (360°); degrees of angles & area of sectors denote relative frequency.
8. **Map diagram / spot map** — geographic distribution of frequencies; **locates the index case in an epidemic.**
9. **Pictogram** — small pictures/symbols used to present data — popular for the man in the street.

> 🎯 Quantitative-grams: **histogram, polygon, curve, line, scatter** · Qualitative-diagrams: **bar, pie, pictogram, map.**

> 📖 Ch-20, Endeavour PDF pp. 591–592

### Mid-year population & its calculation
> 🔴 **Mid-year population** = the population estimated **as on the 1st July** of a year.
- It is taken as the **denominator** for computing vital rates (CBR, CDR, etc.) and **average population** of the year.
- **How to calculate:**
```
Mid-year population =
    Population at the year's beginning + Population at the year's end
                               2
```
or equivalently — **initial population + ½ (births − deaths + immigrants − emigrants)** for the year.

> 🎯 Mid-year = population **on 1st July** = mean of beginning & end populations.

### Classification of bar diagram / charts
A bar chart presents a set of numbers by the **length of a bar**, the length being **proportional to the magnitude** represented.
1. **Simple bar chart** — bars vertical or horizontal, **usually separated by appropriate spaces** for neatness (e.g., population of a country in different years).

> 🎯 Simple = one bar per item · Multiple = grouped bars · Component = bar cut into proportional parts.

## Q21. What are the uses of bio-statistics. (S23,Q4)

### Uses of biostatistics in health sciences
1. **Defining "normal"** — normality of physical & physiological variables (BP, Hb%, birth weight).
2. **Assisting clinical diagnosis** — differential & clinical decisions (does this difference indicate disease?).
3. **Determining the role of environmental factors** in health & disease.
4. **Studying the natural history of disease** & the causes/risk factors of **morbidity & mortality.**
5. **Health planning, evaluation & administration** — needs and outcome of services.
6. **Public health research & surveys** — sampling, inference, interpretation of results.
7. **Vital statistics & demography** — rates, ratios, projections.
8. **Evaluating treatment & preventive measures** (clinical trials, screening).

> 🎯 Biostatistics = **normal · diagnosis · epidemiology · planning · evaluation · research.**

## Q25. What do you mean by Mean, Median and Mode. How pie chart is contructed. (S27,Q5)

*(See Q10.)*

### Construction of pie chart (sector diagram)
> 🔴 **Pie chart** = presentation of data **in a circle subdivided into parts**, to reveal the various components of data.
1. Draw a **circle** (360° at the centre).
2. Express each component as a **percentage of the total.**
3. Convert to degrees: **angle of sector = (component ÷ total) × 360°.**
4. Divide the circle into sectors in proportion to the components and **shade/colour the sectors differently.**
5. This permits **comparison between different components** of the same phenomenon.
> (Example: ANC utilization 50% urban · 30% rural · 20% slum → angles 180°, 108°, 72°.)

> 🎯 Pie angle = **(value ÷ total) × 360°.**

> 📖 Ch-20, Endeavour PDF pp. 595–596, 603
