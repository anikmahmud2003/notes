## Q1. Classify ARI. (S12,Q2)

### Classification of ARI (Acute Respiratory Infection)
**A. Anatomical classification:**
1. **Acute Upper Respiratory Tract Infections (AURI):** common cold, **otitis media**, pharyngitis.
2. **Acute Lower Respiratory Tract Infections (ALRI):** epiglottitis, laryngitis, laryngo-tracheitis, **bronchitis, bronchiolitis, pneumonia**.

**B. Classification according to severity of illness (WHO, 2 months–5 years — case management oriented):**
| Class | Features |
|---|---|
| 1. **Very severe disease** | As in severe + **cyanosis**, unable to drink, lethargic/unconscious, severe respiratory distress |
| 2. **Severe pneumonia** | Persistent cough, **chest indrawing**, RR > 70/min (infants) / > 50/min, stridor, nasal flaring, grunting |
| 3. **Pneumonia (not severe)** | Cough/difficult breathing + **fast breathing** (RR ≥ 50/min in 2–11 months; ≥ 40/min in 12–59 months) |
| 4. **No pneumonia (cough or cold)** | Cough/runny nose, **no fast breathing**, no chest indrawing |

**C. Management-oriented classification (simplified):**
1. **Severe** — persistent cough, chest indrawing, RR > 70/min, stridor.
2. **Very severe** — as severe + **cyanosis**.
3. **Moderate** — as severe but **RR ~50/min**.
4. **Mild** — cough & cold, RR < 50/min.

> 🎯 ARI = **AURI (cold/otitis/pharyngitis) + ALRI (bronchitis/bronchiolitis/pneumonia)** · WHO severity = **no pneumonia → pneumonia → severe → very severe.**

## Q2. How can you prevent ARI in rural community? (S13,Q2)

### Prevention & control of ARI in a rural community
1. **Improved living conditions** — avoid crowding in living & sleeping quarters; **ensure proper (cross) ventilation**.
2. **Better nutritional care** — prevent malnutrition; **exclusive breast-feeding** up to 6 months.
3. **Reduction of indoor air pollution / smoke pollution** — proper kitchen smoke outlet (avoid smoke inhalation).
4. **Provision of better MCH care** & immunization of children (measles, pertussis, diphtheria, Hib).
5. **Hydration maintenance** during illness (fluids).
6. **Health education of families** — recognize warning signs of pneumonia & seek care early (referral knowledge to mothers).
7. **Personal hygiene** and **sanitary disposal of mouth & nose discharges**.
8. **Concurrent disinfection of eating & drinking utensils.**
9. Early recognition & **prompt treatment of cases** (case management, antibiotics for pneumonia).

> 🎯 ARI prevention = **ventilation + nutrition & breast-feeding + immunization + less crowding + early pneumonia recognition.**

## Q3. How can you assess and manage a case of moderate dehydrate patient? (S24,Q2)

> "Moderate (some) dehydration" = **5–10% body weight loss (50–100 ml/kg)**.

### Assessment of dehydration (WHO plan)
| Points | Some dehydration (moderate) |
|---|---|
| **Look at:** general condition | **Restless, irritable** |
| Eyes | **Sunken** |
| Tears | **Absent** |
| Mouth & tongue | **Dry** |
| **Feel:** skin pinch | Goes back **slowly** |
| **Ask:** thirst | **Thirsty, drinks eagerly** |
| **Decide** | If **2 or more signs, including at least one "sign"** (restless/irritable, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly, skin-pinch slow) → **SOME (MODERATE) DEHYDRATION** |

- Fluid deficit 50–100 ml/kg; in adults/adolescents add "absent radial pulse (weak volume), low BP" as severe signs.

### Management (Treatment Plan B — ORS at ORT corner)
1. Amount of ORS: **75 ml/kg body weight within 4 hours**, irrespective of age.
2. Give ORS slowly — **teaspoonful** for infants, sips for older children; continue feeding (breast milk) between ORS.
3. **Reassess after 4 hours:**
   - If **improved (no sign)** → treat as "no dehydration" (Plan A — home ORS after each loose stool).
   - If **still some dehydration** → repeat Plan B.
   - If **worsened → severe dehydration** → refer/hospitalize for **IV cholera saline**.
4. Continue **zinc supplementation** (20 mg/day × 10–14 days) and feeding (extra meal) during & after rehydration.
5. Teach the mother signs of dehydration & how to prepare/use ORS (referral knowledge).

> 🎯 **Moderate dehydration = ORS 75 ml/kg in 4 hours → reassess 4-hourly.** Signs = **irritable + sunken eyes + thirsty + delayed skin tuck.**

## Q4. Define diarrhoea and its causative organism. (S25,Q2)

### Definition of diarrhoea
> 🔴 **Diarrhoea** = passage of **loose, liquid or watery stools three or more times a day**. However, it is the **recent change in the consistency & character of stools** (rather than mere number) that is important (e.g. in breast-fed infants).

**Diarrhoeal diseases** = a group of diseases in which the **predominant symptom is diarrhoea** (convenient expression; not a nosological entity).

### Causative organisms of diarrhoea
**A. Infective causes:**
1. **Viruses:** **Rotavirus** (commonest overall cause of acute watery diarrhoea in children), astrovirus, adenovirus, coronavirus, Norwalk-group viruses.
2. **Bacteria:** **Vibrio cholerae**, **E. coli** (ETEC, EPEC, EIEC), **Shigella** (commonest cause of bloody diarrhoea/dysentery), **Campylobacter jejuni**, *Vibrio parahaemolyticus*, *Bacillus cereus*, *Salmonella* (non-typhoid).
3. **Parasites/others:** **Entamoeba histolytica**, **Giardia intestinalis (lamblia)**, *Cryptosporidium*, *Trichuris*, intestinal worms.

**B. Non-infective causes:** food poisoning, drugs (antibiotics, laxatives), allergy, psychogenic factors.

**Most common in Bangladesh:** Rotavirus, **ETEC**, V. cholerae O1/O139, Shigella, Campylobacter, non-typhoid Salmonella, EPEC.

> 🎯 Diarrhoea = **≥3 loose/watery stools/day (change in consistency more important)** · Causes = **Rotavirus, V. cholerae, E. coli, Shigella, Campylobacter, E. histolytica.**

## Q5. Classify food poisoning. What are the common cardinal features of food poisoning outbreak? (S27,Q2)

### Definition & classification of food poisoning
> 🔴 **Food poisoning** = **acute gastro-enteritis** caused by ingestion of food or drink contaminated with **living bacteria / their toxins / inorganic chemical substances / poisons from plants & animals**.

**Classification:**
**1. Non-bacterial food poisoning:**
- Plant poisons (e.g. poisonous mushrooms, alkaloids).
- Animal poisons/sea-food (fish, poisonous shellfish).
- **Chemical poisons** — arsenic, mercury, cadmium, fertilizers, pesticides.

**2. Bacterial food poisoning:**
- **Non-toxin (infection-) mediated:** Salmonella (aside from S. typhi), *E. coli* O157, Campylobacter jejuni.
- **Toxin-mediated:** **Staphylococcus aureus** (emesis), **Clostridium botulinum** (neuroparalysis), *Bacillus cereus* (rice, two syndromes), *Clostridium perfringens* (prolonged diarrhoea).

### Cardinal features of a food poisoning outbreak
1. **History of ingestion of a common food** (common vehicle) by the affected persons.
2. **Attack of many persons at the same time** (outbreak — sudden, simultaneous illness).
3. **Similarity of signs & symptoms** in the majority of cases (same clinical picture).
4. Latent interval between ingestion & symptoms (corresponding to the causative agent).

> 🎯 Food poisoning = **bacterial (Salmonella, Staph, Botulinum, B. cereus) + non-bacterial (chemicals/plants/seafood)** · Outbreak features = **common food + many ill together + similar symptoms.**
