<summary><b>Q1. What do mean by reproductive health. Mention the component of reproductive health. (S1,Q6)</b></summary>

### Reproductive health
> 🔴 **Reproductive health** means that people are able to have a **responsible, satisfying and safe sex life** and that they have the capability to reproduce and the freedom to decide **if, when and how often** to do so; and that men and women have the right to be informed of and have access to **safe, effective, affordable and acceptable** methods of family planning of their choice.

### Components of reproductive health
1. **Maternal health / safe motherhood** — antenatal, intranatal and postnatal care.
2. **Family planning** — contraception, birth spacing.
3. **Safe abortion / MR and post-abortion care.**
4. **Reproductive tract infections (RTI) & STIs** — prevention, diagnosis & treatment.
5. **Adolescent reproductive health** & sex education.
6. **Infertility** services.
7. **Child health** (interlinked with maternal reproductive health).

> 🎯 Reproductive health = **safe sex life + free decision to reproduce + access to safe contraception & care.**

<summary><b>Q2. Why mother and child are considered as single unit? (S2,Q6)</b></summary>

### Mother and child — one unit (reasons)
1. During the **antenatal period the fetus is a part of the mother**.
2. **Child health is closely related to maternal health.**
3. Certain diseases and conditions of the mother during pregnancy (e.g., **syphilis, German measles, drug intake**) are likely to have their **effects upon the fetus.**
4. **After birth, the child is dependent upon the mother** (feeding, care, protection).
5. The mother is also the **first teacher of the child.**

> 📌 Hence mother and child are managed as a **single unit ("mother-child dyad")** in MCH services.

> 🎯 **Fetus is part of mother → after birth child depends on mother → one unit.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF p. 363

<summary><b>Q3. What are the stages of maternity cycle. Define MMR and mention the common causes of maternal death in Bangladesh? (S3,Q6)</b></summary>

### Maternity cycle — definition
> 🔴 **Maternity cycle** = the period extending from **fertilization of the ovum to the end of the postnatal period.**

### Stages of maternity cycle
1. **Antenatal (prenatal) period** — from fertilization of ovum to the onset of labour.
2. **Intranatal period** — from the onset of true labour pains to expulsion of the placenta.
3. **Postnatal period** — after expulsion of the placenta up to 6 weeks (42 days).
4. **Inter-conceptional period** — the period between two pregnancies.

> 🎯 Maternity cycle = **Antenatal → Intranatal → Postnatal → Inter-conceptional.**

### Maternal mortality rate (MMR)
> 🔴 **MMR** = number of deaths of mothers from pregnancy, childbirth and puerperal causes during a calendar year **per 1000 live births** in that year.
```
       Female deaths due to complications of pregnancy/child-birth, or within
       6 weeks of delivery from puerperal causes (in an area, in a year)
MMR =                                                                    × 1000
                          Total live births in the same year & area
```
> 📌 MMR in Bangladesh ≈ **3.0 per 1000 live births.**

### Common causes of maternal death in Bangladesh
1. **Haemorrhage** — antepartum (APH) & postpartum haemorrhage (PPH).
2. **Toxaemia of pregnancy** (eclampsia/pre-eclampsia).
3. **Infection** (puerperal sepsis).
4. **Obstructed labour.**
5. **Septic (unsafe) abortion.**
6. **Severe anaemia.**
7. Associated diseases — cardiac, renal, hepatic, metabolic.
8. Malignancy, accidents.

> 🎯 Causes of maternal death = **Haemorrhage · Toxaemia · Sepsis · Obstructed labour · (Unsafe) abortion · Anaemia.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 363, 396–397

<summary><b>Q4. What are the common MCH problems in Bangladesh. Mention the common risk factors among the pregnant women in Bangladesh? (S4,Q6)</b></summary>

### Common MCH problems in Bangladesh (developing countries)
1. **Malnutrition** — maternal depletion, anaemia of mother, toxaemias of pregnancy, postpartum haemorrhage, **LBW of the child.**
2. **Infection** — ARI, EPI-6 diseases, diarrhoea, abortion, **puerperal sepsis**, foetal growth retardation, LBW, embryopathy.
3. **Uncontrolled reproduction / unregulated fertility** — increased severe anaemia, abortion, APH, LBW, **maternal & child mortality.**

### Common risk factors among pregnant women in Bangladesh
1. **Age** — too young (<18 yr) or too old (elderly primi ≥30, elderly grand multipara).
2. **Short stature (≤140 cm)** & slim build — risk of CPD.
3. **Malnutrition & anaemia.**
4. **Grand multiparity & short birth spacing.**
5. **Poverty & low socio-economic status.**
6. **Illiteracy & ignorance** — poor health-care seeking, unskilled home delivery.
7. **Uncontrolled reproduction** (no family planning).
8. Infection & chronic diseases complicating pregnancy (HTN, diabetes, TB).
9. Religious & cultural customs favouring early marriage / home delivery.

> 🎯 MCH problems = **Malnutrition → Infection → Uncontrolled reproduction ("M-I-U" triangle).**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 364–365

<summary><b>Q5. What is antenatal care. List its objective. (S5,Q4)</b></summary>

### Antenatal period & antenatal care
> 🔴 **Antenatal period** = the period from **fertilization of the ovum to the delivery of the fetus.**
> 🔴 **Antenatal care (ANC)** = the **care of the woman during pregnancy.**

### Objectives of antenatal care
1. To **promote, protect and maintain the health of the mother** during pregnancy.
2. To **detect "high-risk" cases** and give them special attention.
3. To **foresee complications and prevent them.**
4. To **remove anxiety and dread** associated with delivery.
5. To **reduce maternal and infant mortality and morbidity.**
6. To **teach the mother** elements of child care, nutrition, personal hygiene and environmental sanitation.
7. To **sensitize the mother to the need for family planning**, including advice to cases seeking medical termination of pregnancy.
8. To **attend to the under-fives** accompanying the mother.

> 🎯 ANC objectives = **promote health + detect high-risk + prevent complications + remove fear + teach mother.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF p. 365

<summary><b>Q6. What is the standard and minimum number of ANC visits required during pregnancy. (S6,Q5)</b></summary>

### Antenatal visits (contacts between patient & doctor, after registration of the mother)
> 🔴 **Minimum visits — 3:**
> - 1st visit: **4–12 weeks**
> - 2nd visit: **24–26 weeks**
> - 3rd visit: **36–38 weeks**

- **Standard (ideal) — 14 visits:** every 4 weeks up to 32 weeks · every 2 weeks from 32–36 weeks · once a week from 36–40 weeks.
- **Moderate (intermediate) — 5 visits:** before 12 weeks · 20–22 weeks · 28–32 weeks · 34–36 weeks · 38 weeks to full term.
> 📌 **WHO recommendation: at least 4 visits** — 1st in the first trimester, then around 24–28, 32–34 and near term.
> 📌 Also during ANC, **tetanus toxoid** is given (2 doses), started after the 1st trimester.

> 🎯 Minimum **3** visits (4–12 · 24–26 · 36–38 wks). WHO standard = **≥ 4.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 366–367

<summary><b>Q7. What aspect of pregnant woman are recorded in first ANC visit? (S7,Q6)</b></summary>

### First antenatal visit — what is recorded / done
**1) Registration** of the pregnant woman.

**2) History taking:**
- **Obstetric:** current pregnancy (1st day of LMP, bleeding or pain, menstrual cycle, use of oral pills); past pregnancies (previous pregnancy & labour, abortion, premature labour, intrauterine death, eclampsia, instrumental delivery).
- **General:** cardiac disease incl. hypertension, respiratory disease (asthma), renal disease, diabetes, epilepsy, blood transfusion, STDs & sexual behaviour, previous surgical/gynaecological operations.
- **Family:** genetic diseases such as haemoglobinopathy.

**3) Physical examination:** pallor · pulse · respiratory rate · oedema · **blood pressure** · weight · **breast examination.**

**4) Abdominal examination:** fundal height · **fetal heart sound/rate (FHS/FHR)** · fetal movements · fetal parts · multiple pregnancy · fetal lie & presentation · inspection of abdominal scar.

**5) Assessment of gestational age.**

**6) Laboratory investigations:** complete urine analysis · stool examination · **complete blood count incl. Hb** · serological examination (syphilis) · **blood grouping & Rh determination** · chest X-ray (if needed) · Pap test / gonorrhoea test (if facilities exist).

**7) Advice & specific protection:** diet, rest, iron & folic acid, **tetanus toxoid**, warning signs, follow-up visits.

> 🎯 First ANC = **Register → History → Examination → Investigation → Advice.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 368–370

<summary><b>Q8. Tell us the antenatal advices to the pregnant mother? (S9,Q4)</b></summary>

### Prenatal (antenatal) advice to the pregnant mother
1. **Diet:** nutritious balanced diet with extra protein, iron, folic acid, calcium & vitamins; low salt; proper fetal nutrition.
2. **Personal hygiene:** daily bath, clean clothing, cleanliness of breasts & nipples, care of teeth.
3. **Rest & sleep:** adequate rest & sleep; **avoid excessive physical exertion, stress and heavy work.**
4. **Clothing:** loose, clean clothes; support the abdomen.
5. **Care of breast:** nipple care, mammary support.
6. **Specific health protection:** immunization against **tetanus (TT 2 doses)**; protection from anaemia (iron & folic acid), other nutritional deficiencies, toxaemias, syphilis, German measles (rubella vaccine before pregnancy), Rh status, HIV (voluntary testing), hepatitis-B.
7. **Family planning:** motivate for birth spacing; advice regarding contraception after delivery.
8. **Warning signs:** report immediately — **swelling of feet, fits, headache, blurring of vision, bleeding/discharge per vagina** or any unusual symptom.
9. **Child care & child spacing:** elements of child care, nutrition, personal hygiene.
10. **Mental preparation:** free and frank discussion on all aspects of pregnancy and delivery.

> 🎯 Advices = **Diet · Hygiene · Rest · Breast care · TT · FP · Warning-sign reporting.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 366–369

<summary><b>Q10. Who are the high risk pregnant mothers? (S11,Q5)</b></summary>

### High-risk pregnancy (high-risk mother)
> 🔴 A pregnancy that is **complicated by a factor or factors that adversely affect the pregnancy outcome** (maternal or perinatal or both) is a **high-risk pregnancy.**

### Criteria of high-risk mother
1. **Elderly primi** (30 years & over).
2. **Short stature primi** (140 cm & below).
3. **Malpresentation** (breech, transverse lie).
4. **Antepartum haemorrhage**, threatened abortion.
5. **Pre-eclampsia and eclampsia.**
6. **Anaemia.**
7. **Twins, polyhydramnios.**
8. Previous **stillbirth**, intrauterine death, manual removal of placenta.
9. **Elderly grand multiparas.**
10. **Prolonged pregnancy** (14 days after EDD).
11. History of previous **caesarean or instrumental delivery.**
12. Pregnancy associated with general diseases — cardiovascular, renal, **diabetes**, TB, liver disease.
13. **Treatment for infertility.**
14. Three or more **spontaneous consecutive abortions.**

> 🎯 High-risk = **Elderly primi ≤140 cm · APH/eclampsia · anaemia · twins · previous CS · diabetes.**

<summary><b>Q12. What are the aims and importance of intranatal care. (S13,Q5)</b></summary>

### Intranatal period & intranatal care
> 🔴 **Intranatal period** = the period from **onset of true labour pains up to the expulsion of the placenta.**
> 🔴 **Intranatal care (INC)** = care of the mother and fetus **during delivery.**

### Aims / objectives of good intranatal care
1. **Thorough asepsis.**
2. **Delivery with minimum injury** to the infant and mother.
3. **Readiness to deal with complications** — prolonged labour, antepartum haemorrhage, convulsions, malpresentations, prolapse of cord.
4. **Care of the baby at delivery** — resuscitation, care of the cord, care of the eyes.

### Principles
1. Prevention of infection · 2. Prevention of dehydration & ketosis of mother · 3. Relief of pain · 4. Prevention of trauma · 5. Prevention of postpartum haemorrhage (PPH).

> 🎯 INC = **Anticipate + Asepsis + Minimum trauma + Be ready for complications.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 372–373

<summary><b>Q13. What are the advantages and disadvantages of (domiciliary) home delivery? (S14,Q5)</b></summary>

### Domiciliary midwifery service (home delivery)
> Mothers with a **normal obstetric history** may be advised to have confinement at home if the **home conditions are satisfactory**; delivery conducted by a **Health Worker-Female / trained dai** — this is "domiciliary midwifery service".

### Advantages
1. Mother delivers in the **familiar surroundings of her home** — removes the fear associated with hospital delivery.
2. **Chances of cross infection are fewer** at home than in hospital.
3. Mother can **keep an eye on her children & domestic affairs** — eases mental tension.

### Disadvantages
1. **Less medical and nursing supervision** than in hospital.
2. **Less rest** for the mother.
3. She may **resume domestic duties too soon.**
4. Her **diet may be neglected.**

> 📌 **Institutional delivery is recommended for all "high-risk" cases** and where home conditions are unsuitable.

> 🎯 Home = **familiar + fewer cross-infection – but less supervision & rest.** High-risk → hospital.

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF p. 373

<summary><b>Q14. Mention the post natal complications and their prevention. (S15,Q6)</b></summary>

### Postnatal period
> 🔴 **Postnatal (puerperal) period** = the period after expulsion of the placenta up to **6 weeks (42 days).**

### Complications of the postnatal period
1. **Puerperal sepsis** — infection of the genital tract within 3 weeks of delivery; rise in temperature & pulse, foul-smelling lochia, pain & tenderness in lower abdomen.
2. **Thrombophlebitis** — infection of the veins of the legs (with varicose veins); leg tender, pale, swollen.
3. **Secondary haemorrhage** — bleeding from the vagina any time from 6 hours after delivery to the end of the puerperium.
4. **Others:** urinary tract infection, mastitis.

### Prevention
1. Thorough & frequent check-up of the mother — examination of **breast, lower abdomen & legs**; **lochia** examined for colour, smell & quantity.
2. Hospitalized women — **twice-a-day examination** for the first 3 days.
3. Home deliveries — **daily visit by the birth attendant** till the baby's cord is dry & dropped off.
4. **Puerperal sepsis prevented by attention to asepsis** before & after delivery.
5. Postnatal check-up on similar lines as ANC is done **at the end of 6 weeks** (bimanual vaginal examination to check involution & perineal wounds).

> 🎯 PNS complications = **Sepsis · Thrombophlebitis · Secondary haemorrhage · UTI · Mastitis.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 375–376

<summary><b>Q15. What are the advantages of breast feeding. Mention the difficult situations of a mother for breast feeding. (S16,Q5)</b></summary>

### Advantages of breast feeding
**To the baby:**
1. **Complete food — species specific.**
2. **Easily digested and well absorbed.**
3. **Protects against infection** (secretory IgA, lactoferrin, lymphocytes, macrophages).
4. **Promotes emotional bonding.**
5. Better **brain growth and intelligence.**
6. Lower incidence of **childhood cancer.**

**To the mother:**
1. **Helps involution of the uterus** (oxytocin release).
2. **Delays pregnancy** (lactational amenorrhoea → child spacing).
3. **Lowers risk of breast and ovarian cancer.**
4. Decreases mother's workload & saves money.
5. Promotes family planning.
6. Decreases need for hospitalization; contributes to **child survival.**

### Difficult situations for breast feeding
**Mother side:** inverted/retracted nipple · cracked, fissured or sore nipple · **engorged breast / breast abscess** · acute fever · TB · chronic nephritis · septicaemia · serious psychiatric disorder · acute maternal illness (maternal exhaustion).
**Baby side:** premature/weak (LBW) babies, sick neonates (unable to suck) — may need expressed breast milk; congenital anomalies (cleft lip/palate).

> 🎯 Breast milk = **complete, protective, bonding food** → mother benefits = **involution + spacing + less cancer.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 385–387

<summary><b>Q16. Define and classify low birth weight babies. How low birth weight can be prevented in a community? (S17,Q5)</b></summary>

### Low birth weight (LBW)
> 🔴 **LBW baby** = birth weight of **less than 2.5 kg (up to and including 2499 gm)**, measured preferably within the 1st hour of life before significant postnatal weight loss.
> 📌 Babies < 1501 gm = **Very low birth weight (VLBW).**

### Types of LBW (classification)
1. **Pre-term babies** — born before 37 completed weeks (259 days) of gestation (intrauterine growth may be normal).
2. **Small-for-date (SFD) babies** — birth weight **below the 10th percentile for the gestational age** (term or preterm).

### Causes of LBW
1. **Maternal:** young age, severe malnutrition, iron/folic-acid deficiency, infections (UTI, syphilis), chronic diseases (cardiac, renal, HTN, diabetes), trauma, drugs.
2. **Obstetric:** toxaemia of pregnancy, APH, premature rupture of membranes, placental insufficiency, CPD.
3. **Uterine:** uterine malformations, cervical incompetence.
4. **Foetal:** multiple pregnancy/twins, hydramnios, intrauterine infections, congenital anomalies, Rh incompatibility.
5. **Social:** heavy physical labour, smoking, low socio-economic condition, repeated births, short inter-pregnancy intervals.

### Prevention of LBW in a community
**A. Direct interventions:**
1. **Increasing food intake** — supplementary feeding, distribution of iron & folic acid tablets, fortification & enrichment of foods.
2. **Controlling infection** — diagnose & treat UTI, malaria, CMV, toxoplasmosis, rubella, syphilis.
3. **Early detection & treatment of medical disorders** — hypertension, toxaemias, diabetes.
**B. Indirect interventions:**
1. **Family planning** (spacing, avoid repeated births).
2. Avoidance of excessive smoking.
3. Improved sanitation.
4. Improving health & nutrition of young girls.
5. Improvement in socio-economic & environmental conditions.

> 🎯 LBW = **< 2.5 kg**, typed as **pre-term or small-for-date.** Prevent by **feeding + treating infection/disease + FP.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 379–380

<summary><b>Q17. What do you mean by baby friendly hospital/ward? (S17,Q6)</b></summary>

### Baby-friendly hospital initiative
> 🔴 In 1993 **UNICEF & WHO launched the Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative** to promote, protect and support **breast feeding** in hospitals & maternity centres. A hospital that fulfills the WHO/UNICEF **"10 steps"** and protects breast feeding becomes **baby-friendly.**

### The 10 steps (criteria the hospital must fulfill)
1. Help the mother initiate **breast feeding within the first hour** of birth in normal delivery (within 4 hours after caesarean).
2. **Encourage breast feeding on demand.**
3. Allow mothers and infants to **remain together 24 hours a day** (rooming-in), except for medical reasons.
4. Give newborns **no food or drink other than breast milk**, unless medically indicated.
5. **Exclusive breast feeding up to 4–6 months.**
6. **No advertisement/promotional material/free products** for infant formula in the facility.
7. **Show the mother how to breast feed** and how to maintain lactation even if separated from the infant.
8. **No artificial teats or pacifiers** for breast-feeding infants.
9. Encourage mothers to help each other and form **breast-feeding support groups.**
10. **Nursing staff should counsel families** in support of exclusive breast feeding.

> 🎯 BFHI = 10 steps: **early BF + on demand + rooming-in + exclusive ≤6 mo + no formula ads/teats.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF p. 381

<summary><b>Q21. What is milk injury? Define exclusive breast feeding and weaning. (S21,Q5)</b></summary>

### Milk injury
> 🔴 If a baby is fed **only with milk over a long period** (e.g., up to 2 years) without giving any supplementary food, the baby becomes **flabby and oedematous due to protein deficiency** and **anaemic due to iron deficiency** — this is called **milk injury.**

### Exclusive breast feeding
> 🔴 When a baby is given **only breast milk — not even a drop of water** — up to **5–6 months** of age, it is called exclusive breast feeding.

### Weaning
> 🔴 **Weaning** is the **gradual process of withdrawing a baby from breast feeding**, starting around **4–5 months** of age, and **adding supplementary foods** rich in protein & other nutrients.

> 🎯 Milk injury = **protein-deficiency oedema + anaemia from exclusive milk >2 yrs.** Weaning = 4–5 months.

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 385–386, 389

<summary><b>Q22. Define growth and development. What information we gather from growth chart. (S22,Q6)</b></summary>

### Growth & development
> 🔴 **Growth** = increase in the **size or mass of tissue** — an increase in the physical size of the body (quantity).
> 🔴 **Development** = **maturation of function** — increase in skills & function; quality, depending on maturation & myelination of the nervous system. Growth & development include physical, intellectual, emotional & social aspects.

### Information gathered from a growth chart
1. Helps in **growth monitoring** of a child.
2. **Diagnostic tool:** identifies high-risk children (growth failure).
3. **Planning & policy making** for child health at local & central level.
4. **Educational tool** for the mother to participate actively in growth monitoring.
5. **Tool for action:** makes **referral** easier.
6. **Evaluation:** evaluates effectiveness of corrective measures.
7. **Tool for teaching:** importance of adequate feeding, harmful effect of diarrhoea.
> 📌 Growth chart is a **visible display** of the child's physical growth & development (component of the child health card); designed by **David Morley**, modified by **WHO**; "weight for age" is plotted because **weight is the most sensitive measure** of growth.

> 🎯 Growth = quantity (size) · Development = quality (function). Chart = **weight-for-age on "road to health".**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 382–385

<summary><b>Q25. What do you mean by exclusive breast feeding and weaning. Mention some weaning food. (S25,Q5)</b></summary>

### Exclusive breast feeding & weaning
*(See Q21.)*

### Weaning foods (Bangladesh)
1. **Cow's milk.**
2. **Fruit juice.**
3. **Soft cooked rice, cereals and pulses** — smashed **khichuri** (rice + pulses + vegetables + oil).
4. **Soup** (smashed potato & vegetables).
5. Mashed ripe banana (to start weaning — easily accepted).
6. Commercially available weaning foods (e.g., **cerelac**) — good but expensive.

> 📌 Good weaning practice: add **one food at a time in small quantity**, increase gradually; add the second food after 1–2 weeks.

> 🎯 Weaning foods = **khichuri · cow's milk · fruit juice · soup · mashed banana.**

<summary><b>Q27. Define IMR. Enumerate the causes of infant death in Bangladesh. How LBW can be prevented in a community. (S27,Q6)</b></summary>

### Infant mortality & IMR
> 🔴 **Infant mortality** = the mortality of live-born children who die **before reaching their first birthday** (before 1 year of age).
> 🔴 **IMR** = the ratio of **infant deaths registered in a given year to the total number of live births registered in the same year**, per 1000 live births.
```
       Deaths of children less than 1 year of age (in a year)
IMR =                                                         × 1000
                    Live births in the same year
```
> 📌 BD IMR ≈ **31/1000 live births** (2015). Neonatal mortality rate ≈ 30/1000 (major component of IMR).

### Important causes of high IMR in Bangladesh
1. **Diarrhoea (30%).**
2. **ARI (18%).**
3. **Prematurity & low birth weight (11%).**
4. **Neonatal tetanus (8%).**
5. **Measles (7%).**
6. Whooping cough (2%).
7. Other causes (24%) — congenital anomalies, malnutrition.
> 📌 Neonatal causes: LBW/prematurity, birth injury & difficult labour, sepsis, congenital anomalies, haemolytic disease of newborn, conditions of placenta & cord.

### Prevention of LBW in a community
*(See Q16 — direct: supplementary feeding, iron & folic acid, controlling infection, treating medical disorders; indirect: family planning, avoiding smoking, sanitation, improving young-girl nutrition, socio-economic improvement.)*
> 📌 Also: promote **breast feeding** (most effective measure to lower infant mortality) & **growth monitoring.**

> 🎯 IMR = **infant deaths/1000 live births.** Big killers = **Diarrhoea + ARI + Prematurity/LBW.**

<summary><b>Q28. Why breast milk is superior to cow's milk for human baby. Why mewborn babies are cared immediately? (S28,Q6)</b></summary>

### Why breast milk is superior to cow's milk
1. **Proteins:** breast-milk proteins form a **fine flocculent curd** — easily digested; cow's milk curd is **thick & hard to digest** due to large proportion of **caseinogen.**
2. **Iron:** breast milk contains **twice as much iron** as cow's milk — meets the newborn's needs up to 3 months; **anaemia is common in cow's-milk-fed babies even before 3 months.**
3. **Calcium:** better utilized (more assimilable form).
4. **Vitamin A:** present in large quantities in **colostrum**; mother's milk contains more vitamin A than cow's milk.
5. **Vitamin C:** higher in breast milk; cow's milk loses vitamin C due to boiling, dilution & exposure to air.
6. **Protection from infection** — antibodies (secretory IgA), lactoferrin, live lymphocytes/macrophages (absent in cow's milk).
7. Species-specific, clean, sterile at the right temperature; baby-friendly.

### Why newborn babies are cared immediately (immediate care of the newborn)
1. To establish & maintain **cardiorespiratory functions** (clearing the airway / resuscitation).
2. **Evaluation by APGAR scoring** at 1 & 5 minutes.
3. **Care of the cord** — cut aseptically.
4. **Care of the eyes** — 1% AgNO₃ drops.
5. **Care of the skin** — bath with soap & warm water after a few hours.
6. **Maintenance of body temperature** (avoid hypothermia).
7. **Breast feeding as soon as possible.**
8. Thorough **examination for congenital abnormalities.**
9. **Identification** of the baby (mother's name, sex).
10. **Rooming-in** — keep the baby with the mother.

> 🎯 Breast milk superior = **fine curd (protein) + 2× iron + more Vit-A/C + anti-infective.** Immediate care = **ABC + cord + eyes + temperature + feeding.**

> 📖 Ch-11, Endeavour PDF pp. 377–378, 388–389

### Growth & development
*(See Q22.)*

### Assessment of physical growth in a child
> 🔴 Physical growth is assessed by **nutritional anthropometry** and **growth monitoring** — serial measurement & plotting.
**Methods:**
1. **Growth chart ("road to health")** — serial weight-for-age plotting; flattening/falling of the curve = growth failure.
2. **Anthropometric measurements** — body measurements at successive ages compared with standards.

### Anthropometric aspects (measurements)
1. **Weight** (the most sensitive measure of growth & malnutrition). 
2. **Height (length) for age.**
3. **Weight for height.**
4. **Head circumference.**
5. **Chest circumference.**
6. **Mid-arm circumference (MAC).**
> 📌 The last two are **independent of age** and particularly useful when age is not known.
> 📌 Weight-based grading: 1st degree (75–89%), 2nd degree (60–74%), 3rd degree (<60%) of the reference.

> 🎯 Anthropometry = **Weight · Height · Head/chest circumference · MAC — weight is most sensitive.**
