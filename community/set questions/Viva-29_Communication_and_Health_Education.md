<summary><b>Q1. What do you mean by health education? Mention the components of health education. (S1,Q5)</b></summary>

### Health education (definition)
> 🔴 **Definition (National Conference on Preventive Medicine, USA):** *"Health education is a **process that informs, motivates and helps people to adopt and maintain health practices and life styles**, advocates environmental changes as needed to facilitate this goal, and conducts professional training and research to the same end."*

**Dynamic definition (Alma-Ata Declaration):** *"A process aimed at encouraging people to want to be healthy, to know how to stay healthy, to do what they can individually and collectively to maintain health, and to seek help when needed."*

### Components (contents) of health education — 8 main divisions
1. **Human biology** — need for exercise, rest & sleep; effects of alcohol, smoking, resuscitation & first aid.
2. **Nutrition** — balanced/optimum diet, nutritive value of foods, storage, preparation, cooking, serving & eating of food.
3. **Hygiene** — (a) **personal** (bathing, clothing, washing hands & toilet, care of feet/nails/teeth, spitting/coughing/sneezing, personal appearance); (b) **environmental** — domestic (home, fresh air, light & ventilation, hygienic storage of food & disposal of wastes, pest control) & community.
4. **Family health care** — human growth & development, MCH care, human reproduction, family planning, population dynamics, nutrition.
5. **Disease prevention & control** — elementary knowledge on nature of diseases & participation in control programmes.
6. **Mental health** — keeping people mentally healthy, preventing mental breakdown.
7. **Prevention of accidents** — the predominant factor in accidents is **carelessness**.
8. **Use of health services** — informing the public about available health services & how to use them.

> 🎯 **HE = "inform + motivate + help people adopt & maintain healthy practices"** · 8 components = **biology · nutrition · hygiene · family care · disease control · mental health · accident prevention · use of services.**

<summary><b>Q2. Define health education. Mention the principles of health education. (S2,Q5)</b></summary>

### Definition
*"Health education is a process that informs, motivates and helps people to adopt and maintain health practices and life styles…"* (National Conference on Preventive Medicine, USA).

### Principles of health education (12)
1. **Credibility** — the degree to which the message is perceived as trustworthy by the receiver.
2. **Interest** — people are unlikely to listen to things not in their interest; teaching must relate to people's interests.
3. **Participation** — *the key word in health education*; based on the psychological principle that **active learning is better than passive learning**.
4. **Motivation** — awakening the fundamental desire to learn (primary & secondary motives).
5. **Comprehension** — teaching must match the people's level of understanding, education & literacy.
6. **Reinforcement** — repetition at intervals; every health campaign needs reinforcement.
7. **Learning by doing** — learning is an action process, not mere memorization.
8. **Known to unknown** — start where the people are, with what they understand, then proceed to new knowledge.
9. **Setting an example** — the health educator should set a good example in what he teaches.
10. **Good human relations** — friendliness & personal qualities of the educator matter more than technical qualifications.
11. **Feedback** — educator modifies message & channels in the light of feedback from the audience.
12. **Leaders** — we learn best from people we respect; penetrate the community through local leaders (village headman, school-teacher, political worker).

> 🎯 Principles = **credibility · interest · participation · motivation · comprehension · reinforcement · learning by doing · known→unknown · example · human relations · feedback · leaders.**

<summary><b>Q4. Enumerate the approaches to health education? What are the common barriers of health communications? (S4,Q5)</b></summary>

### Approaches to health education (4)
1. **Regulatory approach (managed prevention)** — any governmental intervention, direct or indirect, designed to alter human behaviour; may seem the simplest & quickest way to bring about desired changes.
2. **Service approach** — providing all health services needed by the people at their doorsteps, assuming they will use them to improve their own health.
3. **Health education approach** — since attitudes & behavioural patterns are formed early in life, start health education with the young population (e.g. cessation of smoking, safe water supply, fertility control).
4. **Primary health care approach** — a radically new approach starting from the people, with their **full participation and active involvement** in planning & delivery of health services based on PHC principles.

### Barriers of communication (4)
1. **Physiological** — difficulties in hearing, expression.
2. **Psychological** — emotional disturbances, neurosis, levels of intelligence, language or comprehension difficulties.
3. **Environmental** — noise, invisibility, congestion.
4. **Cultural** — illiteracy, levels of knowledge & understanding, customs, beliefs, religion, attitudes, economic & social class differences, language variations, cultural difficulties between foreigners & nationals, between urban-educated & rural population.

> 🎯 Approaches = **regulatory · service · health-education · PHC** · Barriers = **physiological · psychological · environmental · cultural.**

<summary><b>Q5. Define communication. What are the components of effective communication? (S5,Q5)</b></summary>

### Communication (definition)
> 🔴 Communication is *"a **two-way process of exchanging or shaping ideas, feelings and information**"*; broadly it refers to *"the countless ways that humans have of keeping in touch with one another."*

### Components of effective communication — 5 key elements
1. **Sender (source)** — originator of the message; must know his objectives, his audience's interests & needs, his message, the channels, and his professional abilities & limitations.
2. **Receiver (audience)** — the consumers of the message; a single person or a group.
3. **Message (content)** — the information transmitted to be received, understood, accepted & acted upon (words, pictures or signs).
4. **Channel(s) (medium)** — the *"physical bridges"* between sender & receiver; based on 3 media systems: **interpersonal, mass media, traditional/folk media**.
5. **Feedback (effect)** — the flow of information (reaction) from the audience back to the sender; obtained through **opinion polls, attitude surveys and interviews**.

> 🎯 5 elements = **sender → message → channel → receiver → feedback** (communication completes only when feedback takes place).

> 📖 Ch-21, Endeavour PDF pp. 615, 617–618

<summary><b>Q6. What are the models of health education? Give example of each. Mention the functions of communication in health education. (S6,Q5)</b></summary>

### Models of health education (3) — standard text (Park)
1. **Medical model** — activities to promote medical measures of preventing disease, diagnosis & treatment. *Example: immunization, screening, MCH services* (doctor tells patient what to do).
2. **Motivational model** — methods designed to bring about **beneficial behaviour change**; motivating people to translate knowledge into action. *Example: group discussions & counselling for family planning, smoking cessation.*
3. **Social intervention model** — efforts to influence the **social/environmental determinants** of disease. *Example: anti-smoking legislation, seat-belt legislation, fluoridation of water.*

### Functions of health communication (8)
1. **Information** — providing scientific knowledge/information about health problems & how to maintain/promote health.
2. **Education** — education of the public is integral to a prevention-oriented approach; basis of all education is communication.
3. **Motivation** — the power that drives a person from within to act (stages: interest, evaluation, decision-making).
4. **Persuasion** — the art of winning friends & influencing people, without force or manipulation.
5. **Counseling** — helping people understand & deal better with their problems and communicate better with those they are emotionally involved with.
6. **Raising morals** — capacity of a group/team to pull together persistently & consistently.
7. **Health development** — diffusing knowledge of development goals & preparing people for expected roles.
8. **Organization** — communication is the *"life and blood of an organization."*

> 🎯 Models = **medical · motivational · social intervention** · Functions = **information · education · motivation · persuasion · counseling · raising morals · health development · organization.**

> 📖 Ch-21, Endeavour PDF pp. 616–617 (functions); models from standard text (Park)

<summary><b>Q7. What are the stages of behavior change in health education? Mention the criteria of a good message. (S7,Q5)</b></summary>

### Stages of behavior change in health education (5)
1. **Awareness** — the person comes to know about the new idea or practice.
2. **Interest** — the person seeks more detailed information.
3. **Evaluation** — the person weighs the pros & cons and evaluates its usefulness to him or his family by mental exercise, reaching a decision.
4. **Trial** — the positive decision is put into practice.
5. **Adoption** — the person decides the new practice is good and adopts it.

### Criteria of a good message
> 🔴 A good message must be **in line with the objective(s); meaningful; based on felt needs; clear & understandable; specific & accurate; timely & adequate; fitting the audience; interesting; culturally & socially appropriate.**

> 🎯 Stages = **A-I-E-T-A (Awareness · Interest · Evaluation · Trial · Adoption)** · Good message = **clear, specific, accurate, timely, interesting, culturally acceptable, based on felt needs.**

> 📖 Ch-21, Endeavour PDF pp. 617–618, 622

<summary><b>Q8. List the components of health communication. What are the characteristics of a good message? (S8,Q5)</b></summary>

### Components of health communication — 6 basic elements
1. **A source** — the message giver (communicator).
2. **An encoder** — process of converting the communicator's purpose into messages by systematic codes.
3. **A message** — the physical end-product of encoding.
4. **A channel** — the vehicle that carries the message from source to receiver.
5. **A decoder** — process of converting the message from its coded stage back to the original purpose.
6. **A receiver** — the person(s) to whom the communication transfers his purpose.

**Chain:** Source → Encoding → Message → Channel → Decoding → Receiver · *(communication becomes completed when feedback takes place).*
*(Alternatively the 5 key elements: sender, receiver, message, channel, feedback.)*

### Characteristics of a good message (9)
In line with the objective(s) · meaningful · based on felt needs · clear & understandable · specific & accurate · timely & adequate · fitting the audience · interesting · culturally & socially appropriate.

> 🎯 6 components = **source, encoder, message, channel, decoder, receiver** (+ feedback completes it) · Good message = **objective-based, clear, specific, accurate, timely, interesting, culturally appropriate.**

> 📖 Ch-21, Endeavour PDF pp. 617–618

<summary><b>Q14. What do you know about seminar and symposium? (S14,Q4)</b></summary>

*(Occupational-health part of this Q → see Viva-28 Q14)*

### Seminar
- A **group discussion technique**: a small group (e.g. 10–15 persons) meets under a **chairperson/leader** to study a specific subject.
- A member presents the **paper/presentation**, after which the topic is **thoroughly discussed** by all to improve proficiency, ability or understanding (e.g. junior teachers' training).
- Purpose: **deep learning through exchange of ideas** in an informal setting.

### Symposium
- A **larger group** meeting/conference in which **several speakers make short presentations** on **different aspects of a single topic**, guided by a chairman.
- After the presentations, **discussion is thrown open to the audience/participants**.
- Purpose: to **present diverse expert opinions** on a subject before a wider audience.

> 🎯 Seminar = **small group, one topic, discuss & exchange** · Symposium = **several speakers, different aspects of one topic, open discussion to audience.** Both are **group approach** methods of communication.

> 📖 Ch-21, Endeavour PDF pp. 618–619 (group methods); definitions per standard text

<summary><b>Q22. Mention the criteria of a good health message. How the psychosocial barriers impede communication? (S22,Q4)</b></summary>

### Criteria of a good health message (9)
**In line with objective(s) · meaningful · based on felt needs · clear & understandable · specific & accurate · timely & adequate · fitting the audience · interesting · culturally & socially appropriate.**

### How psychosocial barriers impede communication
(Psychological + cultural/social barriers — from the barriers of communication)
1. **Emotional disturbances** — anxiety, anger or fear distort the receiver's perception → the message is misheard/misinterpreted, not rationally evaluated.
2. **Neurosis** — the receiver's mental state interferes with normal reception & processing of information.
3. **Low level of intelligence** — the message cannot be comprehended or acted upon.
4. **Language or comprehension difficulties** — misunderstanding when the communicator & audience do not share the same vocabulary.
5. **Illiteracy** — written materials (leaflets/posters) simply cannot be read.
6. **Customs, beliefs, religion** — a message conflicting with existing values/beliefs is **resisted or rejected** ("sociocultural taboos" block acceptance).
7. **Attitudes & social-class differences** — different frames of reference between urban-educated communicator & rural audiences reduce credibility & acceptance.
8. **Language variations & cultural differences** — between foreigners & nationals cause failure of comprehension.
→ Net effect: **message not understood, not accepted or not acted upon; feedback is poor; health education fails.**

> 🎯 Psychosocial barriers = **emotion, neurosis, intelligence, language, illiteracy, beliefs/customs/religion, social-class & cultural differences → message distorted/rejected.**

2. **Service approach** — providing all needed health services at the people's doorsteps, assuming people will use them.
3. **Health education approach** — because attitudes & behaviour form early in life, start health education with the young population (solves problems like smoking cessation, safe water use, fertility control).
4. **Primary health care approach** — a radically new approach starting from the people with **full participation & active involvement** in planning & delivery of PHC-based services.

> 🎯 4 approaches = **regulatory · service · health-education · PHC** (PHC = most participatory/radical).

### Psychosocial barriers in health communication & how they impede communication
The **psychological** and **cultural/social** barriers work together to impede effective communication:

<summary><b>Q30. Mention the functions of communication in health education? State the stages of adopting a new idea. Differentiate between health education and propaganda. (S30,Q5)</b></summary>

### Functions of communication in health education (8)
**Information · Education · Motivation · Persuasion · Counseling · Raising morals · Health development · Organization** (details in Q11).

### Stages of adopting a new idea (5)
1. **Awareness** — person comes to know about the new idea/practice.
2. **Interest** — seeks more detailed information.
3. **Evaluation** — weighs pros & cons, evaluates usefulness, reaches a decision.
4. **Trial** — positive decision is put into practice.
5. **Adoption** — decides the practice is good and adopts it.

### Health education vs propaganda

| Health education | Propaganda |
|---|---|
| 1. Knowledge & skills actively required. | 1. Knowledge spoon-fed & passively received. |
| 2. Makes people think for themselves. | 2. Prevents/discourages thinking by readymade slogans. |
| 3. Disciplines primitive desires. | 3. Arouses & stimulates primitive desires. |
| 4. Develops reflective behaviour (judgment before action). | 4. Develops reflexive behaviour (impulsive actions). |
| 5. Appeals to reason. | 5. Appeals to emotion. |
| 6. Develops individuality, personality & self-expression. | 6. Develops standard pattern of attitude & behaviour. |
| 7. Behaviour-centred (changes attitude & behaviour). | 7. Information-centred (no attitude/behaviour change). |

> 🎯 **Awareness → Interest → Evaluation → Trial → Adoption** · HE = reason/behaviour-centred; Propaganda = emotion/slogan-driven.

> 📖 Ch-21, Endeavour PDF pp. 616–617, 622–623
