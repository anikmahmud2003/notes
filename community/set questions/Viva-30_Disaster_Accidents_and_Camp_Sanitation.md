<summary><b>Q1. Define and classify disasters. What are the common health problems after cyclone? (S1,Q6)</b></summary>

### Disaster (definition)
> 🔴 **WHO definition:** *"Any occurrence that causes damage, economic disruption, loss of human life or deterioration of health and health services on a scale sufficient to warrant an extraordinary response from outside the affected community or area."*

### Classification of disaster (2 types)
- **A) Natural disasters:** cyclone, flood, tidal waves, tornado, earthquake, landslide, volcanic eruption, drought, heat waves, fires, hurricanes, epidemics, building collapse.
- **B) Man-made disasters:** nuclear accidents, leaks of toxic chemicals, armed conflicts/war, ethnic & civil strifes, riot, famine, fire, explosions, environmental pollution, terrorist activities, crashing of aircrafts.

### Common health problems after cyclone (cyclone-affected areas)
1. **Death** — from drowning, trauma & building collapse.
2. **Severe injuries** — crush injuries, wounds, fractures requiring intensive medical care (moderate in cyclones).
3. **Increased risk of infection** — outbreaks of water-borne & infectious diseases (post-flood water logging → diarrhoeal disease).
4. **Malnutrition & food scarcity** — damage of crops & stored food.
5. **Major population movements** — displaced people, crowded shelters → disease spread.
6. **Others:** water pollution (over-flooding of ponds, tanks, rivers), animal bites (tiger, snake, jackal), nuisance from dead man/animal bodies & human excreta.

> 🎯 Disaster = **damage + disruption + death + deterioration of health → needs extraordinary outside response** · Health problems = **injury, infection, malnutrition, displacement, water pollution, animal bites, nuisance.**

<summary><b>Q2. What are the health and social impacts of disaster? (S2,Q6)</b></summary>

### Health impacts (effects of disaster on health)
1. **Deaths** — many perish in earthquakes & tidal waves; immediate deaths in floods are not significant.
2. **Severe injuries** — requiring intensive medical care; overwhelming in earthquakes, moderate in cyclones, very few in floods.
3. **Risk of infection** — increased in all major disasters.
4. **Malnutrition** — food scarcity is a common effect (floods, tidal bore, droughts) with subsequent adverse health effects.
5. **Major population movements** — inevitable in disasters like floods & droughts.
6. **Others** — death of animals; water pollution from over-flooding of ponds, tanks & rivers; animal bites (tiger, snake, jackal); nuisance from dead bodies & human excreta.

### Social impacts
- **Destruction of houses & educational institutions.**
- **Damage of crops, stored food, clothing & water supply.**
- **Damage of roads, markets & houses** → disruption of daily life & economy.
- **Population displacement** → crowding in shelters, breakdown of normal social/family life.

> 🎯 Health = **death, injury, infection, malnutrition, displacement** · Social = **housing/schools/crops/roads destroyed, displacement, disrupted community life.**

### Road traffic accident (definition)
> 🔴 RTA = **any accident involving at least one road vehicle** (motorized), occurring on a road **open to public circulation** and resulting in injury/death — the vehicle may collide with another vehicle, pedestrian, animal or a roadside object. *(Definition per standard text — Park.)*

### Major causes (factors responsible for causation) of RTA
1. **A risky mix of vehicles in the traffic stream.**
2. **Defective roads with poor street lighting.**
3. **Defective layout of cross-roads.**
4. **Poor road environment** — almost no segregation of pedestrians from wheeled traffic.
5. **Poor maintenance of vehicles.**
6. **Overloading of public transports.**
7. **Low driving standards** — widespread disregard of traffic rules & road signs.
8. **Unpredictable behaviour of pedestrians.**

> 🎯 Causes = **mixed traffic + bad roads/lighting/cross-roads + no pedestrian segregation + poor vehicles + overloading + low driving standards + careless pedestrians.**

<summary><b>Q12. What are the reasons for R.T.A? How can it be prevented? (S12,Q5)</b></summary>

### Reasons for RTA
**Risky mix of vehicles · defective roads/poor street lighting · defective cross-roads · no segregation of pedestrians · poor vehicle maintenance · overloading of public transport · low driving standards/disregard of rules · unpredictable pedestrian behaviour.**

### Prevention of RTA
1. **Data collection** — basic reporting system; without adequate data, analysis & interpretation there can be no effective counter-measures.
2. **Safety education** — must begin with school children; drivers trained in vehicle maintenance & safe driving; youths educated on risk factors, traffic rules & safety precautions + trained in first aid.
3. **Safe sitting arrangements** — use of safety helmets, leather clothing & boots; children seated & prohibited from front seats; door locks, proper vehicle design, laminated windscreen glass.
4. **Alcohol and other drugs** — barbiturates, amphetamines, etc. should be avoided totally.
5. **Primary care** — planning, organization & management of trauma treatment & emergency care services.
6. **Elimination of causative factors** — improvement of roads, imposition of speed limits, marking of danger points.
7. **Enforcement of laws** — driving tests, medical fitness to drive, enforcement of speed limits, compulsory seat belts & crash helmets, checking blood alcohol concentration, road-side breath testing, regular vehicle inspection, periodic re-examination of drivers over 55 years.
8. **Rehabilitation services** — medical, social, occupational rehabilitation (to prevent/reduce/compensate disability).
9. **Accident research.**

> 🎯 Prevention = **data + safety education + safety measures + no alcohol/drugs + trauma care + road improvement + law enforcement + rehabilitation + research.**

### Special points for camp sanitation (7)
1. **Site selection** — high land not subject to flooding/water-logging; good approach from the main road.

5. **Medical & public health arrangements** — camps under the supervision of a Health Officer.
6. **Food supply** — unwholesome food, over-ripe fruits & decaying vegetables strictly forbidden.
7. **Health education** — to combat the possibilities of transmission of communicable diseases.

<summary><b>Q21. Classify disaster. Explain green house effects. (S21,Q6)</b></summary>

### Classification of disaster — see Q1 (natural + man-made).

### Green-house effect (phenomenon)
> 🔴 A **green house** is a kind of glass-house designed to rear plants, protecting them against excessive heat/cold. The glass panes let sunlight & solar radiation enter freely, but the **re-radiated solar radiation from within is partially trapped** and returned back → temperature in the greenhouse rises higher than the free air outside.
> **Greenhouse effect:** the situation is analogous on Earth — the sun's heat is absorbed by the earth's surface, but most is released as **long-wave infrared rays**; certain atmospheric gases act as a **barrier** preventing these re-radiated long-wave rays from escaping into space → the atmosphere becomes warmer and warmer (**global warming**).

### Greenhouse gases
**CO₂ (principal, ~49%) · Methane (18%) · CFC (14%) · Nitrous oxide (6%) · Other gases (13%).**

### Sources
Emission of CO₂ from motor vehicles/industrial plants/furnaces · combustion of fossil fuels (coal, gas, diesel, petrol) · **deforestation** (loss of CO₂-absorbing vegetation) · methane from decomposition of paddy waste, plants & marshy flora · synthesis of CFC · nitrous oxide from motor vehicles, power stations & factories.

### Consequences
Rise in global temperature (~2–5 °C by 2050) · **rise in sea level** (melting of Antarctic ice) · submersion of long coastal areas · **Bangladesh at high risk** — loss of coastal land, crop submersion, water contamination, more frequent cyclones/floods, disruption of ecosystems & human habitation.

> 🎯 Greenhouse effect = **atmosphere traps re-radiated long-wave IR → warming** · Gases = **CO₂ (49%) + CH₄ + CFC + N₂O** · Bangladesh → **sea-level rise, coastal loss, floods/cyclones.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 523–525

2. **Severe injuries** requiring intensive care.
3. **Increased risk of infection** — outbreaks of communicable/water-borne diseases.
4. **Malnutrition & food scarcity.**
5. **Major population movements.**
6. **Others** — water pollution, animal bites, nuisance from dead bodies & excreta.

> 🎯 Health problems = **death, injury, infection, malnutrition, displacement, water pollution, bites, nuisance.**

<summary><b>Q27. Define domestic accidents. List the causes of domestic accidents? How domestic accidents can be prevented? (S27,Q6)</b></summary>

### Definition
**Domestic accident = accident taking place in the home or its immediate surroundings** — all accidents not connected with traffic, vehicles or sport.

### Causes of domestic accidents (10)
**Cut/bruise/wound · Fall & concussion · Burn & scald · Bites & stings · Electric shock · Poisoning (drugs, insecticides, rat poison, kerosene) · Suffocation · Foreign body in eye/orifices · Drowning · Animal bites.**

### Prevention of domestic accidents
1. Adult members familiar with **first-aid principles & practice** (mouth-to-mouth resuscitation for electric shock & drowning).
2. **Sharp/pointed instruments in safe cupboards.**
3. **Careful storing of medicine, insecticides, detergents & chemicals** — out of reach of children.
4. **Bathrooms not slippery & kept dry.**
5. **Know emergency contacts** — hospital/ambulance numbers & nearest doctor's address.

> 🎯 Prevention = **first-aid + safe storage + dry non-slippery bathrooms + emergency contacts.**

> 📖 Ch-16, Endeavour PDF pp. 554–555

### Factors responsible for the increase in RTA
1. Risky mix of vehicles in the traffic stream.
2. Defective roads with poor street lighting.
3. Defective layout of cross-roads.
4. Poor road environment — no segregation of pedestrians.
5. Poor maintenance of vehicles.
6. Overloading of public transports.
7. Low driving standards — disregard of traffic rules & road signs.
8. Unpredictable behaviour of pedestrians.

> 🎯 **Mixed traffic · bad roads/lighting/cross-roads · no pedestrian segregation · poor vehicle maintenance · overloading · low driving standards · careless pedestrians.**
