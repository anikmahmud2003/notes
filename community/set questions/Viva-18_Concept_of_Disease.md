## Q1. Mention the types of Rehabilitation with example. (S1,Q1)

### Rehabilitation (definition)
> 📌 Rehabilitation = the **combined and coordinated use of medical, social, educational and vocational measures** for training and re-training the individual to the **highest possible level of functional ability**.

### Types with examples
| Type | Aim | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Medical rehabilitation** | Restoration of function | Provision of **aids for the crippled**; reconstructive surgery in leprosy |
| **2. Vocational rehabilitation** | Restoration of the capacity to earn a livelihood | **Establishing schools for the blind**; vocational training for the disabled |
| **3. Social rehabilitation** | Restoration of family & social relationships | Re-integration into family and community life |
| **4. Psychological rehabilitation** | Restoration of personal dignity and confidence | Counselling, confidence-building |

> 🎯 Medical → Vocational → Social → Psychological.

## Q2. Define & classify various levels of prevention. What do you mean by primordial prevention? (S4,Q1)

### Levels of prevention — 4 levels
1. **Primordial prevention.**
2. **Primary prevention.**
3. **Secondary prevention.**
4. **Tertiary prevention.**

### Primordial prevention
> 🔴 **Primordial prevention** = prevention of the **emergence or development of risk factors** (e.g. obesity, hypertension) in a country/population, in which they have **not yet appeared** — efforts are directed towards discouraging children from adopting harmful life-styles (e.g. smoking, unhealthy eating habits, lack of physical exercise) through **individual & mass education**. Main intervention = **health education**.

### Primary prevention
> 📌 Action taken **prior to the onset of disease**, i.e. removal of the possibility of disease — applies to the **pre-pathogenesis phase**. Two modes of intervention:
- **Health promotion** (health education, environmental modifications, nutritional intervention, life-style & behavioural changes).
- **Specific protection** (immunization, use of specific nutrients, chemoprophylaxis, protection against accidents & occupational hazards).

### Secondary prevention
> 📌 Action which **halts the progress of disease at its incipient stage** and prevents complications — applies to the **pathogenesis phase** (early diagnosis & adequate treatment, screening surveys, surveillance).

### Tertiary prevention
> 📌 All measures to **reduce or limit impairments & disabilities and minimize suffering**; also promotes the patient's adjustment to irremediable conditions (disability limitation + rehabilitation).

> 🎯 **Primordial (prevent risk factors) → Primary (pre-pathogenesis) → Secondary (early disease) → Tertiary (disability).**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 62–63

## Q3. Define eradication of disease. What are the mode of intervention in primary & secondary prevention? (S5,Q1)

### Eradication of disease
> 🔴 Eradication literally means to *"tear out by roots"* — it implies **termination of all transmission of infection by extermination of the infectious agent** through surveillance and containment. **Example: smallpox** (the only disease eradicated so far).

### Modes of intervention
**In primary prevention:**
- **Health promotion** — health education, environmental modification, nutritional intervention, life-style & behavioural changes.
- **Specific protection** — immunization, use of specific nutrients, chemoprophylaxis, protection against accidents & occupational hazards, protection from carcinogens, avoidance of allergens.

**In secondary prevention:**
- **Early diagnosis & adequate treatment** — case finding (individual & mass), screening surveys, surveillance techniques, periodic examination, selective examination of those at high risk.

> 🎯 Primary = **health promotion + specific protection** · Secondary = **early diagnosis & treatment.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 61, 63–64

## Q4. Define rehabilitation and mention its type with example. Differentiate between elimination & eradication of disease. (S6,Q1)

### Rehabilitation
> 📌 Combined & coordinated use of medical, social, educational & vocational measures to train/re-train the individual to the **highest possible level of functional ability**.
- **Types (examples):** Medical (aids for the crippled) · Vocational (schools for the blind) · Social (re-integration into family/community) · Psychological (restoration of dignity & confidence).

### Elimination vs Eradication
| Trait | **Elimination** | **Eradication** |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | **Interruption of transmission** of disease in a large geographic region/area | **Termination of all transmission** by extermination of the infectious agent |
| Agent | Pathogen permitted to persist | Agent absolutely eradicated (teared out by roots) |
| Example | Elimination of **measles, polio, diphtheria** from large areas | **Smallpox** eradication |

> 🎯 Elimination = **stop transmission (agent persists)** · Eradication = **agent exterminated (smallpox).**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 60–61, 65

## Q5. Explain "ice berg" phenomenon of disease. (S8,Q1)

### Definition
> 🔴 It is the representation of a **disease process in a human being in a community in the same manner as an iceberg** — only a small clinical part is visible while a huge hidden mass of disease remains unseen.

### Parts of the iceberg
| Part | What it represents |
|---|---|
| **1. Floating tip** | What the **physician sees** in the community — **symptomatic clinical cases** |
| **2. Water line** | Demarcation between apparent and inapparent disease |
| **3. Submerged (hidden) portion** | **Latent cases, presymptomatic cases, undiagnosed cases, inapparent cases and carriers** |

### Importance
- In TB, alimentary infections, malnutrition, cancer, diabetes, hypertension the **unknown (submerged) morbidity exceeds the known (exposed) morbidity** — an important **undiagnosed reservoir of infection** in the community.
- Example — **Polio:** ~95% subclinical (submerged), minor illness ~4–8%, paralytic <1% (tip).

> 🎯 **Floating tip = clinical cases · submerged = latent/presymptomatic/carrier** — polio ≈ 95% subclinical!

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 59–60

## Q6. State the natural history of disease. (S9,Q1)

### Natural history of disease
> 🔴 **Natural history** signifies *"the way in which a disease evolves over time, from the earliest stage of its **pre-pathogenesis phase** to its termination (recovery, disability or death), in the absence of intervention"*.

### Two phases
**1. Pre-pathogenesis phase:**
- Period preliminary to the onset of disease in man.
- The three factors — **agent, host and environment** — interact during this phase; if the interaction is favourable, disease occurs.

**2. Pathogenesis phase:**
- Begins with **entry of the disease agent into the human host**.
- Usually there is an **incubation period** between entry of the agent and the appearance of clinical signs & symptoms.

> 🎯 **Pre-pathogenesis (agent-host-environment interaction) → Pathogenesis (agent in host → incubation → clinical disease).**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF p. 56

## Q7. Give an example in causation of disease. (S10,Q1)

### Example — causation of cholera (ecological triad model)
| Factor | Example in cholera |
|---|---|
| **Agent** | *Vibrio cholerae* (biological agent) |
| **Host** | Man (susceptible host with low gastric acidity, etc.) |
| **Environment** | Contaminated water supply, poor sanitation, overcrowding, rainy season |

### General principle (multi-factorial causation)
> 🔴 Disease does not occur due to a single cause — it results from the **interaction of agent, host and environment** (the epidemiological/ecological triad). Removing/interrupting any one element blocks the occurrence of disease.

### Another example — Coronary heart disease (risk-factor model)
- Agent/cause is not a single organism; aetiology is discussed in terms of **risk factors**: smoking, hypertension, elevated serum cholesterol, diabetes, obesity, lack of exercise, type-A personality, heredity.

> 🎯 Causation = **Agent × Host × Environment** (e.g. V. cholerae → man → contaminated water = cholera).

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 54, 56–57

## Q8. Explain the concept of control of a disease. Important characteristics of host. (S11,Q1)

### Concept of control of disease
> 🔴 **Disease control** describes operations aimed at **reducing**:
1. The **incidence** of disease.
2. The **duration and risk of transmission** of disease.
3. The **effects of infection**, including physical & psychosocial complications.
4. The **financial burden** to the community.

- Control does **not** eliminate the disease or its agent — it keeps the problem at a level that no longer poses a public health problem (e.g. **malaria control**).

### Important characteristics of host
1. **Demographic** — age, sex, ethnicity.
2. **Biological** — genetic factors, biochemical levels of blood (cholesterol), blood groups, enzymes, blood pressure.
3. **Social & economic** — socio-economic status, education, occupation, marital status, housing.
4. **Life-style** — living habits, food habit (→ colon cancer), exercise (↓ hypertension, heart disease), smoking (→ lung cancer), alcohol use.

> 🎯 Control = **reduce incidence + transmission + effects + financial burden** (agent persists).

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 54, 60

## Q9. Define prevention & control of disease. Steps of control the communicable disease. (S13,Q1)

### Definitions
- **Prevention** implies to **intercept or oppose the "cause"** and thereby the disease process (apply at pre-pathogenesis stage by removing hosts' susceptibility).
- **Control** = operations aimed at **reducing the incidence, duration & risk of transmission, effects of infection and the financial burden** of disease.

### Steps of control of a communicable disease
**1. Control of the reservoir / source of infection:**
- **Early diagnosis** (clinical, laboratory) & **notification**.
- **Isolation** (home/hospital) and **treatment** of cases.
- Detection & treatment of **carriers**; control of animal reservoirs (destruction/dispersion).

**2. Interruption of transmission (cutting the chain of infection):**
- **Environmental sanitation** — safe water supply, safe disposal of excreta & refuse.
- **Disinfection** (concurrent & terminal).
- **Control of vectors & intermediate hosts** (insecticides, anti-larval measures).
- Personal protection (safe food & water, washing hands).

**3. Protection of the susceptible host:**
- **Immunization** (active, passive & combined).
- **Chemoprophylaxis** (e.g. chloroquine in malaria, INH in TB).
- **Health education**, improvement of nutrition & general resistance.

> 🎯 Steps = **Control reservoir → Interrupt transmission → Protect susceptible host.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 60, 62–63 (standard control framework)

## Q12. How health status of a community can be promoted? (S21,Q1)

### Promoting the health status of a community (health promotion)
> 🔴 **Health promotion** = the process of enabling people **to increase control over, and to improve, their own health**.

**Modes / channels:**
1. **Health education** — changing knowledge, attitudes & life-style of the people.
2. **Environmental modifications** — safe water supply, sanitation, pollution control.
3. **Nutritional intervention** — food fortification, supplementation, correction of malnutrition.
4. **Life-style & behavioural changes** — smoking cessation, physical activity, healthy diet.
5. **MCH & family planning services** — for mother & child health.
6. **Immunization** — protection against infectious diseases.
7. **Adequate health services** — equitable distribution, accessibility & availability of primary health care.
8. **Socio-economic development** — poverty alleviation, education, food security (inter-sectoral action).

> 🎯 Promote health = **health education · environment · nutrition · life-style change · immunization · equitable services.**

## Q15. How can you rehabilitate drug addictive person in a community. (S24,Q7)

### Rehabilitation of a drug-addicted person (tertiary prevention)
1. **Detoxification & medical treatment** — supervised withdrawal with medical support at a rehabilitation/treatment centre.
2. **Psychological rehabilitation** — individual & **group psychotherapy**, counselling, cognitive-behavioural therapy to restore dignity & confidence.
3. **Family & social rehabilitation** — involving the family for emotional support; social reintegration; rebuilding family relationships.
4. **Vocational rehabilitation** — training & placement for earning a livelihood (restoring the capacity to work).
5. **Community support services** — self-help groups, day-care centres, halfway homes, long-term after-care & follow-up.
6. **Health education & prevention of relapse** — motivation against re-use; avoidance of peer pressure.

> 🎯 Rehab path = **detoxification → counselling → family/vocational rehabilitation → follow-up.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF p. 65 (rehabilitation types); standard substance-abuse rehabilitation framework

## Q16. What are the mode of intervention in primary & secondary prevention? Explain with example. (S25,Q1)

### Modes of intervention
**In primary prevention** (pre-pathogenesis phase):
- **Health promotion** — health education, environmental modification, nutritional intervention, life-style & behavioural changes.
- **Specific protection** — immunization, use of specific nutrients, chemoprophylaxis, protection against accidents & occupational hazards.
- *Example:* EPI immunization of infants against TB, polio, DPT, measles.

**In secondary prevention** (pathogenesis phase):
- **Early diagnosis & adequate treatment** — case finding (individual & mass), screening surveys, surveillance techniques, periodic examination of high-risk groups.
- *Example:* Screening & early treatment of hypertension or diabetes to prevent complications; sputum examination & treatment of smear-positive TB.

> 🎯 **Primary = health promotion + specific protection · Secondary = early diagnosis + treatment.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 63–64

## Q18. State the natural history of disease. Explain ecological triad. (S28,Q1)

### Natural history of disease
> 🔴 Natural history = *the way in which a disease evolves over time, from the earliest stage of its pre-pathogenesis phase to its termination (recovery, disability or death), in the absence of intervention.*
- **Pre-pathogenesis phase:** agent-host-environment interaction before disease onset.
- **Pathogenesis phase:** entry of the agent into the host → incubation period → clinical signs & symptoms.

### Ecological / epidemiological triad
> 🔴 For a disease process to occur there must be a **harmful agent** contacting a **susceptible host** in a **suitable environment** — these three factors (agent, host, environment) form the **ecological triad**.

- Occurrence of disease can be **blocked by intercepting any of the three sides** (agent, host or environment) or any linkage between them.
- *Example:* In malaria — agent (*Plasmodium*), host (man), environment (mosquito vector, stagnant water).

> 🎯 Triad = **Agent × Host × Environment** — break any corner to block disease occurrence.

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 54, 56
