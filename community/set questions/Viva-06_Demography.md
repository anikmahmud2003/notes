## Q1. What do you mean by demography. Mention its use? (S1,Q4)

### Demography (definition)
> 🔴 **Demography** is the **scientific study of human population.** It mainly concentrates on —
> 1. **Changes in population size** (growth or decline),
> 2. **The composition of the population** (age, sex, ethnic groups etc.),
> 3. **The distribution of population in space** (urban, rural etc.).

### Uses of demography
1. Focuses attention on the 3 observable phenomena — changes in size, composition and distribution.
2. Deals with **five demographic processes:** fertility, mortality, marriage, migration & social mobility — which continuously determine size, composition & distribution.
3. Health & health-care needs of a population **cannot be measured or met** without knowledge of demography.
4. Prerequisite for making **forecasts about future population size & structure** → underpins health-care planning.
5. Answers **how population changes and how its changes can be measured.**

> 🎯 Demography = **scientific study of human population** (size + composition + distribution).

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF p. 317

## Q2. Define demography. What are the demographic stages? (S2,Q4) · also (S17,Q4)

### Definition
*(See Q1.)*

### Demographic cycle / stages (5 — historical pattern of a nation's population)
| Stage | Name | Characteristics | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **High stationary** | **High birth rate + high death rate** (cancel each other) → population stationary | India (till 1920) |
| **2** | **Early expanding** | **Death rate begins to decline** while birth rate remains unchanged → rise in growth | S. Asia & Africa |
| **3** | **Late expanding** | Death rate declines **further**, birth rate tends to **fall** (births still exceed deaths) → continues to grow | **Bangladesh**, India, China, Singapore |
| **4** | **Low stationary** | Both birth & death rates **fall** → growth stationary | UK, Denmark, Sweden, Belgium |
| **5** | **Declining** | Birth rate **lower** than death rate → population declines | Germany, Hungary (E. Europe) |

> 🎯 **Bangladesh is now in the 3rd stage — "Late expanding" (death rate falls, birth rate falls but still > death rate).**

## Q3. What do you mean by growth rate. Enumerate the factors causing high growth rate. (S3,Q4) · also (S18,Q4) · (S26,Q4)

### Growth rate (population growth rate)
> 🔴 **Growth rate** = the **net residual (in %) when the crude death rate is subtracted from the crude birth rate**, exclusive of migration.

```
Growth rate (%) =  Crude birth rate − Crude death rate
                    10
```
*(Example: CBR 18.9 − CDR 4.8 = 14.1 ÷ 10 = **1.41%** — BD census 2001.)*
*(Calculation: CBR 35, CDR 20 → 35−20 = 15 ÷ 10 = **1.5%**.)*

- **Doubling time** = **70 ÷ growth rate** (e.g., growth 2.5% → doubles in 28 years).

### Factors causing high growth rate (high fertility) in Bangladesh
1. **Universality of marriage** — marriage is universal & sacramental; everyone participates in reproduction.
2. **Early marriage.**
3. **Poverty.**
4. **Low standard of living.**
5. **Low level of diet/nutrition.**
6. **Traditional customs and habits.**
7. **Absence of entertainment/recreation.**
8. **Absence of family-planning habit.**
9. **Religion** (large family is valued).
10. **Multiple marriage.**
11. **Breast-feeding** (prolonged lactation wrongly considered protective — early postpartum conception possible).
12. **Low place of women in society** (less education, less empowerment).

> 🎯 Growth rate = **(CBR − CDR) ÷ 10.** Doubling time = **70 ÷ growth rate.**

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF pp. 323, 325

## Q4. Tell us about birth rate. List the causes of high birth rate in Bangladesh? (S4,Q4) · also (S19,Q3)

### Birth rate — Crude birth rate (CBR)
> 🔴 **CBR** = number of **total live births per 1000 estimated mid-year population** in a given year. It is the **simplest indicator of fertility.**

```
CBR =  No. of live births during the year   × 1,000
       Estimated mid-year population
```

> 📌 **CBR is a weaker fertility statistic** — because the denominator includes men and women **outside the reproductive age group** (not at risk of giving birth).

*(BD: CBR ~18.9–21.6 per 1000 population.)*

### Causes of high birth rate in Bangladesh
1. **Universality of marriage** (everyone marries & reproduces).
2. **Early marriage** (early start of reproduction).
3. **Poverty** & low standard of living.
4. **Low level of dietary intake** & malnutrition.
5. **Traditional culture, customs & habits.**
6. **Absence of recreation/entertainment facilities.**
7. **Absence of family-planning practice** (low contraceptive use).
8. **Religious and social beliefs** favouring large families.
9. **Multiple marriage.**
10. **Low status & education of women.**
11. **Prolonged breast-feeding** (falsely relied on as contraception).

> 🎯 High birth rate = **early marriage + universality of marriage + poverty + no FP + low women's status.**

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF pp. 322–323, 325

## Q5. Define death rate. What are the causes of high death rate in Bangladesh? (S5,Q3)

### Death rate — Crude death rate (CDR)
> 🔴 **CDR** = number of **deaths (from all causes) per 1000 estimated mid-year population** in one year, in a given place.

```
CDR =  Number of deaths during the year   × 1,000
       Mid-year population
```
*(BD: CDR ~4.8–6.1 per 1000 population.)*

### Causes of high death rate in Bangladesh
1. **Poverty** and poor socio-economic conditions.
2. **Malnutrition** — especially PEM in children.
3. **Communicable diseases** — diarrhoea, ARI/pneumonia, TB, measles (in children & adults).
4. **High infant, child & maternal mortality** — unskilled deliveries, neonatal tetanus.
5. **Unsafe water supply & poor sanitation** → water-borne diseases.
6. **Illiteracy & ignorance** about health & hygiene.
7. **Inadequate and inaccessible health care** services in rural areas.
8. **Natural disasters** — floods, cyclones, droughts.
9. **Environmental pollution** — arsenic contamination of tube-well water, air pollution.
10. **Overcrowding & rapid population growth.**

> 🎯 High death rate = **poverty + malnutrition + communicable disease + no safe water/mother-child care.**

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF pp. 322–323 (CDR); causes — standard factors (Park/Rashid)

## Q6. Define population pyramid. Compare age-gender pyramid between developed & developing countries? (S6,Q3) · also (S27,Q4)

### Population pyramid (age-sex structure / age pyramid)
> 🔴 **Age & sex structure gives a visual presentation of population growth in a country** — a two-directional bar diagram showing the proportion of males & females in each age group.

### Developing countries (e.g., Bangladesh) — pyramidal shape
- **Wide base → narrow apex** → indicates a **higher proportion in younger age groups** → **rapid population growth in future** (large number will soon enter reproductive age).
- High birth rate, higher dependency ratio (over 97 in BD vs ~50 in developed), lower life expectancy.

### Developed countries — bulbous shape
- **Narrow base, wide in the body (middle), narrow in the apex** → smaller proportion of children, **large middle-aged (working) population**, growing elderly population; low birth rate & death rate, near-stationary/declining growth.

> 🎯 **Developing = triangular (wide base, rapid future growth) · Developed = bulbous (narrow base, ageing).**

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF pp. 320–321

## Q7. Define fertility. Name the influencing factors of fertility. (S7,Q4) · also (S21,Q4)

### Fertility (definition)
> 🔴 **Fertility is the child-bearing performance of individuals, couples or populations.** (It is the **actual reproductive performance** — measured by live births.)

### Factors influencing fertility
1. **Age at marriage.**
2. **Duration of married life.**
3. **Spacing of children.**
4. **Education** — women's education above primary level; general education.
5. **Economic status** — per capita income.
6. **Religion.**
7. **Nutrition.**
8. **Family planning** practice.
9. **Breast-feeding practice.**
10. **Urbanization & industrialization.**
11. **Medical & health-care facilities.**
12. **Gainful employment of women outside home.**
13. **Good housing.**
14. **Modernization of values.**
15. **Social norms** — e.g., prohibition of coitus during specific periods.

> 🎯 Fertility = **actual live-birth performance.** Influenced by **age & duration of marriage, education, economy, religion, FP, nutrition, breast feeding.**

## Q8. Define and classify census. What information that you gather from a census? (S8,Q4) · also (S28,Q4)

### Census (definition)
> 🔴 The **United Nations** defined census as — **"the process of collecting, compiling and publishing demographic, economic and social data pertaining, at a specified time or times, to all persons in a country or delineated territory."**

### Classification (2 types)
| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **De-facto** | A person is counted **at the place where he/she happens to be on the census day or night** |
| **De-jure** | A person is enumerated **at his/her usual place of residence** |

> 📌 **Methods of taking census:** 1) **Enumeration** (enumerator visits every house & elicits information — followed in USA, **Bangladesh**, Pakistan); 2) **Questionnaire** (form handed to head of household); 3) **Combination of both.**

*(BD censuses: 1974, 1981, 1991, 2001 (4th), 2011 (5th, de-facto, 15–19 March).)*

### Information gathered from a census
1. **Demographic, social and economic characteristics** of the people — conditions under which they live, how they work, their income and other basic information.
2. Population census provides **basic data needed to compute vital statistical rates and other health, demographic and socio-economic indicators.**

> 🎯 **De-facto = where present on census night · De-jure = usual residence.** BD follows **de-facto.**

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF pp. 326–327

## Q10. What are the factors influencing declining the death rate. What is statistical averages? (S10,Q3)

### Causes of declining death rate
1. **Absence of natural checks** — e.g., famines & large-scale epidemics.
2. **Mass control of disease** — smallpox, plague, cholera, malaria.
3. **Advances in medical science** — extensive use of chemotherapeutics, antibiotics & insecticides.
4. **Better health facilities** — primary health centres & more treatment centres.
5. **Impact of national health programmes.**
6. **Improvements in food supply.**
7. **International aid.**
8. **Development of social consciousness among the masses.**

### Statistical averages (measures of central tendency)
> **Statistical average** = a single representative value of the centre of a distribution —
> - **Arithmetic mean:** sum of observations ÷ number of observations.
> - **Median:** middle value when observations are arranged in order (50th percentile).
> - **Mode:** most frequently occurring value.

> 🎯 Declining death rate = **no famine/epidemics + disease control + medical advances + better services + food + awareness.**

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF p. 323 (death-rate decline); averages — Ch-20 (Biostatistics)

## Q14. What are the uses of census? (S14,Q4)

The census is the **major source of population data**. Its uses:
1. Provides data on **population size, composition (age, sex), distribution** and density.
2. **Base for calculating vital rates** — birth rate, death rate, growth rate (denominator data).
3. Basis for computing **health & socio-economic indicators** (IMR, MMR, literacy, dependency ratio).
4. **Planning** — health services, food supply, housing, education, transport.
5. **Allocation of resources & representation** (policy/administration).
6. **Forecasting future population** & trends (growth, urbanisation, migration).

## Q15. What do you mean by population explosion. Enumerate the causes. (S15,Q4) · also (S29/Q30,Q4)

### Population explosion
> 🔴 **Population explosion** = a situation **when the growth rate is more than 2%** per year.

### Causes (factors responsible for high growth rate) in Bangladesh
1. **Universality of marriage** & early marriage.
2. **High fertility** — absence of family-planning habit.
3. **Poverty & low standard of living.**
4. **Low level of diet.**
5. **Traditional customs & habits.**
6. **Religious beliefs** favouring large families; multiple marriage.
7. **Declining death rate** (medical advances, mass disease control) — births far exceed deaths.
8. **Low place/education of women in society.**

### Effects of population explosion / over-population
- **National level:** food shortage · poor economy · clothes problem · housing & employment problems · education problem · political instability · increased antisocial activities · poor socio-economic condition · spread of communicable diseases.
- **Family level:** food insufficiency · poverty · housing · education · parental ill-health · family instability.
- **Mother & child:** PEM of children · ↑ maternal & child mortality/morbidity · sanitation & educational problems.

> 🎯 Population explosion = **growth rate > 2%.** Key cause = **falling death rate + unchanged high birth rate.**

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF pp. 324–325

## Q20. Enumerate some mortality rates used in demography. What are the causes of high death rate in Bangladesh? (S20,Q4)

### Mortality rates used in demography
1. **Crude death rate (CDR)** — all deaths/1000 mid-year population.
2. **Age-specific death rate** — deaths in a specific age group.
3. **Cause-specific death rate** — deaths due to a particular cause.
4. **Infant mortality rate (IMR)** — infant deaths/1000 live births.
5. **Child (1–4 yr) mortality rate.**
6. **Maternal mortality ratio/rate (MMR).**
7. **Case fatality rate (CFR)** — deaths among cases of a disease.
8. **Proportional mortality rate (PMR).**
9. **Survival rate / life-table functions** (expectation of life).

### Causes of high death rate in Bangladesh
1. Poverty · 2. Malnutrition (PEM) · 3. Communicable diseases (diarrhoea, ARI, TB) · 4. High infant/child/maternal mortality · 5. Unsafe water & poor sanitation · 6. Illiteracy & ignorance · 7. Inadequate health services · 8. Natural disasters · 9. Environmental pollution (arsenic) · 10. Overcrowding.

## Q26. Define total fertility rate (TFR). List the causes of high birth rate in Bangladesh. (S27,Q4)

### Total fertility rate (TFR)
> 🔴 **TFR** = the **average number of children a woman would have** if she were to pass through her whole reproductive life bearing children at the rates currently observed in each age group (under the current age-specific fertility schedule).

Also defined as: total number of live births a woman aged 15–49 years experiences under the current age-specific schedule.
> 📌 **Importance:** TFR is a **refined measure of fertility**; gives the approximate magnitude of the **"completed family size."**
> *(BD: TFR ≈ 2.45 children per woman — Economic Survey 2014.)*

### Causes of high birth rate in Bangladesh *(see Q4 — 11 causes.)*

> 🎯 **TFR = average completed family size at current rates (≈2 effective when NRR = 1).**

> 📖 Ch-09, Endeavour PDF pp. 328–329, 325
