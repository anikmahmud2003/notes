<summary><b>Q1. List the criteria of a good lighting. (S11,Q3)</b></summary>

### Criteria of good lighting
1. **Sufficiency** — the lighting must be **sufficient to discern the details** of the object (adequate illumination).
2. **Uniform distribution** — same intensity over the **whole field of work** (no dark patches).
3. **Absence of glare.**
4. **Absence of sharp shadows** — slight shadows inevitable but constricting sharp shadows absent.
5. **Steadiness** — the source of light must be constant (no flickering).
6. **Colour of the light** — should, as far as possible, **resemble daylight**.
7. **Proper surroundings/background** — light presented against an appropriate background.

> 🎯 Good lighting = **sufficient + uniform + glare-free + no sharp shadows + steady + daylight colour.**

<summary><b>Q2. What are the causes of air pollution? (S13,Q3)</b></summary>

### Causes/sources of air pollution
1. **Natural phenomena** — volcanoes, forest fires, naturally-occurring radioactive substances, bacteria, viruses, moulds & spores.
2. **Products of combustion** — burning fuel produces **smoke, SO₂, nitrogen oxides, fly ash**.
3. **Industrial wastes** — dust, fumes & gases from factories, mills, power plants, mines, cement plants.
4. **Automobiles** — internal-combustion **petrol & diesel engines** emit hydrocarbons, **CO, lead, nitrogen oxides, smoke, aldehydes**.
5. **Photochemical pollution** — volatile hydrocarbons + oxides of nitrogen in strong sunlight → **ozone** & complex organic compounds.
6. **Acid rain** — sulphuric & nitric acids.
7. **Miscellaneous** — burning of refuse, agricultural activities.

> 🎯 Causes = **combustion + industry + automobiles + natural (volcanoes/fires) + photochemical smog + acid rain.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 509

<summary><b>Q3. What is air pollution? Mention the common source of air pollution in Bangladesh. State the effects of ill ventilation on health. (S16,Q3)</b></summary>

### Definition of air pollution
> 🔴 **Air pollution** = the **presence in the ambient (surrounding) atmosphere** of substances (gases, particulate matter) generated **by the activities of man** in concentrations **injurious to health, safety and comfort** — or injurious to vegetation & animals, resulting in chemicals entering the **food chain** or being present in **drinking water**.

### Common sources of air pollution in Bangladesh
1. **Diesel/petrol vehicles** (three-wheelers with two-stroke engines, buses, trucks) — CO, lead, SO₂, smoke.
2. **Brick kilns** & coal/wood burning — smoke, SO₂, particulate matter.
3. **Industries** (cement, fertilizer, tanneries, mills) & power plants.
4. **Burning of refuse & garbage**, crop-residue burning.
5. **Construction activities & dust**; indoor smoke from kitchen fuel (biomass burning).

### Effects of ill ventilation on health
- **Air changes:** room temperature rises; humidity increases; **unpleasant body odour**; increased bacterial pollution.
- **Clinical features:** **headache, drowsiness, lassitude, inability to concentrate, depression, feeling of fatigue (malaise)**, and **increased chances of contracting communicable diseases** (droplet infections — TB, common cold, diphtheria, whooping cough).

> 🎯 Air pollution = **harmful gases/particulates in ambient air from man's activities** · BD sources = **vehicles + brick kilns + industries + refuse burning + indoor smoke** · Ill ventilation → **headache, drowsiness, lassitude, more infection.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 509–510, 514

<summary><b>Q4. Mention the common air pollutants in a big city like Dhaka. How can you disinfect the indoor and outdoor air. (S17,Q3)</b></summary>

### Common air pollutants in a big city (Dhaka)
- **Carbon monoxide (CO), nitrogen oxides (NOx), sulphur dioxide (SO₂)**.
- **Lead** (from vehicles/petrol), hydrocarbons, **particulate matter/smoke (PM₂.₅, PM₁₀)**, **ozone** (photochemical).
- **Aldehydes**, fly ash, dust; indoor: **tobacco smoke, CO, PAH, NOx** from fuel & tobacco combustion, cooking smokes.

### Disinfection of (indoor) air
1. **Mechanical ventilation** — reduces vitiated air & bacterial density.
2. **UV radiation** — effective in operation theatres & infectious-disease wards.
3. **Chemical mists** — **triethylene glycol** vapours (bactericidal against droplet nuclei & dust).
4. **Dust control** — oiling of ward floors reduces bacteria in air.
5. Ventilation standards: cubic space 1000–1200 cft/person, 2–3 air changes/hour (home), 4–6/hour (assemblies), floor space 50–100 sq ft/person.

### Disinfection/prevention of polluted outdoor air
- Containment & arrestors (filters) at source, replacement of polluting process/fuel (**lead-free & eco-friendly fuels, CNG**), dilution, emission surveillance, greening (plantation), legislation, international monitoring networks.

> 🎯 City pollutants = **CO, NOx, SO₂, lead, smoke/PM, ozone** · Indoor disinfection = **ventilation + UV + triethylene glycol mist + dust control.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 509, 511–512

<summary><b>Q5. Define and classify ventilation. Name the natural ventilation process. Why propulsion system of ventilation is necessary? (S18,Q3)</b></summary>

### Definition of ventilation
> 🔴 **Ventilation** = the **science of maintaining atmospheric conditions comfortable and healthful** to the human body — the *modern concept*: **replacement of vitiated air by fresh outdoor air** and control of the incoming air (temperature, humidity, purity) to provide a comfortable thermal environment free from risk of infection.

**Purposes:** replace vitiated air · supply O₂/fresh air · control temperature & humidity · dilute airborne contamination.

### Classification of ventilation
**1) Natural ventilation:**
- **Wind:** perflation (air blows through), **aspiration** (suction action at tail end of an obstruction), cross-ventilation (doors/windows facing each other).
- **Diffusion** — air passes through smallest openings (slow process).
- **Temperature differences** — air flows from high to low density areas.

**2) Mechanical/artificial ventilation:**
- **Exhaust ventilation** (air extracted → vacuum draws fresh air in).
- **Plenum ventilation** (fresh air blown in by centrifugal fans → positive pressure).
- **Balanced ventilation** (combination of exhaust & plenum).
- **Air conditioning** (simultaneous control of temperature, humidity & air movement).

### Why the propulsion (plenum) system is necessary
- Plenum ventilation **blows fresh air in (propels)** creating **positive pressure**, displacing vitiated air — necessary where **natural/exhaust ventilation is insufficient**: deep windowless rooms, **air-conditioned buildings and factories**, sealed offices/hospitals where cross-flow & suction cannot guarantee clean air.

> 🎯 Ventilation = **replacing vitiated with fresh air** · Natural = **wind (perflation/aspiration/cross) + diffusion + temp difference** · Plenum = **positively propelling fresh air in** (factories, AC buildings).*

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 513–514

### Definition of light
> 🔴 **Light** = the **electromagnetic radiation to which the human eye is sensitive**.

**Sources:** natural lighting (visible light, reflection); artificial lighting — 5 systems (**direct, semi-direct, indirect, semi-indirect, direct-indirect**); methods — **filament lamps & fluorescent lamps**.

### Characteristics (criteria) of good lighting
1. **Sufficiency** — adequate for discerning details.
2. **Uniform distribution** — equal intensity over the whole work field.
3. **Absence of glare.**
4. **Absence of sharp shadows.**
5. **Steadiness** (constant source).
6. **Daylight colour** of the light.
7. Appropriate **surroundings/background**.

> 🎯 Light = **visible EM radiation** · Good lighting = **sufficient, uniform, glare-free, no sharp shadow, steady, daylight colour.**

<summary><b>Q7. List the indicators of air pollution. How can you prevent air pollution? (S22,Q3)</b></summary>

### Indicators of air pollution
1. **Sulphur dioxide (SO₂)** — major contaminant in urban & industrial areas; estimated in all surveys.
2. **Smoke / soiling index** — known volume of air filtered through white filter paper, stain measured by photoelectric meter → mg/m³.
3. **Grit and dust.**
4. **Coefficient of haze.**
5. **Air pollution index.**

### Prevention & control of air pollution (WHO strategy)
1. **Containment** — prevent escape of toxic substances (enclosure, ventilation, air cleaning; arrestors).
2. **Replacement** — replace polluting process/fuel with non-polluting technology (lead-free/eco-friendly fuels).
3. **Dilution** — within self-cleaning capacity of environment (vegetation).
4. **Legislation** — enforcement of pollution-control laws (vehicle emission monitoring).
5. **International action** — WHO network of laboratories, monitoring & early warnings.
6. **City measures** (Dhaka): stop two-stroke three-wheelers, lead-free fuel, greening/plantation, shift industries from residential areas, routine air-quality surveillance.

> 🎯 Indicators = **SO₂ + smoke/soiling index + grit-dust + haze + AP index** · Prevention = **containment + replacement + dilution + legislation + international monitoring.**

> 📖 Ch-15, Endeavour PDF pp. 510–512
