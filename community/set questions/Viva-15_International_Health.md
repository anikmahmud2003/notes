## Q2. What are the activities of UNICEF in Bangladesh? (S6,Q7)

### UNICEF — at a glance
- **UNICEF** = United Nations Children's (Emergency) Fund — a specialized agency of the UN, established in **1946** for rehabilitation of children in war-ravaged countries.
- **Headquarters: New York, USA** · Regional Office (South Central Asia): New Delhi, India.
- Objective: address and fulfil the **rights of the child** (Convention on the Rights of the Child, 1991).

### Activities / functions of UNICEF in Bangladesh
**1. In health:**
- Providing **substantial aid for EPI (Expanded Programme on Immunization).**
- Provision of **safe drinking water** and use of **sanitation latrines.**
- Supporting the national **ARI control programme** (early recognition & prompt case management of pneumonia).
- Three focus areas in **women & maternal health:** (i) **emergency obstetric care**; (ii) **women-friendly initiatives** in facilities; (iii) communication campaign on **high MMR and violence against women.**

**2. In nutrition:**
- Helps combat the **three major micronutrient deficiencies** — vitamin A deficiency, iodine deficiency disorders and nutritional anaemias (especially iron deficiency).
- Initiated & supported the **campaign for promotion and protection of breast-feeding.**
- Works with the health consortium in development of the **sector-wide programme.**
- Conducts **joint nutrition surveys** with the Bangladesh Bureau of Statistics.

> 🎯 UNICEF in BD = **EPI + safe water/sanitation + ARI control + maternal health + micronutrients (VAD/IDD/anaemia).**

> 📖 Ch-23, Endeavour PDF pp. 656–657

## Q3. What are the activities of IRC in Bangladesh? (S8,Q7)

### Red Crescent / Red Cross
> The Red Cross/Crescent is a **non-political international humanitarian organization** devoted to the service of mankind in peace and war, founded by **Henry Dunant** (at the 1859 Battle of Solferino). Working bodies: International Committee of Red Cross (1863, wartime) & International Federation of Red Cross & Red Crescent Societies (1919, peacetime).

### Activities of the Red Crescent Society in Bangladesh
1. **5 general hospitals** in the Dhaka city.
2. **6 MCH hospitals** (total bed ~110) in urban areas.
3. **53 maternal & child welfare centres** in rural areas.
4. **Urban & rural charitable dispensaries.**

> 🎯 IRC in BD = **5 general hospitals (Dhaka) · 6 MCH hospitals · 53 MCHW centres · charitable dispensaries.**

> 📖 Ch-23, Endeavour PDF pp. 658–659

## Q4. What are the activities of FAO? (S9,Q6)

### FAO
> **FAO** = "Food and Agricultural Organization" — a specialized agency of the UN; prime concern is the **increased production of food to keep pace with the growing world population.**

### Aims / objectives / activities of FAO
1. To help nations **raise living standards.**
2. To **improve the nutrition** of the people of all countries.
3. To increase the **efficiency of farming, forestry and fisheries.**
4. To **improve the condition of rural people.**

> 🎯 FAO = **food production ↑ + nutrition ↑ + farming/forestry/fisheries efficiency + rural uplift.**

> 📖 Ch-23, Endeavour PDF p. 658

## Q7. Name the health related activities of national NGOs in Bangladesh? (S12,Q7)

### Health-related activities of national NGOs in Bangladesh (example — BRAC)
> 📌 BRAC (started 1972 by **Fazle Hasan Abed**; now the largest national NGO) runs **BRAC Health Programme** — community health programmes include:

1. **Nutrition:**
   - Nutrition education, monitoring and surveillance.
   - Distribution of **vitamin A capsules.**
   - Motivation for cultivation of nutritious food.
2. **Immunization.**
3. **Maternal & child health care:**
   - Registration of pregnant mothers.
   - Birth registration.
   - **Growth monitoring.**
   - Promotion of breast-feeding.
   - **Training of traditional birth attendants (TBA).**
   - Rural sanitation.
   - **Child Survival Programme.**

Other national NGOs (GSK, DAB, BFRP, Red Crescent) similarly run MCH, family planning, diabetes care, and blindness-prevention programmes.

> 🎯 NGO health activities = **immunization · MCH · family planning · nutrition (Vit-A) · sanitation · health education.**

## Q9. List the functions of WHO. (S15,Q4)

### Functions / activities of WHO
1. **Prevention and control of specific diseases:**
   - Global battle against AIDS.
   - Epidemiological surveillance of communicable diseases.
   - Control of communicable & non-communicable diseases.
   - **Immunization against common childhood diseases.**
2. **Development of comprehensive health care** — promotes & supports national health policy and comprehensive national health programmes.
3. **Family health:**
   - Maternal & child health care.
   - Human reproduction.
   - **Nutrition and health education.**
4. **Environmental health:**
   - Advises governments on provision of basic sanitary services.
   - Protection of quality of air, water and soil; occupational health; radiation protection.
   - Early identification of new hazards from new technology.
5. **Health statistics** — dissemination of morbidity & mortality statistics (Weekly Epidemiological Record, World Health Statistics).
6. **Bio-medical research** — stimulates & co-ordinates research (malaria, filariasis, leishmaniasis, leprosy).
7. **Health literature and information** — clearing house for health information; public information service.
8. **Co-operation with other organizations** — WHO collaborates with the UN and other specialized agencies.

> 🎯 WHO functions = **disease control · comprehensive health care · family health · environmental health · statistics · research.**

> 📖 Ch-23, Endeavour PDF pp. 652–653
