## Q1. Name the nail borne disease. (S9,Q1)

Nail-borne (finger-/hand-borne) diseases are those spread through **unclean hands and fingers** — i.e., when people with **dirty nails / poor personal hygiene** transmit infection (the **"fingers"** of the 5 F's — flies, fingers, fomites, food & fluid).

**Diseases transmitted this way:**
1. **Typhoid fever**
2. **Dysentery** (bacillary/amoebic)
3. **Hepatitis A**
4. **Intestinal parasites** (worm infestations)

> 📌 Transmission takes place **both directly and indirectly** through contaminated hands & nails (unclean skin, fingers, nail bite, hands → mouth route).

> 🎯 Nail borne = **"dirty fingers → food/fluid"** → Typhoid, Dysentery, HAV, Worms.

> 📖 Ch-03 (Principles of Epidemiology), Endeavour PDF p. 102 · Ch-19 (Personal Hygiene) — "care of nails regularly"

## Q2. Explain "iceberg" phenomenon of disease. (S11,Q1)

### Definition
> 🔴 It is the representation of a **disease process in a human being in a community in the same manner as an iceberg** — only a small clinical part is visible, while a huge hidden mass of disease remains unseen.

### Parts of the iceberg

| Part | What it represents |
|---|---|
| **1. Floating tip** | What the **physician sees** in the community — **symptomatic clinical cases** |
| **2. Water line** | The **demarcation between apparent and inapparent disease** |
| **3. Submerged (hidden) portion** | The hidden mass of disease: **latent cases, presymptomatic cases, undiagnosed cases, inapparent cases, and carriers** in the community |

### Epidemiological importance
1. The **unknown morbidity** in diseases like **TB, alimentary infections, malnutrition, cancer, diabetes, hypertension** represents the submerged portion — it **exceeds the known (exposed) morbidity**.
2. The submerged hidden part constitutes an important **undiagnosed reservoir of infection/disease** in the community; its **detection and control is a challenge to modern techniques in preventive medicine**.

### Example — Poliomyelitis (iceberg disease)
- **Subclinical infection ~95%** cases → submerged portion.
- **Minor illness (abortive) ~4–8%** cases → water-line.
- **Paralytic polio <1%** cases → floating tip.

> 🎯 **Floating tip = clinical cases (physician sees) · waterline = visible/hidden border · submerged = latent + presymptomatic + carrier.** Polio ≈ 95% subclinical!

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF pp. 59–60

## Q3. What are the time distributions in describing the disease. (S13,Q2)

Time is one of the **three distributions** (time, place, person) used to describe the occurrence of disease. **Time distribution** = how disease varies with time.

### Different diseases predominate at different times (examples)
| Time (season/period) | Disease |
|---|---|
| **Rainy season** | Diarrhoea |
| **Winter** | Respiratory infection |
| **Winter / early spring** | Measles |
| **Summer** | Food poisoning |
| **Weekend** | Motor car accidents |

### Types of time distribution (classical epidemiology)
1. **Secular (long-term) trends** — changes over years/decades (e.g., decline of TB, decline of coronary heart disease).
2. **Cyclic (periodic) trends** — periodic fluctuation of disease (e.g., measles epidemics every 2–3 years).
3. **Seasonal trends** — seasonal variation (e.g., diarrhoea in rainy season, ARI in winter).
4. **Short-term fluctuations (epidemic clustering)** — epidemic curves:
   - **Point source (Holommiantic) epidemic curve** — single exposure, one peak, rises & falls rapidly (e.g., food poisoning).
   - **Propagated (Prosodemic) epidemic curve** — person-to-person over time, gradual rise & tailing off.

> 📌 An epidemic **curve** = graph of the **time distribution of epidemic cases**.

> 🎯 Time distribution = **secular (long) → cyclic (periodic) → seasonal → epidemic (short-term).**

> 📖 Ch-03 (Principles of Epidemiology), Endeavour PDF pp. 72, 95–96

## Q4. Name the host factors in disease causation. (S15,Q2)

### Host
> A person or other animal (including birds and arthropods) that affords **subsistence or lodgment to an infectious agent** under natural conditions.

### Host (demographic, biological, socio-economic & life-style) factors in disease causation

| Category | Factor examples |
|---|---|
| **1. Demographic characteristics** | Age, Sex, Ethnicity |
| **2. Biological characteristics** | Genetic factors; biochemical levels of blood (e.g., cholesterol); blood groups; enzymes; blood pressure |
| **3. Social & economic characteristics** | Socio-economic status, education, occupation, marital status, housing |
| **4. Life-style factors** | Living habits; food habit (→ colon cancer); exercise (↓ hypertension, heart disease); smoking (→ lung cancer); alcohol use |

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF p. 54

## Q5. Classify rehabilitation with example. (S16,Q1)

### Rehabilitation (definition)
> 📌 Rehabilitation = the **combined and coordinated use of medical, social, educational and vocational measures** for training and re-training the individual to the **highest possible level of functional ability**.

### Classification with examples

| Type | Aim | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Medical rehabilitation** | Restoration of function | Provision of **aids for the crippled**; reconstructive surgery in leprosy |
| **2. Vocational rehabilitation** | Restoration of the capacity to earn a livelihood | **Establishing schools for the blind**; vocational training for the disabled |
| **3. Social rehabilitation** | Restoration of family and social relationships | Re-integration into family & community life |
| **4. Psychological rehabilitation** | Restoration of personal dignity and confidence | Counselling, confidence-building |

> 🎯 **Medical (function) → Vocational (livelihood) → Social (relationships) → Psychological (dignity).**

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF p. 65

## Q6. What do you mean by disease, sickness & illness? (S17,Q1)

| Term | Meaning (Susser's view) |
|---|---|
| **Disease** | Literally means **"without ease" (uneasiness)** — something wrong with body (physiological/psychological) function. = a **physiological / psychological dysfunction**. Oxford Dictionary: "a condition of the body or some part/organ of the body in which its functions are disrupted or deranged." Ecologically: **maladjustment of the human organism to the environment** |
| **Illness** | Refers not only to presence of a specific disease, but also to the individual's **perceptions & behaviour in response to the disease**, plus the impact of that disease on the **psychosocial environment**. = a **subjective state** — the person feels aware of not being well |
| **Sickness** | Refers to a **state of social dysfunction** — a **role that the individual assumes when ill ("sickness role")** |

> 🎯 **Disease = objective dysfunction · Illness = subjective feeling of being unwell · Sickness = social role dysfunction.**

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF p. 51

## Q7. Define and classify agent. List host characteristics. (S20,Q1)

### Agent (definition)
> 🔴 Agent = **a substance, living or non-living, or a force, tangible or intangible, the excessive presence or relative lack of which may initiate or perpetuate a disease process.**

**Characteristics of agent:** can be living or non-living; biological agents exhibit **infectivity, pathogenicity, virulence**; absence/insufficiency/excess of a factor (chemical/nutrient) necessary to health; route of entry; incubation period; generation time; case/carrier.

### Classification of agents

| # | Agent | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Biological agents** (living) | Viruses, Rickettsiae, Fungi, Bacteria, Protozoa, Metazoa |
| **2. Nutrient agents** | Proteins, fats, carbohydrates, vitamins, minerals, water (excess/deficiency → nutritional disorders) |
| **3. Physical agents** | Heat, cold, radiation, electricity, sound, pressure |
| **4. Chemical agents** | **Endogenous:** urea → uraemia; uric acid → gout; bilirubin → jaundice; ketones → ketosis. **Exogenous:** allergens, metals, fumes, dusts, gases, insecticides |
| **5. Mechanical agents** | Chronic friction, crushing/tearing force, (→ accidents) |
| **6. Absence/insufficiency/excess of factors necessary to health** | Chemical factors (hormones, enzymes); nutrients (protein, fat); lack of a part of structure (cardiac defects); chromosomal (Turner's syndrome); immunological (agammaglobulinaemia) |
| **7. Social agents** | Poverty → deficiency disorders; smoking → lung cancer; drug & alcohol abuse → cirrhosis; unhealthy life-style |

### Host & host characteristics
**Host** = a person or other animal (including birds and arthropods) that affords subsistence/lodgment to an infectious agent.

**Host characteristics / host factors:**
| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Demographic** | Age, sex, ethnicity |
| **Biological** | Genetic factors, blood cholesterol, blood groups, enzymes, blood pressure |
| **Social & economic** | SES, education, occupation, marital status, housing |
| **Life-style** | Living habits, food habit, exercise, smoking, alcohol |

> 🎯 Agents = **Biological, Nutrient, Physical, Chemical, Mechanical, Deficiency/excess, Social — "BNPCMD-S".** Host = **4 D's: Demographic, Biological, Social, Life-style.**

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF pp. 54–55
