<summary><b>Q1. Mention the levels of delivery of health care. What are the principles of primary health care? (S15,Q1)</b></summary>

### Levels of delivery of health care
Health services are usually organized at **three levels**, each supported by a higher level to which the patient is **referred**:

| Level | What is provided | Institution (Bangladesh) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Primary health care** | First level of contact; **essential health care** (PHC) | Upazila Health Complex (UHC), Union Health & Family Welfare Centres, Union sub-centres |
| **2. Secondary health care** | More complex, **essentially curative** services; **first referral level** | District hospitals |
| **3. Tertiary health care** | **Super-specialist** care; referral support to primary & secondary levels | Regional / central institutions (medical college hospitals, specialized hospitals) |
| (4. Super-specialized) | National-level super-specialized institutions | National institutes (e.g., NICR, NICVD, NITOR etc.) |

### Principles of primary health care (PHC)
PHC is based on **4 principles**:
1. **Equitable distribution** of health services.
2. **Community participation** at all levels.
3. **Inter-sectoral coordination / Multi-sectoral approach.**
4. **Appropriate technology.**

> 🎯 **"E-C-I-A"** = Equitable distribution · Community participation · Inter-sectoral coordination · Appropriate technology.

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF pp. 44–45, 48

<summary><b>Q2. Define primary health care. List the elements/components of PHC? (S21,Q1)</b></summary>

### Definition
> 🔴 PHC = **"Essential health care based on practical, scientifically sound and socially acceptable methods and technology, made universally accessible to individuals and families in the community through their full participation and at a cost that the community and the country can afford to maintain at every stage of their development in the spirit of self-determination."** (Alma-Ata Declaration, 1978)

### Components / elements of PHC (Alma-Ata — at least 8)
1. **Education** about prevailing health problems and methods of preventing & controlling them.
2. Promotion of **food supply & proper nutrition**.
3. An adequate supply of **safe water and basic sanitation**.
4. **Maternal & child health care**, including **family planning**.
5. **Immunization** against infectious diseases.
6. **Prevention & control of locally endemic diseases**.
7. **Appropriate treatment** of common diseases & injuries.
8. Provision of **essential drugs**.

> 🎯 Mnemonic — **"E-L-E-M-E-N-T-S"**:
> **E**ducation about health problems and control · **L**ocal disease control · **E**PI (immunization) · **M**CH including FP · **E**ssential drugs · **N**utrition/food supply · **T**reatment of common diseases/injuries · **S**afe water & basic sanitation.

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF p. 47

<summary><b>Q3. How primary health care was incepted? Explain equitable distribution of health service. (S22,Q1)</b></summary>

### Inception / background of PHC
- The concept of PHC came into limelight in **1978** following the **International Conference on Primary Health Care in Alma-Ata (former USSR, now Kazakhstan)**.
- It was accepted by member countries of WHO as the **key to achieving the goal of "Health for All by the year 2000 AD."**
- Before Alma-Ata, PHC was regarded as synonymous with "basic health service", "first-contact care", "easily accessible care", "services provided by generalists". **Alma-Ata broadened the earlier restrictive concept**, gave it a **political dimension**, and declared the **strategy** for achieving Health for All.

### Equitable distribution of health service
> 📌 Health services must be **shared equally by all people, irrespective of their ability to pay** — rich or poor, urban or rural must have access to health services (e.g., PHC services should be given **according to priority of needs** — to the needy and vulnerable groups in rural areas and urban slums).

**Implementation in Bangladesh:**
- Health care is delivered all over the country by the government because the most vulnerable groups are **urban slums and rural people**.
- The aim is to **shift the gravity of the health-care system from cities to rural areas (where ¾ of the people live)** and bring services as near to people's homes as possible.
- **Thana/Upazila Health Complex, Union Health Centres and Satellite Clinics** deliver equitable health services successfully.

> 🎯 Difference: **Equitable = according to need (priority to vulnerable) vs equal = same amount to everyone.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 47–48 · Ch-01 (Introduction), Endeavour PDF p. 24

<summary><b>Q4. What are the principles of PHC? Explain community participation. (S23,Q1)</b></summary>

### Principles of PHC (4)
1. Equitable distribution of health services. 2. Community participation at all levels. 3. Inter-sectoral coordination / Multi-sectoral approach. 4. Appropriate technology.

*(Details in Q1 / reverse side ←)*

### Community participation — explanation
> 📌 Defined as **"the process by which individuals and families assume responsibility for their own health and welfare and for those of the community, and develop the capacity to contribute to their and the community's development."**

- Involvement of individuals, families & communities in **promotion of their own health and welfare** is an essential ingredient of PHC.
- Community involvement also implies participation in the **planning, implementation and maintenance of health services**.
- **Example:** To make the **EPI successful in Bangladesh**, community participation has a great role.

**Importance/advantages of community participation:**
1. Leads to a **sense of responsibility** for the project.
2. **More will be accomplished.**
3. Guarantees that a **felt need** is involved.
4. Services can be provided at a **lower rate**.
5. Has an **intrinsic value** for the participants.
6. Acts as a **catalyst for further development** effort.

> 🎯 PAHE = **P**lanning + **A**ct (implementation) + **H**elp (maintenance) + **E**xample (EPI in BD).

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF pp. 48–49

<summary><b>Q5. Mention the levels of health care delivery in BD. What do you mean by intersectoral coordination? Give an example? (S25,Q1)</b></summary>

### Levels of health care delivery in Bangladesh
**Primary (UHC + Union H&FWC/sub-centres) → Secondary (District hospitals = first referral level) → Tertiary (super-specialist; medical college/regional hospitals) → Super-specialized national institutes.**
*(Full table in Q1.)*

### Inter-sectoral coordination / Multi-sectoral approach
> 📌 The components of PHC **cannot be provided by the health sector alone**; PHC involves, in addition to the health sector, **all health-related sectors** — Agriculture, Animal husbandry, Food, Industry, Education, Housing, Public health engineering, Social welfare.

- An important element of the inter-sectoral approach is **planning with other sectors to avoid unnecessary duplication of activities**.

### Example
> To provide an **adequate supply of safe water and basic sanitation** for a community, the **coordinated approach of the Health Department, Public Health Engineering Department and Information Department** are needed together.

> 🎯 Inter-sectoral = **health sector + agriculture + education + housing + PHE + welfare...** — Example: safe water & sanitation (Health + PHE + Information depts).

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF pp. 44–45, 48–49
