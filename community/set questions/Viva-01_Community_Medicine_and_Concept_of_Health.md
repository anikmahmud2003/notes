## Q1. Define community medicine. What do you mean by community diagnosis? (S1,Q1)

### Community Medicine

**Community medicine** has been defined by a **WHO technical committee (1972)** as —

> 🔴 **"A system of delivery of comprehensive health care to the people by a health team in order to improve the health of the community."**

**Comprehensive health care** = provision of **preventive, promotive, curative & rehabilitative** care to the people **from womb to the tomb**.

### Community diagnosis

> 📌 Community diagnosis = *"the pattern of disease in a community described in terms of the important factors which influence this pattern."*
> Or — the **identification and quantification of health problems in a community** in terms of mortality & morbidity rates and ratios, and identification of their correlates to define **individuals/groups at risk** or those **in need of health care**.

**Data needed for community diagnosis:**
1. Age & sex distribution of population; distribution by social groups.
2. Vital statistical rates (birth rate, death rate).
3. Incidence & prevalence of important diseases in the community.

> 🎯 Community medicine = **comprehensive care (P + P + C + R) to a community by a health team; diagnosis = pattern of disease + at-risk groups.**

> 📖 Ch-01 (Introduction to the Community Medicine), Endeavour PDF pp. 16 · Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF pp. 65–66

## Q2. What is community diagnosis & treatment? (S2,Q1)

**Community diagnosis** — as above (Q1): pattern of disease in a community described in terms of factors that influence it; identification & quantification of health problems + their correlates.

**Community treatment / community health action** =
> 📌 The **sum of steps decided upon to meet the health needs of the community**, taking into account the **resources available and the wishes of the people**, as revealed by community diagnosis.

**Components / requirements:**
1. Must **effectively utilize all available resources**.
2. Must **coordinate with efforts of other agencies** in the community (inter-sectoral coordination).
3. Must encourage **full participation of the community** in the programme.

**Examples:** Improvement of water supply, immunization, health education, control of specific diseases, health legislation.

> 🎯 Diagnosis = problem + at-risk groups; Treatment = **action plan using resources + community wishes + inter-sectoral coordination.**

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF pp. 65–66

## Q3. Define health and mention its dimensions? (S3,Q1)

### Definition of Health (WHO)

> 🔴 **"Health is a state of complete physical, mental & social well-being and not merely an absence of disease or infirmity, so that each citizen can lead a socially and economically productive life. Now spiritual well-being has been added in this definition."**

**Criticism of WHO definition (For Viva):**
1. Too broad.
2. Health cannot be defined as a "state" at all — must be seen as a **process** of adjustment to changing demands of living.
3. It is a **dynamic concept**.
4. It helps people live well, work well and enjoy themselves.
5. Considered by many as an **idealistic goal** rather than a realistic proposition.

### Dimensions of health

| **Major dimensions** | **Minor dimensions** |
|---|---|
| 1. Physical | 1. Emotional |
| 2. Mental | 2. Vocational |
| 3. Social | 3. Others: Philosophical, Cultural, Socio-economic, Educational, Environmental, Nutritional, Curative, Preventive |
| 4. Spiritual | |

> 🎯 **4 major (P, M, S, Spiritual) + Emotional & Vocational minors** — rest are "others".

> 📖 Ch-01 (Introduction), Endeavour PDF p. 27 (definition + criticism) · Ch-02, Endeavour PDF p. 29 (dimensions)

## Q4. Major health indicators. (S4,Q1)

**Health indicator** = tools with which we measure the health status of a community.

### Major categories of health indicators

| # | Indicator group | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Mortality indicators** | Crude death rate, expectation of life, IMR, under-5 proportionate mortality, MMR, disease-specific mortality, proportional mortality rate |
| **2** | **Morbidity indicators** | Incidence & prevalence, notification rates, OPD attendance rates, admission/re-admission/discharge rates, duration of hospital stay, spells of sickness / absence from work-school |
| **3** | **Disability rates** | Event-type (days of restricted activity, bed disability days, work-loss days) · Person-type (limitation of mobility, limitation of activity) |
| **4** | **Nutritional status indicators** | Anthropometric measurements of pre-school children, heights at school entry, prevalence of LBW |
| **5** | **Health care delivery indicators** | Doctor-population ratio, doctor-nurse ratio, population-bed ratio, population per health centre, population per TBA |
| **6** | **Utilization rates** | % infants fully immunized (6 → now 8 EPI diseases), % pregnant women with ANC/trained delivery, % using family planning, bed-occupancy rate, average length of stay, patient turnover ratio |
| **7** | **Indicators of social & mental health** | Homicide rate, suicide rate, drug/alcohol abuse, family violence, battered baby/wife syndromes, RTA rates, smoking rate |
| **8** | **Environmental indicators** | % population with safe water supply, safe sanitation, air/water/radiation pollution indices |
| **9** | **Socio-economic indicators** | Population increase rate, per capita GNP, unemployment, dependency ratio, literacy (esp. female), family size, persons per room, per capita calorie availability |
| **10** | **Health policy indicators** | % GNP on health services, % GNP on health-related activities, % total health resources to PHC |
| **11** | **Indicators of quality of life** | IMR, life expectancy at age one, literacy |
| **12** | **Other indicators** | Social, basic-needs (calorie, water access), Health-for-All indicators |

> 🎯 Remember order: **Mortality → Morbidity → Disability → Nutrition → Delivery → Utilization → Social/mental → Environment → Socio-economic → Policy → QoL → Others.**

> 📖 Ch-02 (Concept of Health & Disease), Endeavour PDF pp. 36–38

## Q5. Characteristics of physical, mental & social health? (S5,Q1)

### Physical health
> 🔴 **Perfect functioning of the body; every cell & organ functioning at optimum capacity.**

**Signs of a physically healthy person:**
Good complexion · Clean skin · Bright eyes · Good appetite · Sound sleep · Regular activity of bowel & bladder · Smooth, easy & coordinated movement · All special senses intact.

### Mental health
> 📌 "A state of **balance between the individual and the surrounding world**, a state of harmony between oneself & others, a co-existence between the realities of the self, of other people and of the environment."

**Criteria of a mentally healthy person:**
1. Free from internal conflicts.
2. Well adjusted with family and community.
3. Awareness of one's identity.
4. Strong sense of self-esteem.
5. Knows himself — his needs, problems & goals.
6. Good self-control.
7. Faces problems and tries to solve them intelligently.

### Social health
- **Harmony** with other members of society.
- Proper **social functioning (social response)**.

> 🎯 Physical = **perfect body function (8 signs)** · Mental = **balance/harmony, well-adjusted (7 criteria)** · Social = **harmony + social functioning.**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 29–30

## Q6. What are changing concept of health? (S6,Q1)

### The 4 changing concepts

**1) Biomedical concept** — A person was healthy if he was **free from disease / absence of disease**; based on the **germ theory of disease**.
- **Criticism:** role of environmental, social, psychological & cultural determinants of health is ignored.

**2) Ecological concept** — Health = **dynamic equilibrium between man & his environment**; disease = maladjustment of the human organism to environment.
- Raises two issues: **Imperfect man** and **Imperfect environment**.

**3) Psychosocial concept** — Health is not only a biomedical phenomenon but is influenced by **social, psychological, cultural, economic & political factors**.
- Social factors: poverty, illiteracy, habits & life style (smoking, drugs, multiple sex partners).
- Psychological factors: anxiety, tension, emotional breakdown.

**4) Holistic concept** — Synthesis of all the above; implies that **all sectors of society have an effect on health** (agriculture, animal husbandry, food, industry, education, housing, public works, communication, etc.). Emphasis is on **promotion and protection of health**.

> 🎯 **Biomedical (germ theory) → Ecological (man–environment equilibrium) → Psychosocial (social/mental factors) → Holistic (all sectors).** Holistic = most acceptable concept.

> 📖 Ch-01 (Introduction), Endeavour PDF pp. 27–28

## Q7. What do you mean by positive health? (S7,Q1)

> 🔴 **Positive health** = the state which implies the notion of **"perfect functioning" of the body & mind**; it conceptualizes health **biologically, psychologically and socially**.

A person who enjoys health at **three planes — good physical, mental and social well-being** — is said to be in a state of **positive health**.

> 📌 Note: "The concept of perfect positive health can't become a reality because man will never be so perfectly adapted to his environment that his life will not involve struggles, failures & sufferings."

> 🎯 Positive health = **perfect functioning of body + mind at 3 planes (physical, mental, social).**

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 30–31

## Q8. What is spectrum of health and disease? Enumerate the dimensions of health. (S8,Q1)

### Spectrum of health / health-sickness spectrum
> 📌 The spectral concept of health denotes that **health of an individual is not static** — it fluctuates within a range varying from **optimum well-being** to various levels of dysfunction, including total dysfunction (**death**). The transition from good health to ill health is often **gradual**; where one step ends and another begins is a matter of judgment. The systematic representation of these levels = **health–sickness spectrum**.

**Steps (from top to bottom):**
1. Positive health
2. Better health
3. Freedom from sickness
4. Unrecognized sickness
5. Mild sickness
6. Severe sickness
7. **Death** (lowest point)

> 🔴 Lowest point = **death**; highest point corresponds to the **WHO definition of positive health**.

### Dimensions of health
**Major:** Physical, Mental, Social, Spiritual. **Minor:** Emotional, Vocational; others (philosophical, cultural, socio-economic, educational, environmental, nutritional, curative, preventive). *(Full detail in Q3).*

> 🎯 Specturm = **+ve health → better → free → unrecognized → mild → severe → death.** Health = not static, dynamic.

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 32–33 & p. 29

## Q9. List the determinants of health. (S9,Q1)

**Determinants of health** = factors which influence health, lying both **within the individual and externally in the society** in which he/she lives.

1. **Biological determinants** — genetic traits (chromosomal anomalies, inborn errors of metabolism, mental retardation, some diabetes).
2. **Behavioural & socio-cultural conditions** — way people live, cultural pattern, personal habits, life-style (harmful: smoking; healthy: adequate nutrition, enough sleep, sufficient physical activity).
3. **Environment** —
   - *Internal:* tissue, organ & organ system.
   - *External:* **Physical** (air, water, soil pollution, heat, light, radiation, sound, food, excreta & refuse, housing) · **Biological** (human being, plants, animals, micro-organisms, insects, birds) · **Psychosocial** (customs, belief, moral, occupation, religion, education, culture).
4. **Socio-economic conditions** — economic status (GNP), education (illiteracy → poverty → malnutrition → ill health → maternal, child & infant mortality), occupation, political system.
5. **Health services** — comprehensive, equitable, reach periphery, accessible to all, socially acceptable and affordable.
6. **Aging of the population** — rapid aging → ↑ chronic illness & disability.
7. **Gender** — women's health is an important societal issue.
8. **Other factors** — health-related systems: food & agriculture, education, industry, social welfare, rural development.

> 🎯 **Biological + Behavioural + Environment + Socio-economic + Health services + Aging + Gender + Others** (mnemonic: "BEESSAGO").

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 33–34

## Q10. Who are responsible for health. How can you promote the health of a community? (S10,Q1)

### Who are responsible for health
Health is on one hand a highly **personal responsibility** and on the other a major **public concern** — it involves the joint efforts of the whole social fabric:

| Responsibility | Points |
|---|---|
| **1. Individual** | Primarily a person is responsible for his/her own health; observance of simple rules of behaviour (diet, sleep, exercise); attention to personal hygiene |
| **2. Community** | Can provide facilities, manpower, logistic support & funds; involvement in planning, management & evaluation of health programmes; joining and using health services |
| **3. State** | Health is a constitutional right; Govt. is legally bound to provide health care to every citizen |
| **4. International** | Coordination of different governments (e.g. smallpox eradication); WHO = central coordinating agency; UNICEF = another coordinating agency |

### How to promote the health of a community
From the concept of **community treatment / community health action**:
1. **Effectively utilize all available resources.**
2. **Coordinate with the efforts of other agencies** in the community (inter-sectoral coordination).
3. **Encourage full participation of the community** in the programme.
4. Health promotion tools: immunization, nutritional intervention, life-style & behavioural change, health education, safe water supply, improvement of sanitation, control of specific diseases, health legislation.

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 35 & 66

## Q12. Ideal characteristics of a health indicator. What are the common mortality indicators used in health sector? (S12,Q1)

### Characteristics / criteria of an ideal health indicator
1. **Valid** — actually measures what it is supposed to measure.
2. **Reliable & objective** — same answers if measured by different people in similar circumstances.
3. **Sensitive** — sensitive to changes in the situation concerned.
4. **Specific** — reflects changes only in the situation concerned.
5. **Feasible** — ability to obtain the data needed.
6. **Relevant** — contributes to understanding of the phenomenon of interest.

### Common mortality indicators used in health sector
1. **Crude death rate** — number of deaths per 1000 per year (BD CDR ≈ 4.8/1000/year).
2. **Expectation of life** — average years lived by those born alive if current age-specific mortality persists (BD ≈ 61 years).
3. **Infant mortality rate** — infant deaths per 1000 live births in one year (BD IMR ≈ 57/1000).
4. **Child mortality rate** — deaths at ages 1–4 years per 1000 children in that age group.
5. **Under-5 proportionate mortality rate.**
6. **Maternal mortality rate** — maternal deaths per 1000 live births (BD MMR ≈ 3/1000).
7. **Disease-specific mortality** — deaths from specific diseases (cancer, CVD, communicable diseases, accidents, diabetes).
8. **Proportional mortality rate.**

> 🎯 Ideal indicator = **V-R-S-S-F-R** (Valid, Reliable, Sensitive, Specific, Feasible, Relevant).

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 35–38

## Q13. What is health care? List the ideal characteristics of health care. (S14,Q1)

### Health care
> 📌 **"A multitude of services rendered to individuals, families or communities by the agents of the health services or professions, for the purpose of promoting, maintaining, monitoring or restoring health."**

### Characteristics of ideal health care
1. **Appropriateness (relevance)** — whether the service is needed at all in relation to essential human needs, priorities and policies.
2. **Comprehensiveness** — optimum mixture of preventive, curative and promotional services.
3. **Adequacy** — service is proportionate to requirement.
4. **Availability** — ratio between population of an administrative unit and the health facility (population per centre, doctor-population ratio).
5. **Accessibility** — geographic, economic or cultural accessibility.
6. **Affordability** — cost within the means of the individual and the state.
7. **Feasibility** — operational efficiency of procedures, logistic support, manpower & material resources.

> 🎯 Ideal health care = **A-C-A-A-A-A-F** (Appropriate, Comprehensive, Adequate, Available, Accessible, Affordable, Feasible).

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF p. 43

## Q14. Define health team and mention its importance. (S16,Q1)

### Definition
> 🔴 Health team = **"a group of persons who share a common health goal and common objectives, determined by community needs and toward the achievement of which each member of the team contributes in accordance with his/her competence and skills, and respecting the functions of the others."**

### Structure of health team
1. **Health professionals** (full professional training): Physician (doctor).
2. **Auxiliary staff** (suitable training, less than full professional qualification, supervised by professionals): Nurses, Pharmacist, Medical assistant, Lab technician, Radiographer.
3. **Ancillary staff**: Sweeper, Ambulance driver, Cook, etc.

### Importance
- **Teamwork is extremely necessary** for comprehensive health care (curve of community medicine vs clinical medicine: teamwork essential in community medicine; needed only occasionally in clinical medicine, e.g., operation/emergency).
- Each member contributes **according to competence & skill** → maximum utilization of available skills & resources.
- Compensates for shortage of qualified professionals by using **auxiliaries** in the periphery (essential for PHC/equitable distribution of services).

> 🎯 Health team = **common goal + each member contributes by competence + respect others' functions.** Structure: professionals → auxiliaries → ancillary.

> 📖 Ch-01 (Introduction to the Community Medicine), Endeavour PDF p. 23

## Q16. Define ecology and ecological triad. (S19,Q1)

### Ecology
> 📌 **Ecology** = "the science of **mutual relationship between living organisms and their environments**."

**Human ecology** is a subject of the more general science of ecology — humanity is seen as part of an **ecosystem** (natural environment + all dimensions of the man-made environment: physical, chemical, biological, psychological — i.e., culture and all its products).

**Health (ecological concept)** = a state of **dynamic equilibrium between man and his environment.**

### Ecological / epidemiological triad
> 🔴 This concept states that for a disease process to occur there must be a **unique combination of events: a harmful agent that comes into contact with a susceptible host in a proper environment.**

| Component | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Agent** | Biological, nutrient, chemical, physical, mechanical, social agent |
| **Host** | Human (age, sex, genetic, immunity, nutrition, behaviour) |
| **Environment** | Physical, biological, psychosocial |

The occurrence of a disease can be **blocked by intersecting the triangle at any of its three sides** or at any linkage between the relevant factors.

> 🎯 **Agent + Host + Environment = triangle.** Interrupt ANY side → prevent disease.

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 34 & 53

## Q17. Explain comprehensive health care. (S27,Q1)

### Definition
The term **"comprehensive health care"** was first used by the **Bhore Committee (1946)**.

> 🔴 = **"The provision of integrated preventive, curative, promotive and rehabilitative health care from the womb to the tomb to every individual residing in a defined geographic area."**

### Criteria (Bhore Committee)
1. Provide adequate preventive, curative and promotive health services.
2. Be as close to the beneficiaries as possible.
3. Have the widest co-operation between the people, the service and the profession.
4. Available to all irrespective of their ability to pay.
5. Look after specifically the vulnerable and weaker sections of the community.
6. Create and maintain a healthy environment — in homes and working places.

### Objectives
1. Reduction of **premature death** · 2. Reduction of **disease** · 3. Reduction of **discomfort** · 4. Reduction of **delinquency** · 5. Reduction of **disruption**.

### Examples of the components
| Component | Example |
|---|---|
| **Preventive** | Prevention of diseases by vaccines, e.g., prevention of poliomyelitis |
| **Curative** | Treatment of diseases, e.g., anti-tubercular drug therapy for TB |
| **Promotive** | Health promotion by environmental & personal sanitation |
| **Rehabilitative** | Medical (restoration of function) · Social (family & social relationships) · Vocational (capacity to earn a livelihood) · Psychological (personal dignity & confidence) |

> 🎯 Comprehensive care = **P (preventive) + C (curative) + P (promotive) + R (rehabilitative), womb to tomb, defined area.** First coined by Bhore Committee.

> 📖 Ch-02, Endeavour PDF pp. 46–47

## Q19. State about Family Medicine. (S30,Q1)

**Family medicine** — it may be defined as:

> 📌 "A **field of specialization in medicine** which is neither disease-specific nor age-specific; it is **family-oriented medicine or health care centered on the family as the unit** from ... the ongoing care of chronic problems (**from prevention to rehabilitation**)."

**Key points:**
- It treats the **patient as a member of a family** and cares for the whole family together.
- It is neither **organ-based** nor limited by age/sex — it covers the family across the life cycle.
- Provides **first-contact, continuing, comprehensive and coordinated** care (from health promotion/prevention to rehabilitation).

> 🎯 Family medicine = **family as the unit · from prevention to rehabilitation · ongoing care of chronic problems.**

> 📖 Ch-01 (Introduction to the Community Medicine), Endeavour PDF p. 17
